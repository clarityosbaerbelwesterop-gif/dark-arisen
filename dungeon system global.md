## 3. HIGHMOORE — THE BARROWS

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_region.md Section 6.1, the_lesser_houses.md Section 4.5, highmoore_populations.md.

### 3.1 The Regional Identity

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 5 — each region has a puzzle language.

**HIGHMOORE'S IS THE PRACTICES.**

Cross-reference light_elves.md Section 4, highmoore_weather.md Section 7.2 — the region has a body of customary rules about hours, places, charms, fog, and light, **which every person in the basin observes and denies believing in.**

**Design law:** cross-reference wizard_questline.md Section 4.4 — **the barrows are locked by those rules.** A player who paid attention to farmers gets in. A player who dismissed them as flavour does not.

### 3.2 The Six

**THE LONG BARROW AT BRAEHEAD** *(Tier B)*
**Discovery:** relational — cross-reference the_lesser_houses.md Section 4.5: **House Brae keeps Highmoore's oldest records and cannot read most of them**, and Lord Ewan will let anyone try.
**The Image:** the records continue *inside* the barrow, carved, in the same script, **and they are legible in there and not outside.**
**The Boss:** none. Cross-reference Section 6.1.

**THE DROWNED MILL** *(Tier B)*
Waterlands. Cross-reference physics/water_physics.md, the_lesser_houses.md Section 2.2.
**The Image:** the wheel still turns underwater, driven by the current, and has for eighty years.
**The Boss:** **The Miller.** Cross-reference colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 6.2 (Remnants).

**THE STONE ROW** *(Tier B)*
High Moor. Cross-reference light_elves.md Section 7.1.
**Hazard:** fog. Cross-reference highmoore_weather.md Section 7 — **and the practice for fog is stop moving, sit down, wait**, and it is the only Highmoore rule nobody denies, and **inside the Stone Row it is also the solution.**

**THE ASHBY CROSSING** *(Tier B)*
Beneath the contested fields. Cross-reference the_lesser_houses.md Section 3.1 — **eleven changes of hand in forty years and each occupier dug.**
**The Image:** four separate periods of military tunnelling, intersecting, none of them aware of the others.
**The Boss:** **The Third Dig.**

**THE BOWYER'S WOOD** *(Tier B)*
Cross-reference house_belos.md Section 3.2, highmoore_smiths.md Section 3.2 — **Gruffydd's yew, seasoned two winters, in a wood that has been managed for four hundred years.**
**The Image:** a stand of yew whose oldest trees were planted by people whose names are on the boundary stones.
**Post-questline:** cross-reference princess_quest_belos.md — **the wood is untouched and the man who used it is under a wall.**

**THE UNDER-CHAMBER AT HALFORD** *(Tier C)*
Cross-reference highmoore_settlements.md Section 5 — beneath the abbey.
**The Image:** four generations of hostage-guesting records — cross-reference house_system.md Section 4.1 — and cross-reference highmoore_settlements.md Section 5.4: **Elowen Arion's eighteen months are in there in a fair hand with a date and nothing else**, and a player can find that entry before ever meeting her.

## 4. THE CRYSTAL CAVES — A CATEGORY OF ONE

**Cross-reference:** crystal_caves.md.

### 4.1 It Breaks the Length Rule

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Principle 1.2 — *"fifteen to forty-five minutes."*

**The Crystal Caves are ninety minutes to two hours.** Cross-reference crystal_caves.md Section 1.3.

### 4.2 Why It Is Permitted

**Cross-reference:** crystal_caves.md Section 1.1 — **it is not a dungeon. It is a door with a length**, and its function is to make the player unprepared for one frame.

### 4.3 It Is Not a Precedent

**Design law:** **no other dungeon in Dark Arisen exceeds 90 minutes**, and the Tier C ceiling stays at 90. Cross-reference colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 2.3.

## 5. THE DOCTRINE, GLOBALLY

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Sections 3, 4, and 8.

### 5.1 No Markers

**Cross-reference:** quest_activation.md Section 2.4. **41 named dungeons and 20 minor sites, none of them on any map until entered**, except the cartographic ones. Cross-reference treasure_system.md Section 5.

### 5.2 No Music

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 8.1, cutscene_catalog.md Section 7.1.

**None. In any of them.** Score enters at a boss and at an unforgettable image, and nowhere else.

### 5.3 No Explanations

No audio logs. No journals. No survivor who narrates. Cross-reference colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 8.2 — **three exceptions in sixty-one sites**, all of them people who have been down there too long.

### 5.4 The Return Shortcut Is Mandatory

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 4.1.

**Every Tier B and above opens a route back from the inside.** No exceptions.

### 5.5 No Child Remains

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 8.3, npcs/children_and_families.md.

**In sixty-one sites, in any variant, ever.**

## 6. THE TWO CARVE-OUTS

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Principle 1.5 — *"something waits at the bottom."*

### 6.1 THE ONES WITH NO BOSS

**Four sites in the game have nothing at the bottom.**

1. **The Stone Field's Under-Chamber** (Fjordlund) — cross-reference colonial_war/regional_dungeon_catalog.md Section 3.4: **thousands of names for the sea-taken, carved down the walls, going back.**
2. **The Long Barrow at Braehead** (Highmoore) — Section 3.2.
3. **The First House** (Region 06) — cross-reference colonial_war/regional_dungeon_catalog.md Section 7.2.
4. **The Fifth Connection Site** — cross-reference colonial_war/secret_bosses.md Section 6.4: **no fight, in any branch, under any circumstances.**

### 6.2 THE ONE THAT GIVES NOTHING

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/regional_dungeon_catalog.md Section 7.2, treasure_system.md Section 6.3.

**The First House.** A full dungeon, completable, with no hazard, no boss, and **no reward of any kind.**

**Design law:** cross-reference colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 8.2 — **the game lets the player finish it and receive nothing and never explains.**

## 7. THE BUILD LIST

**Cross-reference:** technical_targets.md.

### 7.1 MVP CORE — 11 sites

Three archipelago Tier B (one each Rexan, Fjordlund, Ashenmoor) · one Tier C (the Hollow City) · **two Highmoore barrows** · **the Crystal Caves** · two colonial · two Tier E.

**Establishes:** four of five puzzle languages · all four discovery modes · the no-marker, no-music, no-explanation discipline · the Return shortcut · both carve-outs represented once.

### 7.2 FULL VISION — 61 sites

All 41 named, all 20 minor, all five puzzle languages including Region 06's unreliable grammar, the full map chain, and all six secret sites.

### 7.3 The Cost

**Cross-reference:** technical_targets.md Section 4.1.

Nanite-heavy, low crowd, low physics. **Dungeons are the cheapest content per hour in Dark Arisen** and cross-reference Section 5.2: **they have no music budget at all.**

## 8. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1:** near-total silence across sixty-one sites, broken by three people who have been underground too long and one lord who cannot read his own records.
- ✅ **Pillar 2:** an abbey under-chamber holding four generations of which child slept in whose hall — including one entry a player can read before they ever meet her.
- ✅ **Pillar 3:** four sites with nothing at the bottom, one of which is thousands of names for the sea-taken going down and back, and no child remains anywhere in sixty-one places.
- ✅ **Pillar 4:** absent by design across the entire layer, which is why the ship afterwards works.
- ✅ **Pillar 5:** Highmoore's dungeons are locked by superstitions its own people deny believing in, and a player who dismissed a farmer cannot get in.

---

## 9. CONCLUSION

Global Scope. Sixty-one sites across two continents in five puzzle languages, none of them on a map until entered, none of them with music, none of them explaining themselves, all of them opening a way back from the inside.

Highmoore's six, locked behind a body of customary rules that every person in the basin observes and denies believing in — so that the barrows open for a player who listened to a farmer describe his grandmother's nonsense, and stay shut for one who did not. A mill wheel turning underwater on the current for eighty years. Four periods of military tunnelling under a field that changed hands eleven times, none of them aware of the others. An abbey under-chamber with four generations of hostage records in it, including eighteen months belonging to a girl the player has not met yet.

A ninety-minute passage that is not a dungeon at all and sets no precedent. Four places with nothing at the bottom. And one that is completable, hazardless, bossless, and gives nothing whatsoever, and never says why.

# HIGHMOORE SETTLEMENTS — THE PLACES PEOPLE LIVE

**Status:** Design Draft v1.0
**Phase:** Phase 8 — Highmoore (Movement Two — Document 11 of ~30)
**Related:** highmoore_overview.md, highmoore_region.md, house_system.md, the_lesser_houses.md, highmoore_smiths.md, highmoore_populations.md, highmoore_reconstruction.md, mounted_travel.md, npcs/building_evolution.md, npcs/regional_populations.md, npcs/children_and_families.md, npcs/tavern_keepers.md, npcs/general_merchants.md, physics/weather_physics.md, style_bible.md
**Purpose:** Highmoore's settlements — the fortified farm, the village, the market town, the abbey, and the ford — plus the pub where the Wizard is met, stabling, the graveyards, and how the burning season is lived through by people who are not nobility.
**Inspirations:** Kingdom Come: Deliverance (village life at ground level), Red Dead Redemption 2 (settlement texture and the feeling of somewhere lived-in), The Witcher 3 (Velen's hamlets), real Yorkshire dales villages, Pennine drystone country, Border Reiver bastle houses

## 1. PHILOSOPHY

### 1.1 Principle 1: The War Is Lived at This Level

**Cross-reference:** house_system.md Section 8, highmoore_region.md Section 5.

The lords conduct the feud. **The settlements absorb it.** Every practice in house_system.md — the burning season, the road-oath, the settlement, the levy — reaches the ground here, and cross-reference highmoore_region.md Section 5.3: the ambient register is exhausted rather than frightened because these people have been absorbing it for six generations.

### 1.2 Principle 2: Fortification Is Ordinary

Cross-reference highmoore_region.md Section 5.2. Every building above a certain size has defensive features and **none of them are new.** Cross-reference Section 2 — the fortified farm is Highmoore's basic domestic unit, and nobody in Highmoore considers it remarkable.

### 1.3 Principle 3: This Is Where Highmoore Is Charming

**Cross-reference:** house_system.md Section 8.3 (the trap).

The Houses supply pageantry. **The settlements supply warmth.** Cross-reference npcs/tavern_keepers.md, npcs/general_merchants.md — the Phase 5 standards apply in full, and Highmoore's village Low register is the funniest writing available in Phase 8.

### 1.4 Principle 4: Building States Read the War

Cross-reference npcs/building_evolution.md — the seven-state system applies, and cross-reference highmoore_region.md Section 4.3: **a player can read which Houses are winning off the roofs of their villages.**

## 2. THE FORTIFIED FARM

### 2.1 The Basic Unit

Highmoore's characteristic building. Cross-reference the Border Reiver bastle house.

**Two storeys.** Livestock below, family above, one door, thick walls, small high windows, and an external stair that can be pulled up. Drystone yard walls. A byre that can be barred.

**Cross-reference physics/destruction_physics.md** — they are built to survive a raid, not a siege. The point is not to win; the point is to still be there afterward.

### 2.2 The Distribution

Cross-reference highmoore_region.md Section 2.3 — the Folds are dense with them, the High Moor is sparse, the Burnt March has their ruins.

### 2.3 The Interaction

Cross-reference mounted_travel.md, highmoore_region.md Section 6.3.

**A rider is watched from a fortified farm from about four hundred metres.** Cross-reference npcs/regional_populations.md — the yeomanry are armed, organised, and not deferential, and a stranger gets a greeting that is exactly as friendly as the road's current safety justifies.

*"Ride on or ride in. Either's fine. Just pick one from here so I know."*

## 3. THE VILLAGE

### 3.1 The Shape

Twenty to sixty households around a green, a church, a smithy, and a well. Cross-reference Section 6 — **and a graveyard that is too large.**

### 3.2 The Named Villages

**ASHBY UNDER MOOR** — cross-reference the_lesser_houses.md Section 3.1. The most-changed-hands village in Highmoore. Its boundary stone has eleven cuts. Cross-reference house_system.md Section 3.3.

The people of Ashby have a specific culture built entirely around being taken and retaken, and cross-reference Section 7.3 — **they have a procedure**, and it is grimly efficient, and they will explain it to anyone.

**LYNNWATER** — the mills. Cross-reference the_lesser_houses.md Section 4.4 — Tam is here.

**HALFORD CROSS** — the abbey village. Cross-reference Section 5.

**STONECROFT** — Stonecountry, quarrying, and cross-reference highmoore_smiths.md: half the village works iron.

**THE MERE VILLAGES** — cross-reference the_lesser_houses.md Section 2.2. Built on piles, reached by causeway, and cross-reference physics/water_physics.md — passable four hours in six.

### 3.3 The Green

Cross-reference npcs/children_and_families.md.

Every Highmoore village green has: a butts (cross-reference house_belos.md Section 4.2 — **the shooting culture is region-wide, not Belos-only**), a stone, and children on it.

## 4. THE MARKET TOWNS

### 4.1 The Three

Highmoore has three real towns, all in the Folds, all at road junctions.

**COLDBECK** — the largest. Cross-reference Section 4.3 — **the Wizard is met here.**
**DUNFORD** — the ford. Cross-reference Section 7.
**WYNNE'S CROSS** — horse market. Cross-reference mounted_travel.md Section 2.3 and the_lesser_houses.md Section 4.8.

### 4.2 What Is In Them

Cross-reference npcs/general_merchants.md, npcs/blacksmiths_and_armorers.md, npcs/apothecaries_and_alchemists.md — the Phase 5 Geschäft Tier structure applies, with Highmoore's own personnel.

- Market square, weekly and on saints' days
- Smiths, cross-reference highmoore_smiths.md
- Stabling, cross-reference mounted_travel.md Section 6.3
- Pubs, cross-reference Section 4.3
- A gaol, a gallows, and a market cross where the House's proclamations are nailed

### 4.3 THE PUB

**Cross-reference:** npcs/tavern_keepers.md, wizard_questline.md.

Highmoore's pub is not the archipelago's tavern. Low-beamed, dark, coal fire, settle benches, and a landlord who has heard everything.

**THE DROVER'S REST, COLDBECK** — cross-reference wizard_questline.md Section 2: **this is where the Wizard is met**, in the corner, being avoided by everyone, and the landlord will not go near his table.

**Landlord: HUW PENNANT**, 57, and cross-reference npcs/tavern_keepers.md Section 1.3 — he shapes the room.

*"He's been there four days. He pays. He doesn't drink much. I'd rather he moved on and I'm not going to be the one who says so."*

**The other pubs:** cross-reference the ambient standard — Highmoore's pubs are where the region's gossip web lives. Cross-reference npcs/regional_populations.md Section 10.

## 5. THE ABBEY

### 5.1 Halford Cross

**Cross-reference:** the_lesser_houses.md Section 4.6.

Highmoore's only abbey and only school. Cross-reference physics/sound_physics.md Section 4.1 — stone, high vault, long reverb, and cross-reference colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 5.5: **the acoustic puzzle language exists here too.**

### 5.2 What It Does

- Teaches. The region's literate class comes from here, and cross-reference house_system.md — **most lords cannot read well and most stewards can.**
- Keeps records. Cross-reference the_lesser_houses.md Section 4.5 — better than Brae's, and legible.
- Shelters. Cross-reference house_system.md Section 4.5 — the abbey takes people in during the burning season, and both sides respect it, and cross-reference Section 7.4: **almost always.**

### 5.3 The Abbot

**BROTHER PADARN**, 61. Practical, unmystical, and cross-reference npcs/named_crew_deep_dives.md — **he and Father Salvio would like each other enormously and will never meet.**

*"We're not holy here. We're useful. Holy is a thing you claim; useful is a thing they let you be."*

### 5.4 The Records

Cross-reference princess_quest_the_truth.md.

**The abbey keeps the hostage-guesting records.** Every exchange, every House, every child, every year, going back four generations. Cross-reference house_system.md Section 4.1.

**Design note:** a player can read them. Cross-reference the_princess.md Section 3 — **Elowen Arion's eighteen months at Belos are in the ledger, in a fair hand, with a date, and nothing else.**

## 6. THE GRAVEYARDS

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_region.md Section 5.2, style_bible.md Pillar 2, npcs/the_named_dead.md.

### 6.1 The Design

Every Highmoore village graveyard is **too large for its village**, visibly, and cross-reference npcs/building_evolution.md — the oldest sections are the largest.

### 6.2 The Stones

Cross-reference highmoore_region.md Section 5.3: *"they'll put a stone up and in forty years someone'll ask who he was and nobody'll know."*

**Readable stones.** Names, years, and cross-reference the design's environmental storytelling standard — **the year clusters are visible.** 1841. 1849. 1856-57. 1863. A player who reads a graveyard can date every bad season in Highmoore's last fifty years.

### 6.3 Dunford

Cross-reference Section 7. **The ford's graveyard is the largest in the basin and it serves a village of forty households.**

## 7. THE FORD

### 7.1 What It Is

Cross-reference highmoore_region.md, house_system.md.

The only reliable crossing of the Highmoore river between the Folds and the Stonecountry. **Everything goes through Dunford**, and therefore everything is fought over at Dunford, and has been for six generations.

### 7.2 The Ambient Weight

Cross-reference highmoore_region.md Section 5.3:

*"...my father fought at the ford, and his father, and I've a boy old enough now and he asks about the ford."*

**Design law:** "the ford" is said constantly across Highmoore's ambient dialogue, by everyone, in every register, and cross-reference house_belos.md Section 3.2 — **Lord Aldric buried two sons there.**

### 7.3 The Ashby Procedure

Cross-reference Section 3.2.

Villages in the contested belt have a drill: what gets buried, what gets driven off, who goes to the abbey, and who stays. Cross-reference npcs/regional_populations.md Section 12.2 — **it is practised**, and children know their part, and cross-reference npcs/children_and_families.md: the protection rules hold and the drill is shown warmly, as competence, not trauma.

*"Two carts. Grain in the byre-pit, beasts up the beck, old folk and littl'uns to the abbey, and the rest of us on the wall looking useless. Takes forty minutes. We've done it four times."*

### 7.4 The Abbey Exception

Cross-reference Section 5.2. Both sides respect the abbey.

**Except in 1857.** Cross-reference Section 6.2 — the year cluster. Nobody discusses it and the abbey's own records have a gap.

## 8. STABLING AND TRAVEL INFRASTRUCTURE

**Cross-reference:** mounted_travel.md Section 6.3.

### 8.1 The Stable Network

Every village and town has stabling. Cross-reference mounted_travel.md — **a horse left at any stable can be whistled up at any other**, and this is Highmoore's only concession to convenience.

### 8.2 The Inns

Cross-reference npcs/tavern_keepers.md. Sleep, food, and cross-reference physics/weather_physics.md — **and shelter, which in a Highmoore winter is not a formality.**

### 8.3 The Road Wardens

Cross-reference house_system.md Section 4.3. Each sworn road has wardens, and they are yeomanry, and they are the player's most common Highmoore interaction.

*"Road's sworn to Pennard from the beck to the crossroads. After the crossroads it's Ridd's and Ridd's roads are Ridd's business. Ride hard after the crossroads."*

- **Whether children are on the green**

## 12. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** the yeoman Middle — *"ride on or ride in, just pick one from here"* — exists nowhere else in Dark Arisen. Huw Pennant's landlord register. Brother Padarn's *useful, not holy.*
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** graveyards too large for their villages with legible year clusters. Boundary stones cut eleven times. Byre-pits, open or filled. Thatch versus slate. **The abbey ledger with Elowen Arion's eighteen months recorded in a fair hand and nothing else.**
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** the ford. Everyone's father fought at the ford. The largest graveyard in the basin serves forty households. And the 1857 gap.
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** **this is where Highmoore's charm lives.** The road wardens, the landlords, Tam, the Ashby villagers explaining their evacuation drill with professional pride, and *"the rest of us on the wall looking useless."*
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** six generations of feud, absorbed at ground level by people who have a forty-minute drill for it and know their part, and a graveyard where you can date every bad year by reading the stones.

## 14. CONCLUSION

The Places People Live. Two-storey farms with the beasts below and the family above and one door and an external stair you can pull up, and none of the fortification is new. Villages round a green with a butts on it and children shooting for fun, and a graveyard too big for the village where the years cluster — 1841, 1849, 1856-57 — so that a stranger who reads the stones can date every bad season for fifty years.

An abbey that is useful rather than holy, keeping the records nobody else can, including four generations of which child slept in whose hall and when. A pub in Coldbeck with a man in the corner who has been there four days and pays and does not drink much and whom the landlord would rather move on and will not be the one to tell.

And a ford. Everyone's father fought at the ford. Everyone's grandfather fought at the ford. There is a boy old enough now and he asks about it.

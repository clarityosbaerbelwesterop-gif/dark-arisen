# TRAVEL SYSTEM — GETTING THERE

**Status:** Draft v1.0
**Phase:** Phase 12 — Interaction, Crew & Travel (Document 5 of 6)
**Related:** ship_as_home.md, crew_system.md, time_and_rest.md, mounted_travel.md, quest_activation.md, physics/ship_physics.md, physics/wind_systems.md, physics/water_physics.md, physics/sun_shadow_systems.md, mechanics/ship_navigation.md, colonial_war/indigenous_liberation_alliance.md, crystal_caves.md, style_bible.md
**Purpose:** How Jake moves through the world — sailing as the primary verb, navigation without a compass, the map, the fast-travel policy and its three exceptions, feared waters, the mountain, and the design law that distance is real.
**Inspirations:** Black Flag's sailing feel with Sunless Sea's dread, Wind Waker's ocean as a place, real celestial and coastal navigation, Death Stranding (travel as the content), and the rejection of the map-click

## 1. THE LAWS

### 1.1 DISTANCE IS REAL

**Cross-reference:** mounted_travel.md Section 6.2.

*"The questline's emotional structure depends on distance being real. If Arion is a menu selection then the ride back after the letter is nothing."*

**The same principle governs the archipelago.**

### 1.2 NO COMPASS, NO MINIMAP, NO PLAYER DOT

**Cross-reference:** technical_targets.md Section 8, quest_activation.md Section 6.4.

**None of these systems exist in the build.**

### 1.3 SAILING IS THE PRIMARY VERB

**Cross-reference:** physics/ship_physics.md, mechanics/ship_navigation.md.

Roughly **30-40% of a thorough playthrough is at sea.** Cross-reference Section 3 — **it is not travel time between content. It is content.**

## 2. SAILING

**Cross-reference:** physics/ship_physics.md, physics/wind_systems.md, physics/water_physics.md.

### 2.1 The Wind Decides

**Cross-reference:** physics/wind_systems.md.

**Point of sail governs everything.** Beating to windward is slow and wet and takes tacks. Running before it is fast and cross-reference physics/ship_physics.md: **and hard to stop.**

**Cross-reference skill_tree.md Section 4.3** — **The Weather Gauge** is a node, taught by Mira **or** Admiral Sterling, and cross-reference skill_tree.md Section 6.2: **Sterling's version is better and requires the alliance path.**

### 2.2 Crew Handle Sail

**Cross-reference:** crew_system.md Section 3, ship_as_home.md Section 4.3.

**Jake gives orders and the crew work the ship**, visibly and audibly. Cross-reference army_system.md Section 6.4 — **detaching crew ashore reduces sail-handling and it is a real trade.**

### 2.3 Weather

**Cross-reference:** physics/weather_physics.md, physics/snow_ice_physics.md.

Storms are survivable and expensive. Cross-reference skill_tree.md Section 3.3 — **surviving a Fjordlund winter storm at sea without losing a mast is one of the twelve specific-act Marks.**

### 2.4 The Sea Is Populated

Cross-reference mission_types.md Section 4, colonial_war/retaliation_system.md — convoys, patrols, other pirates, **and hunters.** Cross-reference colonial_war/assassin_network.md Section 4.2.

## 3. NAVIGATION

**Cross-reference:** mechanics/ship_navigation.md, physics/sun_shadow_systems.md.

### 3.1 By Coast

The default. **Landmarks, headlands, and known water.** Cross-reference highmoore_region.md Section 4.3 for the land equivalent — **silhouettes readable at range are the navigation system.**

### 3.2 By Chart

**Cross-reference:** Section 4, ship_as_home.md Section 6.1.

**Esteban's Sky** — night navigation without instruments, taught by a sixty-year sailor.

**Design note:** cross-reference physics/sun_shadow_systems.md Section 6.2 — **the night sky is modelled and the constellations are real and consistent**, and cross-reference colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 5.3: **Ashenmoor's puzzle language uses the same sky.**

### 3.4 By Being Told

**Cross-reference:** quest_activation.md Section 6, colonial_war/indigenous_liberation_alliance.md Section 5.3.

**The feared waters are opened by guides, not by charts.** Cross-reference colonial_war/indigenous_liberation_alliance.md Section 5.3 — **six regions of water that no map in the archipelago shows and that trust opens.**

**Jake unfolds it and holds it.** Cross-reference camera_system.md Section 2.2 — Anchored, **and the world is visible around it.**

## 5. FAST TRAVEL

### 5.1 The Policy

**There is no fast travel over water. Ever. Anywhere.**

**Cross-reference Section 1.1.**

### 5.2 The Three Exceptions on Land

1. **Stabling.** Cross-reference mounted_travel.md Section 6.3 — **a horse left at any Highmoore stable can be whistled up at any other.** This moves the horse, not Jake.
2. **The mountain.** Cross-reference crystal_caves.md Section 9.1 — **four to six minutes on repeat runs**, and cross-reference Section 7: **it is a traversal, not a menu.**
3. **Within a settlement.** Walking from a harbour to a market is walked. **There is no exception here** — this entry exists to state that there is not.

### 5.3 What Replaces It

**Cross-reference:** Section 6.

### 5.4 The Justification

**Cross-reference:** mounted_travel.md Section 6.2, princess_quest_the_lake.md Section 4.1.

**The lake ride is nine minutes at a capped trot and cannot be skipped**, and cross-reference princess_quest_arion_falls.md Section 2.2: **the ride afterward has the stamina system switched off.** Cross-reference princess_quest_the_return.md Section 2.2 — **and the ride home is forty minutes at a walk.**

**None of that means anything if the world has a travel menu.**

## 8. IMPLEMENTATION

```cpp
struct Travel {
    Vessel      ship;            // singular; no fleet, no swap
    PointOfSail pos;             // drives speed; §2.1
    ChartSet    knownCharts;
    BitSet      openedWaters;    // §3.4, trust-gated
    HorseRef    stabledHorse;    // Highmoore only; §7.3
};
```

**Prohibited:** any fast-travel menu · any map-click movement · a compass · a minimap · a player position indicator · **any function named `TeleportToWaypoint`.**

## 9. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1:** the longest crew dialogue stretches in the game are at sea, in five registers, while nobody is doing anything urgent.
- ✅ **Pillar 2:** charts as physical objects that Jake annotates and that stay annotated, some of them wrong in informative ways.
- ✅ **Pillar 3:** cross-reference Section 6.4 — Highmoore's rides are silent and alone, on purpose, in a game whose default travel is a ship full of people.
- ✅ **Pillar 4:** cross-reference Section 6.2 — a crew with nothing to do for six hours is the best comedy engine in the project.
- ✅ **Pillar 5:** no fast travel anywhere, because a nine-minute capped ride and a forty-minute walk home are two of the most important sequences in the game and a travel menu would make both of them nothing.

---

## 10. CONCLUSION

Getting There. No compass, no minimap, no player dot, and no fast travel over water anywhere in the game — navigation by coastline, by chart, by a sky that is really modelled and shares its constellations with a desert puzzle language, and by being taken through six regions of feared water by people who trust him.

A map that is paper, unfolded and held, showing coasts he has sailed and towns he has been to and nothing else, annotated at a chart table in his own hand.

Thirty to forty percent of a playthrough at sea, and it is not travel between content — it is a walkable ship with ninety people on it where the longest crew conversations in the game happen and where morale is read by whether anyone is on the forecastle at dusk.

And on the other side of a mountain that is both the only route between continents and the game's loading screen, twenty-five-minute rides across open moor with none of that, alone, where the wind has to carry it.

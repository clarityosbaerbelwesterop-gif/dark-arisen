## 1. THE NAMED DEAD PHILOSOPHY — THE FIVE PRINCIPLES

### 1.1 Principle 1: The Dead Drive the Living

Audit Dark Arisen's major questlines: Jake's revenge (Ethan). Don Alejandro's collection (Nicolás). Mira's search (Bjorn). Mormor Astrid's hearth (generations of dead women). Mbah Seruni's tradition (a murdered son, a dead husband). Big Bertha's tavern (a drowned husband). Nearly every living motivation traces to a named dead person.

**Design intent:** The dead are not backstory. They are the engine.

### 1.2 Principle 2: Absence Is Presence

The most important character in Dark Arisen — Ethan Harlow — never appears alive on screen. He exists through: a grove, a tobacco strain, notes in his handwriting, a Dream Fight apparition, other people's memories. His absence is so structured it becomes presence.

**Design intent:** Master the art of the character-shaped hole.

### 1.3 Principle 3: How Cultures Remember Reveals Who They Are

Imperial marble headstones with dates and titles. Indigenous ancestors who remain family members. Fjordlund saga-names spoken by winter fires. Ashenmoor ash returned to the mountain. Sailor names carved into tavern beams. Memorial practice IS cultural portrait.

**Design intent:** Death practices as cultural deep-characterization.

### 1.4 Principle 4: Style Bible Pillar 3 Governs Everything

The Layered Death system (Styles A-D) applies most stringently to the Named Dead. Their deaths are almost always Style D — reverent, weighted, never exploitative. The Named Dead are why the Style Bible exists.

**Design intent:** Maximum death-weight for maximum-meaning characters.

### 1.5 Principle 5: The Dead Are Never Finished

The Named Dead continue changing. Jake learns new facts about Ethan through Chapter 9. Don Alejandro reveals Nicolás in layers. Bjorn's status is a live question. Death ends a life, not a story.

**Design intent:** The dead have character arcs.

## 2. THE CENTRAL DEAD — ETHAN HARLOW

### 2.1 Cross-Reference

**See:** characters/ethan_harlow.md, fauna/tobacco_system.md Section 8.4, fauna/trees_and_fruits.md Section 8.3, fauna/cannabis_system.md Section 6 (Dream-Ethan)

### 2.2 The Character-Shaped Hole

**Ethan exists in the game as:**

**Physical Traces:**
- Ethan's Grove (Chapter 9 discovery — everything cultivated "for when Jake sails again")
- Ethan's Strain (the tenth cigar — the emotional peak of Don Alejandro's quest)
- His notes (handwriting as characterization: careful, hopeful, patient)
- The family land itself

**Memory Traces:**
- Jake's fragmented recollections (unreliable, guilt-shaped)
- NPC memories (villagers who knew him — Chapter 9 conversations)
- What Jake CAN'T remember (design choice: some memories stay lost)

**The Dream Apparition:**
- Dream-Ethan (Dream Fight, Chapter 7) — NOT a ghost, explicitly a projection
- Asks Jake to lay down the sword
- The game's central choice given a face

### 2.3 The Ethan Design Rules

**Absolute Rules:**

- Ethan NEVER appears in flashback with full visual clarity (memory is fog, faces blur)
- Ethan's voice is heard ONLY in the Dream Fight (once) and never confirmed as accurate
- No system ever "resolves" Ethan — no closure mechanic, no final message beyond the grove
- The grove notes are the last words. There is no secret additional letter. Grief has no DLC.

**Design Reasoning:**

Real loss doesn't resolve. Jake (and the player) must live with incompleteness. The game's honesty about death depends on refusing the fantasy of full closure.

### 2.4 What Ethan Teaches the Design

Every other Named Dead follows the Ethan pattern at smaller scale: physical traces + memory traces + the refusal of tidy closure.

## 3. THE NAMED DEAD REGISTRY

### 3.1 The Beloved Dead of the Wisdom Keepers

**Cross-Reference:** npcs/cigar_collector_don_alejandro.md Section 3.4

- Don Alejandro's wife, died "of grief" two years after Nicolás
- **Traces:** The garden she planted (still tended, 14 years later), preserved items

**Cross-Reference:** npcs/healer_mormor_astrid.md Section 3.4

- Mormor Astrid's husband, fisherman, lost at sea in a storm
- Body never recovered
- **Traces:** Preserved items in her home, her phrase: *"Long enough that the missing is quiet. Not gone. Quiet."*
- **Design note:** "Quiet grief" as a modeled endpoint — Mormor Astrid shows players what processed loss looks like. She is the counter-example to Jake's raw wound.

**Olaf Halvorson (died ~1846):**

- Mormor Astrid's son, lost at sea (a different storm than his father)
- **Traces:** Referenced rarely; the sea took both her men and she still lives beside it
- **Cultural weight:** Fjordlund's acceptance of the sea's price, made personal

**Karya (died ~1846):**

**Cross-Reference:** npcs/desert_sage_mbah_seruni.md Section 3.4

- Mbah Seruni's husband, fellow tradition keeper
- **Traces:** Preserved items in the volcanic cave

**Jaya (died age 22):**

- Mbah Seruni's son, killed by Imperial soldiers for refusing to leave his shrine
- **Traces:** Her stillness when soldiers are mentioned; the shrine he died protecting (visitable, unmarked — players can find it, the game never points)
- *"His name was Jaya. It means 'victory.' I gave him that name. Imperial soldiers took it."*
- **Design note:** Jaya is the counterweight to every sympathetic Imperial NPC. The design deliberately holds both truths: Captain Voss's honor AND Jaya's murder. No resolution offered.

## 4. SCHRÖDINGER'S DEAD — BJORN

### 4.1 Cross-Reference

**See:** npcs/named_crew_deep_dives.md Section 2.4 (Mira's Quest), npcs/healer_mormor_astrid.md Section 8

### 4.2 The Unresolved Status Mechanic

**Bjorn is Dark Arisen's only Named Dead whose death is a question.**

**The Three Outcomes:**
1. **Bjorn Dead** — Mira gets a grave, Mormor Astrid leads the mourning, quiet grief begins
2. **Bjorn Alive (Captive)** — rescue, but the brother who returns is not the brother who left
3. **Bjorn Lost to Darkness** — alive, and worse than dead; the hardest outcome

### 4.3 Why the Uncertainty Matters

**Design Reasoning:**

The player experiences what Mira experiences: not-knowing as its own form of grief. Every other Named Dead is settled. Bjorn is the open wound. The contrast teaches players the difference between grief (Mormor Astrid's "quiet") and limbo (Mira's search).

**The Design Rule:**

Until the quest resolves, NO dialogue, item, or system confirms Bjorn's status. Even datamining should find all three outcomes equally weighted. The uncertainty is real.

## 5. CULTURAL MEMORIAL SYSTEMS

### 5.1 Imperial Christian (Pale Isle)

**Practices:**
- Marble headstones, formal cemeteries (visitable locations)
- Candle lighting (Father Salvio's practice — cross-reference)
- Memorial services (Father Vossberg conducts; Jake can attend specific ones)
- Anniversary masses

**The Cemetery as Location:**
- Pale Isle's cemetery is explorable
- Headstones readable (environmental storytelling: dates cluster around wars, plagues)
- Fresh flowers on specific graves (who leaves them? — ambient mysteries)

**Register:** High formal. *"Here lies... beloved... in God's keeping."*

### 5.2 Indigenous Rexan

**Practices:**
- Ancestors remain family members (Casa de los Antepasados — "House of the Ancestors" — cross-reference npcs/tavern_keepers.md)
- Food shared with the dead (plates set at communal meals)
- The dead consulted (Mama Jacinta's practice)
- Names given forward (children carry ancestor names)

**Design Depth:**
- Indigenous NPCs speak of dead relatives in present tense: *"My grandmother says..."* (she died 20 years ago)
- This is not confusion. This is cosmology.
- Jake can learn to hear the difference. Players who notice, notice.

**Register:** Middle warm. The dead are family, not history.

### 5.3 Fjordlund

**Practices:**
- Sea burials (the standard — Erik, Olaf, most of the coast's dead have no graves)
- Stone settings (memorial stones for the sea-taken, no bodies beneath)
- Saga-memory (the dead become stories — Bjorn the Wise as endpoint)
- The hearth as ancestor connection (tending the fire = tending the line)

**The Stone Field:**
- A visitable Fjordlund location: standing stones for the sea-taken
- Mormor Astrid has placed two (Erik, Olaf)
- Mira may place one (Bjorn outcome-dependent)
- **The quietest location in Fjordlund. Wind and stones.**

**Register:** Middle spare. Few words, heavy each.

### 5.4 Ashenmoor

**Practices:**
- Ash returned to the mountain (cremation, ashes carried up)
- The mountain holds all the dead (the volcano as collective ancestor)
- Name-songs (the dead's names sung during ceremonies — Mbah Seruni knows hundreds)
- Shrine tending (Jaya died protecting one)

**Design Depth:**
- Mbah Seruni's night singing (ambient, rare): she is singing the dead's names
- Players who ask are told. Players who don't still heard it.

**Register:** Middle-High ceremonial. The mountain remembers.

### 5.5 Sailor/Pirate

**Practices:**
- Sea burial (canvas, weighted, words said, gone)
- Names carved in tavern beams (The Drowned Cat's ceiling is a memorial — hundreds of names)
- "Pouring one out" (first sip to the dead)
- The bell (ship's bell rung once per lost crew member, La Liberación tradition)

**The Drowned Cat's Ceiling:**
- Visitable, readable
- Captain Black Cat can tell the story behind any name (three rums minimum for the long ones)
- Jake's crew losses (if any occur) are carved here
- **Design note:** If the player loses crew members, their names appear. The ceiling is the game's living casualty list.

**Register:** Low honest. *"He was good in the rigging. Drank too much. We'll miss the bastard."* [glasses raised]

## 6. THE DEAD IN GAMEPLAY

### 6.1 Visitable Graves and Memorials

**System:**
- Specific graves/memorials are interactable
- Jake can stand at them (no prompt spam — presence is the interaction)
- Some trigger optional reflection (brief, internal, skippable)
- Crew members visit their own dead (Mira at the Stone Field, observable)

### 6.2 The Grave Visits That Matter

**Ethan's Resting Place:**
- At the family land, near the grove
- Jake can visit any time after Chapter 9 discovery
- Each chapter visit: one new line of internal reflection (10 total, missable, never repeated)
- No mechanical reward. Ever. This is not content. This is grief.

**Don Alejandro's Grave (If He Dies, Chapter 10):**
- Cross-reference npcs/cigar_collector_don_alejandro.md Section 10.2
- The final Ethan's Strain cigar CAN be smoked here
- If the player saved one. If they think of it. Unprompted.
- **The game's deepest missable moment.**

### 6.3 The Dead in Dialogue

**The Memory Web:**
- NPCs reference their dead naturally (not quest-flagged)
- Trust deepens memorial dialogue (Don Alejandro's Stage 6+ Nicolás revelations)
- Cross-NPC memory: Halvor remembers Mira's father; Mormor Astrid remembers Bjorn; the dead connect the living

### 6.4 The Raven

**Cross-Reference:** Established design — The Old Messenger Raven

**The Design:**
- Follows Jake the entire game
- Cannot be killed (engine-level, like children)
- Appears at: graves, memorials, moments of loss, the grove, the Stone Field
- Never explained. Never named in-game. Never resolved.

**What It Might Be (Design Intent — Never Stated):**
- Fjordlund reading: Odin's messenger
- Indigenous reading: an ancestor watching
- Ashenmoor reading: the mountain's eye
- Imperial reading: a bird
- Jake's reading (if honorable path): he stops shooing it around Chapter 8
- **The Raven is a mirror. Every culture sees its own dead in it. So does the player.**

## 9. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** The dead are remembered in their culture's register — Imperial formal epitaphs, Fjordlund spare stone-words, sailor honest toasts, indigenous present-tense family speech.
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** The Named Dead exist AS objects — the photograph, the tended garden, the preserved coat, the carved ceiling, the standing stones, the grove itself.
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** This document IS Pillar 3's deepest application. All Named Dead are Style D. The refusal of closure is the ultimate death-weight.
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** Sailor memorial culture carries Style C — *"We'll miss the bastard"* is love in Low register. Warmth in remembrance, never mockery.

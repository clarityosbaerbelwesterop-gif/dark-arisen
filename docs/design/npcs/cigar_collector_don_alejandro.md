## 1. CHARACTER OVERVIEW

### 1.1 Quick Reference

**Name:** Don Alejandro Esteban de la Vega y Morales

**Age:** 67 (Chapter 4 introduction) → 70+ (Chapter 10)

**Location:** Rexa Noble District, private estate

**Profession:** Retired Imperial Naval Captain → Cigar Connoisseur

**Cultural Background:** Imperial Spanish nobility (mixed Rexan heritage)

**Register:** High (aristocratic but never cruel)

**Voice Actor Style:** Refined, weathered, philosophical — think early Anthony Hopkins crossed with Édgar Ramírez

### 1.2 Story Role

**Primary Function:**
- Father figure for Jake
- Quest giver (Cigar Collector questline)
- Cultural bridge between Imperial and Pirate worlds
- Source of Chapter 9 emotional revelation
- Final mentor before endgame

**Story Significance:**
- One of game's most emotionally important characters
- His questline spans Chapters 4-10
- Represents Imperial culture done with humanity
- Counterpoint to Imperial antagonists

### 1.3 First Impression

**When Jake First Sees Him:**

A man on a Rexan veranda. Late afternoon. Sun setting over harbor. Cigar smoke rising. Crystal glass with amber liquid beside him. Dressed in fine but worn linen — quality but lived-in. Eyes that have seen everything and still find beauty.

He doesn't rise when Jake arrives. He gestures to the empty chair beside him.

*"Captain Harlow. Please. The sunset is particularly fine tonight."*

### 6.3 The Tenth Cigar — Ethan's Strain

**Cross-Reference:** fauna/tobacco_system.md Section 8.4

**The Discovery:**

**Chapter 9 Story Moment:**

Jake finds Ethan's Grove (cross-reference fauna/trees_and_fruits.md Section 8.3). Discovers his brother cultivated his own tobacco strain. Ethan's notes reveal:

*"For Jake, when he returns. I've been perfecting this for years. It's bittersweet — like him. Like us. The earth of our homeland, shaped by jungle rains, cured the old way grandfather taught us. He won't believe I did this. But I did. For the day he sails back."*

**Don Alejandro's Reaction:**

Bringing Ethan's Strain to Don Alejandro is one of the game's defining scenes:

*The veranda. Late afternoon. Don Alejandro examining the cigars carefully. His hands trembling slightly — the only sign of emotion.*

**Don Alejandro:** *"This... Captain. This is..."*

**Don Alejandro:** *"Your brother?"*

*Jake nods.*

**Don Alejandro:** *"He rolled these himself."*

**Don Alejandro:** *"He knew you would come."*

*Long silence. Don Alejandro lights one. Takes a draw. Closes his eyes. When he opens them, they're wet.*

**Don Alejandro (in Spanish):** *"Mi hijo habría sido un hombre como tu hermano. Creo que ahora entiendo por qué buscamos estos puros."*

[Translation appears]: *"My son would have been a man like your brother. I think I understand now why we sought these cigars."*

**Memorable for the rest of the game.**

## 7. VOICE BIBLE

### 7.1 Speech Patterns

**General:**
- Educated diction
- Measured pace
- Pauses for emphasis
- Slight Rexan-Spanish accent
- Switches to Spanish for emotional moments
- Quotes literature occasionally

### 7.2 Sample Dialogue

**Standard Greeting (Formal):**

*"Captain Harlow. Please, join me. The harbor is particularly active tonight."*

**Standard Greeting (Friendly, after trust):**

*"Jake. Sit, sit. The sunset waits for no one. And neither does this Moran Highland — it's at peak. Try it."*

**Discussing Loss (Stage 6+):**

*"My son. Yes. He would have been... let me think... thirty-eight now. Older than you, Captain."*

*Pause.*

*"He was an idealist. The kind of man who joins wars he shouldn't. The kind of man who dies for ideas while better men live for people."*

*Pause.*

*"I've never quite forgiven him. Or myself for letting him go. Or the Imperial system that took him."*

**Cigar Ritual Talk:**

*"There is a specific way to cut a cigar, Captain. Not because tradition demands it, but because the cigar deserves it. The leaves were grown by hands. Cured by hands. Rolled by hands. The cut should respect that lineage."*

**Philosophy Moment:**

*"Three things give a man's life meaning, my father told me. Work that matters. Love that matters. And ritual that connects us to those who came before."*

*Pause.*

*"I had all three. I lost two. The cigars are what remains."*

**Cultural Bridge Dialogue:**

*"You see, Captain — the Imperial system gives us much. Order. Education. Beauty. But it takes more than it admits. Sons. Daughters. The complexity that makes Rexa what it was before Imperial 'protection.'"*

*"I was born of both worlds. I see both. I love both. And I grieve both."*

**Spanish Emotional Moments:**

*"Mi querido capitán..."* (when speaking from heart)

*"Como un hijo..."* (like a son)

*"Que descansen en paz."* (may they rest in peace)

**Late Game (Chapter 9+):**

*"You came into my life by accident, Captain. Or perhaps not. I no longer believe in accidents at my age."*

*"Whatever path you choose now, know this — you have honored an old man's collection. And his memory of his son. That is no small thing."*

**Final Goodbye (Chapter 10):**

*"Sail well, Captain. Sail with intention. And when you stop, may you find a veranda with a good cigar and good memory."*

## 15. CONCLUSION

Don Alejandro de la Vega. The aristocrat who became a father to a pirate. The collector who completed his life's work through another man's brother. The Imperial loyalist who saw what the system took from him. The man who watched sunsets from his veranda waiting — for the cigar, for the meaning, for the son who never came home, for the captain who eventually did.

When players hand him Ethan's Strain in Chapter 9, when his hands tremble and he speaks Spanish, when he tells Jake "Mi hijo habría sido un hombre como tu hermano" — that's when Don Alejandro becomes more than NPC. That's when he becomes father. That's when Dark Arisen earns its emotional weight.

This is what NPCs can be when given the depth they deserve.

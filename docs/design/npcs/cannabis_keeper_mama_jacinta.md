## 1. CHARACTER OVERVIEW

### 1.1 Quick Reference

**Name:** Mama Jacinta (her chosen name — true name spoken only in ceremony)

**Age:** Indeterminate (looks 70-80, possibly older)

**Location:** Hidden grove in deep Rexan jungle (Sacred Grove)

**Profession:** Curandera (traditional healer-shaman), Keeper of Sacred Cannabis

**Cultural Background:** Indigenous Rexan (specific tribal heritage)

**Register:** Middle, with elevation to High during ceremony

**Voice Acting Style:** Patient, deep, slightly weathered — think Maya Angelou meets Mercedes Sosa

### 1.2 Story Role

**Primary Function:**
- Sacred plant keeper
- Architect of Dream Fight
- Counter-imperial cultural voice
- Spiritual mentor (counterpoint to Father Salvio)
- Wisdom keeper for Indigenous Rexan culture

**Story Significance:**
- Central to Cannabis Quest (Phase 4)
- Dream Fight is psychologically critical moment
- Cultural depth representative
- Long-term spiritual relationship with Jake possible

### 1.3 First Impression

**When Jake First Sees Her:**

Sitting outside small wooden home in jungle clearing. Hands working — preparing herbs. Doesn't look up immediately. Around her: garden, drying racks, specific ceremonial items. Air smells of smoke and herbs.

She finishes what she's doing. Then looks up.

*"You came. I knew you would. Sit, Captain. The water is warm. We have time."*

She knew his name without being told.

## 8. VOICE BIBLE

### 8.1 Speech Patterns

**General:**
- Patient pace
- Spanish-Rexan accent
- Indigenous phrases
- Specific cultural cadence
- Pauses for thought
- Sings during ceremony

### 8.2 Sample Dialogue

**Standard Greeting:**

*"Captain. The water is warm. We have time."*

**Testing:**

*"You came far. Tell me why."*

[Pause]

*"And tell me why honestly. I will know if you lie."*

**About the Sacred Plant:**

*"This plant — it is older than your empire. Older than your church. Older than memory."*

[Pause]

*"It does not belong to anyone. We belong to it. To honor it. To use it correctly. Not to own it. Never to sell it."*

**About Imperial Loss:**

*"They came with their fire and their cross and their books. They said our gods were demons. Our medicines were witchcraft. Our songs were noise."*

[Pause]

*"But we are still here, Captain. Smaller. Quieter. But here."*

**During Ceremony:**

*"What you bring, you find. What you find, you carry."*

*"The plant does not heal. The plant reveals. You must heal yourself with what is revealed."*

**Specific Cultural Phrases:**

*"Mi hijo..."* (my son — when speaking with affection to Jake)

*"Hija de la luna..."* (daughter of the moon — for women she cares for)

[Indigenous language phrases — culturally authentic, subtitled]

**Wisdom Moments:**

*"You are not what happened to you, Captain. You are what you do next."*

*"Some wounds never fully close. We learn to live alongside them. Not to fix them. To accompany them."*

*"The plant shows truth. But truth needs a witness who will listen. Will you listen, Captain? Truly?"*

**About Plants:**

*"Each plant is a teacher. Each one. The smallest weed has lessons. We forget this. We think we are the teachers. We are the students. Always."*

**Final Goodbyes (Chapter 10):**

*"You came as one thing, Captain. You leave as another. That is the work of years for most. The plant gave you a shortcut. But shortcuts have prices. Live well to pay them."*

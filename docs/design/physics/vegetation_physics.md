## 1. VEGETATION PHILOSOPHY — THE FIVE PRINCIPLES

### 1.1 Principle 1: The Jungle Is Alive

Plants in Dark Arisen are not decoration. They breathe. They sway. They part for movement. They snap back. They hold weight or fail to. The jungle is as alive as any creature within it.

**Design intent:** Tactile environmental authenticity through reactive vegetation.

### 1.2 Principle 2: Layers Tell Different Stories

The jungle is not one space — it's many. Canopy is one world (birds, monkeys, sun). Mid-story is another (hanging life, vines). Understory is another (shaded paths, predators). Forest floor is another (decomposition, hunting). Each layer has its own physics.

**Design intent:** Vertical gameplay through layered environments.

### 1.3 Principle 3: Vines Are Roads

To outsiders, jungle is impassable. To those who learn, vines and branches are highways. Cultural knowledge unlocks aerial paths invisible to others.

**Design intent:** Cultural learning unlocks gameplay possibilities.

### 1.4 Principle 4: Hidden and Revealed

The jungle hides paths, treasure, danger, and beauty in equal measure. Players who push through bushes might find shrines or jaguars. The vegetation rewards careful exploration and punishes carelessness.

**Design intent:** Discovery through environmental investigation.

### 1.5 Principle 5: The Jungle Has Spirit

To Mama Jacinta and indigenous Rexan culture, the jungle is alive in ways outsiders don't understand. Specific trees have souls. Specific paths are sacred. Cultural respect is environmental literacy.

**Design intent:** Cultural depth through environmental relationship.

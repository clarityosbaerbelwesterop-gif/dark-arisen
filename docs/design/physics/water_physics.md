## 1. WATER PHILOSOPHY — THE FIVE PRINCIPLES

### 1.1 Principle 1: Water Has Mood

The ocean isn't just water — it's a character with moods. Glassy calm. Restless chop. Angry swells. Murderous storm. Players read the ocean's mood like they read an NPC's face.

**Design intent:** Water as character. Players develop relationship with the sea — sometimes friend, sometimes enemy, always present.

### 1.2 Principle 2: Multiple Wave Systems Layer

Real ocean has multiple wave systems active simultaneously. Long swells from distant storms. Local wind chop on top. Surface ripples from current. They combine to create complex realistic motion.

**Design intent:** Visual and tactile realism through layered systems, not single-frequency waves.

### 1.3 Principle 3: Water Interacts With Everything

Ships float. Bodies sink. Foam forms. Spray rises. Currents pull. Tides shift. Rain joins. Ice forms. Water isn't passive — it actively engages every object that touches it.

**Design intent:** Emergent gameplay through realistic water interaction.

### 1.4 Principle 4: Underwater Is Another World

Beneath the surface is a different reality. Different pressure. Different sight. Different sound. Different danger. Diving in Dark Arisen is entering an alien environment with its own rules.

**Design intent:** Underwater experiences feel distinct, weighted, sometimes terrifying.

### 1.5 Principle 5: The Coast Is a Conversation

Where water meets land, drama happens. Waves break. Foam forms. Tides reveal and hide. The coast is where ocean and earth negotiate constantly. Different coasts have different conversations.

**Design intent:** Coastal physics distinct from open ocean. Beach experience varies by region.

## 2. WAVE SYSTEMS

### 2.1 The Multi-Layer Approach

**Four Wave Layers Combined:**

**Layer 1: Primary Swells**
- Large rolling waves
- Generated by distant winds
- Long period (4-12 seconds)
- Direction can differ from local wind
- Most visible motion
- Carry energy across vast distances

**Layer 2: Secondary Wind Waves**
- Smaller waves on top of swells
- Generated by current local wind
- Shorter period (2-5 seconds)
- Direction matches wind
- Visual texture

**Layer 3: Tertiary Ripples**
- Small surface ripples
- Generated by very light wind
- Very short period (under 1 second)
- Pure surface detail
- Light interaction

**Layer 4: Capillary Waves**
- Tiniest surface motion
- Visual richness
- Adds realism
- Performance-friendly

**These Combine:**
The four layers add together to create complex realistic ocean surface that's never the same twice.

### 2.2 Wave Heights

**Calm Conditions:**
- Primary: 0.3-0.5m
- Secondary: 0.1-0.2m
- Tertiary: 0.05m
- Total: ~0.5m (gentle)

**Light Wind:**
- Primary: 0.5-1.0m
- Secondary: 0.3-0.5m
- Tertiary: 0.1m
- Total: ~1.5m (sailing comfortable)

**Moderate Wind:**
- Primary: 1.0-2.5m
- Secondary: 0.5-1.0m
- Tertiary: 0.2m
- Total: ~3.5m (challenging)

**Strong Wind:**
- Primary: 2.5-5.0m
- Secondary: 1.0-2.0m
- Tertiary: 0.3m
- Total: ~7m (dangerous)

**Storm:**
- Primary: 5.0-10m
- Secondary: 2.0-4.0m
- Tertiary: variable
- Total: 14m+ (deadly)

**Hurricane:**
- Primary: 10m+
- Secondary: chaotic
- Tertiary: lost in chaos
- Total: 20m+ (survival mode)

**Rogue Waves:**
- 2x normal height
- Sudden appearance
- Real danger
- Story moments
- Rare in game

### 2.3 Wave Direction

**Primary Direction:**
- Determined by storm system origin
- Can be hundreds of miles away
- Persists even when local wind shifts
- Strategic information

**Cross-Seas:**
- When local wind differs from swell direction
- Confused water
- Difficult sailing
- Realistic complexity

**Following Sea:**
- Waves move same direction as ship
- Easier sailing
- Faster movement
- But surfing risk

**Beam Sea:**
- Waves hit ship's side
- Most uncomfortable
- Rolling motion
- Cargo shifts

**Head Sea:**
- Waves into ship
- Slow progress
- Pitching
- Spray over bow

### 2.4 Wave Period

**Period = Time Between Wave Peaks**

**Calm:**
- Long period
- Smooth motion
- Comfortable
- Hypnotic

**Storm:**
- Shorter period
- Chaotic motion
- Difficult
- Tiring

### 2.5 Wave Foam and Crests

**Crest Formation:**
- Tops of waves break in strong wind
- Whitecaps visible
- Indicator of wind strength

**Foam Patterns:**
- Following wind direction
- Streaking on surface

**Spray:**

## 5. SHIP-WATER INTERACTION

### 5.1 Buoyancy System

**Ship Floats Realistically:**
- Displacement calculated
- Weight matters
- Cargo affects depth
- Listing under uneven load

**Visible Effects:**
- Loaded ship sits deeper
- Empty ship higher
- Damage causes listing
- Cargo loss raises ship

### 5.2 Ship Motion in Waves

**The Six Motions:**

**1. Heave (Vertical Up-Down):**
- Wave amplitude felt
- Crew experiences
- Affects movement on deck
- Realistic

**2. Sway (Horizontal Side-Side):**
- Cross-current motion
- Subtle usually
- More noticeable in beam seas

**3. Surge (Forward-Backward):**
- Rare but felt
- Wave-driven
- Can affect speed slightly

**4. Pitch (Bow Up-Bow Down):**
- Most dramatic
- Wave crests lift bow
- Troughs drop bow
- Affects gunnery

**5. Roll (Side-to-Side Rotation):**
- Beam seas worst
- Cargo shifts
- Crew compensates
- Capsizing possible (rare)

**6. Yaw (Left-Right Rotation):**
- Twisting motion
- Less common
- Storm conditions
- Steering challenge

### 5.3 Wake and Bow Wave

**Bow Wave:**
- Ship plowing creates V-shape
- Speed determines size
- Splashing visible
- Realistic physics

**Wake:**
- Behind ship
- Spreading V-pattern
- Distance affects size
- Visible from far

**Stopping Effects:**
- Wake catches up
- Brief surge
- Realistic deceleration

### 5.4 Damage and Water

**Hull Breach:**
- Water enters
- Ship slowly sinks
- Pumps fight back
- Race to repair

**Listing:**
- Uneven damage causes lean
- Affects performance
- Crew compensates
- Crisis mode

**Sinking:**
- Realistic process
- Slow descent
- Crew abandons
- Gear lost

### 5.5 Anchoring

**Anchor Physics:**
- Drops realistically
- Takes hold on bottom
- Holds against current
- Drag possible

**Anchor Lines:**
- Length matters (rule: 5x depth)
- Tension visible
- Failure possible
- Realistic mechanics

## 20. CONCLUSION

Water in Dark Arisen is not a flat texture or simple animation. It is a living system with multiple wave layers, currents, tides, moods. It is a character that helps and harms, comforts and kills.

When players steer their ship through a hurricane and feel each wave crashing over the bow, when they dive into clear tropical water and watch sunlight refract through the surface above, when they stand on the deck at glassy calm dawn and watch reflections perfect — that's when Dark Arisen's water transcends graphics and becomes experience.

This is the most important physics system in the game. The ocean is everywhere. The ocean is everything.

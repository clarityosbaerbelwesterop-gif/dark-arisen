# BOSS SPEC — Captain Ramón Rojas "The Iron Captain"

**Status:** Design Draft v1.0  
**Region:** Rexa — The Trading Districts  
**Boss Category:** Story Commander Boss (Chapter 4 — First Rexa Commander Encountered)  
**Difficulty:** Moderate (8-12 attempts — the "tutorial" for Rexa's Commander-level combat)  
**Related:** region_02_rexa_city.md, characters/don_mateo_salazar.md, style_bible.md  
**Inspirations:** Sir Alonne (Dark Souls 2 — disciplined precision), General Grievous (loyal enforcer), Boss fights in Ghost of Tsushima (samurai duels), Sanada Yukimura (the professional soldier)

---

## 1. CONCEPT — LOYALTY IS A WEAPON

Captain Ramón Rojas is the first Rexa Commander Jake will fight. He is also, in many ways, the most honest of the five. Where La Viuda Negra seduces, where El Médico tortures, where Brother Cleaver preaches, where the Twin Hooks kill in pairs — Rojas simply FIGHTS. He is a soldier. He has been a soldier since he was fifteen. He will die a soldier. He has made peace with all of this.

Rojas serves Don Mateo Salazar because Don Mateo is his commander. That is the entirety of his motivation. He does not love Don Mateo. He does not particularly hate anyone Don Mateo orders him to kill. He is not religious, not vengeful, not traumatized. He is a professional in an unprofessional world.

**This is what makes him dangerous.** All of Rexa's other Commanders have ideologies that can be challenged, weaknesses that can be exploited, internal contradictions that can be turned against them. Rojas has none of these. He has only his duty and his skill. Jake cannot talk him down. Jake cannot trick him. Jake cannot appeal to his conscience. Jake can only fight him, and hope to be better.

**Design principle:** Rojas is the MECHANICAL teaching boss of the Commander tier. He demonstrates Rexa's Commander-level combat without narrative complexity. Players who defeat Rojas will have learned enough to engage the more emotionally complex Commanders (La Viuda, El Médico) that follow. He is the Iudex Gundyr of Rexa — the first true test, clean and honest.

## 2. VISUAL DESCRIPTION

- **Height:** 1.78m, athletic but not bulky. The body of a man who has trained every day for forty years.
- **Age:** 56, but looks younger. The military life has been kind to him physically. Mentally, it has been something else.
- **Hair:** Black with streaks of gray at the temples, cut short in the imperial naval style he grew up with.
- **Beard:** Trimmed precisely — a goatee, no more. He shaves every morning without a mirror. He has not needed a mirror for decades.
- **Eyes:** Dark brown, calm, focused. Never angry. Never afraid. The eyes of a man who has killed hundreds and accepted what that means.
- **Skin:** Tanned deeply from decades at sea before he became Don Mateo's land-based enforcer. Scars mapped across his body document forty years of combat — most of them from sword cuts, a few from bullets, one long burn along his left ribs from a fire he does not speak about.
- **Clothing:** Tailored black coat with dark crimson piping — the livery of Don Mateo's enforcers, but more refined, more expensive. A white linen shirt beneath. Black trousers. High leather boots polished to a shine.
- **Weapons:** A matched pair of curved cavalry sabers, one in each hand. He has fought with two swords for thirty years. The blades are Spanish steel, 60 years old, inherited from his father who served in the imperial army. He has not needed to reforge them once.
- **Posture:** He stands the way officers are taught to stand. Weight slightly forward, shoulders back, chin level. Even at rest he is at attention.
- **Voice:** Deep, controlled, quiet. He never raises his voice. He has never needed to.

## 3. ARENA — THE MERCHANTS' PARADE GROUND

### 3.1 Physical Layout

The Merchants' Parade Ground is a large plaza in Rexa's Trading Districts — historically used for imperial military parades when the empire still held the region, now used for public ceremonies and, occasionally, executions. Don Mateo has been using it increasingly as a display ground for his authority. When Jake arrives, the plaza has been cleared. No civilians. Just Rojas, in the center, waiting.

**Arena Specifications:**
- **Size:** 35m x 35m square plaza, enclosed by colonnades on all four sides
- **Surface:** Cobblestone, well-maintained, some loose stones near the edges
- **Central Feature:** A statue of a former imperial admiral on a pedestal at the plaza's center — the statue's face has been chiseled off, presumably by Don Mateo's orders, leaving blank stone where features should be
- **Colonnades:** Covered walkways on all four sides with arched openings. Jake can retreat into these for cover but the openings force single-file movement (tactical disadvantage if Rojas follows)
- **Light:** Open to the sky. The fight occurs in bright tropical daylight unless the player arrives at a specific in-game time. Mid-afternoon is the default. Full sun is disorienting against the white stone.
- **Atmospheric Detail:** The plaza is eerily quiet. Don Mateo has ordered the surrounding neighborhoods evacuated for Jake's encounter with Rojas. The player can hear their own footsteps echoing. Distant waves from Rexa's harbor. Nothing else.

### 3.2 Entrances

Jake enters the plaza from the north colonnade. Rojas is already standing in the center, facing Jake's approach. There is no ambush, no hidden entrance, no trick. Rojas wants Jake to see him clearly. He wants the fight to be honest.

### 3.3 Rest Point

A small shrine outside the north colonnade, 45 seconds back. Several of Don Mateo's standard enforcer patrols respawn between the shrine and the plaza. These are mooks, easily defeated, but they slow Jake's returns from death.

**Note:** Rojas has only TWO phases. This is intentional. He is not a mythic boss. He is not narratively complex. He is a professional soldier. Professional soldiers have trained responses, not arcs. The two-phase structure reflects his functional nature: he fights cleanly, he fights hard, and when he is broken, he dies quickly.

## 5. THE PRE-FIGHT ENCOUNTER

Jake enters the plaza through the north colonnade. Rojas stands in the center, hands resting on the pommels of his two sabers. He does not move as Jake approaches. His posture is textbook. His expression is neutral.

When Jake is 10m away, Rojas speaks. **Middle Register throughout — military formality.**

> **ROJAS:** *"Captain Jake Harlow. I am Captain Ramón Rojas. I serve Don Mateo Salazar. You are here, as I understand it, to kill him. My duty is to prevent that by killing you first. I want you to understand the situation plainly, without pretense."*

He draws his two sabers. He does not advance.

> **ROJAS:** *"I have nothing personal against you. I have nothing personal against most of the people I have killed. I am a soldier. I do what I am told. I do it well. That is the entirety of my purpose in this world. Please do not waste my time or yours with arguments about your cause, your grievances, or the moral weight of Don Mateo's operations. I am not interested. I am fifty-six years old. I have made my peace with what I am. I do not need yours."*

Jake can choose to respond with dialogue or to draw and attack. If Jake attempts dialogue:

### 5.1 Dialogue Options (None Succeed)

**If Jake says: "You don't have to do this. Walk away."**

> **ROJAS:** *"I do have to do this, Captain. It is my duty. And I will not walk away, because I have never walked away from anything in forty years. Please attack or draw your weapon."*

**If Jake says: "Don Mateo isn't worth dying for."**

> **ROJAS:** *"That is not the question. The question is whether my oath is worth dying for. I made my oath a long time ago. I have kept it every day. Today will not be the day I break it. Please begin, Captain."*

**If Jake says: "Why do you serve him?"**

> **ROJAS:** *"Because I do. Not every man requires a reason, Captain. Some men simply choose a path and walk it. I have chosen mine. Your question is a waste of breath. Draw your sword."*

**If Jake says: "I'm sorry it has to be this way."**

> **ROJAS (the only line where something shifts slightly):** *"Thank you. That is decent of you. I do not often hear decent things before combat. Let us both fight well, then. I will give you my best. I trust you will give me yours. Draw."*

Unlike Halvard Grimm, there is no dialogue path that prevents combat with Rojas. The player cannot talk him down. This is not a failure of the dialogue system — this is Rojas's character. He has made his choice. He will not be unmade by words.

**Design principle:** After the Halvard experience (where dialogue CAN save a life), Rojas teaches the player an important lesson: not every conversation leads to a peaceful resolution. Some professionals have committed to their professions so completely that there is nothing left to negotiate. This is a different kind of tragedy than Halvard's.

## 6. PHASE 1 (100% → 60% HP): THE PROFESSIONAL

Rojas fights in his dual-saber style, which he has refined over thirty years. Every movement is precise. Every strike is committed. Every parry is efficient. He does not feint often. He does not overextend. He is what professional combat looks like.

### 6.1 The Cross-Cut
- **Trigger:** Jake within 4m
- **Windup:** 20 frames (fast — he is trained)
- **Active:** Frames 21-28 — both sabers in a crossing X-pattern
- **Recovery:** 20 frames (also fast — he recovers quickly)
- **Damage:** 70 / Posture: 40
- **Counter:** Parry at frame 24 (both sabers must be parried simultaneously — a "heavy parry" that requires timing on the Square button held for 3 frames longer than a normal parry)
- **Audio Tell:** His boot shifts once before the strike — a tell that trained players learn to spot

### 6.2 The Alternating Thrust
- **Trigger:** Jake at his side
- **Windup:** 15 frames
- **Active:** Left saber thrust, then right saber thrust (frames 16-22, then 25-31)
- **Recovery:** 25 frames
- **Damage:** 45 + 50 = 95 if both hit / Posture: 25 per hit
- **Counter:** Each thrust can be parried individually, OR Jake can sidestep the first and counter-attack during the second's windup

### 6.3 The Advancing Combo
- **Trigger:** Jake retreats
- **Windup:** 25 frames
- **Active:** Rojas walks forward deliberately while executing a four-strike combo (left, right, left, thrust)
- **Recovery:** 30 frames
- **Damage:** 30 + 35 + 30 + 60 = 155 if all hit / Posture: 20 per strike
- **Counter:** Parry the thrust (the fourth and heaviest strike) for massive posture damage. The three lesser strikes are easier to parry but less rewarding.
- **Design Intent:** Punishes runners. Forces Jake to engage Rojas in close quarters rather than kite him.

### 6.4 The Dodge
- **Trigger:** When Jake commits to a heavy attack
- **Effect:** Rojas has a 15% chance to dodge incoming heavy attacks (L2+R2 attacks). When he dodges, he repositions 2m to Jake's side and counter-attacks with an Alternating Thrust
- **Damage:** Standard Alternating Thrust damage, but with positional advantage
- **Counter:** Mix heavy and light attacks. Do not become predictable.
- **Design Intent:** Rojas is the first Rexa boss to ACTIVELY defend against the player, not just attack. This teaches players that Commander-tier enemies require tactical variation.

### 6.5 Phase 1 Dialogue

Rojas speaks little during combat. He is focused. When he speaks, it is brief.

**On first parry from Jake:**
*"Good form. Where were you trained?"*

**On landing his first hit:**
*"Your defense is adequate. Not exceptional. You will have to do better."*

**At 80% HP:**
*"You have killed more than Don Mateo warned me about. Interesting. He is not often wrong about men."*

**At 70% HP:**
*"I am beginning to respect you, Captain. This is not a compliment. This is an observation."*

**At 60% HP (Phase 2 trigger):**
*"Ah. You have earned the second form. Congratulations. Most men never see it."*

## 7. PHASE 2 (60% → 0% HP): THE WARMASTER

At 60% HP, Rojas changes his style. He drops the refined dual-saber dueling form and adopts what he calls "the warmaster style" — the style taught in the imperial army to officers facing multiple opponents in field conditions. It is faster, dirtier, more aggressive. He no longer fights like a duelist. He fights like a man at war.

### 7.1 The Whirlwind
- **Trigger:** Every 25 seconds
- **Windup:** 30 frames — Rojas plants his feet and begins rotating
- **Active:** Frames 31-60 — a full spinning attack with both sabers extended
- **Recovery:** 20 frames (minimal — he has trained for this)
- **Damage:** 60 per hit (the spin can hit Jake multiple times if he is caught in the radius)
- **Posture Damage:** 35 per hit
- **Counter:** Move outside the 4m radius during windup, OR time a perfect dodge at the moment of contact
- **Audio Tell:** A deep exhale from Rojas as he plants his feet — his personal preparation for the move

### 7.2 The Shield Break
- **Trigger:** When Jake is blocking repeatedly
- **Windup:** 20 frames
- **Active:** Rojas executes an overhead double-strike with the deliberate purpose of breaking Jake's guard
- **Damage:** 80 direct + guard stamina cost is doubled
- **Counter:** Do NOT block this attack. Parry or dodge only.
- **Design Intent:** Punishes defensive players. Teaches the player that Rojas will not let them turtle.

### 7.3 The Blooded Hand
- **Trigger:** Once, at 40% HP, scripted
- **Effect:** Rojas intentionally cuts his own palm with his left saber and transfers blood to his right blade, then wipes it across his forehead. This is an imperial army ritual before the final push.
- **Duration:** 4 seconds of animation during which Rojas is vulnerable
- **Narrative Note:** Do not attack him during this animation. If Jake does, Rojas will complete the ritual anyway but will add a line: *"You are without honor, Captain. I am disappointed."* This affects the death scene (see Section 9).
- **Design Intent:** A moment of dignity the game tests the player with. The correct response is to let Rojas complete his ritual. The game does not tell the player this.

### 7.4 The Final Form
- **Trigger:** After The Blooded Hand animation completes
- **Effect:** Rojas's damage output increases by 25% for the remainder of the fight. His dodge chance increases to 25%.
- **Visual:** His movements become looser, less formal. He is fighting fully now, holding nothing back.
- **Audio:** His breathing becomes audible for the first time — he is finally exerting himself.

### 7.5 The Last Stand
- **Trigger:** Below 15% HP
- **Effect:** Rojas stops dodging. He stops parrying. He only attacks. Every strike is committed. He is looking for the one opening to kill Jake — or the clean death he has earned.
- **Damage:** +30% on all attacks
- **Counter:** Jake must be patient and precise. One or two well-placed parries will end the fight.
- **Design Intent:** Gives the player a clear path to victory, but demands technique. Button-mashers will die here.

### 7.6 Phase 2 Dialogue

**On Phase 2 start:**
*"The second form. Let us see what you are really made of, Captain."*

**On Blooded Hand completion (if Jake did not interrupt):**
*"Thank you for the courtesy. Now let us finish."*

**On Blooded Hand completion (if Jake interrupted):**
*"You are without honor. I am disappointed. But I will kill you anyway."*

**At 30% HP:**
*"Good. Good. This is how it should be. Not many men have given me this."*

**At 15% HP (The Last Stand trigger):**
*"Very well, Captain. No more defense. Let us see who dies first."*

**At 5% HP:**
*"I do not regret my life, Captain. Remember that, when you speak of me. I do not regret it."*

## 8. THE DEATH SCENE

When Jake delivers the killing blow, Rojas does not stagger. He does not gasp. He simply stops moving. He stands upright for perhaps two seconds, sabers still in hand. Then he sinks slowly to his knees. He sets both sabers down on the cobblestone beside him, crossing them in a specific pattern — an imperial army death-arrangement that signifies a warrior's honorable end.

He looks up at Jake. His breathing is labored now, but controlled.

> **ROJAS (voice steady despite his wounds):** *"You are better than I was told. Don Mateo will be displeased. But he will be displeased AT me, not at you. I should have been more careful. I should have asked more questions about who you were before I accepted this duty."*

> *"I have a request, Captain. I do not expect you to honor it. But I will ask, because I have nothing to lose. There is a boy in Rexa. He is fourteen years old. His name is Julio. He is a trainee in Don Mateo's enforcer ranks. I have been teaching him swordplay for three years. He is not yet committed. He could still leave Don Mateo's service. Please, if you encounter him — if you recognize him — please do not kill him. Let him run. Give him the chance to become something other than what I have been."*

He coughs. Blood comes out. He does not wipe it away.

> *"That is all. I have no great final speech. I have no grand regret. I have been a soldier. I have been a good one. I have killed what I was ordered to kill and I have served the man I was ordered to serve. If that was the wrong life — and I suspect it may have been — I made peace with it many years ago. Do not waste grief on me, Captain. I do not need it."*

He dies.

His body stays upright for a moment longer. Then it topples sideways onto the cobblestones. The two sabers remain crossed where he placed them.

**Style D Death — tragic in its dignity.** Rojas dies as he lived: cleanly, precisely, without drama. This is not a failure death (like Halvard's) or a monstrous death (like Ulfar's). This is the death of a man who chose a path and walked it to the end, and asks for nothing except one small mercy for a boy he trained.

### 8.1 The Julio Quest Hook

After Rojas's death, the player receives a story flag: `rojas_death_julio_request`. This enables a later optional encounter in Rexa's Underdistrict, where Jake can encounter a 14-year-old boy being trained as an enforcer. If Jake recognizes him (through observation of his swordplay style — a clear echo of Rojas's), he can choose to let the boy escape or to kill him.

**If Jake lets Julio escape:** Julio survives. He is never mentioned again in Chapter 4. BUT in the Chapter 10 epilogue, regardless of which ending Jake achieves, a brief scene shows Julio as a young man (18 years old) working as a dock laborer in a foreign port. He has a new name. He has no weapons. He is whole.

**If Jake kills Julio:** The story flag `julio_killed` is set. No immediate consequence. But in Chapter 10, during Elin's encounter in Region 06 (if that ending path is achieved), Elin briefly mentions: *"A boy drowned on his way here. Eighteen months ago. Only one visitor that year. He was very young. I do not know his name."* The player can piece together what this means or not.

**If Jake ignores the hook entirely:** Julio's fate is unknown. He does not appear in the epilogue.

## 9. REWARDS

### 9.1 Core Rewards

**Rojas's Dual Sabers:**
- Legendary dual-wielding weapon set
- +140 base damage each (total +280 when dual-wielded)
- Unique mechanic: While both equipped, Jake can execute Rojas's Alternating Thrust combo
- Flavor text: *"Spanish steel. Sixty years old. Maintained every day by the man who wielded them last. They will not break under any condition short of direct cannon fire."*

**Rojas's Black Coat:**
- Light armor with +25% movement speed
- Flavor text: *"The coat of an enforcer. Wear it, and other enforcers will hesitate before engaging you."*

**Lore Object #009: Rojas's Service Medal**
- An imperial army medal Rojas kept from his pre-Salazar career
- Bringing it to specific NPCs (an old veteran in Rexa's Tavern District, a retired imperial officer in the Quiet Coast) triggers unique dialogue about Rojas's earlier life
- These dialogues reveal that Rojas served honorably for 25 years before joining Don Mateo — and that the imperial army specifically rejected his request for retirement, forcing him to seek "other service" to leave. This adds layers to Rojas's character that the fight itself does not have time to explore.

**800 Doubloons**

**Story Flag:** `rojas_defeated` — enables progress toward the other City Commanders

### 9.2 The Julio Hook

As described in Section 8.1. The request Rojas makes in death is his only vulnerability — and it is the vulnerability of a teacher, not a warrior. Players who honor it change Julio's fate. Players who ignore or violate it do not.

## 10. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE CHECK

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** Rojas speaks in Middle Register throughout, appropriate for a military officer. He is precise, formal, disciplined. He never drops to Low Register or rises to High Register. Consistent.

- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** Rojas's medal, his crossed sabers, his coat — each one carries his story. The Julio hook is an embodied lore element rather than exposition.

- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** Style D (tragic, dignified). Rojas dies with honor, makes a small request, acknowledges his life without regret. This is distinct from Halvard's death (tragic in delusion) and Ulfar's death (pointless, mid-sentence) and Don Mateo's death (monstrous complexity). Each Chapter 4 boss dies differently, and the deaths collectively teach the player that death in Dark Arisen carries weight.

- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** None. Correct. Rojas is not humorous. A humorous Rojas would undermine the purity of his character.

- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** Rojas is an interesting counterpoint to the theme. He is not motivated by revenge. He is motivated by duty. But Jake is killing him FOR revenge — revenge against Don Mateo and the system Rojas serves. The tension is explicit. Jake's revenge path leads through this man who has nothing personally against him. This is the cost of systemic revenge: you kill good men who happen to be in the way of bad ones. Rojas's death is the first time in Dark Arisen that Jake kills someone who has never wronged him personally. That should matter to the player. It is a step toward what Jake will confront in Region 05's civilian populations.

## 11. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

- **The Blooded Hand interrupt system:** Requires a specific mechanic to detect if Jake attacks during the ritual animation. Flag `rojas_ritual_interrupted` affects Phase 2 dialogue and death scene.
- **Dual-wielding mechanics:** Rojas's fighting style requires the animation system to support precisely coordinated two-sword movements. This is more complex than single-sword bosses.
- **Dodge chance system:** Rojas is the first boss to have an active dodge mechanic. This requires AI behavior that evaluates incoming attack types (heavy vs light) and triggers dodge states based on probability.
- **Julio hook dialogue tree:** Requires a separate questline framework that can be triggered in Rexa's Underdistrict but is entirely optional.
- **The Sabers weapon set:** Requires special dual-wielding combat implementation if the player equips both simultaneously. This may be the first dual-wielding weapon in Dark Arisen (check against existing weapon design).

## 12. WHY THIS FIGHT MATTERS

Captain Rojas is the Gatekeeper of the Rexa Commander tier. Players who defeat him have demonstrated they can handle Commander-level combat. Players who struggle here need more practice before attempting La Viuda, El Médico, or the Mire Commanders.

But Rojas is more than a skill check. He is also the first clean Commander — the one without tragic backstory, without complicated motivations, without moral ambiguity. He is simply a soldier doing his duty. Jake kills him anyway. Because Jake's path requires it.

This is Dark Arisen's quiet argument about systemic violence. Rojas did not wrong Jake. Rojas probably did not wrong most of the people he killed in service to Don Mateo. Rojas was a good soldier for a bad cause. Jake's revenge against Don Mateo's network means killing men like Rojas who are not themselves evil, just in the way.

The Julio hook is the game's small redemption for the player. If you listen to Rojas's dying request — if you honor the one small mercy he asks — you can save one boy from becoming the next Rojas. That is not justice. That is not peace. That is just a single fourteen-year-old who gets to grow into something else, because you, the player, paid attention.

Dark Arisen asks the player to pay attention many times. Rojas is one of the moments where paying attention matters most. Most players will not. Some will. The game remembers.

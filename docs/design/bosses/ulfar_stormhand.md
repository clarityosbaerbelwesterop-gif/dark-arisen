# BOSS SPEC — Ulfar Stormhand "The Old Wolf of the Fjords"

**Status:** Design Draft v1.0  
**Region:** Quiet Coast — Jernholm, The Old Imperial Fort  
**Boss Category:** Story Boss (Region 04, Chapter 7 mid-game)  
**Difficulty:** Medium-Hard (8-12 attempts average — pure brawler fight, no gimmicks)  
**Related:** region_04_quiet_coast_overview.md, style_bible.md  
**Inspirations:** Ulfberht raiders (real Viking history), Logan Ninefingers (First Law trilogy), Father Gascoigne Phase 1 (Bloodborne)

---

## 1. Concept — The Anti-Hero Boss

Ulfar Stormhand is the boss who reminds the player that not every villain in Dark Arisen is poetic. Where Don Mateo Salazar dressed his cruelty in elegance, where Draven Voss intellectualized his violence into philosophy, Ulfar is just a man who hurts other people because he can. He has no tragic backstory the player will discover too late. He has no last words that recontextualize him. He raided Sorgsby and killed 310 villagers and then retired to a comfortable life on the coast because that is what he wanted.

**This is intentional.** Dark Arisen needs at least one boss who refuses to be redeemed by narrative. Otherwise the world becomes a place where every monster is secretly understandable, and that is its own kind of lie. Some people are just monsters. Ulfar is one of them.

The player must kill him. The player must feel nothing complicated about it. And the moment after his death, the player should feel uncomfortable about how UNCOMPLICATED it felt — because that is the trick of revenge: the easy kills are the ones that warp you fastest.

**Design principle:** Mechanical brawler fight. No phases of grand revelation. No dialogue that humanizes him. Style B (brutal realism) death. The player should feel like they killed an animal — not a man. And then they should feel bad about feeling that way.

## 2. Visual Description

- **Height:** 1.96m, broad and thick, arms like beer kegs
- **Age:** 52 — old for a raider, which means he is a survivor
- **Build:** Powerful but starting to soften around the middle. Ulfar has been retired for three years and has eaten well. The fight will exhaust him faster than it would have ten years ago, and he knows it.
- **Hair:** Long, grey-blond, braided with iron rings
- **Beard:** Massive, untrimmed, with bones and small carved totems woven into it
- **Face:** Weather-beaten. A web of old scars across his left cheek and jaw. One of his front teeth is gold — taken from an imperial officer he killed twenty years ago, melted down and replaced.
- **Eyes:** Pale blue, alert, intelligent in the way a wolf is intelligent — not a man's intelligence, an animal's
- **Tattoos:** Crude blue tattoos of raid tally marks running down both forearms. Hundreds of them. He stopped counting at three hundred years ago.
- **Clothing:** Heavy fur-trimmed cloak in dark crimson (the color is deliberate — Crimson Armada heritage), iron-studded leather chest piece, fur-lined boots, no helmet. He fights without protection because he believes scars are advertisements.
- **Weapons:** A single massive bearded axe (two-handed but he wields it one-handed sometimes for the show) and a heavy round wooden shield reinforced with iron bands. The axe has a name — *Brytari* (Old Fjordlund for "Breaker"). The shield has no name. He doesn't believe defensive weapons deserve names.

## 3. Arena — The Old Imperial Fort Throne Hall

- **Setting:** The main hall of the abandoned imperial fort at Jernholm, which Ulfar has converted into his personal "throne room"
- **Size:** ~20m x 12m rectangular hall
- **Architecture:** Old imperial military stonework — square pillars, vaulted ceiling, narrow windows. The original imperial murals on the walls have been crudely defaced with raid trophies — broken weapons, torn flags, animal skulls.
- **The Throne:** At the far end, an old imperial commander's chair that Ulfar has draped with furs and called his throne. Above it on the wall, a crude wooden plaque carved by Ulfar himself: *"Ulfar Konung av Jernholm"* (Ulfar King of Iron-Home). He carved it. Nobody calls him king except himself.
- **Floor:** Stone, scattered with rushes and bones (animal bones, mostly — he eats meals here and throws bones over his shoulder). The footing is treacherous in places.
- **Furniture:** A massive wooden table covered in half-eaten food, ale jugs, weapons, and trophies. Two heavy wooden chairs. A barrel of ale near the throne.
- **Atmosphere:** It STINKS. Even in the cinematic, the player should sense that this room smells of grease, sweat, and old blood. This is not a noble warrior's hall. This is a brute's den dressed up as a kingdom.
- **Lighting:** Torches in iron brackets along the walls, smoke pooling against the ceiling. Firelight only. No windows let in much daylight.
- **Rest Point:** Just outside the fort gates, a 90-second walk back. Multiple respawning enemies between the gate and the throne room (bandit followers of Ulfar).

## 5. Pre-Fight Cinematic — The Throne Room

When Jake enters the Old Imperial Fort, he fights through a small group of Ulfar's bandit followers (about 8 mooks across multiple rooms). When he reaches the throne hall, the heavy oak doors are already open. Ulfar is sitting on his "throne" eating a piece of roasted meat with his hands. He looks up casually as Jake enters, like a man being interrupted at dinner.

**Low Register throughout. Ulfar speaks with a thick Fjordlund accent and rough vocabulary.**

> **ULFAR:** *"Hm. Another one."*

Jake walks slowly across the hall, sword drawn. Ulfar continues eating. He doesn't stand up.

> **ULFAR:** *"You're not from these villages. You smell different. Where did you sail from, boy?"*

Jake doesn't answer. He keeps walking.

> **ULFAR:** *"Quiet one. Good. The talkers tire me. I had a man from Graavig come here last winter to demand I leave the coast. He talked for twenty minutes. I let him finish. Then I broke his jaw and sent him home. You know what he learned from that? Nothing. Men like that never learn. They come back the next winter with longer speeches."*

He takes another bite. Chews. Watches Jake.

> **ULFAR:** *"You've come about Sorgsby. Yes? I can see it on your face. That's the only thing strangers come about anymore. Those wailing women on the mountain still telling their story to anyone who will listen. The pole with the names on it. Yes? Yes."*

> **JAKE (finally speaking):** *"You burned that village."*

> **ULFAR:** *"I did. Three years ago. With my own hands and the hands of my men. And do you know what I think about it now, three years later, with a full belly and a warm fire and a quiet life on the sea?"*

Long pause. Ulfar puts down the meat. Wipes his mouth on his sleeve. Looks at Jake directly.

> **ULFAR:** *"I think nothing about it. I have not thought about Sorgsby in two years. I did the job. I took what we came for. I came home and I retired and I have been living well ever since. The villagers? They were in the way of the cargo. I do not remember any of their faces. They were not interesting people. They were villagers."*

Jake's grip tightens on his sword.

> **ULFAR:** *"This is the part where you get angry, yes? Where you tell me they were people, they had names, they had children. I have heard this speech before. Many times. From many strangers. They all come here thinking they will be the one whose words finally make me feel something. None of them have ever been right. I am not built for guilt. The empire trained that out of me thirty years ago and the Crimson Armada finished the job. I am very tired of being lectured by men who think they understand me."*

He stands up. He is enormous. He picks up Brytari from where it rests against the throne.

> **ULFAR:** *"So skip the speech, little wolf. Pick up your sword properly. Come at me. If you kill me, I die. If I kill you, I finish my dinner. Either way the speech is unnecessary. We both know what we are."*

The cinematic ends. The fight begins immediately — no formal start animation. Ulfar charges across the hall as soon as the player regains control.

## 6. Moveset — Phase 1 (100% → 50% HP): The Brawler

In Phase 1, Ulfar fights aggressively but predictably. He is a veteran brawler who has won a hundred fights with the same techniques and sees no reason to change. Every attack is straightforward, telegraphed, and brutal.

### 6.1 Attack: Brytari Overhead Chop
- **Trigger:** Jake within 4m
- **Windup:** 40 frames (very telegraphed — Brytari is heavy)
- **Active:** Frames 41-48
- **Recovery:** 35 frames
- **Damage:** 65
- **Posture Damage:** 35
- **Counter:** Easy parry at frame 43, OR step-dodge sideways
- **Audio Tell:** Ulfar grunts loudly as he raises the axe

### 6.2 Attack: Shield Bash
- **Trigger:** When Jake is parrying repeatedly
- **Windup:** 20 frames
- **Active:** Frames 21-25
- **Recovery:** 25 frames
- **Damage:** 30, knocks Jake back 3m and stuns for 1 second
- **Counter:** Cannot be parried. Must dodge.
- **Design Intent:** Punishes parry-spamming. Forces the player to vary their defense.

### 6.3 Attack: Horizontal Sweep
- **Trigger:** Jake at his side
- **Windup:** 25 frames
- **Active:** Frames 26-38 (wide arc, hits 6m radius around Ulfar)
- **Recovery:** 30 frames
- **Damage:** 50
- **Counter:** Roll under, jump over, or back-dodge

### 6.4 Attack: Two-Hit Combo
- **Trigger:** When Jake is close after dodging
- **Windup:** 15 frames
- **Active:** First strike frame 16 (low diagonal), second strike frame 28 (rising opposite)
- **Recovery:** 30 frames
- **Damage:** 40 + 45 = 85 if both hit
- **Counter:** Parry one, dodge the other

### 6.5 Phase 1 Dialogue Triggers

Ulfar TALKS during combat. He is mocking, contemptuous, and entertained by the fight. His lines are delivered between attacks, not during animation interruptions.

**On first attempt (when Jake first dies and reloads):**  
*"Back already? Did you bring better moves this time?"*

(Note: Ulfar mocks the player for dying. This is the only boss in Dark Arisen who acknowledges death-restarts in dialogue. It is slightly fourth-wall-breaking and intentional — Ulfar is treating the player with the same contempt he treated Sorgsby.)

**On Jake landing first hit:**  
*"Hah. There it is. You can hit. Good. Most of them can't even do that."*

**On parrying Brytari Overhead Chop:**  
*"My old commander used to parry like that. He was a better fighter than you. He's also dead. I killed him. Take from that what you will."*

**At 80% HP:**  
*"Tell me, little wolf — was anyone in Sorgsby actually related to you? Or are you just one of those men who hunts other men's wars?"*

**At 70% HP:**  
*"You move like you have been trained. By who? Some old pirate? Some retired naval bastard? They are all dead too. The world is cleaner without them."*

**At 60% HP:**  
*"I am almost enjoying this. Almost. You are too quiet to be properly enjoyable. Say something interesting."*

**At 50% HP (Phase 2 trigger):**  
*"Alright. Alright, you have my attention. Let me show you what an old wolf still has in him."*

He throws his shield aside. It clatters on the stone floor. He grips Brytari with both hands and his stance changes — wider, lower, more committed.

## 7. Moveset — Phase 2 (50% → 0% HP): The Old Wolf

In Phase 2, Ulfar drops the shield and goes all-in. He is older than he used to be and he knows he cannot outlast Jake. So he commits to overwhelming offense. Every attack is dangerous now. He bleeds from a dozen cuts. He doesn't care.

### 7.1 Attack: Brytari Two-Handed Spin
- **Trigger:** Jake within 5m
- **Windup:** 30 frames — Ulfar plants his feet and starts the spin
- **Active:** Frames 31-60 — full 360-degree spin attack with Brytari extended
- **Recovery:** 25 frames
- **Damage:** 70 per hit (the spin can hit Jake twice if he's caught in the radius)
- **Posture Damage:** 50
- **Counter:** Move outside the 5m radius before the spin starts, OR jump over the axe at the right moment (frame 45)
- **Audio Tell:** A loud roar from Ulfar as he plants his feet

### 7.2 Attack: Charging Tackle
- **Trigger:** Jake at >7m distance
- **Windup:** 20 frames — Ulfar drops his head and runs forward
- **Active:** Frames 21-40 — full sprint with shoulder lowered
- **Recovery:** 25 frames (briefly stunned if he hits a wall)
- **Damage:** 80 if he hits, knocks Jake prone for 2 seconds
- **Counter:** Sidestep at the last moment (frame 35), or interrupt with a firearm shot during the windup
- **Design Intent:** Punishes ranged kiting. Forces commitment to close-range fighting.

### 7.3 Attack: Brytari Throw
- **Trigger:** Once per fight, at 30% HP, scripted
- **Windup:** 35 frames — Ulfar suddenly throws Brytari at Jake like a massive tomahawk
- **Active:** Frame 36 — projectile travels in a straight line at high speed
- **Damage:** 95 if it hits
- **Counter:** Dodge sideways
- **Aftermath:** Brytari sticks in a wall or pillar 8m past Jake. Ulfar must run to retrieve it. During this 8-second window, he is unarmed and vulnerable. Players who exploit this can deal massive damage.
- **Design Intent:** A risk-reward moment. Ulfar trusts that he can throw his axe and still win because he has done it a hundred times.

### 7.4 Attack: Bare-Handed Grab (only after losing Brytari)
- **Trigger:** If Brytari is across the room and Jake is between Ulfar and his axe
- **Windup:** 15 frames
- **Active:** Frame 16 — Ulfar lunges to grab Jake by the throat
- **Damage:** 40 + Bleeding debuff
- **Counter:** Dodge backward

### 7.5 Phase 2 Dialogue Triggers

**On Phase 2 start:**  
*"You are not dead yet. Interesting. Most are dead by now."*

**On landing the spin attack:**  
*"YES. Yes, that is the feeling. That is why I do this."*

**At 40% HP:**  
*"You are good. I want you to know that. Before I open you up, I want you to know I respect what you have done in this room. Not many men get this far against me. None of them survive. But the trying is worth my respect."*

(This is the closest Ulfar comes to a noble moment in the entire fight. Players might briefly think he is going to be redeemed. He is not.)

**At 25% HP:**  
*"Bleeding. I am bleeding. I have not bled this much in five years. You have made an old man feel young, little wolf. Thank you."*

**At 15% HP:**  
*"Come on, then. Come and finish it. I am not going to make it easier for you. You will have to walk into the axe."*

**At 5% HP:**  
*"I have killed three hundred and twelve men with this axe. You will be the three hundred and—"*

He never finishes the sentence.

## 8. The Death Scene — Style B Brutal Realism

Jake's killing blow lands across Ulfar's chest, cutting deep into his ribs. Ulfar staggers backward two steps. He looks down at the wound. He looks up at Jake. He opens his mouth to say something — clearly an attempt to finish his dialogue line about the count — but only blood comes out. He coughs. He tries again. Coughs more blood. Falls to his knees.

He is trying to say something. The player can see he is trying. He is failing. His mouth opens and closes. His eyes are angry — not afraid, not regretful, just ANGRY that he doesn't get to finish his sentence.

He falls forward onto his face.

He is dead.

**There is no monologue. There is no last word. There is no music swell.** The fight ends with a cough and a fall. The combat music fades. Jake stands in the throne room of a man who is now dead on the stone floor, surrounded by the half-eaten food he was enjoying twenty minutes ago.

Outside the throne hall, the player can hear the wind. Inside the hall, there is only the soft crackle of the torches and the dripping of Ulfar's blood pooling on the stone.

This is **Style B layered death** at its purest. Brutal. Anticlimactic. Real.

The player should feel cheated of catharsis. That is the point.

## 10. Combat Flow — How the Fight Should Feel

**Pre-Fight Cinematic:** Anger. Pure, uncomplicated anger. The player should want to punch Ulfar in the face the moment he opens his mouth.

**Phase 1 (The Brawler):** Cathartic combat. The player gets to fight a hateable opponent without moral complication. This is the rare Dark Arisen fight where the player can just be ANGRY and let it out.

**Phase 2 (The Old Wolf):** Earned challenge. Ulfar becomes genuinely dangerous. The player must fight at peak skill. The mocking dialogue continues, which keeps the anger fresh.

**The Final Hit:** Anticlimax. The player kills him mid-sentence. There is no closure. No revelation. No catharsis. Just a dead man on a stone floor.

**The Sorgsby Return:** Quiet acknowledgment. The villagers do not celebrate. Mormor Yrsa nods. The world does not feel better. The dead are still dead. The mountain still has 310 names on it. Ulfar's death is not a victory in any meaningful sense — it is just one less monster in the world. The player should sit with this feeling.

## 11. Style Bible Compliance Check

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** Ulfar speaks in Low Register with a rough Fjordlund accent. He is a brute, and he sounds like a brute. Correct.
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** Ulfar's Logbook is the only lore connection — but it is a powerful one. The list of 27 unavenged villages is a quiet horror that the player must process alone.
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** Style B at its purest. Brutal. Anticlimactic. Mid-sentence. No dignity. The contrast to the mythic boss deaths (Style D) is intentional and educational for the player about how the game treats different kinds of antagonists.
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** Ulfar has a mocking, dry humor that fits his character — but it is contemptuous humor, not likeable humor. The player should not laugh with him. The player should be irritated by him.
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** This fight asks: *"What does it feel like when revenge is justified and easy?"* The answer Dark Arisen gives is: *"It feels too easy. And that should worry you."* Mormor Yrsa's reaction afterward — no thanks, no celebration, just acceptance — drives this home. Killing Ulfar does not undo Sorgsby. It only adds another body to the world. Jake should feel slightly less heroic after this fight, not more.

## 12. Implementation Notes

- The "death-restart mockery" line requires a flag system that tracks player deaths against this specific boss
- Brytari's tally counter requires a unique weapon component that increments per kill
- The mid-sentence death needs custom animation and dialogue cutoff timing
- Ulfar's Logbook (Lore Object #007) must be added to the Lore Object Catalog
- Mormor Yrsa's reaction dialogue requires a story flag check for `ulfar_killed`
- The bandit followers in the fort hallways are standard mooks but should respawn if the player retreats to the rest point

## 13. Why This Fight Matters

Dark Arisen needs Ulfar Stormhand because Dark Arisen needs to teach the player that not every kill is a story. Some kills are just necessary. Some monsters are just monsters. Some men deserve to die in the middle of a sentence about their kill count, and the world is not improved by their dying — it is only slightly less infested.

If the player learns this lesson, they will approach Draven Voss differently in the finale. They will not expect Draven to be "the same as Ulfar but bigger." They will understand that Draven is something else entirely — a man who once was capable of becoming an Ulfar, and chose differently, and then chose differently again, and then chose differently a third time, and is now neither hero nor villain but exhausted complexity.

Ulfar exists so that Draven can mean something.

Without Ulfar, Dark Arisen would have no clean villains — and a world without any clean villains is just as much a lie as a world with only clean villains. Reality is mixed. The Quiet Coast is where Dark Arisen tells the truth about the mixture.

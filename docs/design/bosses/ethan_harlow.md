# BOSS SPEC — Ethan Harlow

**Status:** Design Draft v1.0  
**Region:** Open Sea — Draven's Flagship "The Crimson Verdict"  
**Boss Category:** Story Main Boss (Chapter 9 Climax — The Heart of Dark Arisen)  
**Difficulty:** Hard but designed to be EMOTIONALLY harder than mechanically (12-15 attempts average)  
**Related:** characters/ethan_harlow.md, characters/jake_harlow.md, lore_object_catalog.md, style_bible.md  
**Inspirations:** Abby vs Ellie (Last of Us 2), Baldur final fight (God of War 2018), Sephiroth (the brother who chose differently), Micah Bell (RDR2 — the betrayal that hurts)

---

## 1. Concept — The Fight You Don't Want to Win

Ethan Harlow is not a boss the player fights to defeat. Ethan is a boss the player fights to UNDERSTAND. Every other boss in Dark Arisen is an obstacle on Jake's path. Ethan IS the path. He is the reason Jake has been fighting for nine chapters. He is the brother Jake has been trying to save. And in the moments before the fight starts, the player learns the most devastating truth in the game:

**Ethan was never a prisoner. Ethan chose this.**

The fight that follows must serve this truth. It cannot feel like a normal boss fight. It cannot feel triumphant. It must feel like watching two people you love trying to kill each other while you hold the controller and cannot stop either of them. The player must want to put the controller down. The player must keep playing anyway.

This is the boss that decides whether Dark Arisen is a 7/10 game or a 10/10 game. If Ethan's fight is generic, everything else collapses. If Ethan's fight HURTS, everything else is justified.

**Design principle:** Mechanical difficulty matters less than emotional weight. Every dialogue line, every animation, every sound design choice must serve the question: *"Do I have to do this?"*

## 2. Visual Description

- **Height:** 1.88m (same as Charakter-Bibel — Jake is 1.75m, Ethan is significantly taller and broader)
- **Build:** Powerful, athletic, the body of a man who has spent a year training with the Crimson Armada's best
- **Hair:** Dark brown, longer than in the prologue flashbacks, tied back in a sailor's knot. There is gray at his temples now — too young for it, but the year has aged him.
- **Face:** Their father Thomas's face. Jake will see it the moment Ethan turns toward him on the burning deck. The resemblance should hit the player like a physical blow.
- **Clothing:** A captain's coat in deep crimson — not pirate-rags, but something tailored, dignified. He wears it like he has earned it. Because he has.
- **The Scar:** A new scar runs from his left temple to his jawline. Jake doesn't know how he got it. (Hidden lore: He got it during his first dragon-shard induction ritual. It's the mark of Draven's "true sons.")
- **The Sword:** Ethan carries a massive two-handed blade — their father's old officer's saber, modified by Crimson Armada smiths. The sword has a name now, etched into the crossguard: *"Untergang"* (German for "Downfall"). Ethan named it himself.
- **Eyes:** This is the most important detail. Ethan's eyes still recognize Jake. The player must be able to see — through the armor, through the scar, through the captain's coat — that Ethan KNOWS who Jake is. He is not a brainwashed husk. He is making a choice.

## 3. Arena — The Burning Deck of the Crimson Verdict

- **Setting:** The main deck of Draven's flagship, set on fire by Jake's ship ramming into it (the Chapter 9 cinematic that triggers the fight)
- **Shape:** Long rectangular deck, ~40m x 15m, with masts, rigging, and crates as cover/obstacles
- **Hazards:** Active fires across the deck that grow larger as the fight progresses. Spreading flames force movement. Burning sails fall periodically as scripted hazards.
- **Edges:** Both sides drop into the dark sea below. Falling off = death. The opposite end of the deck has the path back to Jake's ship; the near end has Draven's quarters (locked, will be the next fight).
- **Atmospheric Detail:** Draven Voss watches the entire fight from the helm of his flagship at the far end. He does not interfere. He stands silently with his hands folded behind his back, observing his "son" and the "brother" who came to take him back. His presence in the background is constant. The player should never forget he is watching.
- **Sky:** Storm clouds. Lightning flashes occasionally. Rain falls heavily — the rain hisses against the fire, creating steam and reduced visibility. The whole arena feels like the world itself is grieving.
- **Music:** Begins with a single cello playing a melody the player heard once before, in a flashback during Chapter 4: Ethan teaching young Jake how to swordfight on the family ship. The same melody. The player will recognize it before they consciously remember why.
- **Rest Point:** None inside the arena. The last rest point was on Jake's ship before the ramming cinematic. There is no going back.

## 5. Pre-Fight Cinematic — The Conversation

Before any combat begins, there is a 4-minute cinematic that the player CANNOT skip on first playthrough. This is intentional. The cinematic establishes everything the fight needs to mean.

**The scene:**

Jake's ship has rammed the Crimson Verdict at full speed. Both ships are burning. Jake leaps across the gap with his sword drawn. He lands on the burning deck. He sees Draven Voss at the far end, motionless, watching. He sees armed Crimson Armada soldiers everywhere — but they do not attack. They part to let him through. Draven has given orders.

Jake walks toward the helm. He passes through smoke. He passes through bodies. He passes through fire. He reaches the center of the deck.

A figure steps out of the smoke between Jake and Draven.

It is Ethan.

For three full seconds, neither of them speaks. Jake's sword is raised. Ethan's hand rests on the hilt of Untergang at his hip. The fire crackles. The rain falls. Draven watches.

Then Ethan speaks first. **Middle Register.** His voice is calm. Tired. Older than it should be.

> **ETHAN:** *"You came all this way."*

> **JAKE:** *"...Ethan?"*

> **ETHAN:** *"Yes."*

A long pause. Jake's sword lowers slightly. Not all the way. Just slightly.

> **JAKE:** *"I came to get you out. Where are your chains? I came to..."*

> **ETHAN:** *"Jake. Look at me."*

Jake looks. Really looks. He sees the captain's coat. He sees the scar. He sees the way Ethan's hand rests on his sword — not gripping it in fear, but resting on it like a man at peace with what he carries. He sees the new gray in his brother's hair. He sees Untergang.

Jake's sword drops a little more.

> **JAKE:** *"...no."*

> **ETHAN:** *"Yes."*

> **JAKE:** *"They didn't take you. You went."*

> **ETHAN:** *"I went."*

Another long pause. The rain falls. Jake's hands are shaking now. The player can see his sword tremble.

> **JAKE:** *"Mom and Dad are dead, Ethan. They killed Mom. Right in front of me. She tried to shield me and they SHOT her and—"*

> **ETHAN:** *"I know."*

> **JAKE:** *"—and you—"*

> **ETHAN:** *"I know what they did. I was there too. I have not forgotten."*

> **JAKE:** *"Then HOW—"*

> **ETHAN:** *"Sit down, little brother."*

Ethan gestures to a wooden crate on the deck. Jake does not sit. Ethan does not sit either. They stand in the rain, surrounded by fire, Draven watching from the helm.

> **ETHAN:** *"Father was a merchant. Do you remember what that means? He bought sugar from people who beat children to harvest it. He sold guns to men who used them to hold villages hostage. He paid imperial taxes that built the ships that burned coastal towns. He told us we were good people because we did not pull the triggers ourselves. He lied to us, Jake. He lied to us our whole lives."*

> **JAKE:** *"He LOVED us."*

> **ETHAN:** *"Yes. And that did not make him good. That just made him our father."*

Jake is crying now. He doesn't realize it.

> **ETHAN:** *"Draven showed me. Not by force. By conversation. He sat me down and he told me the truth and then he showed me the proof. And I asked him for two days alone to think about it. And after two days I came back and I asked him to teach me. He did not turn me. I turned myself. Do you understand?"*

> **JAKE:** *"...you LEFT me."*

> **ETHAN:** *"I thought you were dead, Jake. I watched them throw you overboard with a wound in your side and I thought my brother was at the bottom of the sea. By the time I learned otherwise, I was already... this."*

> **JAKE:** *"You could have come back. You could have FOUND me."*

> **ETHAN:** *"And said what? 'Hello, little brother, I am now a pirate, and I think our father deserved what he got'? You would have killed me on the spot. You would not have understood. You barely understand now."*

Jake's grip tightens on his sword.

> **JAKE:** *"I came nine chapters for you."*

> **ETHAN:** *"I know."*

> **JAKE:** *"Through Moran. Through Rexa. Through the volcanoes. I killed a dragon. I killed Don Mateo Salazar. I killed five of his commanders. For YOU."*

> **ETHAN:** *"I know."*

> **JAKE:** *"Come back with me. Come HOME with me."*

A very long pause. Ethan looks at Jake. Then he looks at Draven, far away at the helm. Then he looks back at Jake.

> **ETHAN:** *"This is my home now."*

Jake closes his eyes for a moment. When he opens them, his sword is fully raised again. The trembling has stopped. Something in him has settled into something terrible.

> **ETHAN:** *"I was hoping you would walk away, Jake. I really was. I told Draven 'he is too good to come this far.' Draven said you would come. Draven was right. He usually is."*

Ethan draws Untergang. The blade is enormous in his hands.

> **ETHAN:** *"I will not pretend I want this. But I will not pretend I will hold back, either. You taught me that yourself. You remember? On the ship, when I was teaching you to fight? 'A half-swung sword is the worst betrayal.' That was Father's line. He taught it to me. I taught it to you. So do not insult me by holding back."*

> **JAKE:** *"...okay."*

> **ETHAN:** *"Good. Then come, brother. Show me what you have become."*

The cinematic ends. The fight begins.

## 6. Moveset — Phase 1 (100% → 70% HP): The Brother

In Phase 1, Ethan is HOLDING BACK and the player should know it. Every attack he makes is technically perfect but emotionally restrained. He is fighting Jake the way he used to spar with Jake on the family ship — with skill, with care, with the goal of teaching, not killing. The dialogue throughout this phase reflects this.

### 6.1 Attack: Heavy Overhead
- **Trigger:** Jake within 5m
- **Windup:** 50 frames (very telegraphed)
- **Active:** Frames 51-58
- **Recovery:** 45 frames (massive punish window)
- **Damage:** 45 (lower than other bosses' heavy attacks — Ethan is pulling punches)
- **Posture Damage:** 30
- **Counter:** Parry on frame 53 for perfect timing — and Ethan will SAY something when parried (see dialogue triggers below)
- **Audio Tell:** Ethan's grunt as he raises the sword

### 6.2 Attack: Horizontal Sweep
- **Trigger:** Jake at his side
- **Windup:** 30 frames
- **Active:** Frames 31-45 (wide arc)
- **Recovery:** 30 frames
- **Damage:** 40
- **Posture Damage:** 25
- **Counter:** Dodge under or step back

### 6.3 Attack: Three-Hit Combo
- **Trigger:** Jake within 4m
- **Windup:** 20 frames
- **Active:** Three sequential strikes (frames 21-30, 35-44, 48-57)
- **Recovery:** 35 frames
- **Damage:** 25 + 25 + 30 = 80 if all hit
- **Counter:** Parry the first to interrupt the chain, OR dodge backward

### 6.4 Phase 1 Dialogue Triggers
Ethan speaks DURING the fight, not just between phases. These lines play based on player actions:

**On first parry from Jake:**  
*"You learned well. Who taught you to parry like that?"*

**On Jake landing first hit:**  
*"...good. Don't stop. If you stop, this gets worse."*

**On Jake taking heavy damage:**  
*"Get up. Get UP, Jake. Don't make me carry this for both of us."*

**On Jake healing:**  
*"Rest while you can."*

**At 90% HP:**  
*"You're holding back. I told you not to."*

**At 80% HP:**  
*"You used to cry when you missed a parry. Do you remember?"*

**At 70% HP (Phase 2 trigger):**  
*"Alright. Alright, Jake. You are not a child anymore. I see that now."*

He stops. He flips Untergang into a different grip — two-handed, lower stance. The fight changes.

## 7. Moveset — Phase 2 (70% → 30% HP): The Captain

Ethan stops holding back at 70% HP. He fights now the way Draven taught him — efficient, brutal, no wasted motion. This is the longest phase of the fight. The player will die here several times. The dialogue gets harder to listen to.

### 7.1 Attack: Devastating Charge
- **Trigger:** Jake at >8m distance
- **Windup:** 30 frames — Ethan crouches, then sprints forward
- **Active:** Frames 31-60 (full sprint across the deck)
- **Recovery:** 30 frames
- **Damage:** 70 if the charge connects
- **Posture Damage:** 50
- **Counter:** Sidestep at the last moment, OR shoot him with a firearm during the windup to interrupt
- **Audio Tell:** Ethan's grunt + the sound of his boots on the burning deck

### 7.2 Attack: Untergang Slam (Heavy Two-Hander)
- **Trigger:** Jake within 6m
- **Windup:** 35 frames
- **Active:** Frames 36-44 — devastating downward slam that creates a small shockwave
- **Recovery:** 35 frames
- **Damage:** 75 direct, 30 shockwave
- **Posture Damage:** 55
- **Counter:** Parry is risky (very tight window), dodge sideways recommended

### 7.3 Attack: The Father's Strike
- **Trigger:** Every 25 seconds
- **Windup:** 40 frames — a precise stance Jake recognizes
- **Active:** A combination of three precise strikes — the same combination Thomas Harlow taught both his sons in their childhood swordfighting lessons
- **Damage:** 30 + 35 + 50 = 115 if all hit
- **Counter:** Jake KNOWS this combination. The parry timing is the same as the lessons from his childhood. Players who have read Jake's character bible context will understand.
- **Narrative Note:** Ethan says nothing when he uses this attack. He just does it. The player should feel what it means.

### 7.4 Attack: Throwing Knife
- **Trigger:** Jake retreats to range
- **Windup:** 15 frames
- **Active:** Frame 16
- **Recovery:** 10 frames
- **Damage:** 35 with bleed DoT
- **Counter:** Dodge or block

### 7.5 Phase 2 Dialogue Triggers

**On Phase 2 start:**  
*"This is who I am now, Jake. Look at me. LOOK AT ME."*

**On Ethan landing The Father's Strike:**  
*"Do you remember? He taught us this on the way back from Cádiz. You were nine. You cried because I beat you twelve times in a row."*

**At 60% HP:**  
*"I am sorry it has to be me. Draven offered to do it himself. I asked for the privilege. I owed you that much."*

**At 50% HP (mid-combo, no animation interrupt):**  
*"You have gotten so STRONG, Jake. I am proud of you and I do not have the right to be proud of you."*

**At 40% HP:**  
*"Stop crying. STOP CRYING. You are a man, not a boy. Show me the man."*

(Note: Jake is not actually crying in the gameplay model — but the LINE makes the player feel as if he is. This is intentional emotional manipulation, and it works.)

**At 35% HP:**  
*"There is something I should tell you. Something Draven took from a dragon. It is in me now. It is what makes me certain. You will not understand until you see what it does."*

**At 30% HP (Phase 3 trigger):**  
*"Ah. There it is. The shard wants to come out and play. Forgive me, brother. This part is not mine anymore."*

Ethan staggers. Green-gold light briefly flickers from beneath his collarbone — the dragon shard from the Ashen Wyrm responding to the threat. His eyes change. His stance changes. Something is rising in him that is not Ethan.

## 8. Moveset — Phase 3 (30% → 0% HP): The Shard

In Phase 3, Ethan is no longer fully in control. The dragon shard from the Ashen Wyrm — given freely by the dragon to Draven, given by Draven to Ethan — is now actively burning in him. He is faster. He is stronger. He no longer hesitates. But he is also no longer fully Ethan, and that is why this phase is the hardest emotionally: the player is fighting something that is wearing their brother's face but is no longer entirely him.

This phase is mechanically the most challenging of the fight. Players will die here repeatedly.

### 8.1 Attack: Shard Burst
- **Trigger:** Every 15 seconds
- **Windup:** 25 frames — green-gold light gathers in Ethan's chest
- **Active:** Releases a wave of dragon-energy in a 6m radius
- **Damage:** 80, applies Burning debuff (10 HP/sec for 8 seconds)
- **Counter:** Move outside the radius during windup
- **Visual:** This is the first time in Dark Arisen the player sees dragon-energy used by a human. It should look beautiful AND wrong.

### 8.2 Attack: Inhuman Speed Combo
- **Trigger:** Jake within melee range
- **Windup:** 12 frames (faster than humanly possible)
- **Active:** A 5-strike combo at superhuman speed
- **Damage:** 25 per strike (125 total)
- **Counter:** Parry the first strike to interrupt — but the window is brutal

### 8.3 Attack: The Brother's Voice
- **Trigger:** Once, at 15% HP
- **Windup:** 60 frames — Ethan stops moving entirely
- **Active:** The shard's grip on him weakens for one moment. The green light dims. Ethan's eyes return to normal. He looks at Jake. He says, in his real voice, in **High Register** for the only time in the entire game:

> *"Jake. The shard is in my chest. Cut it out. Do not stop. I cannot hold it for long. Cut it OUT, brother."*

- **What happens next depends on the player:**
  - **If the player attacks his chest in the next 5 seconds:** Triggers the secret third ending (see Section 10)
  - **If the player attacks anywhere else, OR hesitates:** The shard reasserts control. Ethan's eyes go gold again. He resumes the fight.

This is the single most important mechanical-emotional moment in Dark Arisen. The player has 5 seconds to decide if they trust their brother enough to attack his exposed chest — or if it's a trick.

### 8.4 Phase 3 General Dialogue (Shard Voice)

The shard speaks through Ethan in Phase 3. Its voice is different — deeper, layered, alien. Subtitles distinguish them: ETHAN's lines are normal, the SHARD's lines are in italics with green text.

**On Phase 3 start:**  
*"Step aside, little human. Your brother is mine now."*

**On Jake landing a hit:**  
*"You hurt this body. The body does not matter. There are always more bodies."*

**On The Brother's Voice moment failing (Jake didn't attack the chest):**  
*"Did you see his face? He thought you would do it. I knew you would not. You are too small for this."*

## 9. The Three Endings

Ethan's fight has three possible outcomes. The player chooses through their actions, not through a menu.

### Ending A — The Killing
**Trigger:** Player defeats Ethan normally (depletes HP through Phase 3 without responding to The Brother's Voice moment, OR responds but attacks somewhere other than the chest).

**What happens:** Ethan falls to his knees on the burning deck. The shard's light dims as his life drains. The shard does not die — it merely loses its host. As Ethan's body fails, the SHARD inside him continues speaking in its alien voice for a moment, then goes silent. In the last moments, Ethan returns. His eyes clear. He looks at Jake.

> **ETHAN (in his real voice, weak):** *"You... you didn't hold back. Good. Mama hated when I held back at chess, do you remember? She said... she said it was the worst kind of disrespect."*

> **JAKE (kneeling beside him):** *"Ethan, I'm sorry, I'm sorry, I—"*

> **ETHAN:** *"Don't. Don't. I made my choices. You made yours. We both came here on purpose."*

A long pause. Ethan reaches up with his free hand and touches Jake's face.

> **ETHAN:** *"My little... storm."*

He uses Mama's nickname for Jake. The same nickname from the wax-sealed letter. Jake breaks completely. Ethan dies in his arms. Draven Voss, watching from the helm, lowers his head briefly — for the first time in nine chapters, Draven shows visible grief — and then turns and walks into his cabin to wait for Jake.

**Reward:** The Cutlass of the First Mate (Ethan's first sword from childhood, found in the cabin afterward), 500 Doubloons, Story Flag `ethan_killed`.

### Ending B — The Sparing
**Trigger:** Player reduces Ethan to 1 HP without delivering a killing blow, then waits 10 seconds without attacking.

**What happens:** Ethan, broken on the deck, looks at his sword. He looks at Jake. He sees that Jake is not striking the killing blow. Something passes across his face — not relief, not gratitude, something more complicated. Then he stands. With effort. He limps to the railing of the burning ship.

> **ETHAN:** *"You should have killed me, Jake. Now I have to live with this. You are crueler than you know."*

He throws himself overboard into the dark sea. Jake runs to the railing. Ethan is gone. The storm and the sea swallow him.

It is unclear whether he survives. The game treats it as ambiguous — Ethan may return in a future chapter, or he may have drowned. The player will not know until much later in the game, possibly never.

**Reward:** Ethan's Crimson Coat (light armor, +20% to all stats but counts as "stolen from family" with NPC dialogue consequences), Story Flag `ethan_spared`.

### Ending C — The Cutting (Secret Third Ending)
**Trigger:** Player must respond to The Brother's Voice moment in Phase 3 by attacking Ethan's chest within the 5-second window. Additionally requires the player to have collected ALL of the following throughout the game:
- Object #002 (Mother's Wax-Sealed Letter)
- Object #019 (Ethan's Hidden Journal — found earlier on the same flagship before the cinematic)
- Story flag `wyrm_truth_revealed` from the Ashen Wyrm fight

**What happens:** Jake's blade strikes Ethan's chest at the exact moment Ethan asked for it. The dragon shard erupts from the wound — a glowing green-gold crystal the size of a fist. It hovers for a moment above Ethan's body, screams in the alien voice of the shard, then crumbles into dust on the wind. Ethan collapses, gasping, but alive.

The shard's grip on him is broken. He is himself again. He is broken — physically, emotionally, mentally — but he is HIM.

> **ETHAN (weak, real voice):** *"Did... did you really do it? You really cut me?"*

> **JAKE:** *"You told me to."*

> **ETHAN:** *"I am the worst brother who ever lived. I do not deserve this."*

> **JAKE:** *"Shut up. We're going home."*

Jake helps Ethan to his feet. They limp together back toward Jake's ship. Draven Voss watches them go from the helm. He does not stop them. He does not call his men to attack. He just watches, expressionless, as the two brothers walk past him.

When they pass Draven, Ethan stops. He looks at the man who was his second father.

> **ETHAN:** *"Goodbye, Father."*

Draven says nothing. He inclines his head once. Ethan keeps walking.

In Chapter 10 of Ending C, Ethan fights ALONGSIDE Jake against Draven Voss. He is permanently injured (lower stats, slower movement) but he is there. The final boss fight is a 2v1 with the brothers reunited.

**Reward:** Ethan as a permanent companion in Chapter 10. Story Flag `ethan_saved`. The unique secret ending of Dark Arisen unlocks.

## 10. The Death Scene Variations

Each of the three endings has a different death scene for Ethan, ranging from devastating (A) to bittersweet (B) to triumphant-but-broken (C). All three are valid endings to Dark Arisen. None of them is "the right one." That is the point.

## 11. Combat Flow — How the Fight Should Feel

**Pre-Fight Cinematic:** The player should be SHAKING by the end of the conversation. Many will need to pause. That is correct.

**Phase 1 (The Brother):** Cautious. Sad. The player can see Ethan holding back and it makes them angry — angry that their brother is treating them like a child even now. They press harder than they should.

**Phase 2 (The Captain):** Brutal. The player is now fighting their brother for real, and Ethan is a full Crimson Armada captain. This phase tests mechanical skill. Players will die. Each death feels personal.

**Phase 3 (The Shard):** Otherworldly. The shard is in control. The player is fighting a thing that is wearing their brother. The Brother's Voice moment is the entire game distilled into 5 seconds: do you trust him?

**The Final Hit / Resolution:** Whatever ending the player gets, they will sit with it. Ending A makes them grieve. Ending B makes them wonder. Ending C makes them weep with a kind of broken happiness.

## 12. Style Bible Compliance Check

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** Ethan speaks in Middle Register normally — that's correct for an antagonist with relationship to the protagonist. He shifts to High Register ONCE in The Brother's Voice moment, which is the most important line of the fight. The Shard speaks in alien High Register because it is not human.
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** The dragon shard from the Ashen Wyrm fight is referenced and made physical here. The wax-sealed letter from Object #002 pays off in Ethan's final words. The journal (#019) is required for the secret ending. Lore connects through objects, not lectures.
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** Style D (tragic, meaningful) at maximum intensity. Three different death variations, all heavy. Critical multiplier is REDUCED (2x instead of 3x) so kills don't feel triumphant.
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** None. Correct. There is no place for humor in this fight.
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** This IS the theme made flesh. Jake came nine chapters for revenge against the people who took Ethan. He learns Ethan was never taken. His entire revenge journey was built on a misunderstanding. Now the question is: what does revenge mean when the person you were avenging is the person you must kill? This is the central question of Dark Arisen, and Ethan's fight is where it must be answered.

---

## 13. Implementation Notes

- Pre-fight cinematic must be unskippable on first playthrough (can be skipped on NG+ only)
- Dialogue trigger system must support mid-attack interruption — Ethan speaks DURING animations
- The Brother's Voice moment requires precise hit detection on the chest hitbox
- Three story flags required: `ethan_killed`, `ethan_spared`, `ethan_saved`
- Ethan as Chapter 10 companion (Ending C) requires a full companion AI behavior implementation
- Music: The cello melody from Jake's childhood flashback must be composed FIRST and integrated into Chapter 4 BEFORE this fight, so the player recognizes it
- Voice acting for Ethan is critical — possibly the most important voice acting in the entire game

## 14. Why This Fight Matters

If Dark Arisen does ONE thing perfectly, it should be this fight. Everything else can be 8/10. This fight has to be 11/10. Because this fight is what people will tell their friends about. This fight is what they will write retrospective videos about three years after launch. This fight is what makes Dark Arisen unforgettable instead of just good.

The technical mechanics are solid Souls-Like fundamentals. But the emotional mechanics — the dialogue, the music, the recognition, the three endings — those are what elevate this fight from "boss battle" to "experience." 

We are not making a fight. We are making the moment a player will remember a decade later when they think about Dark Arisen.

That is the bar. Nothing less is acceptable.

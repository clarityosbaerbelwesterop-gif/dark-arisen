# REGION 04 — THE QUIET COAST: VILLAGES IN DETAIL

**Status:** Design Draft v1.0  
**Related:** region_04_quiet_coast_overview.md, style_bible.md  
**Purpose:** Detailed breakdown of the four main settlements of the Quiet Coast. Each village is a self-contained emotional experience, building toward Chapter 7's themes of loss, listening, and the aftermath of revenge.

---

## HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

Each village follows the same template:
1. **First Impression** — What Jake sees when he first arrives
2. **Layout** — Key locations and NPCs
3. **Core NPCs** — Named characters with dialogue snippets (in correct register)
4. **Side Content** — Quests and activities unique to this village
5. **What Jake Takes Away** — The emotional/narrative residue

All dialogue follows the Style Bible: Fjordlund villagers speak in **Low Register** (slow, weighty, practical, rarely poetic). Exceptions are noted.

## 1. KALDVIK — THE FISHING VILLAGE

### First Impression
Jake's ship approaches Kaldvik through a thick morning fog. The harbor only becomes visible when La Liberación is already inside the sheltered bay — which is how the Fjordlund designed it, centuries ago. The village rises out of the mist in layers: stone cottages with turf roofs clinging to the hillside, wooden drying racks heavy with salted cod, a stave church at the top of the hill with smoke rising from its chimney, and above everything, the cry of seabirds.

The smell hits before the sight: wet wool, wood smoke, fish guts, salt. It is the smell of a place where people work for their food every day and have done so for generations.

A dozen villagers stop what they are doing to watch La Liberación dock. They do not wave. They do not shout greetings. They just watch — with the same patient attention you would give a large animal that has wandered into your yard.

An old man with a fishing net draped over one shoulder is the first to speak. He walks down the dock alone. He looks at Jake's crew. He looks at the damaged hull of La Liberación. He looks at Jake's sword.

> **OLD MAN:** *"You've come a long way. The coast is not kind to ships from the south. You should rest before you go further. Our inn has beds and our cook makes a soup that hurts less than most. Come."*

He turns and walks back up the dock without waiting for an answer.

This is the player's introduction to the Quiet Coast.

### Layout

- **The Harbor** — Shallow, sheltered, room for three ships. La Liberación can dock here.
- **The Drying Racks** — Lines of wooden frames along the shore, heavy with salted fish. NPCs work here daily.
- **The Longhouse Inn** — A large stone-and-timber building near the harbor. Three rooms upstairs, a main hall downstairs with fire pit and long tables. Run by Mira's cousin (yes, that Mira — the Rexa-born crew member. A coincidence that Jake will notice.)
- **The Stave Church of Kaldvik** — Built in 1683, one of the oldest buildings on the coast. Houses a small congregation and a very old priest. Holds cultural and narrative importance.
- **The Fish Market** — A small open-air space where daily catches are sold. Jake can buy and sell here.
- **Olaf's Forge** — A one-man smithy run by a deaf blacksmith. Useful for weapon upgrades.
- **The Cliffside Path** — Leads north out of the village, toward the first lighthouse (Old Thornwatch).

### Core NPCs

#### Torvald Fisknev — The Harbor Master
**Age:** 61  
**Description:** The old man who greeted Jake at the dock. Weather-beaten face, hands like worn leather, one eye clouded by cataracts. He has been harbor master of Kaldvik for thirty years. Before that, he was a fisherman. Before that, he was a child who watched his father die in a storm at age nine.

**What He Knows:** Everything that happens on the Kaldvik docks. Every ship that comes and goes. Every rumor carried by every sailor. He is the unofficial intelligence network of the Quiet Coast.

**Key Dialogue — When Jake asks about the region for the first time:**
> *"The Quiet Coast is not a place. It is what the world becomes when the world stops paying attention. Look at us. Look at what we have built. Stone and fish and stories. Three hundred years of stone and fish and stories. Nobody comes here anymore. Nobody threatens us. Nobody saves us. We just... continue. That is the Quiet Coast. It continues."*

**Key Dialogue — When Jake asks about Draven Voss:**
> *"Ah. That name. I have heard it three times in my life. The first time from an old imperial officer who came here to die forty years ago. The second time from a Crimson Armada raider who came here to drink seven years ago. The third time is now, from you. Each man said the name differently. Each man meant a different thing. I do not ask you what you mean by it. I will find out when you come back — or when you do not come back."*

**Quest Offered:** *"The Shepherd's Loss"* (see Side Content below)

---

#### Inga Fisknev — Torvald's Granddaughter
**Age:** 14  
**Description:** A quiet girl with her grandfather's cloudy eye and her dead mother's stubborn jaw. She works the drying racks alongside the adults and says almost nothing. She has been orphaned twice — once by her father's drowning when she was three, once by her mother's illness when she was nine. Torvald is the only family she has left.

**What She Knows:** The old Fjordlund songs. Her mother taught her. Inga is the youngest person in Kaldvik who can still sing them correctly.

**Key Dialogue — Only accessible if Jake has earned Torvald's trust first:**
> *"Grandfather says you carry heavy weight on your back. He says I should not speak to you. But I am going to speak to you because I want to know one thing. Did you love the people you lost?"*

If Jake says yes:
> *"Then you will not stop. Grandfather says men who loved do not stop. Only men who did not love stop, because they were never really going anywhere. Go safely, stranger. I will sing your name if you do not come back."*

If Jake says he does not know:
> *"Then you have more hunting to do than you think. I am sorry."*

(Inga's dialogue is one of the rare moments in Dark Arisen where a child delivers narrative weight. The player should remember her.)

---

#### Father Knut — Priest of the Stave Church
**Age:** 74  
**Description:** A tall, thin priest in simple black robes. White beard down to his chest. His hands shake slightly from age. He conducts services in a mix of Fjordlund Old Tongue and imperial common. Most of his congregation is elderly.

**What He Knows:** The history of the Quiet Coast going back to the founding of Kaldvik. He has a library — small but carefully preserved — of handwritten histories, church records, and personal journals donated by dying parishioners.

**Register Shift:** Father Knut is the ONE NPC in Kaldvik who occasionally shifts from Low Register to Middle Register when discussing matters of faith and history. This is intentional — he is the closest thing the village has to a scholar.

**Key Dialogue — When Jake enters the church:**
> *"Welcome, traveler. You are the first stranger to enter this church in four years. The last was a young woman looking for her lost brother. I could not help her, but she sat in the back pew for two hours and then left without saying anything. I prayed for her afterward. I will pray for you as well."*

**Key Dialogue — When Jake asks about the old religion:**
> *"We are a church of two gods, you see. The imperial God we inherited from the deserters three hundred years ago, and the old gods who were here before. I serve both. I see no contradiction. The villagers here believe the dead live in the mountain, not in heaven. I do not argue with them. I have buried too many people to be certain about where any of them went. Faith is what we give to each other when certainty has failed. That is all."*

**Quest Offered:** *"The Old Priest's Confession"* (see Side Content below)

---

#### Olaf the Deaf — Blacksmith
**Age:** 58  
**Description:** A massive bald man who communicates through grunts, gestures, and a surprisingly expressive face. Deafened at age 16 when an imperial cannon fired too close to his head during a raid on Kaldvik (the raid was repelled, but Olaf lost his hearing forever). Works alone in his forge. Has no family.

**What He Knows:** How to upgrade weapons. He reads lips imperfectly but remembers faces. If Jake brings him specific materials, he can craft unique Fjordlund-style weapons.

**Dialogue:** Olaf does not speak. He grunts. He gestures. He points. Jake interacts with him through a simplified interface (choose item to upgrade, show materials, accept price). When the upgrade is done, Olaf claps Jake on the shoulder once — hard — and returns to his forge.

The player should find this strangely moving. Olaf has lost more than his hearing. He has lost the world of voices. And yet he still gives his work with care.

### Side Content — Kaldvik

#### Quest: "The Shepherd's Loss"
**Giver:** Torvald Fisknev  
**Type:** Side quest, combat-light  

An old shepherd named **Bo** has lost his entire flock to wolves in the hills above Kaldvik. He is too old to hunt them himself. Torvald asks Jake to track and kill the wolf pack — or find another solution.

The twist: when Jake tracks the wolves, he finds they are starving. Their usual prey has been driven off by unusual imperial patrols in the north (foreshadowing Region 05). Jake has three options:

1. **Kill the wolves** — Straightforward, earns 200 Doubloons from Bo
2. **Lead the wolves away from Bo's flock** toward the north (requires a specific herb lure) — Wolves survive, Bo's flock is safe, but a nearby village starts losing sheep in the following week. Jake will hear about it.
3. **Return to Bo and ask him to move his flock to a safer pasture** — Bo refuses at first but agrees if Jake helps him physically move the sheep. Takes an in-game day. Wolves survive, sheep survive, Bo develops respect for Jake.

**Reward (Option 3 only):** **The Shepherd's Charm** — a small carved wooden sheep that Bo's wife made before she died. It has no combat stats. But wearing it grants Jake a subtle passive: wild animals in future regions are slightly less aggressive toward him. The effect is never explained in the menu. Players who pay attention will notice it. Players who do not will get killed by a bear in Region 06 that would have left them alone.

**Design Note:** This quest is a small teaching moment about consequences. Option 1 is what most players will do instinctively. Option 3 is the answer the game wants them to find. The gap between the two is the lesson.

#### Quest: "The Longhouse Song"
**Giver:** Inga Fisknev (requires Torvald's trust first)  
**Type:** Cultural, no combat  

Inga asks Jake to find a specific old song her mother used to sing — a song about "the whale who remembered his mother." Inga remembers the melody but has forgotten most of the words. She wants the full song before Torvald dies, so she can sing it at his funeral someday.

Jake must visit all four villages (Kaldvik, Graavig, Sorgsby, Jernholm) and ask elderly villagers about the song. Each village contributes one verse. In Sorgsby, a widow sings the final verse for Jake — the verse is about loss and remembering, and she cannot finish it without breaking down.

When Jake returns to Inga with all four verses, she writes them down in her own hand on a scrap of parchment. She thanks him formally. Then she asks if he will come back someday, and he does not know what to say. The quest ends.

**Reward:** **The Whale Song** — a rest point bonus. If Jake rests at the Kaldvik Longhouse Inn, Inga will sing the song for him (if she is still in the village). The rest heals extra HP and restores a consumable cigar for the Rache system. The effect persists for the entire game as long as Inga is alive.

This is the kind of quest that rewards attention, not completion speed. Players who rush will never find it.

#### Activity: Fishing
In Kaldvik, Jake can take one of Torvald's boats out for a few hours to fish. This is a minigame, not a quest. It produces raw materials for cooking and crafting. Rare catches include:
- **The Old Whaler's Hook** — A legendary trophy that unlocks a cutscene flashback of Torvald's father dying in the storm, told through Torvald's own words when Jake brings him the hook.

---

### What Jake Takes Away From Kaldvik

Kaldvik is Jake's introduction to the Fjordlund rhythm. Slow. Patient. Tired. Kind in the way that people are kind when they have already buried too many things to waste energy on cruelty. Torvald and Inga and Father Knut and Olaf are not heroic. They are not even particularly interesting. They are just people who live here, and have been living here, and will continue living here long after Jake sails south toward Fort Carrion.

When Jake leaves Kaldvik, he should feel that he has been somewhere real. Not a game-world village. A PLACE. With people in it. And he is the strange thing passing through — not them.

## 2. GRAAVIG — THE TRADING POST

### First Impression
Graavig sits at the mouth of a narrow fjord, where a river from the inland valley meets the sea. It is the largest of the four villages and the only one with anything resembling a proper market. When Jake arrives, the harbor is busier than Kaldvik — small trading boats move in and out, wool wagons roll down from the hills, and the smell is different: less fish, more sheep, more iron, more bread.

Graavig is where the Quiet Coast meets the inland. The Fjordlund here are slightly more comfortable with strangers because strangers bring trade. They are still slow to warm up, but they will sell to anyone who has coin.

### Layout

- **The Market Square** — Open-air market with ~12 stalls. Vendors sell wool, fish, iron tools, herbs, smoked meat, and occasional rare goods.
- **The Iron Trader's House** — The wealthiest house in Graavig, owned by the only merchant who deals in imperial-quality iron. Jake can buy armor upgrades here.
- **The Sheep Pens** — Large enclosures where wool and meat are traded. Smells powerful.
- **The Stave Church of Graavig** — Smaller and newer than Kaldvik's, but better maintained.
- **The Fjord Inn** — A proper inn with six rooms, a large hall, and the best cook on the Quiet Coast. Run by a family of four generations.
- **The River Path** — Leads inland to the valley and the herb springs. Rarely traveled by strangers.

### Core NPCs

#### Signe Järnsdottir — The Iron Trader
**Age:** 48  
**Description:** A sharp-eyed woman in a heavy wool dress with iron clasps. Widow. Three adult children who have left the coast for the cities. Runs the iron trade alone and does so better than any man in the region.

**What She Knows:** Prices. Supply routes. Which ships have been seen where. She keeps a ledger of every trade she has done in the last twenty years.

**Key Dialogue:**
> *"You are the stranger from the south. I saw your ship from my upper window yesterday. I have been expecting you to come find me. Everyone eventually comes to find me — I am the only one on this coast who will actually sell you useful things instead of just fish and sheep. Sit. What do you need? I warn you, my prices are fair but they are not cheap. Fair and cheap are not the same word."*

**Services:** Signe sells weapon upgrades, armor crafting materials, iron-quality tools, and rare herbs. She also buys anything Jake loots from imperial or Crimson Armada sources at a markup — she has contacts in the cities who pay well for these items.

**Hidden Quest:** If Jake sells her a specific rare item (the **Crimson Armada Officer's Ring**, which can be looted from Ulfar Stormhand's corpse), Signe will lean forward and ask where he got it. If Jake answers honestly, Signe quietly reveals that her youngest daughter — Astri — was taken by the Crimson Armada twelve years ago and never returned. Signe has been trying to find out what happened. She gives Jake a locket with Astri's portrait and asks him to show it to any captive he might free in Region 05. 

(This is a minor but haunting questline. Most players will miss it.)

---

#### Bjorn Fjordmann — Innkeeper of The Fjord Inn
**Age:** 52  
**Description:** A round, jovial man with a perpetually red face and a booming laugh. The most cheerful person on the Quiet Coast by a significant margin — and the player will eventually learn that this cheerfulness is a mask over something specific and painful.

**Register Note:** Bjorn is the rare Low Register NPC who uses CHARACTER-BASED HUMOR (Style C, Pillar 4). His humor is quirky, specific, and personal — not joke-telling, just the way he sees the world.

**Key Dialogue — First meeting:**
> *"Welcome, welcome! A new face! I have not seen a new face in four months! Four months! The last new face was a cooper from Jernholm and he only came to complain about his wife. You are better than a cooper from Jernholm, I can already tell. Sit. Eat. My wife makes a stew with four kinds of meat and I cannot tell you what any of them are, but it is very good and nobody has died from it yet, which I consider a win."*

**Bjorn's Quirks (Pillar 4 Humor):**
- He names every barrel of ale in his cellar after a different Fjordlund mythical creature. ("This is Gundrak, he is the small one. This is Morthgrim, he makes you sad if you drink too much. This is Ylfthara, she will change your life, but only if you have earned it.")
- He believes his dead wife visits him as a crow every morning. He leaves her bread on the windowsill. The bread is always gone by noon. He has been doing this for eleven years and has never doubted it once.
- He keeps a list of every song he has ever heard and rates them out of ten. His favorite song is a Kaldvik sea shanty that he gives 9/10 "because the last verse makes me cry and I deduct one point for inconvenience."

**What He Knows:** Everything that happens in Graavig and half of what happens in Kaldvik. He is a gossip. He pretends he is not.

**Dialogue at 3am if Jake is still in the inn:**
> *"The crow came early tonight. That means a storm is coming. Or it means she is worried about me. Or it means nothing, because she is a crow and crows come when they come. I have stopped knowing which is which. I have stopped caring. I am just happy she still comes."*

This is Pillar 4 humor done right: it is funny AND it is devastating, and the two things are inseparable. The player might laugh the first time they hear it. They will not laugh the second time.

---

#### Mormor Astrid — The Wool Seller
**Age:** 82  
**Description:** The oldest living person in Graavig. She runs a small wool stall in the market and has been selling wool at that exact spot for sixty-three years. She has outlived two husbands, four children, and nine grandchildren. Only one great-granddaughter is still alive, and she lives in a city somewhere Astrid has never been.

**What She Knows:** The old ways. The songs. The cairn-rituals. The names of every Fjordlund ancestor she can remember — and she can remember a LOT. She is a living history book.

**Key Dialogue — When Jake asks about the old religion:**
> *"The dead live in the mountain. Everyone knows that. What most people forget is that the mountain listens back. If you speak to your dead at a cairn, the mountain hears you and carries the words to them. You do not believe me. I can see it on your face. That is alright. Belief is for the young. The old have seen the mountain answer."*

**Activity:** Jake can help Mormor Astrid by carrying her wool to the dock at the end of each market day. This costs him nothing but time. After doing it three times, she begins telling him stories of the Quiet Coast's past — including, eventually, the true story of the village before Sorgsby was a mourning village. She knew people from Sorgsby before the raid. She knew their names.

**Reward for three days of helping:** **Mormor Astrid's Song of Naming** — a short prayer that Jake learns by heart. If he speaks it at any cairn in the Quiet Coast or in Region 05, a small visual effect occurs: a brief warmth, a whisper of wind, nothing mechanical. But something intangible happens to Jake's inner state, and the next boss fight he enters has slightly altered music — a distant hum of Fjordlund voices under the combat score. It is not a mechanical buff. It is the mountain listening.

### Side Content — Graavig

#### Quest: "The Old Priest's Confession"
**Giver:** Father Knut (from Kaldvik, but triggered in Graavig)  
**Type:** Dialogue-heavy, no combat  

Father Knut tells Jake that Graavig has a priest named **Father Eivind** who is dying. Before he dies, he wants to confess something he did forty years ago but has never spoken about. Father Knut asks Jake to travel to Graavig and listen — because a stranger is the only person Father Eivind will confess to. His own congregation loves him too much.

When Jake arrives at the Stave Church of Graavig, he finds Father Eivind in a small back room, thin and gray and barely able to sit upright. What Eivind confesses is this: forty years ago, when he was a young priest, a group of imperial deserters came to his church seeking sanctuary. They were wounded. They had been pursued by their own fleet for desertion.

Eivind turned them away. He was afraid of the imperial patrols that might follow them. He told them to leave and they left. Later that night, the imperial patrol found them on the road and killed all six of them. Eivind has lived with this for forty years.

> **FATHER EIVIND:** *"I was twenty-three years old. I was afraid. That is the whole reason. Not because they deserved to die. Not because I believed in the empire. Just because I was twenty-three and afraid. I have been a priest for fifty-one years and I have never spoken a truer sermon than the one I am giving you right now, stranger, and the sermon is this: the worst things most of us do in our lives are done out of fear. Not cruelty. Not ambition. Just fear. I want you to know that before I die. Because you carry a sword and you are going to kill people with it, and I want you to know that most of the people you kill will be doing what they do because they are afraid. That does not mean you should not kill them. It means you should know what you are killing when you kill them."*

Jake can ask for a formal absolution on Eivind's behalf, or he can simply sit with the old priest in silence. Both options end the quest.

**Reward:** No mechanical reward. The player receives the knowledge. That is all.

**Style Note:** This is the kind of side quest that Dark Arisen exists to deliver. No combat. No loot. Just a small devastating piece of truth about human weakness. Players who engage with it will carry Eivind's sermon into every future fight.

#### Activity: The Graavig Market
Standard market activity. Jake can buy and sell goods, upgrade his weapons at Signe's shop, restock consumables, and so on. The market is the only reliable commerce in Region 04.

---

### What Jake Takes Away From Graavig

Graavig is the "comfortable" village — and that is precisely why it hits hardest, in its own way. Bjorn's humor hides his grief. Signe's professionalism hides her missing daughter. Father Eivind's sermons hide forty years of regret over one act of cowardice. Mormor Astrid's wool stall is the last connection to a family she has lost piece by piece.

The lesson of Graavig is that normal life is not what it looks like. Everyone is carrying something. The trade goes on. The inn stays open. The church holds services. The wool gets sold. And underneath all of it, every single person is quietly holding grief they never show.

This is the Quiet Coast's hidden argument: **everyone on this coast has lost someone. Everyone is still living anyway. The living is the point.**

## 3. SORGSBY — THE MOURNING VILLAGE

### First Impression
Jake's first view of Sorgsby comes from the cliff path above the village. He looks down and sees: seventy-one houses. Forty-four of them are rebuilt, clearly newer than the others. Twenty-seven are empty shells — stone foundations, burned timbers, never rebuilt. The village is too quiet. No children shouting. No dogs barking. Only the wind and the sea and one distant sound — a slow, rhythmic knocking.

As Jake descends the path, he identifies the sound: an old woman carving names into a wooden pole with an iron chisel. She works outside a small stone house at the edge of the village. She does not look up as he approaches. She simply continues carving.

This is Jake's introduction to Sorgsby, and to Mormor Yrsa.

### Layout

- **The Stav Pole Plaza** — A small open space at the center of the village where the stav poles stand. The newest pole is half-carved. Mormor Yrsa carves it.
- **The Burned Houses** — Twenty-seven empty shells around the village. None of them have been rebuilt. The survivors cannot bring themselves to tear them down either.
- **The Mountain Path** — Leads up behind the village to the caves where survivors hid during the Raid of 1849. Still used for rituals and burials.
- **The Fishing Pier** — Rebuilt after the raid. Quiet. Used by six remaining fishermen.
- **The Widow's Row** — A single street of stone cottages where all the widows of the raid now live together. They share meals, raise each other's children, and mourn together.
- **The Hall of Names** — A small wooden building that contains the older stav poles from centuries past. Jake can enter and read the names carved on them.

### Core NPCs

#### Mormor Yrsa — The Carver
**Age:** 71  
**Description:** A tall woman whose back has bent with age. Long white hair in a single braid down her spine. Iron-gray eyes. Hands scarred from decades of carving. She lost her husband, both sons, a daughter-in-law, and two grandchildren in the Raid of 1849. She has been carving the new stav pole for the victims ever since — a pole she has been working on for three years and has not yet finished.

**Register Note:** Mormor Yrsa speaks in **Low Register** most of the time, but shifts to **Middle Register** when she speaks about the dead. This is the ONLY NPC in Kaldvik/Graavig/Sorgsby who shifts registers mid-conversation. It is intentional and it is important.

**Key Dialogue — First meeting:**
> *"Sit. You are not the first stranger to come here after the raid. You will not be the last. I have a question for every stranger who arrives. May I ask it?"*

If Jake says yes:
> *"What are you looking for here?"*

Jake's answers:
- *"I'm looking for someone."* — *"Who?"* (If Jake says his brother: *"Your brother is not here. No brothers are here. Only names on a pole. You are looking in the wrong place."*)
- *"I'm looking for the man who did this."* — *"His name is Ulfar Stormhand. He lives in Jernholm. I do not know if you are the one who will kill him. Many strangers have said they would. None of them have come back to tell me they did it. Perhaps you will be different. Perhaps not. I will be here either way."*
- *"I'm not sure what I'm looking for."* — *"Then you are the right kind of stranger to sit with me. Most of the strangers who come here already know. The ones who do not know are the ones who find things."* (This unlocks the Names in the Mountain questline.)

**Middle Register Shift — When Mormor Yrsa speaks about the dead:**
> *"My husband's name was Olav. He was a fisherman, like all the men of Sorgsby, and he was not a brave man, and I loved him for that. Brave men die young and leave their wives with silence. Olav was careful. He came home every night. Until the night he did not. My sons were Ivar and Rolf. They were twenty-six and twenty-three. Neither of them had married yet. That is the thing I think about most now — that they did not get to love anyone properly before they died. Rolf was in love with a girl from Graavig but he was too shy to speak to her. She is still in Graavig. She does not know Rolf loved her. I have never told her. I do not know if telling her would be a gift or a cruelty. This is what I think about while I carve."*

**Quest Offered:** *"The Names in the Mountain"* (the main emotional questline of Region 04 — see below)

---

#### Asger Olafsson — The Silent Fisherman
**Age:** 44  
**Description:** One of the six remaining fishermen of Sorgsby. Survived the raid by being out on his boat when it happened. Lost his wife, both daughters, his mother, his father, and his younger brother. Returned to a burning village. Has barely spoken in three years.

**What He Knows:** Sorgsby before the raid. He was a normal man with a normal family and he knows what was lost. He cannot speak about any of it.

**Key Dialogue — When Jake approaches him on the pier:**

Asger looks at Jake. He does not respond to greetings. After a long silence:

> *"You are going to ask me about my family. I can see it in your face. Everyone asks. I will tell you what I tell everyone: I do not talk about them. Not because it hurts. Because I cannot remember their voices. My wife's voice, my daughters' voices, my mother's voice — I have forgotten them. It has been three years and the voices are the first thing to go. I can remember their faces. I can remember their clothes. I cannot remember how they sounded. And so if I talk about them, I am talking about strangers, and that feels worse than not talking about them. Please leave me alone now."*

He returns to mending his fishing net. Jake cannot continue the dialogue. But Asger will be here every time Jake returns to Sorgsby, and the player will see him working — always working, always silent, always mending nets he will never use because he no longer fishes for a living. He fishes only enough to feed himself. The rest of the day he mends nets because it gives his hands something to do.

The player might return to Sorgsby at the end of Chapter 7 and see Asger still there. Still mending. Still silent. That is his ending. He does not get a quest. He does not get closure. He just is.

---

#### Old Hilde — The Widow of the Mountain
**Age:** 68  
**Description:** One of the oldest survivors. She was in the mountain caves during the raid with her grandchildren. When they came down after the raiders left, she found her entire family dead except for the three grandchildren she had saved. She has raised them alone for three years.

**Key Dialogue:**
> *"I lost nine people that day. My son, his wife, my daughter, her husband, my husband, and four grandchildren who were too old to hide with us in the caves. I saved three. The three I saved are alive today because I dragged them up the mountain path while the village burned below us. I do not feel like a hero. I feel like a woman who chose which of her grandchildren to save, and that choice is what I think about when I cannot sleep at night. People tell me I did the best I could. I did. And the best I could do was not enough."*

Jake can bring Old Hilde small gifts (food, warm cloth, tools) and she will share more memories. She is one of the key sources for *The Names in the Mountain* questline.

---

#### The Three Grandchildren
**Ages:** 6, 9, and 11  
**Names:** Thor (6), Freya (9), Eskel (11)

The youngest three survivors Old Hilde saved. They live with her in a small cottage at the edge of the village. They are quiet children — not the kind of quiet that means well-behaved, but the kind of quiet that means they remember too much for their ages.

**Key Interaction:** If Jake speaks with the children, they will not answer him. Only Eskel, the oldest, will respond — and only briefly. He says:

> *"Are you going to kill the man who did it?"*

If Jake says yes:
> *"Good. Come back and tell us when it is done. We will not be happy. But we want to know."*

If Jake says he doesn't know:
> *"Then you are like the other strangers. They all say they will and then they do not come back. I am eleven. I have seen six strangers say they would kill him. I am not a child who believes promises anymore."*

If Jake returns to Sorgsby after killing Ulfar Stormhand, Eskel is the first NPC he should visit. Eskel listens. He nods. He says: *"Thank you. I will tell my brother and sister when they are old enough to understand what thank you means in this context."* Then he goes back to the fishing net he was helping Asger mend.

**This is the most understated emotional payoff in the region.** Players who did the Ulfar fight for the rush of revenge will now sit with what their revenge meant to an eleven-year-old who watched his parents die.

### The Names in the Mountain — Main Questline

This is the emotional heart of Region 04. It is not optional. Jake must complete it (or at least part of it) to progress the Chapter 7 story.

#### Structure
Mormor Yrsa asks Jake to help her record the names of the 310 dead onto the new stav pole she is carving. She has names for about 200 of them — she knew them personally — but 110 names are still missing because the survivors cannot remember all of their dead. Jake must visit survivors across Sorgsby and ask them to name people they lost. Each conversation is slow, careful, and devastating.

#### The Conversations
Jake will speak with approximately 15 survivors. Each conversation is short (2-4 minutes of dialogue) but emotionally weighty. The design rule for every conversation: the NPC speaks in **Low Register** but shifts briefly to **Middle Register** when they name their dead. This tonal shift is the craft of the questline.

**Example Conversation 1 — Birgit, a widow who lost her husband and seven-year-old daughter:**

> **BIRGIT:** *"You want names. I have two. Thormund Birgisson. My husband. He was a cooper. He built barrels for the whole village. He was not famous for his barrels but he was good at them and he loved doing it. When he died, his last barrel was half-finished in our shed. I have left it there. I cannot bring myself to finish it and I cannot bring myself to burn it. It has been three years. It is still there."*

*(Register shift to Middle here)*

> *"My daughter was Astrid. She was seven. She liked songs about whales. She was going to be a shepherdess, she had decided. She was going to have ten sheep and name them all after her friends. She never got to have even one sheep. I want her name on the pole. I want it bigger than mine will be. She was the whole reason I was alive before the raid, and now she is the reason I am still alive after it — because I am waiting to join her in the mountain. Write her name carefully. Do not misspell it. It is A-S-T-R-I-D."*

**Example Conversation 2 — An unnamed old fisherman:**

> **OLD FISHERMAN:** *"I lost my wife, my two sons, my brother, and my dog. You can put them all on the pole if there is room. My wife was Gudrun. My sons were Lars and Halvard. My brother was Johan. The dog was called Skalar and I know that dogs do not usually go on the pole but Skalar was family and the rules are whatever we say they are now. Write Skalar on the pole. If Mormor Yrsa says no I will speak to her myself. She will understand."*

(Jake brings this to Mormor Yrsa. She is silent for a long moment. Then she says: *"Put Skalar on the pole. Asger is right. The rules are whatever we say they are now."* And she carves the dog's name with the same care she carves every other name.)

**Example Conversation 3 — The man with no one:**

> **UNNAMED MAN:** *"I do not have names to give you. I was not married. I had no children. I worked alone as a cobbler. The people who knew me best are all dead. My parents died years before the raid. I had a sister who left for the city thirty years ago and I have not heard from her since. If she is alive, she does not know I am alive. If she is dead, I do not know she is dead. I have no names to give you. But I have a request. When you write the names on the pole, leave a blank space for me. I am not dead yet. But when I die, there will be no one to carve my name. I would like the blank space to be there waiting. So that when I die, the pole will already be ready for me."*

Jake brings this request to Mormor Yrsa. She nods slowly. She carves a blank rectangular space into the pole — smaller than a full name, but clearly intentional. Then she continues carving the next name.

#### The Pole Raising
When all names are collected, Mormor Yrsa works on the pole for three more in-game days. Jake can watch her carve or move on to other content. When she is finished, the village gathers for the pole raising ceremony. Every survivor is there. The children are there. Asger is there (first time Jake has seen him outside his pier).

Mormor Yrsa raises the pole with the help of six other survivors. It takes effort — the pole is heavy and tall. When it stands, the village is silent for a long moment. Then, one by one, each survivor approaches the pole and touches a name. Some speak to the pole. Some do not.

The last person to approach the pole is Jake. Mormor Yrsa gestures for him to come forward. He walks to the pole. At the bottom of the pole, in Jake's own language (not Fjordlund Old Tongue), she has carved:

> ***"Jake Harlow, stranger from the south, who listened."***

**Reward:** **The Listener's Pendant** — a small carved wooden charm on a leather cord. It has no combat stats. It has no mechanical effect in the inventory system. But wearing it unlocks one additional dialogue option in every remaining conversation of the game: Jake can choose to *listen instead of speaking*. Sometimes this gives him information he would not otherwise get. Sometimes it changes an outcome. Sometimes it does nothing at all. The pendant teaches the player that listening is a kind of power.

**Story Flag:** `names_in_mountain_complete` — unlocks specific reactions from other NPCs for the rest of the game.

---

### What Jake Takes Away From Sorgsby

Sorgsby is what makes Dark Arisen different from every other souls-like. Other games have villages destroyed by enemies. Other games have survivors. What Dark Arisen does that others do not is this: it sits with the survivors for hours. It asks the player to name the dead. It makes the player understand that the 310 were not numbers. They were a cooper named Thormund and a seven-year-old girl named Astrid who wanted ten sheep. They were a dog named Skalar who was family. They were a lonely cobbler who asked for a blank space on the pole because no one would remember him otherwise.

Jake leaves Sorgsby heavier than he arrived. The player leaves Sorgsby changed. That is the purpose.

## 4. JERNHOLM — THE OLD FORT VILLAGE

### First Impression
Jernholm is visible from a long way off. The old imperial fortification sits on a promontory above the sea, its black stone walls still standing after 180 years. The village that grew up around the fort uses the walls as shelter from the wind — houses lean against the fortifications, streets follow the fort's perimeter, and the whole settlement has a defensive, closed-off feeling that the other three villages lack.

When Jake arrives, villagers are less warm than elsewhere. They have reason to be cautious: Ulfar Stormhand has been ruling them from the fort for three years, and strangers usually mean trouble one way or another.

### Layout

- **The Gatehouse** — The main entrance to the fort. Guarded by two of Ulfar's men (before the Ulfar fight). After the fight, unguarded.
- **The Wall Houses** — Cottages built directly against the fort's outer wall. Most of the village's permanent residents live here.
- **The Courtyard Market** — A small market in the courtyard between the wall houses and the fort proper. Smaller than Graavig's market.
- **The Harbor** — Smaller than Kaldvik's, hidden behind the fort's seaward wall. Good for ships that want to stay hidden.
- **The Abandoned Officers' Quarters** — A stone building outside the main fort, empty for forty years. Contains some lore objects for players who explore.
- **The Old Imperial Fort Proper** — Where Ulfar lives (and dies). Separate dungeon-style area.

### Core NPCs

#### Gunnar Svensson — The Reluctant Mayor
**Age:** 55  
**Description:** A tired, worn-out man with thinning hair and a permanent slump to his shoulders. Technically the mayor of Jernholm, but in practice he is just the person who negotiates with Ulfar and tries to keep the villagers alive. He has been doing this for three years and aged ten in the process.

**Key Dialogue — First meeting:**
> *"You are the stranger with the sword. I have been expecting you. Or I have been expecting someone like you. Ulfar Stormhand rules this village and he will kill you the moment he hears you are here, unless you kill him first. I am not asking you to do either. I am telling you what is true. Do what you want with the information."*

**Key Dialogue — If Jake asks what Jernholm wants:**
> *"We want to be left alone. That is all we have ever wanted. Before Ulfar came, we were left alone. Before the Crimson Armada came, we were left alone. Before the empire abandoned this fort, we were left alone. Every time someone comes to rule us, we wait for them to leave. We are still waiting for Ulfar to leave. If you kill him, we will wait for the next one. This is Jernholm."*

**Key Dialogue — After Ulfar is killed:**
> *"You did it. I did not think you would. Thank you. I mean that. I want you to understand I mean that. But I also want you to know we will not celebrate. We will not throw a feast. We will clean the bodies out of the fort and then we will wait for the next Ulfar. This is not your failure. This is our life. Go with whatever blessing I can give, which is not much, but it is real."*

---

#### Old Björn — The Widow's Widower
**Age:** 63  
**Description:** An old man who lives alone in one of the wall houses. His wife died two years ago — natural causes, not the raid. He has not recovered.

**Key Dialogue — Introduces Jernholm's questline "The Widow of the Wreck":**
> *"My wife's name was Aslaug. She was from Jernholm like me. We were married for forty years. When she died, I thought I would die too. I did not. Instead I started seeing her at night, walking in the abandoned officers' quarters outside the fort. I am not a man who believed in ghosts before. Now I am a man who knows his dead wife is walking in an old ruin every night and I do not know what to do about it. The priest says it is grief. The neighbors say it is madness. I say it is Aslaug. I would like someone to check, because I am too afraid to go there myself at night. Would you check?"*

**Quest:** *"The Widow of the Wreck"* — see Side Content below.

---

#### Elin Rolfsdottir — The Young Woman Who Remembers Too Much
**Age:** 22  
**Description:** A quiet young woman who works at the courtyard market. She was nineteen when Ulfar took over Jernholm three years ago. She saw things that have not left her.

**Key Dialogue — Only accessible after Ulfar's death:**
> *"You killed him. Yes. I heard you walked out of the fort with his axe in your hand. Good. I watched him kill a man with that axe once. Two years ago. In the market. The man was a trader from Graavig who refused to pay Ulfar's tax. Ulfar walked up to him, asked him to reconsider, and when the trader refused, Ulfar split his head open right there in front of me. The trader was named Stig. He had a wife and a baby in Graavig. I do not know if you will ever go back to Graavig, stranger, but if you do, there is a young widow named Mette with a toddler named Stig the Younger. Tell her that her husband was brave. She does not know he was brave. She thinks he was stupid for refusing. He was both. You can tell her both."*

(Jake can carry this message to Mette in Graavig. It is a small optional beat. Mette thanks Jake but says little. Life continues. The widow has a son to raise. The dead are dead.)

### Side Content — Jernholm

#### Quest: "The Widow of the Wreck"
**Giver:** Old Björn  
**Type:** Mystery, light combat  

Björn asks Jake to visit the abandoned Officers' Quarters at night and see if his dead wife Aslaug is really walking there. The quarters are haunted — not by Aslaug, but by echoes of the imperial officers who lived there 180 years ago. They are not hostile ghosts. They are just echoes. Memory ghosts.

In the deepest room of the quarters, Jake finds a journal belonging to an imperial officer's wife from 1672. The wife's name was also Aslaug. She lived here. She loved her husband. She grew old in this very room. She died here.

Björn's wife was named after this first Aslaug — a family tradition going back generations. The old Aslaug's "ghost" is not a ghost at all. It is a shared name echoing through the centuries. Björn's grief for his wife has woven itself together with the centuries-old echo of a woman who once loved her husband in this same room.

When Jake returns to Björn and tells him the truth, Björn is silent for a long time. Then he says:

> *"So it is not my Aslaug. It is the first Aslaug. They are not the same. But they are also not nothing. Four hundred years of women named Aslaug have lived in these houses and loved their husbands and died here. I am the last husband of the last Aslaug in that line. There will not be another. Our daughters had sons. The name ends with my wife. Maybe the echo will end too, eventually. Or maybe it will not. Thank you for going. I will sleep better tonight."*

**Reward:** **Aslaug's Wedding Ring** — an old silver ring the first Aslaug left behind. Wearing it gives Jake +10% resistance to fear effects in future regions. But the real reward is the conversation with Björn, which unlocks a new dialogue branch where Björn tells Jake stories about Jernholm's past.

---

### What Jake Takes Away From Jernholm

Jernholm is the village of resignation. These are people who have been ruled by imperial soldiers, abandoned by the empire, raided by pirates, threatened by the Crimson Armada, ruled by Ulfar, and who will be ruled by someone else next. They have stopped hoping that strangers bring freedom. They have learned that strangers always leave.

When Jake kills Ulfar, Jernholm does not celebrate. They clean up the bodies. They rebuild the gatehouse. They wait for the next thing. This is not cynicism. This is survival. And it is the quietest indictment of revenge in the entire game: even when revenge works, even when the monster dies, the world does not become better. It just becomes slightly less infested, until the next infestation.

Jake leaves Jernholm with Ulfar's axe in his hand and the understanding that the axe will need to kill more monsters, and more after that, and more after that, and there is no final monster. Only the next one.

## 5. INTERCONNECTIONS BETWEEN VILLAGES

The four villages of the Quiet Coast are not isolated. They are connected by characters, stories, and echoes:

- **Inga's song** connects all four villages — each contributes a verse
- **The Crimson Armada Officer's Ring** (looted from Ulfar) triggers Signe's daughter quest in Graavig
- **Stig the Trader's story** (from Elin in Jernholm) can be delivered to his widow Mette in Graavig
- **Father Eivind's confession** (Graavig) is set up by Father Knut (Kaldvik)
- **Ulfar's death** triggers emotional reactions in Sorgsby (Mormor Yrsa, Eskel) and Jernholm (Gunnar)
- **Mormor Astrid's Song of Naming** (Graavig) can be spoken at any cairn on the coast, including ones in Sorgsby and near Kaldvik

Players who visit villages in different orders will experience the content differently. The game does not force a path. It rewards attention.

## 6. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE CHECK

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** Villagers speak in Low Register consistently. Mormor Yrsa and Father Knut shift briefly to Middle Register for specific weighted moments. No one speaks in High Register in the villages. Correct.
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** Every quest reward is an object that tells a story — The Listener's Pendant, The Shepherd's Charm, The Whale Song, Aslaug's Wedding Ring, The Old Whaler's Hook. No codex entries. No lecture-lore. Only physical things with human weight.
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** No boss deaths in the villages themselves (those are separate files). But the DEAD are handled with Style D weight — 310 names on a pole, each one specific, each one mourned. Even the cobbler with no one to remember him gets a blank space.
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** Bjorn Fjordmann in Graavig is the clearest example of Pillar 4 Style C character humor. His quirks (barrel names, crow wife, song ratings) are character-based, not joke-based. Players laugh with him AND feel his grief simultaneously.
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** Every village interrogates the theme from a different angle. Kaldvik asks "what is a life that just continues?" Graavig asks "what does everyone secretly carry?" Sorgsby asks "what is lost cannot be returned by killing?" Jernholm asks "does revenge even make things better?" Together, they form Dark Arisen's most sustained meditation on the cost of Jake's chosen path.

---

## 7. FINAL NOTE

The villages of the Quiet Coast are the soul of Region 04. Most players will remember a single village more than the others — different players will remember different ones, based on which NPCs resonated with them. That is by design. The four villages are four different entry points into the same grief.

Kaldvik teaches patience. Graavig teaches that everyone carries weight. Sorgsby teaches that killing the killer does not undo the killing. Jernholm teaches that even successful revenge leaves the world in need of more revenge.

Together, they are the reason players will remember Dark Arisen as more than a souls-like. They are the reason players will write essays about Chapter 7. They are the reason Dark Arisen will be on "Best Quiet Moments in Gaming" lists for years after its release.

## 1. GATHERING PHILOSOPHY — THE FIVE PRINCIPLES

### 1.1 Principle 1: The World Is a Medicine Cabinet

Every region of Dark Arisen contains plants with real uses. Jake can walk through a jungle and see dozens of useful species — if he knows what to look for. The world itself is the pharmacy, the pantry, the spice cabinet. Players who pay attention see this. Players who don't pass through oblivious.

**Design intent:** The world rewards observation. Walking becomes reading.

### 1.2 Principle 2: Knowledge Is Earned

Jake doesn't know every plant at the start. He learns through:
- Observation (identifying plants over time)
- NPC teaching (Ines, regional herbalists)
- Books (field guides, manuals)
- Experimentation (with consequences)

Plant knowledge is a genuine progression system. By Chapter 9, Jake is a walking encyclopedia — but he earned every entry.

**Design intent:** The growth from novice to master herbalist is part of the character arc.

### 1.3 Principle 3: Similar Looks, Different Effects

Some plants look alike. Some are dangerous. Jake's identification skill determines what he sees:
- **Low skill:** "Green plant"
- **Medium skill:** "Fjordlund medicinal herb"
- **High skill:** "Late-bloom Fjordlund Valerian, ready for harvest, will yield superior tonic"

**Design intent:** The game rewards growing expertise with deeper perception of the world.

### 1.4 Principle 4: Timing Matters

Plants have seasonal and time-of-day windows:
- Some bloom only in spring
- Some harvest at dawn for potency
- Some only after rain
- Some during specific moon phases

Missing the window means waiting — or finding alternative sources in other regions.

**Design intent:** Travel and planning become strategic. Players learn the calendar of their world.

### 1.5 Principle 5: Sustainability Builds Relationships

Harvesting all of a rare plant destroys future supply. Harvesting respectfully — taking some, leaving some — builds ongoing resource. Some NPCs notice. Ecosystems respond. Over-harvesting has consequences.

**Design intent:** Same philosophy as hunting: take what you need, respect the source.

## 2. THE IDENTIFICATION SYSTEM

### 2.1 Plant Recognition Levels

Jake's plant knowledge grows throughout the game:

**Novice (Chapter 1):**
- Identifies: Common fruits, basic vegetables, obvious herbs
- Cannot tell: Medicinal from poisonous (similar species)
- Visual: Plants show generic descriptions
- Interaction: "Unknown plant. Harvest at your own risk."

**Apprentice (Chapter 2-3):**
- Identifies: Regional herbs (Rexa tropical herbs)
- Distinguishes: Common dangerous plants (poison ivy, toxic berries)
- Visual: Most known plants have names
- Interaction: Basic use information

**Herbalist (Chapter 4-6):**
- Identifies: Most regional varieties
- Distinguishes: Look-alike species
- Visual: Detailed plant info
- Interaction: Know primary uses, quality indicators

**Master (Chapter 7-9):**
- Identifies: Rare species, regional specialties
- Distinguishes: Peak harvest times, best specimens
- Visual: Complete plant data
- Interaction: Multiple uses, alchemical interactions

**Legendary (Post-Game):**
- Identifies: Everything, including Region 06's unique plants
- Knowledge: Approaches Ines's level
- Can teach: Other NPCs
- Special: Some plants only visible to trained eyes

### 2.2 Gaining Knowledge

**Through Practice:**
- Harvest plant 5 times: basic knowledge
- Harvest plant 15 times: standard knowledge
- Harvest plant 30 times: expert knowledge
- Harvest plant 50+ times: master knowledge

**Through Teaching:**
- Ines teaches at crafting stations
- Regional herbalists teach their specialties
- Books read in cabin
- Field guides purchased/found

**Through Questlines:**
- Herbalist apprentice quests
- Material gathering missions
- Cultural exchanges (regional plant knowledge)
- Exploration rewards

**Through Experimentation:**
- Try unknown plants
- Sometimes poisoning occurs
- Sometimes discoveries made
- Ines treats poisonings (for a fee)

### 2.3 The Observation Mode

**Activation:** Hold L3 while near plants (similar to hunting tracking)

**Effects:**
- Plants become identified (to Jake's level)
- Harvest quality indicators appear
- Danger indicators for toxic plants
- Seasonal indicators shown

**Limitations:**
- Range: 15m
- Requires Jake to be still
- Drains focus stamina slowly
- Cannot use while moving fast

### 2.4 Plant Quality Tiers

Every harvestable plant has quality levels:

**Wilted (Poor):**
- Past peak
- Reduced materials
- Lower quality crafting
- Avoid if possible

**Fresh (Standard):**
- Normal harvest
- Standard materials
- Common quality
- Most common tier

**Peak (Excellent):**
- Perfect moment
- Full materials
- Higher crafting quality
- Rewarded attention

**Exceptional (Rare):**
- Unusual growth conditions
- Maximum materials
- Best crafting possible
- Legendary-tier use
- Environment + timing + luck

### 2.5 Identifying Quality

**Visual Cues:**
- Color vibrancy (faded vs. vivid)
- Size (stunted vs. robust)
- Flowering state (early, peak, late)
- Leaf condition (damaged vs. pristine)
- Location (poor soil vs. ideal)

**Audio Cues (subtle):**
- Bees busy around peak flowers
- Quiet around wilted plants
- Wind patterns differ
- Subtle differences for attentive players

## 3. HARVESTING MECHANICS

### 3.1 The Harvest Animation

Different plants require different approaches:

**Quick Pick (2-3 seconds):**
- Berries, small herbs, fruits on low branches
- Simple bending motion
- Jake stores in satchel
- Minimal fuss

**Careful Harvest (8-15 seconds):**
- Medicinal herbs requiring specific part
- Tobacco leaves (specific cut)
- Flowering plants (preserve root)
- Focused animation, knife work

**Tool-Required (10-30 seconds):**
- Tree bark (needs knife)
- Roots (needs digging stick)
- High branches (needs ladder or reach tool)
- Mushrooms (needs careful extraction)

**Hazardous Harvest (15-40 seconds):**
- Dangerous plants (gloves required)
- Steep locations (climbing needed)
- Underwater (swimming/diving)
- Cliff flowers (climbing mechanic)

### 3.2 Harvest Choices

For many plants, Jake can choose:

**Take Everything:**
- Maximum immediate yield
- Destroys the plant
- No future harvesting from that source
- Ecological impact

**Take Part (Sustainable):**
- Less immediate yield
- Plant survives
- Regrows for future harvesting
- Builds up supply over time

**Take Minimum:**
- Barely affects plant
- Respectful harvest
- Guaranteed regrowth
- Slower but sustainable

**Design Intent:** Sustainability is a player choice. Some players will min-max. Others will harvest respectfully. The game responds to both.

### 3.3 Tools for Gathering

**Basic Tools (Available From Start):**
- Hunting Knife (also for harvest)
- Hands (basic picking)
- Small Bag (for berries etc.)

**Intermediate Tools (Chapter 4+):**
- Herbalist's Knife (specialized, quality bonus)
- Digging Stick (roots)
- Gathering Basket (preservation)
- Gloves (dangerous plants)

**Advanced Tools (Chapter 7+):**
- Precision Blade (minimal plant damage)
- Extension Pole (high harvests)
- Diving Gear (underwater plants)
- Climbing Gear (cliff plants)

**Legendary Tools (Post-Game):**
- Master's Harvest Set (all bonuses)
- Unique quality modifier
- Aesthetic distinction
- Prestige item

### 3.4 Environmental Factors

**Weather Effects:**
- **Rain:** Some plants harvest better (mushrooms)
- **Sun:** Ideal for most herbs at peak
- **Heat:** Some plants wilt, wait for cool
- **Cold:** Fjordlund plants only in right weather

**Location Effects:**
- **Altitude:** Mountain plants vs. valley
- **Moisture:** Swamp plants vs. desert
- **Soil:** Volcanic vs. coastal
- **Sunlight:** Canopy vs. open

**Time of Day:**
- Dawn: High-potency harvests (dew-touched)
- Morning: Most plants ready
- Midday: Some wilt in heat
- Evening: Night-bloomers appearing
- Night: Specific nocturnal species

## 4. CATEGORIES OF GATHERED MATERIALS

### 4.1 Medicinal Herbs

**Purpose:** Healing, tonics, medicines
**Primary User:** Ines's alchemy
**Examples:**
- **Red Valerian:** Calming, sleep aid, pain relief
- **Mountain Sage:** Antiseptic, wound healing
- **Blessed Thistle:** Detoxifying, poison cure base
- **Devil's Claw:** Anti-inflammatory, joint relief
- **Crimson Cap Mushroom:** Energy booster, rare

**Harvesting Notes:**
- Peak at specific times
- Quality deeply matters
- Multiple similar species (identification challenge)
- Regional specialties

### 4.2 Food Plants

**Purpose:** Direct consumption, cooking ingredients
**Primary User:** Galley cook, campfires
**Examples:**
- **Wild Berries:** Blueberries, strawberries, raspberries (regional variants)
- **Tuber Roots:** Potatoes (wild), yams, cassava
- **Fruits:** Apples, oranges, bananas (regional)
- **Nuts:** Acorns, cashews, almonds (regional)
- **Grains:** Wild rice, oats

**Harvesting Notes:**
- Seasonal availability
- Ripeness important
- Storage considerations
- Cooking transforms value

### 4.3 Fiber Plants

**Purpose:** Crafting materials (rope, cloth, baskets)
**Primary User:** Big Tom, general crafting
**Examples:**
- **Cotton:** Rexan plantations and wild
- **Hemp:** Multiple uses, widespread
- **Flax:** Temperate regions
- **Silk Worms:** (Technically animal but plant-related)
- **Palm Fiber:** Tropical

**Harvesting Notes:**
- Processing required
- Quality depends on harvest
- Industrial potential
- Trade goods

### 4.4 Aromatic Plants

**Purpose:** Perfumes, incense, flavoring
**Primary User:** Trade, luxury goods
**Examples:**
- **Lavender:** Calming, perfume base
- **Sandalwood:** Incense, oils
- **Mint:** Culinary, medicinal
- **Cinnamon:** Spice (tree bark)
- **Vanilla:** Rare, valuable

**Harvesting Notes:**
- Specific extraction methods
- High value for weight
- Cultural significance
- Luxury markets

### 4.5 Tobacco and Cannabis

**Dedicated Documents:** `fauna/tobacco_system.md` and `fauna/cannabis_system.md`

These have unique systems beyond standard gathering due to:
- Multiple varieties
- Curing processes
- Cultural integration
- Special quest content

### 4.6 Poisonous Plants

**Purpose:** Poisons, specific alchemy, throwables
**Primary User:** Ines (controlled), Jake (careful)
**Examples:**
- **Nightshade:** Classic poison base
- **Hemlock:** Paralytic
- **Angel's Trumpet:** Hallucinogen

**Harvesting Notes:**
- GLOVES REQUIRED for many
- Specific preparation
- Ines teaches safely
- Ethical questions arise

### 4.7 Alchemical Specialties

**Purpose:** Ines's advanced recipes
**Examples:**
- **Moonflower:** Blooms only at night

- **Sea-Witch's Veil:** Coastal, extracts from seaweed

**Harvesting Notes:**
- Rare locations
- Specific conditions
- Legendary quests often
- Major crafting components

### 4.8 Ritual/Spiritual Plants

**Purpose:** Father Salvio's blessed items, ceremonies
**Examples:**

- **Holy Basil:** Sacramental uses
- **Madonna Lily:** Symbolic flowers

**Harvesting Notes:**

## 6. SEASONAL CYCLES

### 6.1 The Year in Dark Arisen

Dark Arisen tracks in-game seasons:

**Spring:**
- Plants emerging
- First harvests
- Flowering period starts
- Ideal for observation

**Summer:**
- Peak harvest season
- Most diversity
- Long days
- Storage for winter planning

**Autumn:**
- Final harvests
- Root crops
- Preservation time
- Preparation season

**Winter:**
- Limited harvesting
- Preserved foods used
- Hardy plants only
- Planning for spring

### 6.2 Regional Season Variation

**Rexa/Moran:**
- Wet/Dry seasons instead
- Rainy season: certain mushrooms, fresh greens
- Dry season: tobacco harvest, sun-curing

**Fjordlund:**
- Extreme seasons
- Summer: brief, intense
- Winter: long, dormant
- Some plants only available weeks per year

**Ashenmoor:**
- Desert cycles
- Post-rare-rain: brief blooms
- Normal: survival-hardy only
- Night vs. day distinct

**Temperate (Pale Isle, Quiet Coast):**
- Standard four seasons
- Traditional European cycles
- Familiar to players

### 6.3 Lunar Cycles

Some plants follow moon phases:

**New Moon:**
- Dark-thriving plants
- Certain mushrooms
- Night-blooming species

**Full Moon:**
- Moonflower bloom
- Legendary plants
- Maximum potency for some herbs

**Quarter Moons:**
- Transition periods
- Harvesting balance

### 6.4 Time Investment

Players checking seasons learn to:
- Plan expeditions for peak seasons
- Travel to different climate regions
- Preserve seasonal abundance
- Time quests for plant availability

**Design Intent:** The world has rhythm. Players who sync with it gain advantage. Players who ignore it face scarcity.

## 7. SUSTAINABLE HARVESTING

### 7.1 Population Tracking

Each plant species tracked per region:

**Abundant:**
- Common, widespread
- Harvest freely
- No ecological concern

**Common:**
- Standard availability
- Moderate harvest sustainable
- Monitor over time

**Scarce:**
- Limited populations
- Careful harvesting required
- Over-harvest quickly depletes

**Rare:**
- Very limited
- Sustainable practices essential
- Single location often

**Endangered:**
- Critical populations
- Harvesting ethical concern
- NPC reactions

### 7.2 Impact of Over-Harvesting

**Immediate Effects:**
- Specific location depleted
- Reduced respawn rate
- Lower quality regrowth
- NPCs notice

**Medium-Term Effects:**
- Species population drops
- Scarcity in that region
- Must travel further for supply
- Economic effects

**Long-Term Effects:**
- Local extinction possible
- Ecosystem damage
- Permanent loss of some plants
- Reputation consequences

### 7.3 Sustainable Practices

**Leave the Root:**
- For many plants
- Takes only usable part
- Plant survives, regrows
- Ideal practice

**Seasonal Harvesting:**
- Only in peak season
- Respects plant cycles
- Maximum yield for effort
- Most efficient

**Rotation:**
- Harvest different areas in sequence
- Give previous areas time
- Builds sustainable supply
- Strategic approach

**Cultivation Support:**
- Some quests: plant seeds
- Build future supply
- Long-term investment
- Particularly for rare species

### 7.4 Reputation Effects

**Respectful Gatherer Reputation:**
- Herbalists share secrets
- Regional access expanded
- Better prices from merchants
- Special quests unlock

**Exploitative Reputation:**
- NPCs refuse to teach
- Merchants overcharge
- Some regions hostile
- Bad reputation spreads

## 8. PROCESSING GATHERED PLANTS

(Brief overview — full system in `fauna/processing_and_cooking.md`)

### 8.1 Fresh Use

Many plants used immediately:
- Fresh herbs for cooking
- Raw fruits for eating
- Immediate medicinal needs
- Short shelf life

### 8.2 Drying

Most common preservation:
- Air drying (hang in ship/home)
- Sun drying (careful with herbs)
- Smoke drying (specific use)
- 1-7 days process
- Concentrates potency

### 8.3 Grinding/Pounding

For powders and extracts:
- Mortar and pestle
- Larger grinders (ports)
- Creates specific forms
- Easier combining

### 8.4 Extraction

Complex processing:
- Oil extraction (aromatic, medicinal)
- Alcohol tinctures (herbalism)
- Water infusions (tea-like)
- Pressing (cold or heat)

### 8.5 Preservation

Long-term storage:
- Drying (most herbs)
- Salting (specific foods)
- Smoking (meat and some plants)
- Alcohol storage (tinctures)
- Cold storage (Fjordlund)

## 17. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** Herbalists speak in Middle Register typically; Ines elevates to High during teaching; regional herbalists use local registers authentic to their cultures
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** Every plant has story (Moon-Blossom Cliff Flowers are myth subjects; Dragon's Tongue tied to Ashen Wyrm; each region's plants reflect its character)
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** Plants don't die in Dark Arisen's full Style sense, but over-harvested ecosystems carry a Style D tragedy (quiet, preventable loss)
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** Style C quirks in plant-related moments (Big Tom's poisonous plant accident stories; Ines's dry comments; regional herbalist eccentricities)
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** The sustainable vs. exploitative harvest mirrors the larger question of Jake's revenge — does he take what he needs, or destroy indiscriminately?

## 1. ECONOMY PHILOSOPHY — THE FIVE PRINCIPLES

### 1.1 Principle 1: Markets Are Living Systems

Prices fluctuate. Demand shifts. Supply varies. Wars affect trade. Disease wipes out crops. Pirate raids disrupt routes. The economy of Dark Arisen breathes.

**Design intent:** Players who pay attention to economic patterns profit. Static price tables would betray this principle.

### 1.2 Principle 2: Sustainability Is Profitable

Long-term thinking pays better than short-term exploitation. Over-hunting collapses markets. Sustainable practices build reputation that opens premium markets. Greedy traders earn fortunes once. Wise traders earn fortunes forever.

**Design intent:** The game rewards conservation economically, not just morally.

### 1.3 Principle 3: Every Region Wants What Others Have

Tropical regions want furs. Cold regions want spices. Imperial cities want exotic luxuries. Pirate havens want everything. Trade exists because no region is self-sufficient.

**Design intent:** Travel itself becomes economic activity. Routes become livelihood.

### 1.4 Principle 4: Legality Is Local

What's legal in Rexa may be banned in Pale Isle. What pirates trade openly, the imperial customs would seize. Smuggling is profitable but dangerous. Reputation determines which markets stay open.

**Design intent:** Moral and legal complexity adds depth. Black markets exist because regulation creates them.

### 1.5 Principle 5: Reputation Shapes Pricing

Master hunters get premium prices. Conservation reputation opens luxury markets. Pirate reputation enables black market access. Reputation IS economic capital.

**Design intent:** Player choices create economic identity. Not just stats — actual market positioning.

## 2. THE FUNDAMENTAL TRADE PRINCIPLE

### 2.1 The Triangle Trade

Dark Arisen's economy fundamentally moves in triangles:

**Classic Triangle (Hunting/Gathering Focus):**
- **Tropical Goods** → Imperial Markets (high markup)
- **Imperial Goods** → Northern/Frontier (high markup)
- **Northern Goods** → Tropical Markets (high markup)

**Examples:**
- Rexan tobacco → Pale Isle (3-5x markup)
- Imperial silk → Fjordlund (3-4x markup)
- Fjordlund furs → Rexa (4-6x markup)

### 2.2 Round Trips and Profitability

**Empty Holds Lose Money:**
- Sailing back without cargo = lost potential
- Smart captains plan round trips
- Each leg should profit

**The Captain's Calculation:**
- Buy in market A at low price
- Sell in market B at high price
- Buy DIFFERENT goods at B
- Sell at market C
- Eventually return to A with goods they want

### 2.3 Risk-Reward Spectrum

**Low Risk, Low Reward:**
- Common goods, regular routes
- Steady but small profits
- Reliable income

**Medium Risk, Medium Reward:**
- Slightly contraband goods
- Some weather risks
- Better profits

**High Risk, High Reward:**
- Heavy contraband
- Pirate-controlled waters
- Major profits or major losses

**Extreme Risk, Extreme Reward:**
- Sacred items, forbidden goods
- Most dangerous routes
- Could fund years OR destroy career

## 3. REGIONAL ECONOMIC PROFILES

### 3.1 Rexa / Moran (Tropical Producer)

**Major Exports:**
- Tobacco (all varieties)
- Tropical fruits (mango, papaya, coconut, citrus)
- Hardwoods (mahogany, rosewood, teak)
- Sugar cane
- Coffee
- Rum
- Exotic feathers
- Tropical pelts (jaguar, etc.)
- Hardwood furniture

**Major Imports:**
- Iron and steel
- Wool textiles
- Wheat
- Salted fish
- Imperial manufactured goods
- Northern furs (luxury)

**Economic Character:**
- Plantation economy
- Colonial complexity
- Exploitation history
- Indigenous economy parallel
- Pirate-friendly markets

**Currency:** Mixed (doubloons standard, indigenous trade goods accepted)

**Tax Status:** Imperial taxation officially, often evaded

### 3.2 Fjordlund (Northern Producer/Specialist)

**Major Exports:**
- Furs (premium — arctic fox especially)
- Whale oil and products (if hunting)
- Salmon (smoked and salted)
- Reindeer products
- Iron (Fjordlund-quality)
- Mead and northern beers
- Amber
- Specific medicinal herbs

**Major Imports:**
- Tropical fruits (rare luxury)
- Tobacco (premium product north)
- Spices (essential for preservation)
- Cotton textiles
- Sugar
- Wine (rare, expensive)

**Economic Character:**
- Self-sufficient survival economy
- Limited but valuable exports
- Long-term thinking
- Community-based
- Conservation-minded

**Currency:** Doubloons accepted, regional silver coins, sometimes barter

**Tax Status:** Loose imperial control

### 3.3 Ashenmoor (Specialist Trader)

**Major Exports:**
- Volcanic-grown tobacco
- Desert herbs (specific medicinal)
- Iron (volcanic-rich)
- Salt (salt flat mining)
- Specific gems and minerals
- Camel/desert mount products
- Date palm products

**Major Imports:**
- Water (luxury commodity!)
- Wood (extremely scarce)
- Most foods
- Textiles
- Almost everything except minerals

**Economic Character:**
- Trade hub specifically (not producer)
- Caravans cross desert
- Oasis-based markets
- Limited but unique products
- Nomadic flexibility

**Currency:** Mixed, sometimes specific desert tokens

**Tax Status:** Limited imperial control, nomadic exemptions

### 3.4 Pale Isle (Imperial Hub)

**Major Exports:**
- Manufactured goods
- Silk and textiles
- Wine
- Refined sugar
- Imperial currency
- Tools and weapons
- Luxury goods
- Education and services

**Major Imports:**
- Raw materials (everything)
- Exotic foods
- Slaves (in dark variant — moral choice)
- Spices
- Specific pelts
- Tropical commodities

**Economic Character:**
- Sophisticated economy
- Heavy taxation
- Class-based markets
- Manufacturing focus
- Imperial regulation

**Currency:** Doubloons standard, imperial coins premium

**Tax Status:** Heavy taxation, customs enforcement strong

### 3.5 Quiet Coast (Settler Economy)

**Major Exports:**
- Lumber (homestead managed)
- Grain
- Dairy products
- Fresh produce
- Hand-crafted goods
- Specific herbs

**Major Imports:**

## 5. TRADE ROUTES — THE MAJOR PATHS

### 5.1 The Tropical Triangle

**Route:**
- Rexa → Pale Isle → Fjordlund → Rexa

**Cargo Flow:**
- **Rexa to Pale Isle:** Tobacco, sugar, rum, hardwoods, exotic foods (3-5x markup)
- **Pale Isle to Fjordlund:** Manufactured goods, wine, silk, spices (3-4x markup)
- **Fjordlund to Rexa:** Furs, whale oil, salmon, mead (4-6x markup)

**Round Trip Profit:** Significant if managed well
**Risk:** Imperial customs, weather, distance
**Time:** 3-6 months real-world economic cycles

### 5.2 The Northern Triangle

**Route:**
- Fjordlund → Pale Isle → Quiet Coast → Fjordlund

**Cargo Flow:**
- Northern goods to Imperial luxury market
- Imperial goods to settler frontier
- Settler goods (lumber, grain) to Fjordlund

**Profit:** Steady, lower risk
**Risk:** Weather (Fjordlund storms), imperial regulations
**Time:** Faster than tropical

### 5.3 The Caribbean Spice Route

**Route:**
- Rexa → Moran → Various Caribbean → Rexa

**Cargo Flow:**
- Inter-island trading
- Spices, tobacco, sugar specialization
- Rum exchange
- Pirate haven connections

**Profit:** Variable, often high
**Risk:** Pirates (you ARE one!), storms, imperial patrols
**Time:** Quick cycles

### 5.4 The Forbidden Route

**Route:**
- Pirate Havens → Imperial Black Markets → Pirate Havens

**Cargo Flow:**
- Contraband everything
- Sacred plants (cannabis, etc.)
- Stolen imperial goods
- Slave goods (moral choice in some servers)

**Profit:** Massive
**Risk:** Hanging if caught
**Time:** Tense, fast

### 5.5 The Desert Caravan Route

**Route:**
- Ashenmoor → Pale Isle → Trade Hubs → Ashenmoor

**Cargo Flow:**
- Desert specialties out
- Water and wood in
- Specific minerals exchange
- Cultural goods

**Profit:** Specialized but reliable
**Risk:** Heat, sandstorms, raiders
**Time:** Slow due to terrain

### 5.6 Jake's Personal Routes

**Player-Developed:**
- Discover own profitable patterns
- Optimize based on play style
- Reputation creates personal routes
- Late-game economic mastery

## 6. SUSTAINABILITY ECONOMICS

### 6.1 The Long Game

**Sustainable Practices Reward:**
- Repeated profitable harvests
- Premium pricing (sustainability brand)
- Reputation-locked premium markets
- Long-term ecosystem health
- Specific buyer relationships

**Exploitation Punishes:**
- One-time large profits
- Then markets crash
- Reputation damage
- Some buyers refuse trade
- Long recovery periods

### 6.2 The Specific Mechanics

**Population Tracking:**
- Each region tracks species populations
- Over-harvesting triggers warnings
- Specific NPCs note imbalances
- Eventually no supply

**Recovery Timing:**
- 6 months minimum for most species
- Some legendary populations never recover
- Plant ecosystems faster
- Tree populations slowest

**Premium Brand:**
- "Sustainably Hunted" mark
- Buyers pay 30-50% premium
- Aristocratic connections
- Reputation-locked

### 6.3 Examples of Sustainability vs Exploitation

**Beaver Trade Example:**

**Year 1 (Both Approaches):**
- Sustainable: 50 beavers, normal price = 5,000g
- Exploitative: 200 beavers, normal price = 20,000g

**Year 2:**
- Sustainable: 50 beavers, premium price (60g vs 100g) = 5,000g
- Exploitative: 50 beavers (population crashed), normal price = 5,000g

**Year 3-5:**
- Sustainable: Maintains 50/year at premium
- Exploitative: 0 beavers (locally extinct)

**Long-term Total:**
- Sustainable: 5,000g × 5 years = 25,000g
- Exploitative: 20,000g + 5,000g + 0 + 0 + 0 = 25,000g

**SAME TOTAL** — but sustainable preserves the resource. And often, sustainable players unlock premium markets exploitative players never see.

### 6.4 The Imperial Conservation Office

**Late Game NPC Faction:**
- Tracks conservation reputation
- Provides specific quests
- Premium contracts
- Long-term relationship

**Jake's Standing:**
- Master Hunter title
- Conservation-focused alternative
- Different from poacher
- Both have ending paths

## 17. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** Aristocratic markets High register, common merchants Middle, pirate fences Low with cunning, indigenous traders Middle with cultural specifics
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** Trade goods tell regional stories. Specific items reveal economic relationships. Currency itself carries history.
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** Failed trades carry weight (Style D ruin), successful trades celebrate (Style B brutal/practical), economic collapse mourns (Style D)
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** Style C quirks (Big Tom's haggling disasters, Ines's serious approach to alchemy markets, crew trade humor)
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** Wealth can fund revenge OR fund renewal. Sustainable trading reflects choice for life. Exploitative trading reflects continued violence. The economic path mirrors the moral path.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Birds fill the air of Dark Arisen with life. They are atmospheric storytellers — the morning dawn chorus in Rexa's jungles, the lonely albatross on open sea, the ravens that follow death. This document defines 52 bird species across all regions.

**Content Summary:**
- **Songbirds:** 12 species
- **Birds of Prey:** 8 species
- **Water Birds (Freshwater):** 6 species
- **Seabirds:** 10 species
- **Exotic Birds:** 6 species (parrots, macaws)
- **Corvids:** 5 species (crows, ravens, magpies)
- **Game Birds:** 5 species (pheasants, grouse)

**Plus 4 Legendary Birds** (special encounters)

## 2. BIRDS AS ATMOSPHERE

### 2.1 The Soundscape

Birds define how each region SOUNDS:

**Rexa (Tropical Jungle):**
- Howler monkey calls + macaw screeches at dawn
- Continuous chirping, chattering
- Frog-bird overlap at dusk
- Parrot calls distinctive
- The jungle never sleeps

**Fjordlund (Arctic):**
- Gull cries over ocean
- Puffin clatter on cliffs
- Raven croaks in forests
- Silence of deep winter
- Loon calls on inland lakes

**Ashenmoor (Volcanic Desert):**
- Vulture circles silently
- Dawn sparrows briefly
- Owl calls at night
- Otherwise surprisingly quiet
- Wind dominates over birds

**Pale Isle (Imperial Temperate):**
- Songbirds in gardens
- Pheasant calls in forests
- Imperial falcons trained
- Ordered soundscape
- Managed wildlife

**Quiet Coast (Temperate):**
- Familiar dawn chorus
- Hawks and owls
- Wood warblers
- Classical American sounds
- Home feeling

**Region 06 (Post-Game):**
- Bird calls that aren't quite right
- Songs in unknown languages
- Silences where birds should be
- Unsettling atmosphere

### 2.2 Players Learn Regions by Ear

An experienced player should be able to close their eyes and know:
- Which region they're in (within seconds)
- What time of day it is
- Weather conditions
- Even sometimes specific sub-areas

This is atmospheric design at its finest.

### 2.3 Ambient vs. Interactive

**Most birds are ambient:**
- Visual presence
- Audio presence
- Cannot be interacted with directly
- Fly away if approached
- Atmospheric only

**Some birds are interactive:**
- Can be hunted
- Can be trained (falconry)
- Can be companions
- Can be messengers
- Full mechanical involvement

## 3. SONGBIRDS

### 3.1 Tropical Songbirds

**Rexan Tanager (Piranga rubra):**
- **Size:** 15-18 cm
- **Habitat:** Rexa jungles, forest edges
- **Behavior:** Brilliant red male, olive female, active feeder
- **Song:** Clear whistling phrases
- **Uses:** Feathers (red, valuable), atmosphere
- **Catch Difficulty:** Hard (small, agile)

**Moran Warbler (Setophaga morana):**
- **Size:** 12-14 cm
- **Habitat:** Moran swamps and forests
- **Behavior:** Migratory, colorful
- **Song:** Complex trills
- **Uses:** Feathers (various colors)
- **Catch Difficulty:** Very Hard

**Tropical Sparrow (Passer rexana):**
- **Size:** 14-16 cm
- **Habitat:** Towns, agricultural areas
- **Behavior:** Common, flocks
- **Uses:** Common feathers, minor meat

**Jungle Flycatcher:**
- **Size:** 15-18 cm
- **Habitat:** Jungle mid-story
- **Behavior:** Catches insects in flight
- **Uses:** Specimen collection, atmosphere

### 3.2 Temperate Songbirds

**American Robin (Turdus migratorius):**
- **Size:** 23-28 cm
- **Habitat:** Woodlands, yards

- **Uses:** Classic bird, atmosphere, eggs seasonal

**Cardinal (Cardinalis cardinalis):**
- **Size:** 21-23 cm
- **Habitat:** Forests, gardens
- **Behavior:** Brilliant red male
- **Uses:** Red feathers (valuable)
- **Cultural Significance:** Quiet Coast beloved

**Chickadee (Poecile atricapillus):**
- **Size:** 12-14 cm

**Goldfinch (Spinus tristis):**
- **Size:** 12-14 cm

- **Uses:** Yellow feathers, songs

### 3.3 Cold-Climate Songbirds

**Snow Bunting (Plectrophenax nivalis):**

## 4. BIRDS OF PREY

### 4.1 Eagles

**Bald Eagle (Haliaeetus leucocephalus):**
- **Size:** 80-100 cm, 4-6 kg, 2 m wingspan
- **Habitat:** Near water (coastal, rivers)
- **Behavior:** Fish hunters, territorial, long-lived
- **Hunt Difficulty:** Very Hard (protected in some regions)
- **Uses:** Massive feathers (prestige), talons
- **Cultural Significance:** Imperial symbol (illegal to hunt?)
- **Notes:** Fjordlund variant common

**Golden Eagle (Aquila chrysaetos):**
- **Size:** 80-90 cm, 3-7 kg, 2.2 m wingspan
- **Habitat:** Mountains, cliffs
- **Behavior:** Hunter of mammals and large birds
- **Hunt Difficulty:** Extreme (legal issues, dangerous)
- **Uses:** Legendary feathers, talons
- **Notes:** **Potential legendary bird**

**Sea Eagle (Haliaeetus albicilla):**
- **Size:** 80-95 cm, 4-7 kg
- **Habitat:** Fjordlund coasts
- **Behavior:** Fish hunter, coastal
- **Uses:** Similar to Bald Eagle
- **Cultural Significance:** Fjordlund sacred bird

### 4.2 Hawks

**Red-Tailed Hawk (Buteo jamaicensis):**
- **Size:** 45-65 cm, 0.7-1.5 kg
- **Habitat:** Open areas, forests
- **Behavior:** Soaring hunter
- **Hunt Difficulty:** Hard
- **Uses:** Feathers, trained falconry possible

**Cooper's Hawk (Accipiter cooperii):**
- **Size:** 35-50 cm
- **Habitat:** Forests
- **Behavior:** Bird hunter (kills other birds)
- **Uses:** Feathers, swift

**Peregrine Falcon (Falco peregrinus):**
- **Size:** 35-50 cm
- **Habitat:** Cliffs, cities
- **Behavior:** Fastest animal, diving hunter
- **Hunt Difficulty:** Extreme (skilled, fast)
- **Uses:** Prestige feathers, falconry prize
- **Cultural Significance:** Imperial falconry status symbol

### 4.3 Owls

**Great Horned Owl (Bubo virginianus):**
- **Size:** 45-60 cm, 1-2.5 kg
- **Habitat:** Forests (widespread)
- **Behavior:** Nocturnal predator
- **Hunt Difficulty:** Hard (night, silent)
- **Uses:** Feathers, cultural significance
- **Cultural Significance:** Various lore (wisdom, death)

**Snowy Owl (Bubo scandiacus):**
- **Size:** 55-65 cm, 1.5-3 kg
- **Habitat:** Fjordlund tundra
- **Behavior:** Diurnal hunter (arctic)
- **Uses:** White feathers (luxury), striking
- **Notes:** Unique arctic bird

**Tropical Screech Owl:**
- **Size:** 20-25 cm
- **Habitat:** Rexa, Moran
- **Behavior:** Small nocturnal
- **Uses:** Specimen, alchemy
- **Notes:** Distinctive calls

### 4.4 Ospreys and Fishers

**Osprey (Pandion haliaetus):**
- **Size:** 50-65 cm, 1.5-2 kg
- **Habitat:** Coasts, lakes
- **Behavior:** Fish hunter, diving
- **Uses:** Feathers, wide distribution
- **Notes:** Spectacular diving

- **Size:** 45-55 cm

**Pintail (Anas acuta):**
- **Size:** 55-75 cm

- **Uses:** Game hunting, food

### 5.2 Geese

**Canada Goose (Branta canadensis):**
- **Size:** 75-110 cm, 3-8 kg

**Snow Goose (Anser caerulescens):**
- **Size:** 70-85 cm, 2-3.5 kg
- **Habitat:** Fjordlund (migrating)

- **Notes:** Spectacular migrations

### 5.3 Swans and Herons

## 10. LEGENDARY BIRDS

### 10.1 The Emperor of the Wind (Legendary Eagle)

**The Emperor of the Wind (Aquila imperatorius):**
- **Classification:** Legendary Golden Eagle
- **Size:** 100 cm body, 2.5 m wingspan (abnormally large)
- **Age:** Estimated 40+ years (far beyond normal)
- **Appearance:** Massive golden eagle with unusual white feathers streaking through plumage, one leg has ancient injury that never healed
- **Territory:** Highest mountains of Pale Isle
- **Behavior:**
  - Apex aerial predator
  - Has killed shepherds' dogs
  - Has taken young sheep
  - Regional legend for generations
- **Signs of Presence:**
  - Ancient nests on inaccessible cliffs
  - Feathers found rarely
  - Shepherd stories
  - Only seen from distance normally
- **The Hunt:**
  - Requires mountaineering
  - Aerial target
  - Rifle with extreme range
  - Single shot opportunity usually
  - Weather critical
- **Requirements:**
  - Chapter 6+
  - Mountaineering equipment
  - Long-range rifle
  - Clear weather
- **Materials:**
  - **Emperor's Feathers:** Legendary fletching (best arrows)
  - **Emperor's Talons:** Weapon components (legendary)
  - **Emperor's Wings:** Trophy display (dramatic)
- **Narrative Weight:**
  - Mountain cultures mourn
  - Imperial nobility may notice (they claimed hunting rights)
  - Ecosystem shift (apex removed)
  - Sky feels emptier

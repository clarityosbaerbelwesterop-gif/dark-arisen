# REGIONAL ECOSYSTEMS

**Status:** Design Draft v1.0  
**Phase:** Phase 4 — Fauna & Flora (Subsystem 14 of 16)  
**Tier:** Content Tier (Integration Document)  
**Related:** All previous fauna documents, mechanics_overview.md, style_bible.md  
**Purpose:** Define how all species, plants, weather, and time interact across the six regions of Dark Arisen — creating living, breathing ecosystems that respond to player actions and exist independently of Jake.  
**Inspirations:** Red Dead Redemption 2 (ambient ecosystem), Far Cry Primal (predator-prey dynamics), real-world ecology, Monster Hunter (food webs), The Witcher 3 (regional distinctness)

---

## 1. ECOSYSTEM PHILOSOPHY — THE FIVE PRINCIPLES

### 1.1 Principle 1: The World Exists Without Jake

Animals hunt without Jake watching. Plants grow whether he gathers them or not. Predators kill prey, scavengers consume the dead, the cycle continues. Jake is a visitor, not a center.

**Design intent:** When Jake walks into a forest, things were happening before he arrived and will happen after he leaves.

### 1.2 Principle 2: Every Action Has Consequence

Kill all the wolves in an area, the deer overpopulate. Over-harvest the herbs, the bees lose food. Burn the forest, generations are lost. The world remembers and responds to Jake's actions.

**Design intent:** Players who play sustainably are rewarded. Players who exploit pay prices.

### 1.3 Principle 3: Regions Are Distinct

A Rexan jungle isn't a Fjordlund forest with palm trees. Each ecosystem has its own logic, its own species relationships, its own rhythms. Travel between regions feels like travel between worlds.

**Design intent:** Regional identity through ecological distinctness.

### 1.4 Principle 4: Time and Weather Shape Everything

Day is different from night. Wet season different from dry. Storm aftermath different from calm. Ecosystems respond to time and weather constantly, creating dynamic experiences.

**Design intent:** No two visits to the same place are identical.

### 1.5 Principle 5: Ecosystems Tell Stories

A field of skeletons tells of disease. A grove without songbirds tells of poisoning. A river with too many fish tells of predator absence. Players who observe carefully read environmental stories.

**Design intent:** Ecosystem state is environmental storytelling.

## 2. THE FOOD WEB CONCEPT

### 2.1 Trophic Levels

Every Dark Arisen ecosystem has trophic structure:

**Level 1: Primary Producers**
- Plants, herbs, fruit trees
- Foundation of all food webs
- Sunlight to sugar
- Most abundant biomass

**Level 2: Primary Consumers (Herbivores)**
- Deer, rabbits, herbivorous birds
- Eat plants directly
- Common species
- Hunted by predators

**Level 3: Secondary Consumers (Predators)**
- Wolves, big cats, hawks
- Eat herbivores
- Less common
- Top of most food chains

**Level 4: Apex Predators**
- Bears, jaguars, lions
- Eat anything they can catch
- Rare, territorial
- Ecosystem regulators

**Level 5: Scavengers (Cross-Level)**
- Crows, vultures, hyenas
- Eat dead from any level
- Cleanup crew
- Disease prevention

### 2.2 Energy Flow

Energy moves up the chain:
- Sun → Plants
- Plants → Herbivores
- Herbivores → Predators
- Predators → Scavengers (death)
- Decomposition → Soil → Plants

**Game Implementation:**
- Population density reflects energy availability
- Predator absence = herbivore explosion
- Plant death = herbivore death = predator loss
- All connected

### 2.3 Keystone Species

**Definition:** Species whose presence/absence dramatically affects ecosystem

**Dark Arisen Keystones:**
- **Wolves (Fjordlund, Quiet Coast):** Control deer populations
- **Jaguars (Rexa):** Control jungle herbivores
- **Bees (universal):** Pollinate crucial plants
- **Bears (Quiet Coast):** Carrion creators, salmon spreaders
- **Whales (oceans):** Massive nutrient cycling

**Player Impact:**
- Killing keystones has cascading effects
- Some legendary hunts ARE keystones
- Moral weight of keystone removal
- Long recovery times

- All species present
- Ratios balanced
- Full diversity
- Legendary creatures present

- Standard diversity

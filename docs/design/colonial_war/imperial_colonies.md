## 1. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY — COLONIES AS CHARACTERS

### 1.1 The Design Law

Cross-reference colonial_war_overview.md Section 7.1. **No generic colonies.** Each of the five Imperial holdings has a distinct silhouette, economy, moral texture, and a General worth a boss fight. A player who has visited all five should be able to describe each in a sentence — and each sentence should be different.

### 1.2 The Five Silhouettes

- **Puerto de la Corona** — the crown jewel where the old wound never closed
- **Nueva Esperanza** — the plantation where the arithmetic favors revolt
- **Fuerte San Rafael** — the extraction post that murdered a shaman's son
- **Bahía Dorada** — the gilded port where everything is for sale
- **Puesto del Norte** — the frozen outpost nobody wanted, held by a man who can't let go

### 1.3 Each Colony Answers Three Questions

**What does feeding it look like?** (Support scenario)  
**What does bleeding it look like?** (Break scenario)  
**What does freedom look like HERE?** (Liberation scenario — different for every colony)

The third question is the important one. Liberation is not a template. A freed Nueva Esperanza looks nothing like a freed Puesto del Norte.

## 2. THE IMPERIAL COLONIAL MODEL

### 2.1 How the Empire Takes

**The three-step doctrine, applied for two centuries:**

1. **The Sword** — military conquest, the indigenous resistance broken
2. **The Cross** — cultural conquest, the old gods named demons (cross-reference npcs/cannabis_keeper_mama_jacinta.md, desert_sage_mbah_seruni.md — both sages describe this)
3. **The Tax** — administrative conquest, permanent extraction codified as law

### 2.2 What This Produces

- Old wealth, deep roots, complacency
- Generational grievance in the conquered populations (nobody has forgotten)
- Internal division (Reform vs Status Quo vs Reactionary — cross-reference npcs/imperial_contacts.md Section 12.2)
- Military doctrine built on fortress endurance, not speed
- **A power that assumes it will always be here**

### 2.3 Pale Isle Is Not a Colony

**Design note:** Pale Isle is the Empire's archipelago SEAT — the administrative capital, Margarethe's world, the Medical College, the Guild Halls, the cathedral district. It is not besiegeable and not one of the five contested colonies.

Pale Isle's role in the war: it is where the letters are written (retaliation_system.md), where policy is set, where Jake's diplomacy happens (imperial_contacts.md), and where the consequences of the colonies' fates are DISCUSSED by people who have never seen them. That contrast — the aristocrats at the garden party debating tariffs while Nueva Esperanza's workers die in the cane — is the Empire's portrait in miniature.

## 3. PUERTO DE LA CORONA — THE CROWN COLONY

### 3.1 Quick Reference

**Region:** Rexa  
**Type:** Administrative capital colony  
**Size:** Jewel (largest, richest — war_state_system.md growth stage 5)  
**Economy:** Administration, trade hub, tobacco export, tax collection for the whole archipelago  
**Castle:** **La Ciudadela de la Corona** — Imperial star fort, cathedral-fortress hybrid  
**Commander:** General-Gobernador Alonso de Herrera  
**Starting Control:** IC 82, AC 3, LS 12, CT 3

### 3.2 History

Taken in 1651 after a three-year campaign against the indigenous Rexan confederacy. The conquest was thorough and the records were kept — which is the problem. The Empire wrote down what it did here, and the archives still exist in La Ciudadela's vaults.

Two centuries of building have made it the Empire's showpiece: cobbled plazas, a cathedral with a bell that can be heard six miles out to sea, a Noble District where Don Alejandro watches sunsets, and an indigenous quarter the administration calls "the old town" and the residents call *el barrio que recuerda* — the quarter that remembers.

### 3.3 The General — Alonso de Herrera

**Full boss design:** colonial_war_bosses.md Tier 1

**Quick Portrait:**

**Name:** General-Gobernador Alonso de Herrera, Marqués de Valdeña  
**Age:** 54  
**Register:** High (aristocratic-military, absolute)

**Character:**

The Empire's iron fist wrapped in excellent tailoring. Herrera is not a brute — he is worse: an intelligent man who has decided the Empire's continuation justifies everything, and who sleeps well. Twenty-six years of colonial service. Three suppressed uprisings. A reputation for surgical, proportionate, utterly ruthless response.

He is scrupulously honest, personally brave, genuinely devout, and would order a village burned before breakfast if the reports justified it. He does not enjoy it. He simply does it, and then attends Mass.

**Voice Samples:**

*"Captain Harlow. I have read your file. It is thicker than I would prefer and thinner than it will be. Sit. We will speak plainly, because I have no talent for the other thing."*

*"You imagine yourself a liberator. Every man who has stood where you stand imagined that. I have their files also. Would you care to know how the stories end? No. I thought not."*

*"The Empire is not loved here. I am aware. Love was never the instrument. Order was the instrument. Ask the old ones what came before order, if you can find one who remembers honestly."*

**His Blind Spot:**

Herrera believes the indigenous quarter is pacified because it is quiet. He has never understood that *el barrio que recuerda* has been keeping its own archive for two hundred years — oral, precise, and waiting.

### 3.4 Garrison Character

- **Strength:** the archipelago's largest Imperial garrison; veteran officers, disciplined ranks
- **Doctrine:** fortress-centered, rapid urban response, brutal efficiency in suppression
- **Weakness:** built for crushing uprisings, not for fighting a naval captain who never stands still
- **Notable presence:** an Inquisition office (cross-reference npcs/imperial_contacts.md — Inquisitor Blanc visits, and when she does, the barrio goes silent)

### 3.5 Economy & Supply Lines

**Three supply lines (colony_system_core.md Section 4.2):**
1. **The Pale Isle Run** — administrative traffic, payroll, orders (cut this: −E, and Herrera's correspondence stops arriving)
2. **The Tobacco Route** — export to Pale Isle and beyond (cross-reference fauna/tobacco_system.md; raiding this hits the Empire's purse hardest)
3. **The Coastal Provision Line** — food from Moran's plantations (cut this and the city gets hungry — but so do the poor first, a moral cost)

### 3.6 Population & Demographics

**Cross-reference:** npcs/regional_populations.md Section 4

- **Imperial Quarter:** administration, aristocracy, complacent High-register commerce
- **Harbor Mixing Zone:** three languages in a sentence, the war's rumors, Salazar's intelligence
- **El Barrio Que Recuerda:** the indigenous quarter — outwardly compliant, internally organized, Tito Diego's underground runs through it

**Ambient — Imperial Quarter:**

*"...the Gobernador's new patrol schedule is tiresome but one adapts..."*

**Ambient — El Barrio:**

*"...two hundred years they've written their version in the fort. Two hundred years we've kept ours in our mouths. Ours is more accurate..."*

### 3.7 The Castle — La Ciudadela de la Corona

**Full design:** castle_catalog.md

- Imperial star fort with a cathedral built into its northern face
- Garrison barracks, the Gobernador's residence, the Inquisition office
- **The Archive Vaults** — two centuries of colonial records
- **State Treasure:** the archipelago's tax reserve + **the conquest archives** (documents proving what the Empire did in 1651 — a weapon, not just loot)

### 3.8 The Weakness

**El barrio que recuerda.** Herrera's garrison can crush a riot. It cannot fight a quarter that has spent two centuries memorizing exactly where every wall is thin, every tunnel runs, and every soldier drinks.

Mechanically: **Popular attacks here are disproportionately effective** (colony_system_core.md Section 2.2). And the conquest archives, if taken and published, deliver a P-collapse no military action could match.

### 3.9 Support Scenario

Feeding Puerto de la Corona is the most lucrative Support work in the game — and the most morally loaded. Escort the tax fleets, deliver settlers into the expanding Imperial Quarter, fund Herrera's garrison.

**Growth looks like:** the Imperial Quarter expanding INTO the barrio's edges. New Imperial homes where old ones stood. **The player watches the colony grow by watching the old town shrink.** (building_evolution.md ownership transitions)

**Reward tier:** highest Imperial standing available; the villa here is the finest in the archipelago; Margarethe's social world opens fully.

### 3.10 Break Scenario

**The three-front approach:**
- **E:** raid the tobacco route and the tax fleets (rich plunder, huge E damage)
- **M:** sabotage La Ciudadela's magazines; eliminate Herrera's senior officers
- **P:** the barrio's networks — free prisoners, expose the Inquisition office's work, and above all **take the conquest archives**

**Herrera's response escalates fast** (retaliation_system.md) — he is the Empire's most competent retaliator. Breaking Corona means the hardest reprisal campaign in the game.

### 3.11 Liberation Scenario — "The Quarter That Remembers"

**What freedom looks like HERE:**

Not the Alliance storming the fort. The barrio rising from inside while Jake's guns speak from the harbor. The conquest archives read aloud in the plaza — two hundred years of Imperial record turned into indictment. The cathedral bell rung by hands that were never allowed to touch it.

**The liberated Corona:** the star fort becomes an Alliance bastion; the archives become a museum of what was done; the Imperial Quarter remains but no longer rules; Don Alejandro's veranda still watches sunsets, over a city that has changed hands.

**The cost:** the largest, hardest, most retaliation-drawing liberation in the game. LS 50 is not enough here — Corona requires the Alliance at full strength with multiple networks connected.

### 3.12 Cross-References — Who Lives Here

Don Alejandro de la Vega (Noble District) · Mama Jacinta (deep jungle beyond) · Maestro Domingo Vargas (smith) · Tía Esperanza Morales (smith) · Doña Mercedes Ramírez (healer) · Tito Carlos (healer) · Tía Rosa Vega (food) · Madame Yolanda Cabrera (supply) · Doña Esmeralda Ortega (textiles) · Don Eduardo Mendez (tools) · Don Tomás Aguilar (jeweler) · Doña Carmen Ruiz (art) · Don Manuel Ortega (spirits) · Doña Patricia Velasquez (La Sirena) · Tía Catalina (Casa de los Antepasados) · Don Eduardo Mendoza (community leader) · Mama Cecilia Bautista + Paloma · Capitán Roberto Salazar · Tito Diego (underground)

**Design note:** Corona has the densest NPC population in the game. Breaking it endangers the most people Jake knows (retaliation_system.md hostage pool). This is deliberate.

## 4. NUEVA ESPERANZA — THE PLANTATION COLONY

### 4.1 Quick Reference

**Region:** Moran  
**Type:** Plantation/forced-labor colony  
**Size:** Colony (growth stage 4)  
**Economy:** Sugar, tobacco, coffee — worked by forced labor  
**Castle:** **Fuerte Esperanza** — squat, functional, built to face inward  
**Commander:** General Baltasar Reyes  
**Starting Control:** IC 71, AC 8, LS 18, CT 3

### 4.2 History

Founded 1698 as an agricultural venture. "Nueva Esperanza" — New Hope. The name was chosen by an investor in Pale Isle who never visited.

The colony's entire architecture answers one design question: how do you hold ground when the people who work it outnumber you twenty to one? The answer is Fuerte Esperanza, whose guns point at the fields, not the sea.

### 4.3 The General — Baltasar Reyes

**Full boss design:** colonial_war_bosses.md Tier 1

**Name:** General Baltasar Reyes  
**Age:** 47  
**Register:** Middle-High (a functionary who acquired polish)

**Character:**

Not aristocracy. Reyes rose from nothing on competence and a talent nobody wants to name: he is very, very good at managing people who hate him. He knows every laborer's name in the eastern fields. He uses the names. It works better than the whip, and he is proud of that in a way that turns the stomach.

He is genuinely convinced he is the humane option. Compared to his predecessor, he may be right. That is the horror of him.

**Voice Samples:**

*"You've seen the fields. Yes. Everyone reacts that way the first time. Then they have some rum and dinner and they leave and nothing changes. I have watched forty visitors react. Would you like a drink, Captain?"*

*"Twenty to one. That is the arithmetic. Every morning I wake and the arithmetic is still twenty to one, and every evening the cane is cut and nobody has died, and I consider that a professional achievement. Judge me if you like. Bring me a better system and I will implement it."*

*"My predecessor used the lash. I use their names, their families, their small hopes. It is more effective. I am aware of what that makes me. I ask you to notice that I am aware."*

**His Blind Spot:**

Reyes believes he has bought loyalty with decency-relative-to-atrocity. He has bought silence. He mistakes the two, and the arithmetic is patient.

### 4.4 Garrison Character

- **Strength:** small but brutally efficient; inward-facing artillery; overseer network with informants
- **Doctrine:** deterrence through certainty of consequence, not force of numbers
- **Weakness:** **twenty to one.** The garrison's entire strategy is preventing the laborers from realizing what they already know. One spark, correctly placed, and the arithmetic resolves.

### 4.5 Economy & Supply Lines

**Two supply lines:**
1. **The Export Run** — sugar and tobacco to Pale Isle (the colony's whole purpose; raiding it is pure E damage)
2. **The Labor Line** — **people convoys.** The Empire moves forced laborers along this route.

**The Labor Line is the game's most important supply line.** Raiding it triggers the raid-to-liberation pipeline (colony_system_core.md Section 4.5): free the cargo, lower P, raise LS, and take aboard the heaviest passengers Jake will ever carry.

### 4.6 Population & Demographics

- **The Overseer Town:** small Imperial population, tense, well-paid, always armed
- **The Labor Quarters:** the overwhelming majority. Multi-origin — indigenous Rexan, transported peoples, debt-bound. Cultural survival under maximum pressure.
- **The Free Fringe:** freed or escaped people in the hills, in contact with Alliance networks

**Ambient — Overseer Town:**

*"...three years' contract, then I go home rich. Three more years. Just three more..."*

**Ambient — Labor Quarters (quiet, overheard):**

*"...twenty to one, mi hermano. Twenty to one. Say it like a prayer. Someday it will be an instruction..."*

### 4.7 The Castle — Fuerte Esperanza

- Squat, thick, unlovely; guns facing the fields
- The overseer barracks, the payroll vault, the punishment block (**never depicted in use — Style D restraint; the building's existence is the statement**)
- **State Treasure:** the payroll chest + **the labor ledgers** (names, origins, debts — the documents that prove what was done, and that let freed people find their scattered families)

### 4.8 The Weakness

**The arithmetic.** Popular attacks here are the most explosive in the game. Free one convoy, and the labor quarters hear. Free three, and the overseers stop sleeping. Expose the ledgers, and Nueva Esperanza doesn't need Jake's cannons — it needs a door left open.

### 4.9 Support Scenario

**The most morally difficult Support content in the game.** Feeding Nueva Esperanza means escorting the export runs and — the line the game will not blur — the Empire will ask Jake to escort the Labor Line.

**The design handles this honestly:** the option exists, the game does not prevent it, the money is excellent, and every sage, every crew member, and the world itself will react. Father Salvio will say something quiet. Ines will not look at Jake for a while. Mama Jacinta will simply stop receiving him.

**Growth looks like:** more fields, more quarters, more people arriving in chains. The building evolution here is the ugliest expansion in the game, and it is drawn without flinching.

### 4.10 Break Scenario

The most efficient Break in the game, because the colony is a lever waiting for a hand:
- Raid the export runs (E)
- **Raid the Labor Line and free the cargo** (P collapse + LS surge)
- Take the ledgers (P collapse, and a gift to every freed family)
- Reyes himself: defeating him removes the one man managing the arithmetic

### 4.11 Liberation Scenario — "The Arithmetic Resolves"

**What freedom looks like HERE:**

Not a siege. An uprising. Jake's role is the spark and the shield: free enough convoys, deliver enough weapons, expose the ledgers, and then be in the harbor with guns when the quarters rise — because the moment they rise, the guns on Fuerte Esperanza turn toward the fields.

**The liberated Nueva Esperanza:** the fields still exist, and now the people who worked them own them. Cross-reference colony_system_core.md — Jake's ethical-plantation model has its origin here. The punishment block is pulled down stone by stone; the ledgers become the archive that reunites families.

**The cost:** the most VIOLENT liberation. The overseers do not surrender. And if Jake's timing is wrong — if the rising happens before he can shield it — the reprisal is a massacre he caused.

### 4.12 Cross-References

Moran Highland cigar masters (fauna/tobacco_system.md — the Moran Highland cigar comes from these fields; Don Alejandro's quest has a source, and it is this) · Wilhelm Müller's brewing supply chain · Alliance free-fringe cells (Tito Diego's network reaches here)

**Design note:** The Moran Highland cigar in Don Alejandro's collection was grown by forced labor. Whether the game states this depends on Jake's investigation. It is available to find. Don Alejandro does not know. What Jake does with that knowledge is a conversation.

## 5. FUERTE SAN RAFAEL — THE EXTRACTION POST

### 5.1 Quick Reference

**Region:** Ashenmoor coast  
**Type:** Extraction colony  
**Size:** Town (growth stage 3)  
**Economy:** Volcanic glass, ore, sulfur — mining and shipping  
**Castle:** **Fuerte San Rafael** (colony and fort share the name — the fort IS the colony)  
**Commander:** Comandante Isabel Cruz  
**Starting Control:** IC 64, AC 6, LS 24, CT 6

### 5.2 History

Founded 1794 when Imperial surveyors identified the volcanic glass deposits. The mountain peoples were told the Empire came for stone. The Empire came for stone, and then for the shrines that stood on the stone.

**In 1846, Imperial soldiers from this garrison killed a young man named Jaya for refusing to leave his shrine. He was twenty-two. His name meant victory. His mother is still alive.**

**Cross-reference:** npcs/desert_sage_mbah_seruni.md Section 3.4, npcs/the_named_dead.md Section 3.1

### 5.3 The General — Comandante Isabel Cruz

**Full boss design:** colonial_war_bosses.md Tier 1

**Name:** Comandante Isabel Cruz  
**Age:** 43  
**Register:** Middle (a soldier, not a courtier)

**Character:**

The hardest of the five to hate, and the design does not let the player off.

Cruz is competent, fair to her troops, personally decent, and has twice refused orders she considered atrocities — refusals that cost her promotion and put her here, commanding a mining post at the edge of the world. She has stopped three shrine-clearances since taking command. She keeps a list of the ones she couldn't stop.

**She was nineteen years old and a junior officer in this garrison in 1846.** She was not in the patrol that killed Jaya. She was in the fort. She heard the report come in. She has never told anyone what she thinks about that, and she has never left.

**Voice Samples:**

*"Captain. I command a mining post. I ship stone. I keep my soldiers from becoming animals, which is more work than you would think. That is the whole of my ambition now. It was not always."*

*"You want me to be the villain. It would be tidier. I am the reason this place is merely bad instead of what it was. Twice I refused orders and twice it cost me, and the cost is this rock, and I am still here refusing. Ask the mountain people whether they would prefer my replacement."*

*"Eighteen forty-six. Yes. I know what you are asking. I was in this fort. I was nineteen. I did not give the order and I did not stop it and I have had thirty years to decide which of those matters more."*

**Her Blind Spot:**

Cruz believes staying is the moral choice — that her presence prevents worse. She may be right. She is also the Empire's presence, and the Empire's presence is the harm. She has built a career on being the least-bad enforcer of an unjust order, and she cannot see that "least bad" is still the thing itself.

### 5.4 Garrison Character

- **Strength:** small, disciplined, unusually well-treated troops loyal to Cruz personally
- **Doctrine:** minimum force, hold the mine, avoid provocation
- **Weakness:** the mountain. Mbah Seruni's networks know every path, every cave, every ash-fall pattern. Cruz's soldiers are competent visitors in a place that has never accepted them (cross-reference physics/lava_heat_physics.md, sand_desert_physics.md — the environment itself is Alliance territory)

### 5.5 Economy & Supply Lines

**One supply line, and it is fragile:**
1. **The Ore Run** — volcanic glass and ore out, everything else in. Cutting this line strangles San Rafael faster than any other colony (single point of failure, hostile hinterland, no local agriculture)

### 5.6 Population & Demographics

**Cross-reference:** npcs/regional_populations.md Section 6

- **The Fort Town:** small Imperial mining population, transient, heat-miserable
- **The Mountain Peoples:** the majority presence in the region, largely OUTSIDE Imperial control — Cruz's writ ends where the ash begins
- **The Mine Workers:** mixed — some paid, some debt-bound, all sick

**Ambient — Fort Town:**

*"...two more years and I transfer somewhere with water. Two more years..."*

**Ambient — Mountain Peoples:**

*"...the Comandante is not the worst of them. That is the most anyone has ever said about an Imperial in this place, and it is not praise..."*

### 5.7 The Castle — Fuerte San Rafael

- Small, heat-adapted Imperial fort; thick walls, water cisterns as the critical infrastructure
- **State Treasure:** the ore reserve and glass stockpile (valuable) + **the 1846 patrol reports** (the documents naming the soldiers who killed Jaya, and the officer who ordered it)

### 5.8 The Weakness

**The mountain, and the cisterns.** Cut the ore run, sabotage the water, and San Rafael cannot hold. Militarily it is the easiest of the five to break.

**Which is the design's trap:** the easiest colony to break is commanded by the most sympathetic officer, and breaking it is what Mbah Seruni has waited thirty years for.

### 5.9 The Jaya Thread

**A dedicated quest chain, available once Jake has earned Mbah Seruni's trust (npcs/desert_sage_mbah_seruni.md Chapter 6+):**

1. Jake learns the garrison's name from her stillness, not her words
2. The patrol reports exist in the fort
3. **The officer who gave the order** — Teniente-Coronel Marcos Ibarra — is not dead. He is retired, comfortable, in Bahía Dorada (Section 6). Cruz knows exactly where.
4. **Cruz will tell Jake where Ibarra is.** She has been carrying the address for thirty years, waiting for someone to ask who had a right to know.
5. What Jake does with the address, and whether Mbah Seruni is told, and whether she wants him to act or wants to know or wants nothing — that is the quest, and it does not resolve cleanly (cross-reference the_named_dead.md Section 2.3 design rules)

**Design note:** This thread is the strongest emotional connection between Phase 5 and Phase 7. It requires no new lore — Jaya, Mbah Seruni, and her thirty years of stillness were established in Phase 5. Phase 7 gives the wound an address.

### 5.10 Support Scenario

Feeding San Rafael is small, thankless, and quietly damning: escort ore runs, deliver cistern supplies, fund Cruz's garrison.

**And Cruz is grateful, and reasonable, and asks Jake to help her hold a post she believes prevents worse — while Mbah Seruni watches from the mountain.**

**Growth looks like:** the mine expanding. Which means more shrines cleared, whatever Cruz's list says.

### 5.11 Break Scenario

Mechanically easy, emotionally the heaviest:
- Cut the ore run (single line — rapid E collapse)
- Sabotage the cisterns (M collapse)
- The mountain peoples need almost no incitement (P is already fragile)
- **Take the 1846 reports**
- Cruz will not surrender the fort. She will fight for it, well, and lose, and the game does not pretend that is triumphant.

### 5.12 Liberation Scenario — "Thirty Years of Stillness"

**What freedom looks like HERE:**

The quietest liberation in the game. No uprising, no massed battle — the mountain simply closes. Cut the line, take the water, and the Alliance walks in through paths Imperial maps never recorded.

**And Mbah Seruni comes down from the caves for the first time in the game's timeline to stand at the fort her son died refusing to leave.** She sings names. Hundreds of them. One of them is Jaya's.

**The liberated San Rafael:** the mine becomes locally controlled and sustainably worked (cross-reference the Phase 4 sustainability economics — the same total profit, the resource preserved). The shrines are restored. The fort is not torn down — it is left standing, empty, as a marker.

**The cost:** Cruz. However Jake handles her — killed in the fight, allowed to leave, or something more complicated — she is a person who tried, and the liberation requires her removal. The Style Bible's Layered Death is doing its heaviest work here.

### 5.13 Cross-References

## 6. BAHÍA DORADA — THE GILDED PORT

### 6.1 Quick Reference

**Region:** Coastal, near Silvera Island  
**Type:** Luxury trade colony  
**Size:** Colony (growth stage 4)  
**Economy:** Luxury goods, banking, the Silvera trade, discreet services  
**Castle:** **Castillo Dorado** — beautiful, well-funded, under-manned  
**Commander:** Gobernador Francisco de Silva  
**Starting Control:** IC 76, AC 11, LS 7, CT 6

### 6.2 History

Founded 1721 as a customs post; became something else entirely. Bahía Dorada is where Imperial money goes to be comfortable — the gateway to Silvera's aristocratic enclave (cross-reference npcs/luxury_merchants.md Section 6.4), the archipelago's banking house, and a port with the unofficial motto *todo se vende* — everything sells.

It has never been attacked. Nobody has ever needed to attack it. You buy Bahía Dorada.

### 6.3 The General — Gobernador Francisco de Silva

**Full boss design:** colonial_war_bosses.md Tier 1 (the unusual one — a boss who tries very hard not to fight)

**Name:** Gobernador Francisco de Silva  
**Age:** 61  
**Register:** High (charming, oily, genuinely cultured)

**Character:**

Corrupt in the fullest sense — not merely bribable but constituted of transactions. De Silva has monetized every function of his office and does not perceive this as wrongdoing. He is warm, funny, an excellent host, a real connoisseur, and would sell the colony's defense schedules for the right consideration. He has, in fact, sold them twice.

He is also a coward, and this is his most dangerous quality: cornered, de Silva will sell ANYONE. Including the officers he'd previously protected. Including Jake.

**Voice Samples:**

*"Captain Harlow! Sit, sit, the '58 is open and it is behaving beautifully. Now. Before we become friends, let us establish what you can afford, so that neither of us wastes an afternoon."*

*"You want the garrison rotation? My dear Captain, of course. I want three things and none of them are difficult, and afterward we shall both pretend this conversation concerned wine."*

*"Loyalty. Yes. I have an excellent relationship with the Crown, which pays me, and with several other parties, who also pay me, and I have found that everyone is happier when nobody examines the arrangement too closely."*

**His Blind Spot:**

De Silva believes everything is purchasable, which means he cannot recognize the people who cannot be bought until they are already inside his walls. He has no framework for Mbah Seruni, or for Jake if Jake refuses the transaction.

### 6.4 Garrison Character

- **Strength:** well-equipped, beautifully uniformed, generously paid
- **Doctrine:** deterrence by appearance; nobody attacks Bahía Dorada
- **Weakness:** the garrison has never fought. Half the officers bought their commissions. Behind the beautiful walls is a peacetime militia with excellent tailors.

### 6.5 Economy & Supply Lines

**Three supply lines, all lucrative:**
1. **The Silvera Run** — luxury goods to and from the aristocratic enclave (raiding this is the richest plunder in the game per convoy)
2. **The Banking Line** — bullion and notes (cross-reference the Empire's finances archipelago-wide; hitting this hurts EVERY Imperial colony's E)
3. **The Discretion Route** — the traffic nobody logs (smuggling, and de Silva's private arrangements)

### 6.6 Population & Demographics

- **The Gilded Quarter:** aristocrats, bankers, luxury merchants, High register everywhere
- **The Service Town:** the people who make the gold quarter function, largely invisible to it
- **The Docks:** where the discretion route lands, and where Mateusz's network has friends

**Ambient — Gilded Quarter:**

*"...one hears things about the northern colonies. One prefers not to hear them at length..."*

**Ambient — Service Town:**

*"...they'd sell the harbor if someone brought a big enough purse. Some of us have started wondering who's buying..."*

### 6.7 The Castle — Castillo Dorado

- Elegant, well-maintained, more palace than fortress
- **State Treasure:** the largest doubloon reserve in the game (the banking vault) + **de Silva's private ledgers** — the record of every bribe, every sold schedule, every Imperial officer he has compromised

**The ledgers are the game's best blackmail artifact.** They can break careers across the entire Imperial hierarchy. Cross-reference retaliation_system.md de-escalation, npcs/imperial_contacts.md (Inspector Kessler's corruption investigation ends here).

### 6.8 The Weakness

**Everything is for sale, including him.** Bahía Dorada can be taken with money, patience, and a purse — no siege required. Jake can buy the garrison rotation, buy the gate, buy de Silva's cooperation in his own colony's fall.

And the Kessler connection makes it richer: Inspector Kessler's multi-chapter corruption investigation (npcs/recurring_quest_givers.md Section 2.1) has been building toward exactly these ledgers. Jake can hand Bahía Dorada to the Empire's own reformers.

### 6.9 The Ibarra Connection

**Cross-reference Section 5.9.** Teniente-Coronel Marcos Ibarra — the officer who ordered Jaya's death — lives here in comfortable retirement, a respected member of the Gilded Quarter. He grows orchids. He is charming at parties. Nobody in Bahía Dorada knows or would care.

He is findable, reachable, and entirely undefended. What happens next is the game's most concentrated statement about revenge — and Mbah Seruni's wishes, whatever they are, are the only guidance the game offers.

### 6.10 Support Scenario

Easy, pleasant, and enormously profitable: escort Silvera runs, deliver luxury cargo, invest in the banking house.

**Bahía Dorada is the Support path's paradise** — the villa here is a palace, the Silvera access unlocks the game's luxury tier (npcs/luxury_merchants.md), and de Silva is a delightful business partner who asks no uncomfortable questions.

**Growth looks like:** more gold, more marble, more service-town crowding at the edges.

### 6.11 Break Scenario

**Three approaches, and the third is the interesting one:**
- **Force:** the garrison folds fast (they have never fought)
- **Economics:** raid the banking line and cripple Imperial finances archipelago-wide
- **Purchase:** buy the colony out from under itself. Bribe the gates open. Take the vault and the ledgers without a shot fired. **The Empire's most corrupt holding falls to its own methods.**

### 6.12 Liberation Scenario — "Nothing to Liberate"

**What freedom looks like HERE — and this is the honest answer: unclear.**

Bahía Dorada has no oppressed majority waiting to rise. It has a service town that would like better wages and a gilded quarter that would like the unpleasantness to stop. LS starts at 7 — the lowest in the game — and it climbs slowly, because there is no generational wound here, only inequality.

**The design's answer:** Bahía Dorada's third path is not liberation but **exposure**. Take the ledgers, hand them to Kessler and the Reform faction, and let the Empire clean its own house. The colony does not become free. It becomes *honest*, which is a smaller and stranger victory, and the only one available.

**Cross-reference:** npcs/imperial_contacts.md Section 12.2 — this is the Reform faction's greatest possible win, and Jake can hand it to them.

## 7. PUESTO DEL NORTE — THE FROZEN OUTPOST

### 7.1 Quick Reference

**Region:** Fjordlund edge  
**Type:** Strategic military outpost  
**Size:** Settlement (growth stage 2 — the smallest)  
**Economy:** Timber, furs, whale oil — barely profitable  
**Castle:** **Puesto del Norte** — a stone fort against the cold  
**Commander:** General Rodrigo Vega  
**Starting Control:** IC 48, AC 22, LS 19, CT 11

### 7.2 History

Established 1839 to claim the northern approaches before Albion could. It has never turned a profit. It exists because a map in Pale Isle has a line on it, and lines must be held.

**And directly across the strait, visible on clear days, flies Albion's Fort Resolute.** Two empires, one frozen channel, and a Fjordlund population that regards both with the patience of people who have outlasted worse (cross-reference colonial_war_overview.md Section 7.4 — the Northern Front).

### 7.3 The General — Rodrigo Vega

**Full boss design:** colonial_war_bosses.md Tier 1

**Name:** General Rodrigo Vega  
**Age:** 58  
**Register:** Middle-High, deteriorating (a man losing his polish to the cold)

**Character:**

Bitter, brilliant, and abandoned. Vega was a rising star who backed the wrong faction in a Pale Isle political fight and was sent here to freeze quietly. He has been requesting reinforcement for eleven years. He has received four letters of encouragement.

He is the most dangerous of the five because he has nothing to lose and he knows the Empire has already discarded him. Vega will do things Herrera would consider excessive and Cruz would refuse, because Vega has no career left to protect and one obsession remaining: he will not be the general who lost the northern line.

**Voice Samples:**

*"Eleven years, Captain. Eleven years I have written to Pale Isle requesting three hundred men. Do you know what I have received? Commendations. They send me paper and expect me to build walls with it."*

*"Across that water is Colonel Thorne, who is twenty-nine years old and has four times my supply. I have held this line against him for six years with a garrison that eats fish and resentment. Do not mistake me for a failure, Captain. I am the only thing here that has not broken."*

*"The Fjordlunders wait for us both to freeze. They may be correct. But I will be the last one standing when it happens, and someone in Pale Isle will finally have to write my name."*

**His Blind Spot:**

Vega thinks the war here is between the Empire and Albion. It is not. It is between two exhausted garrisons and a people who have been holding this coast for a thousand years and are simply waiting.

### 7.4 Garrison Character

- **Strength:** hardened, cold-adapted, fiercely loyal to Vega (he has suffered with them for eleven years)
- **Doctrine:** hold the line, endure, deny Albion
- **Weakness:** **understrength, undersupplied, and eleven years tired.** Also: they fight the cold badly. Cross-reference physics/snow_ice_physics.md — Fjordlunders learn from the cold; the garrison fights it, and loses a little every winter.

### 7.5 Economy & Supply Lines

**One long, fragile supply line:**
1. **The Northern Run** — everything comes from the south, across dangerous water, seasonally. Cut it in winter and Puesto del Norte does not merely weaken. It starves.

**Design note:** This is the most severable supply line in the game and the most morally complicated to cut, because the garrison Jake would be starving is a group of forgotten men who have already been abandoned by the Empire he is fighting.

### 7.6 Population & Demographics

**Cross-reference:** npcs/regional_populations.md Section 5

- **The Fort:** the garrison, and a handful of Imperial civilians who couldn't leave
- **The Fjordlund Communities:** the actual population of the region, resisting BOTH empires quietly, organized around Jarl Olaf and the old ways
- **The Traders:** seasonal, opportunistic, playing both forts against each other

**Ambient — Fort:**

*"...four hours of light and the General's still on the wall. He doesn't sleep. I don't think he can anymore..."*

**Ambient — Fjordlund community:**

*"...two forts, two flags, two garrisons freezing. We have watched this coast for a thousand years. We can wait another winter..."*

### 7.7 The Castle — Puesto del Norte

- Small, thick-walled, cold-adapted; the fort IS the colony
- **State Treasure:** modest (the poorest state treasure in the game) + **Vega's eleven years of correspondence** — the complete record of a general begging an empire that stopped reading. Worthless as loot. Devastating as a document. Published in Pale Isle, it is an indictment of Imperial colonial policy that the Reform faction could build a decade on.

### 7.8 The Weakness

**Winter, and Fjordlund's patience.** Cut the Northern Run before the freeze and the fort cannot survive to spring. No assault required.

### 7.9 The Three-Way Front

**Puesto del Norte is the game's richest strategic puzzle because there are three players, not two:**

- Weaken the Empire here and **Albion's Fort Resolute takes the region** (war_state_system.md Path C — Rival Conquest). Jake has traded a tired old empire for a hungry new one.
- Weaken BOTH forts and the region can go to **Fjordlund** — but LS here starts at 19 and Jarl Olaf's people are the slowest in the game to trust (npcs/recurring_quest_givers.md Section 4.1: *"You earn trust differently here. With time. With consistency. With showing up."*)
- Support one against the other and Jake becomes a factor in a colonial war that Fjordlund never asked to host

**The correct play — freeing Fjordlund by breaking both empires — requires the most patience the war system asks of the player. Which is exactly what Fjordlund culture demands.** The design and the culture agree.

### 7.10 Support Scenario

Support here is thankless and cheap: run the Northern Line, deliver winter supplies, fund a garrison the Empire has forgotten.

**And Vega is pathetically, dangerously grateful.** He will call Jake his only ally. He will mean it. He will also, if Jake ever turns on him, react with the totality of a man who has nothing left.

**Growth looks like:** barely anything. A few more buildings. A garrison that eats properly for one winter. The smallest visible growth in the game, and oddly the most affecting.

### 7.11 Break Scenario

The easiest and coldest:
- Cut the Northern Run before winter and wait (E and M collapse together)
- Or help Albion take it (fast, and Jake will regret it)
- Vega will not surrender. He will hold an indefensible fort with a starving garrison because being the general who lost the northern line is the one thing he cannot survive. **The final fight at Puesto del Norte is Style D from the first shot.**

### 7.12 Liberation Scenario — "A Thousand Years of Waiting"

**What freedom looks like HERE:**

Both flags come down. Not one — both. The Fjordlund communities do not want the Empire replaced by Albion; they want the coast back. This is the only liberation in the game requiring Jake to break TWO colonial powers in the same region.

**The liberated north:** the two forts stand empty across the strait from each other, and the fishing fleets sail between them, and Bjornshallen's long tables fill, and Jarl Olaf says something short. Mormor Astrid's hearth burns without a garrison patrol passing the window.

**The cost:** patience, two campaigns, and the knowledge that Vega died holding a wall for an empire that had forgotten his name.

## 8. CROSS-COLONY SYSTEMS

### 8.1 The Imperial Network

The five colonies are not independent. Damage propagates:

- **Bahía Dorada's banking line** funds all five. Cripple it and every colony's E suffers.
- **Nueva Esperanza's food** provisions Puerto de la Corona. Starve Moran and Corona gets hungry.
- **Puerto de la Corona's administration** issues orders to all. Break Corona and the others lose coordination (slower retaliation, slower reinforcement).
- **Fuerte San Rafael's ore** supplies Imperial forges. Cut it and Imperial fortification/materiel costs rise archipelago-wide.
- **Puesto del Norte** consumes and produces almost nothing — its only value is the line on the map.

### 8.2 Mutual Reinforcement

At high Control, colonies reinforce each other: Corona sends troops, Dorada sends money, and a besieged colony can be relieved. **Strategic implication: Jake should isolate before striking.** Cut the connections, then break the colony.

### 8.3 The Herrera Coordination

**Full Vision:** while Herrera lives and Corona stands, Imperial retaliation is COORDINATED (faster, smarter, multi-colony). Defeating Herrera degrades the Empire's whole reprisal capability. He is the nervous system.

### 8.4 The Priority Question

Each colony offers a different strategic key:
- **Corona:** the Empire's brain and its oldest guilt
- **Nueva Esperanza:** the fastest LS growth in the game
- **San Rafael:** the easiest break, the heaviest cost
- **Bahía Dorada:** the money, the ledgers, and Ibarra
- **Puesto del Norte:** the three-way puzzle and Fjordlund's freedom

## 9. THE SAGE MIRRORS

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war_overview.md Section 10.3

### 9.1 Don Alejandro and Puerto de la Corona

The hardest mirror in the game. Don Alejandro lives in Corona's Noble District. He served the Empire for thirty-three years. His son died in an Imperial war he disagreed with. He grieves the Empire and he grieves what it took from him, and both are true.

If Jake feeds Corona, Don Alejandro says nothing and pours the wine and something in his face changes. If Jake breaks Corona, Don Alejandro's veranda looks out over a fallen city, and he is an old Imperial officer whose world has ended, and he still receives Jake, and that is worse than if he refused.

**No dialogue in this thread should state the tension. Style Bible restraint, absolute.**

### 9.2 Mbah Seruni and Fuerte San Rafael

Thirty years of stillness with an address. If Jake feeds San Rafael, she stops receiving him — no speech, just the closed grove. If Jake breaks it, she comes down from the caves and sings her son's name at the fort's gate.

### 9.3 Mormor Astrid and Puesto del Norte

Two empires pressing her coast, and a hearth that has burned through worse. She will feed Jake either way and ask about his health and say something about winter. Whether the patrol still passes her window in Chapter 10 is the whole answer.

### 9.4 Mama Jacinta and the Barrio

Corona's indigenous quarter is her cultural territory. Its liberation is her life's work made real. Its expansion-over is her life's work erased. She will not lecture. She will simply be there, or not be there.

## 14. CONCLUSION

The Old Power's Holdings. Puerto de la Corona, where Herrera keeps two centuries of records in a vault and the quarter outside keeps its own version in its mouth. Nueva Esperanza, where Reyes uses names instead of the lash and calls it progress while the arithmetic waits at twenty to one. Fuerte San Rafael, where Comandante Cruz has spent thirty years being the least-bad enforcer of an unjust order, in the fort whose soldiers killed a boy named Victory. Bahía Dorada, gilded and buyable, where the man who gave that order grows orchids and is charming at parties. And Puesto del Norte, where a forgotten general holds a worthless line against a young colonel across a frozen strait, while a thousand-year-old people wait for both of them to freeze.

Five colonies, five state treasures that are all documents, five Generals who each believe something that is nearly true. And five different answers to the only question that matters: what does freedom look like HERE?

**Phase 7 Subsystem 5 of 16 — complete.**

**The old flags fly. The Generals hold. The archives wait in the vaults. The Empire endures — for now.** 🏰

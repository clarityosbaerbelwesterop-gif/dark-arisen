# CASTLE CATALOG — THE FORTRESSES OF THE ARCHIPELAGO

**Status:** Design Draft v1.0  
**Phase:** Phase 7 — Colonial War, Castles & Dungeons (Castle Tier Document 1 — Document 8 of 16)  
**Tier:** Castle Tier  
**Related:** colonial_war_overview.md, colonial_war/imperial_colonies.md, colonial_war/krone_colonies.md, colonial_war/indigenous_liberation_alliance.md, colonial_war/castle_sieges.md, colonial_war/castle_ownership.md, colonial_war/colonial_war_bosses.md, npcs/building_evolution.md, physics/destruction_physics.md, physics/sound_physics.md, style_bible.md  
**Purpose:** Complete individual character portraits of every castle in Phase 7 — layout identity, garrison character, commander, state treasure, weakness, siege approaches, and ownership potential. Ten fortresses, none generic.  
**Inspirations:** Elden Ring (legacy dungeon identity), Dark Souls (level as character), Assassin's Creed (fortress infiltration variety), real Vauban star forts and East India Company factory-fortresses, Mount & Blade (siege variety), Dishonored (multiple-approach architecture)

## 1. CATALOG PHILOSOPHY — THE FIVE PRINCIPLES

### 1.1 Principle 1: The Layout Is the Arena

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war_overview.md Section 9.1 (Tier 2 bosses)

Every castle's commander is fought inside their own fortress, and the fortress's architecture IS the fight. Herrera's star fort geometry, Ashcroft's manor corridors, Sterling's harbor batteries — the boss encounter is inseparable from the place.

**Design intent:** Level design as boss design.

### 1.2 Principle 2: Every Castle Has Three Doors

Cross-reference colony_system_core.md's Support/Break/Liberate and war_state_system.md's M/E/P decomposition. Each fortress is takeable at least three ways:

- **The Wall** — assault, siege, force
- **The Back** — infiltration, sabotage, stealth
- **The Inside** — bribery, uprising, betrayal, or paperwork

**Design intent:** No castle has one solution. Cross-reference Dishonored's architecture philosophy.

### 1.3 Principle 3: The Treasure Tells the Story

**Cross-reference:** imperial_colonies.md Section 12 (Pillar 2 note), krone_colonies.md Section 12

Every Imperial state treasure is a **document** — the Empire's crimes are its own archives. Every Albion treasure is an **instrument** — the Company's crimes are still legally in force. The Old Fortresses hold something else entirely.

**Design intent:** Loot as lore, and lore as weapon.

### 1.4 Principle 4: Fortresses Remember

**Cross-reference:** physics/destruction_physics.md, npcs/building_evolution.md

Siege damage persists. A castle Jake stormed in Chapter 7 still shows the breach in Chapter 10. A castle he took intact and rebuilt shows the work. The fortress is a ledger of what was done to it.

**Design intent:** Persistent damage as memory.

### 1.5 Principle 5: A Castle Is Not a Fight — It Is a Place

Players should be able to describe each fortress from memory without mentioning combat. The bell you can hear six miles out. The guns that face inward. The counting halls. The stoves. Architecture first, encounter second.

**Design intent:** Memorable geography, in the Elden Ring tradition.

## 2. THE FIVE CASTLE TYPES

### 2.1 Imperial Star Fort

Geometric, angled bastions, overlapping fields of fire, deep magazines. Built by military engineers to hold against artillery for months.

**Strength:** the hardest walls in the archipelago  
**Weakness:** built to face outward; designed for a siege, not a betrayal  
**Feel:** cold mathematics in stone

### 2.2 Imperial Cathedral-Fortress

A church built into a fort, or a fort built around a church. Cultural weight and military function fused. Cross-reference npcs/imperial_contacts.md — Inquisition presence.

**Strength:** legitimacy; attacking it is a political act  
**Weakness:** consecrated space cannot be freely fortified; the congregation is not the garrison  
**Feel:** incense and gunpowder, and cross-reference physics/sound_physics.md Section 4.1 — massive reverb

### 2.3 HAC Trade Citadel

Warehouse-fortress. Counting halls, storage levels, document vaults, a modest garrison. Built to protect *value*, not to survive war.

**Strength:** vaults, and the legal architecture inside them  
**Weakness:** it is a bank with cannons; the walls are an afterthought  
**Feel:** ledgers, dust, and locked doors

### 2.4 Albion Naval Bastion

Harbor-locked, fleet-supported, batteries pointed at the water. The fortress and the fleet are one weapon.

**Strength:** unassailable from the sea while the fleet lives  
**Weakness:** separate the fleet from the fort and the fort is a stone box  
**Feel:** salt, drill, and professional competence

### 2.5 The Old Fortresses (Pre-Colonial)

Built by peoples who were here first, abandoned by empires that could not hold or understand them. Cross-reference indigenous_liberation_alliance.md Section 5.3-5.4.

**Strength:** the terrain; they were sited by people who knew the land  
**Weakness:** centuries of neglect; they must be *reclaimed*, not just captured  
**Feel:** older than everything, and waiting

## 3. LA CIUDADELA DE LA CORONA

### 3.1 Quick Reference

**Colony:** Puerto de la Corona (imperial_colonies.md Section 3)  
**Type:** Imperial Star Fort + Cathedral-Fortress hybrid  
**Commander:** General-Gobernador Alonso de Herrera  
**Garrison:** ~600 (the largest in the archipelago)  
**State Treasure:** the archipelago tax reserve + **the Conquest Archives (1651)**  
**Difficulty:** the hardest castle in Phase 7

### 3.2 The Silhouette

Five angled bastions in a star, and rising from the northern point, **the cathedral** — its bell tower doubling as the fort's highest observation post. From the sea, La Ciudadela reads as a church wearing armor. The bell can be heard six miles out. Sailors set their watches by it.

Below and around: the plazas, the Noble District where Don Alejandro's veranda faces the harbor, and *el barrio que recuerda* pressed against the fort's southwestern approach like a held breath.

### 3.3 Layout Identity — "The Geometry and the Undercity"

**Above ground:** textbook Vauban geometry. Interlocking fields of fire, no dead angles, a killing ground at every approach. Assaulting La Ciudadela frontally is the closest thing Phase 7 has to suicide.

**Below ground:** the fort was built on top of something. The 1651 conquest buried a pre-colonial site, and the Imperial engineers — pragmatists — incorporated what they found into their foundations rather than clearing it.

**The barrio knows about the undercity. The garrison does not.**

Cross-reference indigenous_liberation_alliance.md Section 2.2 — access to the undercity is a Trust Tier 3 reward from the Cultural Web, and it is the single most valuable piece of tactical intelligence in Phase 7.

### 3.4 The Districts

- **The Bastions** — artillery, garrison quarters, drill yards
- **The Cathedral** — nave, crypt, bell tower; **the Inquisition office** attached (Blanc's presence when she visits)
- **The Governor's Residence** — Herrera's quarters, austere to the point of monastic
- **The Archive Vaults** — two centuries of colonial records, catalogued, indexed, and damning
- **The Undercity** — pre-colonial foundations, flooded in places, mapped only in memory

### 3.5 The Commander — Herrera

**Full boss:** colonial_war_bosses.md Tier 1

**The arena:** Herrera fights in the bastion geometry — using the fort's angles the way he uses everything, correctly and without waste. The killing ground is his weapon. Cross-reference his blind spot (imperial_colonies.md Section 3.3): he does not defend the undercity approach because he does not know it exists.

### 3.6 State Treasure — The Conquest Archives

The tax reserve is the largest cash haul available from an Imperial holding. **The Archives are worth more.**

Two hundred years of Imperial record: the 1651 campaign orders, the shrine-clearance authorizations, the census of what was taken and from whom. The Empire wrote it all down because bureaucracy demands it, and never imagined anyone would read it back.

**Three uses:**
- **Read aloud in the plaza** (Liberation Event — imperial_colonies.md Section 3.11): P collapse, the barrio's two-century oral record confirmed in the Empire's own hand
- **Given to Kessler / the Reform faction** (npcs/recurring_quest_givers.md Section 2.1): political detonation in Pale Isle
- **Returned to the Cultural Web:** Mama Jacinta's network gains the documentation of everything taken — cross-reference Tito Diego's recovery work, which now has an inventory

### 3.7 The Three Doors

**The Wall:** frontal assault is a slaughter. Viable only with Alliance numbers at high LS plus naval bombardment, and expensive even then.

**The Back:** **the undercity.** Trust Tier 3 from the Cultural Web opens a route beneath the star fort into the archive vaults and the cathedral crypt. The infiltration route through La Ciudadela is the best stealth content in Phase 7.

**The Inside:** the barrio rising (Liberation Event), or — Full Vision — the cathedral. The clergy are not the garrison, and not all of them are Blanc's. Father Konstantin Vossberg's correspondence network reaches here (npcs/recurring_quest_givers.md Section 2.3).

### 3.8 Ownership Potential

**Cross-reference:** castle_ownership.md

Taking La Ciudadela intact makes Jake the master of the archipelago's most powerful fortress — and its most politically loaded. As an Alliance Bastion it becomes the Free Archipelago's heart. As Jake's own holding, it makes him something uncomfortably close to a governor.

**Design note:** the game should let players feel that discomfort. Cross-reference indigenous_liberation_alliance.md Principle 5.

## 4. FUERTE ESPERANZA

### 4.1 Quick Reference

**Colony:** Nueva Esperanza (imperial_colonies.md Section 4)  
**Type:** Imperial Star Fort (degraded — an inward-facing variant)  
**Commander:** General Baltasar Reyes  
**Garrison:** ~120 plus the overseer network  
**State Treasure:** the payroll chest + **the labor ledgers**  
**Difficulty:** militarily easy, morally the heaviest

### 4.2 The Silhouette

Squat, thick, unlovely. Two bastions instead of five. And the detail every player notices within thirty seconds of arriving:

**The guns face the fields.**

Not the sea. Not the road. The cane. Fuerte Esperanza was built by engineers answering one question — how do you hold ground when the people who work it outnumber you twenty to one — and the architecture is the answer, stated in artillery.

### 4.3 Layout Identity — "The Fort That Faces Inward"

Compact and functional. The whole structure is oriented toward the plantation:
- Observation platforms with sightlines down every field row
- The gate faces the villages, not the harbor
- Firing steps on the inner wall, not the outer

**Design note:** a player who circles Fuerte Esperanza before attacking learns everything about Nueva Esperanza without a word of dialogue. Cross-reference style_bible.md Pillar 2 — architecture as lore.

### 4.4 The Districts

- **The Overseer Barracks** — well-appointed, defensible, isolated
- **The Punishment Block** — cross-reference imperial_colonies.md Section 4.7: **never depicted in use.** The building exists. Players may enter it. It is empty and clean and the design says nothing.
- **The Payroll Office** — where the arithmetic is done
- **The Ledger Room** — four decades of labor records

### 4.5 The Commander — Reyes

**Full boss:** colonial_war_bosses.md Tier 1

**The arena:** the inner yard and the firing steps — Reyes fights where he has spent twenty years preparing to fight, against the direction he always expected the attack to come from. Cross-reference his blind spot: he mistook silence for loyalty, and the attack comes from exactly where the guns point.

### 4.6 State Treasure — The Labor Ledgers

Names, origins, families, debts, and dispositions. Forty years of who was brought here, from where, and what happened to them.

**As loot:** modest.  
**As an artifact:** the single most valuable document in the game to the people it describes. **Freed families use these ledgers to find each other.** Cross-reference indigenous_liberation_alliance.md Section 3.3 (+10 network strength for destroying enforcement records — but the ledgers should be *taken*, not burned, and the game should make that distinction available).

### 4.7 The Three Doors

**The Wall:** trivially breachable. Two bastions, thin garrison, a fort designed to shoot the other way.

**The Back:** the overseer network is corruptible — some are frightened, some are indebted, at least one wants out.

**The Inside:** **the arithmetic.** Free enough convoys, expose the ledgers, and Fuerte Esperanza does not need to be stormed. It needs a gate left open at the right moment. Cross-reference imperial_colonies.md Section 4.11 — and the timing is everything, because the moment the villages rise, those inward-facing guns have a target.

### 4.8 Ownership Potential

Fuerte Esperanza is the strongest argument in the game for **not** keeping a castle. Cross-reference castle_ownership.md: the villages may ask Jake to pull it down. The guns face their homes. No amount of new ownership changes what the architecture was for.

**Full Vision:** a demolition option, stone by stone, across chapters — and the fields visible where the walls were.

## 5. FUERTE SAN RAFAEL

### 5.1 Quick Reference

**Colony:** Fuerte San Rafael (imperial_colonies.md Section 5)  
**Type:** Imperial Star Fort (small, heat-adapted)  
**Commander:** Comandante Isabel Cruz  
**Garrison:** ~80  
**State Treasure:** ore and glass stockpile + **the 1846 patrol reports**  
**Difficulty:** the easiest to break, the hardest to live with

### 5.2 The Silhouette

Small, sun-bleached, built low against the heat. Thick walls, deep shade, and — the fort's actual heart — **the cisterns.** Everything at San Rafael is arranged around water storage. The magazines are secondary. The cisterns are the fortress.

Above it, the mountain. Cross-reference physics/lava_heat_physics.md, sand_desert_physics.md — the terrain that owns this place is not the Empire's.

### 5.3 Layout Identity — "The Fort Built Around Water"

- Central cistern complex, roofed and guarded
- Shade galleries connecting every structure (movement without heat exposure)
- Thick-walled quarters oriented for night cooling
- The mine works below and outside the walls

**The design consequence:** San Rafael's every architectural decision is a concession to a place that does not want it there.

### 5.4 The Districts

- **The Cistern Complex** — the fort's life, and its weakness
- **The Garrison Quarters** — small, unusually decent (Cruz's doing)
- **The Ore Yard** — stockpiled volcanic glass, sulfur, ore
- **Cruz's Office** — and in the locked cabinet, thirty years of a list she keeps: **the shrine-clearances she could not stop**
- **The Records Room** — where the 1846 patrol reports have sat since Cruz was nineteen

### 5.5 The Commander — Cruz

**Full boss:** colonial_war_bosses.md Tier 1

**The arena:** the shade galleries and the cistern complex — close, hot, and Cruz fights like someone defending a home she does not believe she deserves. Cross-reference imperial_colonies.md Section 5.12: *"she fights for it, well, and loses, and the game does not pretend that is triumphant."*

### 5.6 State Treasure — The 1846 Patrol Reports

Cross-reference npcs/the_named_dead.md Section 3.1, npcs/desert_sage_mbah_seruni.md.

The reports name the patrol, the date, the shrine, the young man who would not leave it, and the officer who gave the order: **Teniente-Coronel Marcos Ibarra.**

They also contain Cruz's own filed statement, written at nineteen, which says less than it should and more than she wanted.

**Design note:** whether Jake shows Mbah Seruni the reports — and whether she wants to read them — is her decision, not his. Cross-reference the_named_dead.md Section 2.3 design rules: no system resolves grief on the player's schedule.

### 5.7 The Three Doors

**The Wall:** breachable, but Cruz's garrison is loyal and competent and will make it cost.

**The Back:** **the cisterns.** Sabotage the water and San Rafael cannot hold a week. Cross-reference physics/sand_desert_physics.md Section 5.3 — this is the desert's own weapon, handed to Jake by people who have always known it.

**The Inside:** the mountain paths. At Trust Tier 3+ with the Mountain Paths network, Alliance fighters simply arrive from directions Imperial maps do not record. Cross-reference indigenous_liberation_alliance.md Section 6.3 — the quietest liberation in the game.

### 5.8 Ownership Potential

**Cross-reference:** imperial_colonies.md Section 5.12 — the liberated San Rafael is **left standing and empty as a marker.** Not demolished, not garrisoned, not rebuilt. A door in the desert that nobody uses.

**Design note:** the Alliance's choice, not Jake's. If Jake insists on holding it, the mountain network's trust suffers.

## 6. CASTILLO DORADO

### 6.1 Quick Reference

**Colony:** Bahía Dorada (imperial_colonies.md Section 6)  
**Type:** Imperial Star Fort (ceremonial — a palace wearing a fort's shape)  
**Commander:** Gobernador Francisco de Silva  
**Garrison:** ~200 beautifully uniformed men who have never fought  
**State Treasure:** the banking vault (largest doubloon reserve in the game) + **de Silva's private ledgers**  
**Difficulty:** the easiest to take, if you bring a purse

### 6.2 The Silhouette

Gorgeous. Pale stone, gilded ironwork, a bastion geometry that is architecturally correct and functionally decorative. Castillo Dorado was designed by a military engineer and finished by an architect, and you can see exactly where the handover happened.

Gardens on the seaward bastion. Actual gardens. On a firing platform.

### 6.3 Layout Identity — "The Fort That Became a Palace"

- The bastions hold reception rooms, not magazines
- The parade ground hosts parties
- The armory is under-stocked and over-decorated
- **The vault, however, is real** — the one part of Castillo Dorado built by someone taking the job seriously

**Cross-reference:** physics/sound_physics.md — the acoustics here are for music, and that is not a metaphor the design needs to explain.

### 6.4 The Districts

- **The Reception Wing** — where the archipelago's business is done over wine
- **The Gardens** — de Silva's genuine pride
- **The Vault** — deep, thick, and the only military-grade construction in the building
- **The Private Study** — where the ledgers are, and where de Silva will absolutely take a meeting if the consideration is right

### 6.5 The Commander — de Silva

**Full boss:** colonial_war_bosses.md Tier 1 (the boss who tries hardest not to fight)

**The arena:** the reception wing and the gardens — and de Silva spends the entire encounter negotiating, fleeing, and offering. His guard captain does the actual fighting. De Silva's "phases" are escalating offers.

### 6.6 State Treasure — The Bribe Ledgers

The vault holds more coin than any other location in Phase 7. **The study holds the thing that matters.**

De Silva records everything, because a man who sells favors must remember what he sold and to whom. Every bribe, every sold garrison rotation, every compromised Imperial officer across the archipelago, in his own precise hand.

**Cross-reference:** npcs/recurring_quest_givers.md Section 2.1 — **Inspector Kessler's multi-chapter corruption investigation terminates here.** This is the artifact his entire arc has been building toward.

**Uses:** hand to Kessler (Reform faction detonation) · leverage over dozens of Imperial officers · sell to Helga Brennan (npcs/imperial_contacts.md Section 10) · or use it yourself, which is exactly what de Silva would do and the game notes the symmetry.

### 6.7 The Three Doors

**The Wall:** the garrison folds. They are not soldiers; they are a very expensive parade.

**The Back:** unnecessary. There is a front door and de Silva will open it.

**The Inside:** **buy it.** Cross-reference imperial_colonies.md Section 6.11 — bribe the gate rotation, buy the vault combination, purchase de Silva's cooperation in his own colony's fall. **The Empire's most corrupt holding taken by the Empire's own methods, without a shot.**

### 6.8 Ownership Potential

The best villa-tier holding in the game if kept intact. Cross-reference castle_ownership.md: as a trade post it is unmatched (Silvera access, banking infrastructure). As anything else it is oversized and absurd.

**Design note:** Castillo Dorado is the one castle where "sell it" should be an option.

## 7. PUESTO DEL NORTE

### 7.1 Quick Reference

**Colony:** Puesto del Norte (imperial_colonies.md Section 7)  
**Type:** Imperial Star Fort (cold-adapted, minimal)  
**Commander:** General Rodrigo Vega  
**Garrison:** ~90, and eleven years tired  
**State Treasure:** modest stores + **Vega's eleven years of unanswered correspondence**  
**Difficulty:** easy to break, hard to justify

### 7.2 The Silhouette

Small, grey, and stubborn. Three bastions, one of which has never been finished — the funding stopped in 1867 and the scaffolding is still there, greyed and frozen, eight years later.

Across the strait, on clear days: **Fort Resolute's flag.** The two fortresses can see each other. Neither has ever fired on the other. Both garrisons watch, daily, through glass.

### 7.3 Layout Identity — "The Fort That Was Never Finished"

- Two functional bastions, one skeletal
- Interior structures built for winter: thick, low, connected
- The wall walk faces the strait, and the sentries all look the same direction
- **Vega's quarters have a window facing Fort Resolute.** He has not moved his desk in eleven years.

### 7.4 The Districts

- **The Finished Bastions** — guns, magazine, and a very good field of fire nobody has used
- **The Skeleton** — the unfinished third bastion, scaffolded and abandoned; the fort's obvious breach point
- **The Winter Quarters** — cramped, warm, and where the garrison actually lives
- **The Correspondence Room** — eleven years of copies, filed with obsessive care

### 7.5 The Commander — Vega

**Full boss:** colonial_war_bosses.md Tier 1

**The arena:** the wall walk and the unfinished bastion, in the cold, cross-reference physics/snow_ice_physics.md. Vega fights on the walls because he will not be found anywhere else. He knows the skeleton is his weakness. He is standing in it.

### 7.6 State Treasure — The Correspondence

Cross-reference imperial_colonies.md Section 7.7, krone_colonies.md Section 7.7.

Eleven years of a general writing to an empire that stopped reading. Requests, then arguments, then reports, then — in the last three years — something closer to a journal. Worthless as loot.

**Paired with Colonel Thorne's dispatch copies from Fort Resolute, the two document sets are the definitive indictment of colonial vanity in the archipelago**, and a player who collects both has assembled something the reform factions of *both* empires would trade heavily for.

**Design note:** collecting both is a Full Vision achievement with real consequences in both Pale Isle and New Coventry. Cross-reference npcs/recurring_quest_givers.md Section 2.1 (Kessler) and krone_colonies.md Section 5.3 (Sterling).

### 7.7 The Three Doors

**The Wall:** the unfinished bastion. It has been the obvious answer for eight years and Vega has requested the funding to close it eleven times.

**The Back:** the supply line. Cut the Northern Run before the freeze and no assault is needed. Cross-reference imperial_colonies.md Section 7.11 — and it is a cold thing to do to men the Empire already abandoned.

**The Inside:** none. Vega's garrison is loyal because he has suffered with them. There is no bribe, no faction, no wedge. **Puesto del Norte is the only Imperial castle with no interior door.**

### 7.8 Ownership Potential

Nearly none, and that is correct. Cross-reference imperial_colonies.md Section 7.12 and krone_colonies.md Section 7.12: in the liberated north, **both forts stand empty across the strait from each other** while the fishing fleets sail between them.

Two abandoned fortresses staring at each other forever is the Northern Front's monument.

## 8. COVENTRY HOUSE

### 8.1 Quick Reference

**Colony:** New Coventry (krone_colonies.md Section 4)  
**Type:** HAC Trade Citadel  
**Commander:** Lord-Director Edmund Blackwood  
**Garrison:** ~150 marines + Company Regulators (elite)  
**State Treasure:** the Company treasury + **THE MASTER LEDGER**  
**Difficulty:** thin walls, hard men, and the highest-value objective in Phase 7

### 8.2 The Silhouette

Not a castle. A **counting house that grew walls.**

Coventry House is four storeys of warehouse and office in dressed brick, wrapped in a curtain wall that was added in 1871 as an afterthought and looks it. The chimneys outnumber the gun positions. The largest structure on the site is the strongroom annexe.

It is eighteen years old and it owns a coastline.

### 8.3 Layout Identity — "The Fortress Made of Filing"

- **Ground floor:** receiving, warehousing, the goods that justify everything
- **First floor:** the counting halls — long rooms, high windows, rows of desks, and clerks who do not look up
- **Second floor:** Company offices, the Regulator station, the Director's rooms
- **Third floor:** archives — copies, duplicates, records of records
- **The Strongroom Annexe:** separate, deep, and the only genuinely fortified structure

**Design note:** cross-reference physics/sound_physics.md Section 4.1 — the counting halls have a specific acoustic: pen scratch, page turn, and nothing else. A stealth sequence through the counting halls at night, with the Regulators patrolling between desks, is a signature Phase 7 set-piece.

### 8.4 The Districts

- **The Counting Halls** — the Company's actual weapon, at work
- **The Regulator Station** — cross-reference retaliation_system.md Section 3.3: **the coordination office for the entire Albion retaliation network.** Burn this and the Regulators stall archipelago-wide.
- **The Director's Rooms** — warm, well-read, tea always available
- **The Strongroom Annexe** — treasury, and the Master Ledger

### 8.5 The Commander — Blackwood

**Full boss:** colonial_war_bosses.md Tier 1 (the pursuit boss)

**The arena:** the whole building, vertically. Cross-reference krone_colonies.md Section 4.3 — Blackwood does not fight. He attempts to leave with the Master Ledger, and the encounter is a chase up through the counting halls and archives while his Regulators fight and he negotiates continuously, all the way to the roof or the dock or wherever it ends.

**He is still explaining when it finishes.**

### 8.6 State Treasure — The Master Ledger

Cross-reference krone_colonies.md Sections 4.7-4.8.

The consolidated debt portfolio for the entire archipelago. Every contract, every obligation, every foreclosure right. Bound in one volume, with summary copies at three removes and the *originals* distributed to the holdings (the Blackwood Landing contracts being the largest single set).

**Three uses, each with distinct consequences:**
- **Take it** — debts become unenforceable in practice; three settlement councils walk free
- **Burn it** — faster, and it destroys the record of what was owed *to* people as well as by them
- **Publish it** — Full Vision: the Company's legal architecture becomes public, and Blackwood's civilizational argument dies in the open air

### 8.7 The Three Doors

**The Wall:** the curtain wall is 1871 and it shows. Coventry House folds faster than any Imperial fort — **but the Regulators are the hardest garrison troops in Phase 7.** Easy walls, expensive rooms.

**The Back:** the night infiltration through the counting halls. The signature stealth run.

**The Inside:** **profitability.** Cross-reference krone_colonies.md Section 3 — drive New Coventry below the threshold and the Company withdraws. The garrison boards transports. Jake walks in. **The only castle in Phase 7 that can be taken by accounting.**

### 8.8 Ownership Potential

**Cross-reference:** castle_ownership.md, krone_colonies.md Section 4.11.

The liberated New Coventry **keeps the city and loses the claims** — the hospital, the school, the aqueduct all stay. Coventry House itself becomes the free port's administration, which is the strangest ownership outcome in Phase 7: **the counting house continues, counting different things.**

## 9. THE STERLING BASTION

### 9.1 Quick Reference

**Colony:** Port Sterling (krone_colonies.md Section 5)  
**Type:** Albion Naval Bastion  
**Commander:** Admiral Katherine Sterling  
**Garrison:** ~200 marines + the squadron (the real defense)  
**State Treasure:** naval stores and payroll + **Sterling's protest correspondence**  
**Difficulty:** the hardest naval problem in Phase 7 — and possibly unnecessary

### 9.2 The Silhouette

Functional, well-maintained, and entirely honest about what it is. Batteries along the harbor mouth, a dry dock, magazines, barracks, and the Admiral's quarters — which are visibly the smallest officer's accommodation in the archipelago.

No decoration. Cross-reference krone_colonies.md Section 5.6: **there is no indentured district at Port Sterling.** The absence is the loudest architectural statement in Phase 7.

### 9.3 Layout Identity — "The Fortress That Is Half a Fleet"

The Bastion and the squadron are one weapon. The harbor batteries cover the anchorage; the anchorage shelters the fleet; the fleet screens the approaches. Remove any leg and the structure fails.

- **The Harbor Batteries** — excellent guns, excellent crews
- **The Dry Dock** — the archipelago's best repair facility
- **The Yard** — shipbuilding, paid skilled labor
- **The Admiral's Quarters** — spartan, and the desk drawer that matters

### 9.4 The Commander — Sterling

**Full boss:** colonial_war_bosses.md Tier 1 — **and the game's most significant potential ally-boss**

**The arena:** the sea. Sterling fights at the head of her squadron, not on a wall. Cross-reference krone_colonies.md Section 5.10 — the hardest naval engagement in Phase 7.

**And it may never happen.** Cross-reference krone_colonies.md Section 5.4: the alliance path, the neutral standoff, or the fight. Three outcomes, and the design should make the fight feel like a failure of imagination even when it is a victory.

### 9.5 State Treasure — The Protest Correspondence

Cross-reference krone_colonies.md Section 5.7.

Sterling's complete written record of protests to the Crown against Company practices. The most credible indictment of the HAC in existence, written by Albion's most respected officer.

**She kept the copies deliberately. She wanted a record to exist.**

**Design note — the crucial mechanic:** this treasure can be **given rather than taken.** On the alliance path Sterling hands it over. *The Company's greatest vulnerability sits in the desk drawer of its own best defender, and she has been waiting for someone to ask.*

### 9.6 The Three Doors

**The Wall:** the batteries are excellent and the marines are professionals, but the Bastion alone is a stone box. **Separate the fleet from the fort.**

**The Back:** the yard workers are paid, skilled, and not oppressed. There is no wedge here. Sterling does not create disaffection.

**The Inside:** **her honour.** Cross-reference krone_colonies.md Section 5.3 — give her proof of Company abuses and she must act. The Master Ledger, the Blackwood Landing contracts, Ashcroft's foreclosure records. **The interior door at Port Sterling opens with evidence.**

### 9.7 Ownership Potential

If taken by force: the archipelago's best naval infrastructure, and Jake will think about how he got it every time he uses the dry dock.

If Sterling allies: not owned, but *available* — and cross-reference the escort-fleet reward tier.

## 10. ASHCROFT HALL

### 10.1 Quick Reference

**Colony:** Blackwood Landing (krone_colonies.md Section 6)  
**Type:** Fortified estate-compound (a manor with a garrison)  
**Commander:** General Percival Ashcroft  
**Garrison:** ~100 marines + the bailiff force  
**State Treasure:** the season's revenue + **THE FOUR THOUSAND CONTRACTS**  
**Difficulty:** moderate militarily, decisive strategically

### 10.2 The Silhouette

A great house on a rise, with outbuildings, a walled yard, and a view of everything it owns. Ashcroft Hall does not look like a fortress because it is not one — it is the administrative center of the richest acre in the archipelago, with enough walls to make a point.

The view from the terrace takes in the fields, the villages, and — on a clear day — the harbor where the export runs load.

### 10.3 Layout Identity — "The Manor That Owns the Horizon"

- **The Hall** — reception, dining, Ashcroft's study; landed-gentry architecture in a colony eighteen years old
- **The Contract Offices** — where obligations are calculated, extended, and enforced
- **The Bailiff Barracks** — armed men with legal warrants
- **The Walled Yard** — where the bonfire happens, if it happens
- **The Strongroom** — deep, dry, climate-controlled: **because paper must be preserved**

**Design note:** the strongroom at Ashcroft Hall is better built than the magazine. Ashcroft understands exactly what his real weapon is.

### 10.4 The Commander — Ashcroft

**Full boss:** colonial_war_bosses.md Tier 1

**The arena:** the Hall's interior — corridors, the study, the terrace. Cross-reference krone_colonies.md Section 6.10: *"He will attempt to litigate, then use his bailiffs, then be genuinely astonished."* The fight moves through rooms designed for dinner parties, and Ashcroft's astonishment is the encounter's emotional note.

### 10.5 State Treasure — The Contracts

Four thousand original signed instruments. Cross-reference krone_colonies.md Sections 6.7-6.8.

**Company law requires the original to enforce.** The New Coventry Master Ledger summarizes; these are the enforceable documents.

**Destroy them and the colony does not fall — it ends.** Four thousand legally-free people standing on land they have worked for fifteen years, and Ashcroft with a manor, some bailiffs, and a legal position that no longer exists.

### 10.6 The Three Doors

**The Wall:** the yard walls are decorative-adequate. The bailiffs fight for wages.

**The Back:** **the strongroom heist.** Cross-reference krone_colonies.md Section 6.8 — infiltrate, take or burn the originals. The best heist content in Phase 7.

**The Inside:** the uprising. Arm the villages, take the Hall, and the bonfire in the walled yard with four thousand people watching. Cross-reference indigenous_liberation_alliance.md Section 6.3.

**And the fourth door, Full Vision:** the legal route. Get the contracts to Sterling, to Kessler, to a Crown court, and argue them void for impossibility. **Slower, bloodless, and it sets a precedent that threatens every HAC holding in the archipelago.**

### 10.7 Ownership Potential

Cross-reference krone_colonies.md Section 6.11 — the liberated Blackwood Landing is **renamed by the villages**, and the Hall becomes something communal. Ashcroft's strongroom stands open and empty as a monument.

**Design note:** the empty strongroom, door ajar, nothing inside. Cross-reference npcs/the_named_dead.md aesthetic — the weight is in the absence.

## 11. FORT RESOLUTE

### 11.1 Quick Reference

**Colony:** Fort Resolute (krone_colonies.md Section 7)  
**Type:** Albion Naval Bastion (modern, small, badly sited)  
**Commander:** Colonel Marcus Thorne  
**Garrison:** ~110, rotating seasonally  
**State Treasure:** modest stores + **Thorne's dispatch copies**  
**Difficulty:** easy — and possibly unnecessary

### 11.2 The Silhouette

Modern, tidy, and wrong for the place. Fort Resolute was designed in New Coventry by engineers who had never seen ice, and built in 1870 to a specification that assumes a temperate coast.

The stoves, however, are excellent. Thorne requisitioned them personally and they are the single best-functioning thing in the fort.

### 11.3 Layout Identity — "The Fort Built by People Who Had Never Been Cold"

- Good batteries, poor thermal design
- Barracks that need constant fuel
- A well-designed magazine sited where the spring melt reaches it
- **The Colonel's office, facing the strait** — with a very good glass, aimed at Puesto del Norte

**Cross-reference:** physics/snow_ice_physics.md — Fort Resolute is an argument the environment wins slowly.

### 11.4 The Districts

- **The Batteries** — modern, well-drilled, aimed at an enemy who has never attacked
- **The Barracks** — warm, and consuming fuel that arrives three transports a season
- **The Factor's Office** — the Company's man, keeping books nobody in New Coventry questions
- **The Dispatch Room** — six years of copies, filed and confident

### 11.5 The Commander — Thorne

**Full boss:** colonial_war_bosses.md Tier 1

**The arena:** the batteries and the wall walk. Thorne fights well — he is genuinely capable — and loses gracefully, and is bewildered throughout. Cross-reference krone_colonies.md Section 7.11: *"he never understood what game he was in."*

**And the Full Vision alternative:** Thorne can be **educated instead of defeated.** Cross-reference krone_colonies.md Section 7.3.

### 11.6 State Treasure — The Dispatches

Six years of confident reports describing a decisive, winnable front.

**Cross-reference:** krone_colonies.md Section 7.9 — **the dispatches ARE the fort's weakness.** They are why New Coventry keeps subsidizing a post at Profitability 12. Take them to New Coventry, expose the gap between the reports and the reality, and the shareholder review triggers immediately.

**The paper power defeated by better paperwork.**

### 11.7 The Three Doors

**The Wall:** modern guns, thin walls, a rotating garrison that has never adapted.

**The Back:** the supply line. One long seasonal run. Cut it in autumn.

**The Inside:** **the dispatches.** Or Thorne himself — show him what eleven years does, take him to see Vega, and let him write the honest report. **The bloodless victory over a young man who was only ever ambitious.**

### 11.8 Ownership Potential

Cross-reference Section 7.8 — in the liberated north, Fort Resolute stands empty across the strait from Puesto del Norte. Two abandoned fortresses, watching each other, with fishing boats passing between.

## 12. THE OLD FORTRESSES

### 12.1 What They Are

**Cross-reference:** indigenous_liberation_alliance.md Sections 5.3-5.4, colonial_war_overview.md Section 8.2

Pre-colonial fortresses built by the archipelago's original peoples. The empires found them, occupied some briefly, and abandoned them all — because they were sited according to principles Imperial engineering did not recognize, and they required knowledge the occupiers did not have.

**They are not ruins. They are waiting.**

### 12.2 Access

**Alliance-exclusive.** Reaching an Old Fortress requires:
1. Trust Tier 3+ with the relevant network
2. An ancient map in that culture's map-language (Section 5.2 of the Alliance doc)
3. Often, a route through the feared waters or jungles
4. Sometimes, a recovered artifact as a key (cross-reference Tito Diego's network)

**An Empire-path player will never see one.**

### 12.3 The Four Known Fortresses

**LA GUARDIA VIEJA (Rexa, deep jungle)**  
Swallowed by canopy. Cross-reference physics/vegetation_physics.md — the vine networks *are* the vertical circulation. Built into and around living trees, and still structurally sound because it was designed to grow.  
**Reclaiming it:** clearing without killing. The Cultural Web's engineers know how.

**THE ASH BASTION (Ashenmoor, high slopes)**  
Half-buried by a 1741 eruption and preserved by it. Cross-reference physics/lava_heat_physics.md, sand_desert_physics.md. Volcanic-glass construction, heat-channeling architecture, cisterns that still work.  
**Reclaiming it:** digging out, and Mbah Seruni's people know exactly which chambers to open and which to leave.

**THE DROWNED HOLD (Fjordlund, coastal)**  
Partly submerged since the sea rose. Cross-reference physics/water_physics.md — underwater sections, tidal access windows, and a great hall that floods twice a day.  
**Reclaiming it:** the tides, and a thousand years of Fjordlund knowing exactly when to walk in.

**THE FOURTH** — unnamed, unplaced, Full Vision. Its existence is rumored across all five networks and confirmed by none. Cross-reference secret_bosses.md.

### 12.4 Reclamation Mechanics

**Cross-reference:** castle_ownership.md

An Old Fortress is not captured — it is **reclaimed**, over time, as a joint project:
- Multi-stage restoration (cross-reference npcs/building_evolution.md, run in reverse from ruin)
- Alliance labor and knowledge; Jake's ship and resources
- Each stage unlocks capability: shelter → garrison → workshop → bastion

### 12.5 What They Become

**Alliance Bastions** (indigenous_liberation_alliance.md Section 5.7) — and the best ones in the game:
- Cannot be sold, converted, or taxed
- Free, loyal, culturally-rooted garrison
- Strong regional LS defense (resists reconquest)
- **Cross-network crafting** — this is where Pak Setyo's volcanic work and Halvor Eriksson's northern steel actually meet (cross-reference indigenous_liberation_alliance.md Section 4.4)
- Host the network gatherings

**And Jake has a room, not a keep.**

### 12.6 The Design Statement

The empires built star forts to hold ground against people. The Old Fortresses were built to hold ground *with* it — sited by tide, canopy, and volcanic flow, requiring knowledge to inhabit rather than force to defend.

Every Old Fortress reclaimed is an argument, made in architecture, about the difference.

## 15. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** Each fortress has an acoustic and a register — the cathedral's reverb, the counting halls' pen-scratch silence, the mead-hall warmth of Fjordlund's winter quarters, the reception wing's music. Cross-reference physics/sound_physics.md.
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** Nine state treasures, and every one of them is a document or an instrument. The guns that face the fields. The unfinished bastion. The strongroom better-built than the magazine. **The architecture is the lore.**
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** Cruz in her shade galleries. Vega on his unfinished wall. The empty punishment block that the design refuses to depict in use. Style D throughout — no fortress falls as a trophy.
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** Gardens on a firing platform. Thorne's excellent stoves. De Silva's escalating offers as boss phases. The 1871 curtain wall that looks exactly as afterthought as it was.
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** The Old Fortresses are the theme's quiet counter-argument, made in stone: the empires built to hold ground *against* people; the originals built to hold it *with* the land. Every reclamation is an answer to a question Jake has been asking with cannons.

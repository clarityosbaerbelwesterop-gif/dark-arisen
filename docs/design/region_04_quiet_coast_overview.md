# REGION 04 — THE QUIET COAST (Overview)

**Estimated Playtime:** 12-16 hours  
**Story Role:** Chapter 7 — The breath before the storm  
**Inspirations:** Skellige Isles (Witcher 3), Iceland's Westfjords, Northern Norway fishing villages, Lofoten archipelago, Bloodborne's Hamlet, the Faroe Islands

## 1. Overview

After the volcanic mythical intensity of Ashenmoor, Jake sails north into cold grey waters. The wind changes. The sun gets thinner. The ocean turns the color of iron. And somewhere on the horizon, the first snow-capped cliffs of the Quiet Coast appear through the fog.

The Quiet Coast is where the Empire ended. Not officially — the Empire still claims these waters on its maps. But practically, the imperial fleet has not patrolled here in forty years. The settlements that remain are the descendants of people who came here to escape something: imperial conscription, religious persecution, the slave trade, or simply the noise of the world. They built stone cottages. They learned to fish in rough seas. They raised sheep on cliff pastures. They buried their dead in the old ways.

Then the Crimson Armada came. Not often — not with full force. Just raids. Just occasionally. Just enough to remind the Quiet Coast that the world had not forgotten them, even if they had tried to forget the world.

Now Jake arrives, and the villagers look at his burning sword and his wounded crew and they think: *"another one. Another man who has come north to finish something."*

**Region Scale:** ~18 km of coastline, 4 main settlements, 3 lighthouses, 1 inland valley. Much smaller than Ashenmoor, but dense with meaning.

## 2. Cultural Identity — The Fjordlund

The people of the Quiet Coast call themselves the **Fjordlund** ("those of the fjord-land"). They are descended from a mix of imperial naval deserters who fled north two centuries ago, indigenous coastal peoples who were here long before the Empire, and the survivors of a failed religious colony that came looking for their God and found only fish.

Their language is a blend — imperial common mixed with older tongues, slowed down, roughened. They speak slowly. They answer questions after long pauses. They laugh rarely but when they do, it is loud and brief and surprising. They are not cold people. They are just people who have decided that words are expensive and should only be spent on things that matter.

### Visual Markers
- Stone cottages with turf roofs and smoking chimneys
- Wooden churches with steeply-pitched roofs, built in the old Stave Church style
- Carved wooden poles (**stavar**) planted outside every doorway — family ancestry records carved in runes down the length of the pole
- Drying racks for fish along every shoreline
- Longboats with hand-carved prows, many featuring animal spirits
- Cairns at significant locations — piles of stones raised by passers-by as a sign of respect to the dead
- Sheep everywhere, grazing on impossible cliffs
- The constant smell of smoke, salt, and wet wool

### The Old Religion
The Fjordlund practice a mix of half-remembered gods and practical ancestor worship. They believe:
- The dead live in the mountain, not in a sky-heaven
- The sea is an old thing with memory — it remembers every name that drowned in it
- Fire must be fed carefully, or it turns on you (a practical belief from centuries of cold)
- Strangers carry luck on their skin — good or bad, you cannot tell until they leave
- The Aurora is the breath of the mountain-dead, rising to greet relatives who still walk the earth

**Practical Gameplay Effect:** Certain Fjordlund NPCs will share information with Jake only after he performs small rituals — leaving a stone on a cairn, pouring mead into a household fire, speaking the names of his dead parents at a stav pole. These rituals cost nothing but time and attention, and are a way of showing the player that the Fjordlund world works on different rules than the colonial world of Rexa.

### The Fjordlund and the Empire
Two hundred years ago, a group of imperial naval officers deserted during a long northern patrol. They had seen what the Empire did in the southern colonies and refused to go home. They sailed their ship north until they ran out of food, then beached it on a coast they had no maps for. They intermarried with the people they found there. Their descendants still use naval terminology for everyday things — "port" and "starboard" instead of left and right, even in their houses. A village bell is still called a "watch-bell." The oldest family names on the coast are imperial names, twisted and shortened over generations.

**This matters for the story.** The Fjordlund know what the Empire is because their ancestors fled it. When Jake arrives, one of the first things any village elder asks him is: *"Are you running from the Empire? Or running toward something?"* Jake's answer determines how much the elder trusts him.

## 3. Settlement Layout

The Quiet Coast has four main settlements plus three lighthouses. Unlike Ashenmoor (which was organized by elevation) or Rexa (which was one big city), the Quiet Coast is organized by **purpose**. Each settlement exists because of a specific reason people came there.

### 3.1 Kaldvik — The Fishing Village
- **Purpose:** Fishing. Pure and simple. The deepest sheltered harbor on the coast.
- **Population:** ~250
- **Role:** Jake's landing point. The first village he sees on the Quiet Coast. This is where the cultural introduction happens.

### 3.2 Graavig — The Trading Post
- **Purpose:** Trade between the inland valley and the coast. Sheep wool, salted fish, iron tools.
- **Population:** ~180
- **Role:** The only place on the Quiet Coast where Jake can buy and sell regularly. Has a proper inn, a proper smith, and a proper marketplace.

### 3.3 Sorgsby — The Mourning Village
- **Purpose:** Originally a regular fishing village. Now, a village of survivors.
- **Population:** ~90 (down from ~400 before the Crimson Armada raid of 1849)
- **Role:** The emotional heart of the Quiet Coast. The village the Crimson Armada came to three years ago and nearly destroyed. Everyone here has lost someone. Everyone here remembers.

### 3.4 Jernholm — The Old Fort Village
- **Purpose:** Built around an abandoned imperial coastal fortification. The villagers live in the shadow of the fort and use its walls for their houses.
- **Population:** ~150
- **Role:** Strategic. Has the best defenses on the coast. Also the most complicated relationship with the Empire, because the fort is still technically imperial property.

### 3.5 The Three Lighthouses
Scattered along the coast, connected to no village. Each manned by a single retired imperial naval officer who came here to hide and forget. Each has a different story about Draven Voss.

- **Old Thornwatch Lighthouse** — the southernmost, manned by Commander Ivar Holt, 73
- **The Widow's Light** — the middle, manned by Commander Astrid Vang, 68
- **Black Stone Lighthouse** — the northernmost, manned by Commander Sigur Brandt, 81

## 4. The Mourning Village — Sorgsby (Central Narrative)

Sorgsby deserves special mention because it is the **narrative center** of Region 04. Everything else in the region is good. Sorgsby is where the player's heart gets broken.

### The Raid of 1849
Three years before Jake arrives, a Crimson Armada raiding party landed at Sorgsby on a grey winter morning. The village had seen raids before — small ones, hit-and-run — but this was different. This was a full assault with three ships and over a hundred men. They were looking for something specific. The villagers never learned what.

The raid lasted eight hours. When it was over, 310 villagers were dead. 90 survived, mostly children and elderly who had hidden in the mountain caves behind the village. The raiders left with three prisoners and whatever cargo they had been looking for. Sorgsby burned for two days.

The 90 survivors rebuilt. They did not leave. They would not leave. This is their place. Their ancestors' bones are in the mountain. Their names are on the stav poles. They rebuilt and they kept living, quieter than before.

### Why This Matters
When Jake arrives in Sorgsby, the village looks wrong in ways he cannot initially describe. Too few houses. Too many empty doorways. Children playing but no shouting. Adults working but no laughing. He walks through the village and NPCs stop their work to watch him pass — not with hostility, just with a kind of tired attention, as if they are wondering whether another man with a sword is about to ruin their day.

**The quest that unlocks in Sorgsby** is called *"The Names in the Mountain"* — and it is the saddest questline in Dark Arisen. Jake is asked to help an old woman named **Mormor Yrsa** record the names of the 310 dead onto a new stav pole she is carving by hand. This requires visiting survivors across the village and asking them to name the people they lost. Each conversation is slow, careful, and devastating.

One old fisherman names his wife, his two children, his brother, and his dog. He says *"the dog mattered to us"* and refuses to explain further.

One widow names her seven-year-old daughter and then says *"I cannot remember what her voice sounded like anymore. Is that forgetting her? Am I forgetting her?"* Jake has no answer.

One man names no one. He just looks at Jake and says *"I was not married. I had no children. I worked alone. The people who knew me are all dead, and there is no one to say my name when I die either. When you write the names on the pole, leave a blank space for me. I would like that."*

### The Reward
At the end of the questline, Mormor Yrsa finishes carving the stav pole. She raises it in the center of the village. All 310 names are on it, plus Jake's own written hand at the bottom of the pole: *"Jake Harlow, stranger from the south, who listened."*

The reward is not gold. It is not a weapon. It is a single item called **The Listener's Pendant** — a small carved wooden charm on a leather cord. It has no stats. It has no mechanical effect. But wearing it unlocks one additional dialogue option in every remaining conversation in the game: Jake can choose to *listen instead of speaking*. Sometimes this gives him information he would not otherwise get. Sometimes it changes an outcome. Sometimes it does nothing at all. The pendant teaches the player that listening is a kind of power.

**This is the quest that most defines the Quiet Coast.** It is slow. It is sad. It has no enemies. And it will be the thing players remember about Region 04 years after they finish the game.

## 5. The Three Lighthouse Veterans (Questline Overview)

The three lighthouse veterans are former imperial officers who served at different points in Draven Voss's naval career. Each knows a different Draven. Together, their stories paint the most complete picture of the man Jake is hunting.

### Quest Structure
The questline is called **"What They Saw"**. It is triggered when Jake first speaks with Commander Ivar Holt at Old Thornwatch Lighthouse (the southernmost lighthouse, which Jake naturally encounters first when arriving from the south). Ivar tells Jake about his years with Draven and mentions that two others who served with him also live on this coast. Jake can then seek them out.

### The Three Stories (Summary)

**Commander Ivar Holt — Old Thornwatch Lighthouse**
- **When he served with Draven:** 1819-1824 (young officers, early career)
- **What he saw:** Draven as a young man. Idealistic. Bookish. Considered a rising star. Ivar was Draven's first commanding officer and taught him how to navigate by stars. He remembers Draven as brilliant but too intense — a young man who took everything personally, including the successes and the failures. *"He cared too much, even then. I warned him. Caring too much in the navy is a wound that takes decades to bleed out."*

**Commander Astrid Vang — The Widow's Light**
- **When she served with Draven:** 1832-1839 (middle career, the years before the village massacre)
- **What she saw:** Draven as a seasoned officer. Married to Cassia. Father to Elin. She attended Elin's fifth birthday party at Draven's home in the capital. She remembers him as the best father she ever saw in a naval officer. *"Most of us treated our families like souvenirs to come home to. Draven treated Cassia and Elin like they were the main thing and the navy was the distraction. I admired him for it. I think it made what came next worse."*

**Commander Sigur Brandt — Black Stone Lighthouse**
- **When he served with Draven:** 1849 (the single year, the year of the village massacre)
- **What he saw:** Draven on the day of the massacre. Sigur was on the same patrol. He carried out similar orders at a different village fifty miles away. Sigur knew what Draven did because he did the same thing. Unlike Draven, Sigur did not desert. He completed his tour, retired with honors, and came to the Quiet Coast to hide from what he did. *"I am not a better man than Draven Voss. I am a worse man, because I stayed. He ran because he had the courage to believe he could become something else. I stayed because I did not. When you find him, Jake Harlow — and I know you will — ask him if it was worth it. I need to know the answer before I die."*

### The Belohnung (Reward for Completing All Three)

When Jake has spoken with all three veterans and gathered their stories, he can return to any of the three lighthouses for the final conversation. The three veterans have never spoken to each other directly (they each came to the coast separately and kept to themselves), but Jake is the one who now carries all three perspectives.

The chosen lighthouse veteran (based on which one Jake returns to) asks Jake to tell them what the others said. Jake recounts the three stories. The veteran listens. At the end, they give Jake an item called **The Officer's Log** — a heavy leather-bound book written across all three veterans' careers, passed from one to the other secretly across decades, recording imperial atrocities the Empire covered up. The book contains evidence of over a hundred imperial crimes, including the original orders that Draven, Astrid, and Sigur all received.

**The Officer's Log has two uses:**

1. **Mechanical:** Bringing it to the final confrontation with Draven Voss unlocks a unique dialogue option during Phase 1 of the Draven boss fight. Jake can say: *"Your officers remember you. All three of them. They have been waiting to hear if it was worth it."* This line triggers a special response from Draven and briefly lowers his defense, giving the player a free damage window. But more importantly, it gives Draven something he has wanted for twenty years: to know that his old comrades are still alive and still thinking about him.

2. **Narrative:** After the game ends, in the epilogue scenes, the Officer's Log is shown being delivered to Elin (Draven's daughter) by an unnamed messenger. She reads it. She cries. She understands her father for the first time. The game never shows whether this changes anything in her life — only that she knows now.

## 6. Story Beats — Chapter 7

### Chapter 7 Structure: "The Breath Before the Storm"

The chapter is deliberately slower than the two that preceded it (Rexa's commanders and Ashenmoor's volcanoes). This is intentional. The player needs to breathe before Chapters 8 and 9.

**Opening:** Jake's ship arrives at Kaldvik in early morning mist. The crew is tired from Ashenmoor. Mira suggests they stay a few days to resupply and recover. Jake agrees — reluctantly, because every day here is a day Ethan is not saved. But his crew needs rest.

**First half of chapter:** Jake explores Kaldvik and meets the Fjordlund for the first time. He experiences the slower culture. He visits Graavig to trade. He hears rumors of a mythical creature in the northern waters. He might visit his first lighthouse if he wanders.

**Middle of chapter:** Jake arrives at Sorgsby. He learns about the Raid of 1849. He meets Mormor Yrsa. He begins *"The Names in the Mountain"* questline. This takes real in-game time and emotional investment.

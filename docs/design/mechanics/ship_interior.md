# SHIP INTERIOR SYSTEM — LA LIBERACIÓN AS MOBILE BASE

**Status:** Design Draft v1.0  
**Phase:** Phase 3 — Mechanics (Subsystem 9 of 10)  
**Tier:** World Tier — Depends on Interaction System  
**Related:** mechanics_overview.md, naval_combat_system.md, mechanics/interaction.md, style_bible.md  
**Purpose:** Define La Liberación's interior spaces — how Jake lives aboard, interacts with crew, rests, plans, stores items, and experiences the ship as home rather than just transport.  
**Inspirations:** Assassin's Creed Black Flag (ship as exploration hub), Mass Effect (Normandy as mobile base), Red Dead Redemption 2 (camp as living space), Sea of Thieves (ship as social space), Ghost of Tsushima (Jin's camp rituals), Skyrim (Hearthfire homes)

---

## 1. SHIP INTERIOR PHILOSOPHY — THE FIVE PRINCIPLES

### 1.1 Principle 1: La Liberación Is Home

Unlike most games where the ship is transport, La Liberación is where Jake LIVES. He sleeps here. He eats here. He has conversations with crew here. He writes in his journal here. He plans strategy here. The ship accumulates Jake's life as it travels. Over 60+ hours of gameplay, it should feel like home.

**Design intent:** When Jake returns to the ship after a long mission ashore, the player should feel "I'm back." That feeling matters.

### 1.2 Principle 2: The Ship Tells Jake's Story

Over time, the ship accumulates evidence of Jake's journey. Trophies from defeated bosses. Gifts from allies. Letters from Margarethe Fitzmueller. Mormor Astrid's tea set in the cabin. Don Mateo's signet ring on the captain's desk. The ship becomes a physical record of everything Jake has done.

**Design intent:** The ship is a memory palace. Returning to it is returning to the accumulated weight of the campaign.

### 1.3 Principle 3: Crew Are People, Not Systems

Mira is not "Navigation NPC." Father Salvio is not "Healing NPC." Big Tom is not "Smith NPC." They are people Jake lives with. They have routines. They have bad days. They have conversations among themselves that Jake can overhear. The crew's presence is constant.

**Design intent:** The player should care about the crew not because the game tells them to, but because they have spent time with them.

### 1.4 Principle 4: The Cabin Is Sacred

Jake's cabin is his most private space. It is where he writes, plans, sleeps, and occasionally weeps alone. The player can customize it over time. Decorate it. Add trophies. It becomes the player's space as much as Jake's.

**Design intent:** The cabin is the single space in Dark Arisen that belongs entirely to the player-Jake hybrid entity.

### 1.5 Principle 5: The Ship Responds to Jake's Choices

Jake's decisions throughout the game affect the ship's atmosphere. After killing Havfrue Modor, the crew is quieter. After a major victory, they celebrate. After losing a crew member, there is mourning. The ship's mood is dynamic, not scripted.

**Design intent:** The player's choices have emotional weight that extends beyond their immediate consequences.

## 2. SHIP LAYOUT — THE FOUR DECKS

### 2.1 Upper Deck (Weather Deck)

The open-air deck where most of the crew works during the day.

**Areas:**

**The Helm (Stern):**
- Ship's wheel
- Navigation tools laid out
- Mira's station when at sea
- Where Jake steers the ship in naval combat
- View of the ocean in all directions

**The Main Deck (Center):**
- Work area for crew
- Rope coils, barrels, crates
- Where boarding combat occurs
- Cannons along both sides

**The Foredeck (Bow):**
- Lookout position
- Big Tom's work area sometimes
- Figurehead visible from above
- Good spot for conversation

**The Crow's Nest:**
- Accessed via rigging climb
- Highest point on the ship
- Spyglass available
- Jake can spend time here alone

### 2.2 Main Deck Interior (Under Weather Deck)

**Captain's Cabin (Stern, port side):**
- Jake's private quarters (detailed in Section 3)
- Large, wood-paneled
- Bed, desk, map table, personal items
- Locked to non-crew

**Officer's Mess (Stern, starboard):**
- Where Jake and senior crew eat
- Long table seats 8
- Where formal discussions happen
- Coffee/tea always brewing

**Navigation Room (Center, port):**
- Maps and charts
- Esteban's domain
- Route planning happens here
- Astrolabes, sextants on display

**Armory (Center, starboard):**
- Weapon storage
- Boarding supplies
- Ammunition reserves
- Big Tom's adjacent workspace

### 2.3 Lower Deck (Crew Level)

**Crew Quarters (Bow):**
- Hammocks for 12 crew
- Personal chests for each
- Dim, communal living
- Where crew relaxes off-duty

**Galley (Center):**
- Ship's kitchen
- Where food is prepared
- Ramón (cook NPC) works here
- Always smells of something

**Wardroom (Stern):**
- Senior crew's dining area
- Separate from officers
- Card games happen here
- Big Tom, Father Salvio, Ines, Mira use this

**Chapel (Port, small):**
- Father Salvio's domain
- Small altar, candles
- Where crew can pray
- Blessed items crafted here

### 2.4 Cargo Hold (Bottom)

**Main Cargo (Large):**
- Bulk storage
- Food, water, supplies
- Trade goods
- Loot stored here

**Weapons Stores (Section):**
- Gunpowder magazine (isolated for safety)
- Cannonball stores
- Spare weapons

**Workshop Area:**
- Big Tom's forge
- Ines's alchemy station
- Father Salvio's ritual workspace
- Active crafting happens here

## 3. THE CAPTAIN'S CABIN

### 3.1 The Sacred Space

Jake's cabin is the heart of the ship. It is where the player spends significant time between missions.

**Physical Layout:**
- **Size:** 6m x 4m, larger than most Age of Sail captain's cabins
- **Bed:** Against the stern, beneath large paned windows
- **Desk:** Substantial wooden desk with brass fittings, facing the port wall
- **Map Table:** Center of room, covered in charts
- **Bookshelf:** Against starboard wall, growing over time with acquired books
- **Wardrobe:** Jake's clothing and armor storage
- **Display Shelves:** For trophies, gifts, and mementoes

### 3.2 The Desk

Jake's desk contains:
- His personal journal (interactive — writes automatic entries)
- Unfinished letters
- Current quest items needing decisions
- Father's old compass (personal heirloom)
- Writing materials

**Interactions:**
- **Write in journal:** Reflect on recent events
- **Read letters:** From allies, enemies, or finding
- **Review plans:** Strategic decisions for next destination
- **Sit at desk:** Jake can sit and look at the sea, ambient moment

### 3.3 The Map Table

The central feature of the cabin. Used for:

**Route Planning:**
- Visual map of traveled regions
- Markers for visited locations
- Quest destinations highlighted
- Discovered secrets noted

**Strategic Planning:**
- Naval combat tactical overview
- Enemy fleet positions
- Ally locations

**Memory:**
- Pins and notes accumulate over time
- Jake's journey visible at a glance
- Past routes shown in faded ink

### 3.4 The Display Shelves

As Jake progresses, trophies and gifts accumulate:

**From Bosses:**
- Mateo's signet ring (Chapter 4)
- Halvard's naval compass (Chapter 7)
- Havfrue's scale (Chapter 7, Peace Route)
- Fitzmueller's pocket watch (Chapter 8, Boarding)
- Corazón's matched sabers (Chapter 8)
- Twin sisters' letters (Chapter 8)

**From Allies:**
- Mormor Astrid's tea set (after friendship milestone)
- Mbah Seruni's Ashen Wyrm tooth (from Peace)
- Various crew gifts

**From Journey:**
- Regional items collected
- Meaningful lore objects
- Personal mementos

**Design Intent:** The cabin physically represents Jake's journey. A player in Chapter 2 has an empty cabin. A player in Chapter 10 has a cabin full of the weight of what they have done.

### 3.5 Cabin Activities

**Sleep:**
- Rest in bed (Cross/A button)
- Saves game, advances time
- Cabin during sleep: crew continues their lives
- Dreams possible at specific story moments

**Write:**
- Journal writing animation
- Specific entries at key story moments
- Player can read back previous entries

**Plan:**
- Use map table for strategic decisions
- Select next destination
- Review mission objectives

**Reflect:**
- Sit at desk, look out window
- Ambient moment, no mechanical reward
- For players who want to be with Jake in quiet

**Customize:**
- Rearrange trophies
- Choose which items displayed
- Personal expression

## 4. CREW AREAS AND INTERACTIONS

### 4.1 The Helm (Day)

**Mira's Station:**
- She is here during sea travel
- Jake can approach for conversations
- Navigation talk, reading ocean
- Mira teaches Jake her sailor traditions

**Conversations Available:**
- Current route and conditions
- Next destination planning
- Personal topics (her family, her dreams)
- Reflection on crew dynamics

### 4.2 The Galley (Mealtimes)

**Ramón the Cook:**
- Short, heavyset, always complaining
- Makes surprisingly good food
- Keeps secrets of everyone's food preferences
- Background character with depth

**Meal Times:**
- Morning (dawn)
- Midday
- Evening (sunset)
- Jake can eat with crew
- Skip meals = crew morale affected

**Conversations:**
- Daily ship gossip
- Crew disputes (often funny)
- Regional food memories
- Recipe exchange

### 4.3 The Wardroom (Evenings)

**Evening Gatherings:**
- Senior crew relaxes after duties
- Card games (Jake can join)
- Storytelling
- Rum sharing

**Big Tom:**
- Dominates conversations
- War stories from his past
- Tobacco smoking
- Deep friendship develops

**Father Salvio:**
- Quiet presence
- Philosophical conversations
- Offers counsel
- Religious discussions if desired

**Ines:**
- Sharp wit
- Alchemy talks
- Past experiences
- Hints about her pre-Armada life

**Esteban:**
- Navigation lore
- Maritime traditions
- Old pirate tales
- Wisdom moments

### 4.4 The Chapel (Father Salvio)

**Available Activities:**
- Confession (Jake can unburden)
- Blessings on items
- Funeral services (for dead crew)
- Philosophical discussions

**Confession Mechanics:**
- Jake can confess specific actions
- Father Salvio provides perspective
- Not traditional gameplay rewards
- Emotional weight for player

### 4.5 The Workshop (Crafting)

**Big Tom's Forge:**
- Main weapon crafting
- Sound of hammer always present
- Tom welcomes help or conversation
- Commissioned work

**Ines's Alchemy Corner:**
- Potion brewing
- Herb drying racks
- Chemical warnings posted
- Dangerous but controlled

**Father Salvio's Ritual Space:**
- Blessed items preparation
- Rarely occupied (he blesses when needed)
- Subtle religious atmosphere

## 5. CREW ROUTINES AND SCHEDULES

### 5.1 Daily Schedule

Each crew member has a daily routine that creates the ship's living rhythm:

**Big Tom:**
- Dawn: Forge work begins
- Late morning: Quick break, coffee
- Afternoon: Forge work
- Evening: Wardroom socializing
- Night: Sleep in quarters

**Ines:**
- Dawn: Herb garden check
- Morning: Alchemy work
- Afternoon: Research and writing
- Evening: Wardroom
- Night: Reading in quarters

**Father Salvio:**
- Dawn prayers
- Morning: Chapel maintenance
- Afternoon: Crew counseling
- Evening prayers
- Night: Study

**Mira:**
- Constant helm presence during travel
- Breaks for meals
- Evening: Quiet time with maps
- Sleep in officer's quarters

**Esteban:**
- Dawn: Sunrise navigation check
- Morning: Chart work
- Afternoon: Observations and updates
- Evening: Wardroom (briefly)
- Night: Stargazing when clear

### 5.2 Weather Responses

Crew behavior changes with weather:

**Clear Weather:**
- Everyone on deck frequently
- Good mood, jokes
- Productive work

**Rain:**
- Crew below decks more
- Quieter, contemplative
- Conversations about past rains

**Storm:**
- All hands on deck
- Tense focus
- Jake's leadership tested

**Becalmed:**
- Frustration grows
- Crew invents activities
- Morale slowly drops

### 5.3 Crew Interactions With Each Other

Jake can overhear crew conversations:

**Big Tom and Father Salvio:**
- Philosophical debates
- Teasing about Salvio's piety
- Shared war memories (they knew each other before)

**Ines and Mira:**
- Discuss navigation and medicine
- Professional respect
- Occasional rivalry

**Esteban with everyone:**
- Grandfather figure to all
- Stories and wisdom
- Beloved by entire crew

### 5.4 Crew Response to Jake

As relationships build, crew respond to Jake specifically:

**Early Game:**
- Professional respect
- Formal address ("Captain")
- Duty-focused interactions

**Mid Game:**
- Warmer greetings
- Share personal stories
- Offer advice unsolicited

**Late Game:**
- Family atmosphere
- Jake becomes center of crew life
- Deep loyalty evident

## 6. SHIP MOOD SYSTEM

### 6.1 Mood Factors

The ship's overall mood is affected by:

**Recent Events:**
- Victories boost morale
- Losses depress it
- Difficult moral choices create tension

**Crew Morale:**
- Individual moods aggregate
- Low morale = quieter ship
- High morale = lively environment

**Environmental:**
- Weather affects atmosphere
- Time of day changes mood
- Port visits refresh spirits

**Jake's Choices:**
- Merciful actions: crew approves
- Brutal actions: some approve, some disturbed
- Consistent behavior shapes culture

### 6.2 Mood Expressions

How mood manifests:

**High Morale:**
- Singing in galley
- Card games frequent
- Laughter common
- Decoration of ship

**Normal Morale:**
- Routine behavior
- Standard interactions
- Professional atmosphere

**Low Morale:**
- Quiet ship
- Fewer conversations
- Crew keeps to themselves
- Visible signs of depression

**Post-Tragedy:**
- Mourning atmosphere
- Specific death acknowledgments
- Black banner flown
- Subdued activities

### 6.3 Mood Recovery

Ways to restore morale:

**Port Visits:**
- Crew can disembark
- Entertainment, food, rest
- Return refreshed

**Celebration:**
- Special meal organized
- Rum distribution
- Jake's toast/speech

**Personal Attention:**
- Talk to each crew member
- Address specific concerns
- Show leadership

**Time:**
- Days passing naturally heal
- Not instant but inevitable

## 7. THE JOURNAL SYSTEM

### 7.1 Journal Mechanics

Jake's journal is a persistent document that grows throughout the game:

**Automatic Entries:**
- After major events
- When entering new regions
- After significant character deaths
- Written in Jake's voice, reflecting his mindset

**Content Types:**
- Narrative summaries
- Personal reflections
- Sketches (provided visually)
- Maps and diagrams

### 7.2 Journal Access

**In Cabin:**
- Full reading experience
- Browse chronologically
- Re-read any entry
- Skip ahead with table of contents

**On the Go:**
- Quick-glance summary
- Recent entries only
- Voice-over option during gameplay

### 7.3 Journal as Memory

**Player Uses:**
- Remember earlier choices
- Track relationship progression
- Verify mission details
- Emotional touchstone

**Story Integration:**
- Some characters can read Jake's journal
- Reveals character depth
- Creates intimate connections
- Potentially discovered by enemies

## 9. SHIP AS SAFE SPACE

### 9.1 No Combat On Ship (Usually)

Within La Liberación:
- No enemies appear
- Safe space for resting
- Crafting without pressure
- Conversation without interruption

**Exceptions:**
- Boarding actions (Jake's ship boarded)
- Specific story events
- Betrayal moments (late game)

### 9.2 Rest and Recovery

**Full Rest:**
- Sleep in cabin
- Restores HP completely
- Restores stamina fully
- Advances time to chosen period

**Quick Rest:**
- Sit in crew quarters
- Partial recovery
- Chat with crew
- No time advance

**Meditation:**
- Practice Rache focus
- Chapter 9+ only
- Mental preparation

### 9.3 The Return Home Feeling

When Jake returns to La Liberación after long missions:

**Audio Cues:**
- Familiar sounds (crew voices)
- Ship creaking
- Waves against hull
- Musical theme (subtle)

**Visual Cues:**
- Warm lighting
- Familiar faces
- Jake's posture relaxes
- Everything feels safe

**Mechanical Benefits:**
- Health regeneration
- Stamina refill
- Crew greetings
- Access to crafting

## 16. SHIP INTERIOR FEEL TESTS

Before the ship interior is final, it must pass these tests:

### 16.1 The Mass Effect Test
> *"Does the ship feel like the Normandy? A home that you return to, with characters you care about?"*

### 16.2 The RDR2 Camp Test
> *"Does the ship feel lived-in? Do crew routines make it feel real?"*

### 16.3 The Emotional Attachment Test
> *"Do players miss the ship when they leave it? Do they enjoy returning?"*

### 16.4 The Character Depth Test
> *"Do crew members feel like real people with their own stories and personalities?"*

### 16.5 The Customization Test
> *"Does the ship reflect the player's specific journey? Does it become theirs?"*

All five must pass.

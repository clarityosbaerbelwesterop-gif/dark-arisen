# DARK ARISEN — LORE OBJECT CATALOG

**Status:** v1.0 — Living Document  
**Style Bible Compliance:** Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects), Pillar 5 (Theme: Revenge)  
**Purpose:** This catalog is the lore of Dark Arisen. The world's history is not told. It is found, picked up, read, and pieced together by the player. Each entry below is a physical object or specific encounter the player can interact with.

---

## HOW TO USE THIS CATALOG

Each lore object follows a strict template:

1. **What It Is** — Physical description, what the player sees
2. **Where Jake Finds It** — Location, story trigger
3. **What Is Said About It** — NPC dialogue (in correct register)
4. **What It Truly Is** — Hidden truth (NEVER shown to player directly)
5. **Connections** — Other objects/events it ties to
6. **Theme Connection** — How it serves "Revenge and What It Takes From You"

If an entry cannot fill all six fields, it is not strong enough to exist. Cut it.

## OBJECT #001 — THE GLOWING BONE

### What It Is
A bone, roughly 40 centimeters long, thick as a man's forearm. Cream-colored, polished smooth by age. There is a clean break across the middle. From the break, a faint warm light seeps out — not bright, just enough to see in a dark room. The bone is always warm to the touch, even on the coldest nights. It does not weigh what it should. Old Bones keeps it on a wooden shelf above his fire pit, wrapped in a strip of dark cloth.

### Where Jake Finds It
Jake first sees it during his first conversation with Old Bones in his hut deep in the Moran jungle. Old Bones takes it down from the shelf without ceremony and sets it on the table between them while they talk. Jake can interact with it, pick it up, examine it. Old Bones lets him.

### What Is Said About It
*Old Bones, in his Middle Register voice — quiet, considered, the voice of a man who has lived too long:*

> "My grandfather found it. In the cave where the river goes into the mountain. He had it on his shelf for thirty years and it never stopped warming. My father had it for twenty more. I have had it for forty. I think when I die, someone else will have it. The bone does not care."

If Jake asks what animal it came from, Old Bones shrugs.

> "I have asked travelers. None of them have seen anything that this would fit. Some say it is from the sea. Some say from the sky. One man said it was from a god. I think they were all guessing."

If Jake tries to take it, Old Bones does not stop him — but the bone goes cold in Jake's hand and stays cold. It only warms for the person who has earned it. Jake has not earned it yet.

### What It Truly Is (Hidden from Player)
A finger bone from a juvenile dragon related to the Ashen Wyrm. The dragon was killed by an imperial dragonhunter expedition approximately 280 years ago — one of the last 891 dragons systematically eliminated by the Empire's Specimen Recovery Program. The warmth is the last echo of dragon-fire, slowly burning out. In another 200 years, the bone will be cold and dead. Old Bones has no idea what he holds.

### Connections
- **Object #047** — Dragon Skull in the Cave Where the River Goes Into the Mountain (Ashenmoor, optional discovery in The Ashen Depths)
- **Object #112** — The Specimen Recovery Ledger (Imperial Outpost in The Mire, Rexa)
- **Boss Encounter** — The Ashen Wyrm (Region 3, Chapter 7) — when Jake mentions Old Bones during the dragon's dialogue, the Wyrm pauses and says: *"A man on a small island, keeping the bone of one of my children. He does not know. Of course he does not know."* This is the only place in the game where the connection is even hinted at, and only if the player has interacted with the bone before fighting the dragon.

### Theme Connection
The bone is the first quiet introduction to Dark Arisen's central question. Old Bones inherited grief he didn't earn. Jake inherits revenge he didn't choose. Both carry weight from the past that warms them and burdens them at the same time. The bone teaches the player, without ever saying it, that some things are passed down through generations whether we want them or not.

## OBJECT #002 — THE WAX-SEALED LETTER

### What It Is
A folded letter, weathered by salt water but somehow still readable. The wax seal is the Harlow family crest — a simple anchor crossed with a feather quill. The letter is written in Elena Harlow's handwriting (Jake's mother). It was tucked inside a small wooden box that floated free from the sinking ship and washed ashore on the Shattered Coast in Moran.

### Where Jake Finds It
Jake finds the box on the beach during his first 30 minutes of gameplay, very close to where he washes ashore. It is the second interactable object in the game (after his broken cutlass). The player MUST pick it up to progress — it is in the path Jake walks.

### What Is Said About It
There is no NPC to explain this object. Jake reads it himself. When Jake opens the box and unfolds the letter, the screen darkens slightly. Elena's voice plays in voiceover (Low Register, warm, intimate — the voice of a mother writing to herself):

> "My storm. I am writing this and not sending it. You will never read this. But I want to say it anyway, because I am afraid I will forget how to feel it.
>
> You are seventeen. You sit at the back of the ship every night and look at the stars. Your father thinks you are lazy. Your brother thinks you are slow. They are both wrong. You see things they do not see. I have always known this.
>
> One day you will not be the quiet one anymore. One day something will wake in you. I am writing this so that when it does, you will know I saw you first. I always saw you.
>
> — Mama"

The voiceover ends. The letter remains in Jake's inventory permanently. The player can re-read it at any rest point.

### What It Truly Is (Hidden from Player)
Elena wrote this letter three weeks before the pirate attack. She had a feeling. Mothers sometimes do. She did not know who would attack or when, but she knew her quiet son would have to become something else, and she wanted him to have one piece of her voice for that journey. She never told Thomas. She never told Ethan. The letter was meant for Jake alone, eventually.

### Connections
- **Object #003** — Thomas Harlow's Pocket Compass (also from the wreckage, found in Moran's Mangrove Shallows)
- **Object #019** — Ethan's Hidden Journal (found in Chapter 9 on Draven's flagship — reveals Ethan also remembered Elena's nickname "my storm" but never told Jake)
- **Story Trigger** — In Chapter 9, when Ethan calls Jake "my little storm" with his dying words, the player who has read this letter understands the full weight of it. Players who skipped or forgot the letter will hear the line and feel something is significant without knowing why. **This is intentional.** The catalog rewards attentive players without punishing casual ones.

### Theme Connection
This is the only object in Dark Arisen that has nothing to do with revenge. It is about love, remembered. Its existence is a quiet rebuke to everything Jake will become. Every time he reads it again at a rest point, the player should feel the gap between who Elena saw and who Jake is becoming. The letter does not change. Jake does. That is the theme made physical.

## OBJECT #003 — DON MATEO'S POCKET WATCH

### What It Is
A silver pocket watch on a delicate chain, slightly tarnished. The face is white enamel with elegant black numerals. The hands no longer move — they are frozen at 11:47. On the back, engraved in tiny cursive: *"Para mi querido hijo, en su décimo cumpleaños. — Papá, 1791."* (For my dear son, on his tenth birthday. — Papa, 1791.) The watch is heavier than it looks. It clicks softly when shaken, as if something inside is loose.

### Where Jake Finds It
Jake takes it from Don Mateo Salazar's body after defeating him in The Slaughterhouse (Rexa, Chapter 4 climax). It is one of the boss drops, but unlike the Butcher's Blade or the Aristocrat's Tincture recipe, the watch is easy to miss — it is in Salazar's vest pocket, requiring the player to actually loot the body rather than just walk away.

### What Is Said About It
Salazar himself, mid-fight in Phase 2, taunts Jake while consulting the watch:

> *"Forgive me, I am tracking the time. I have a dinner appointment at nine. You are taking longer than I expected — but not so long that I will be rude to my host."*

After his death, no NPC ever directly explains the watch. But if Jake brings it to specific NPCs throughout the game, they react:

- **Esteban "El Viejo" (Quartermaster, recruited in Rexa)**, in Low Register: *"That belongs to a dead man. I knew him when he was a boy. His father loved him. The world ate him anyway."*

- **Father Salvio (Crew Surgeon, recruited in Rexa)**, in Middle Register: *"I baptized that boy. Do you know that? I held him over the font and named him Mateo and said the holy words. I should have known. I should have seen something. There must have been a sign."*

- **An unnamed old woman in Tanjung Hitam (Ashenmoor)**, in Low Register, refusing to touch the watch: *"Take it away. Please. I knew his mother. Take it away from my house."*

### What It Truly Is (Hidden from Player)
The watch was a gift from Don Mateo's father on his tenth birthday — when Mateo was still a child capable of being loved. The watch stopped at 11:47 on the night Mateo's father died, when Mateo was twelve. He never repaired it. The "loose thing" inside the watch is a small folded photograph of his father holding him as a baby. Mateo carried it for forty years. He killed children for forty years. Both things are true.

### Connections
- **Story Flag** — Carrying this watch through the game unlocks unique dialogue with three NPCs (Esteban, Father Salvio, the woman in Tanjung Hitam)
- **Object #054** — Don Mateo's Childhood Diary (hidden in his estate vault, optional discovery during the Slaughterhouse dungeon)
- **Final Game Lore Reveal** — If Jake gives the watch to Father Salvio in Chapter 8 or later, Salvio prays over it and breaks it open, revealing the photograph. Salvio weeps. Jake watches. Nothing more is said. This is one of Dark Arisen's quietest, most devastating moments — the realization that monsters are made, and what is made can be mourned.

### Theme Connection
This object is the heart of the catalog's connection to revenge. Jake killed Don Mateo in righteous fury. Don Mateo deserved death — there is no doubt of this. But the watch in Jake's pocket reminds the player, every time they see it in the inventory, that the man Jake killed was once a child who was loved. The watch does not excuse Mateo. It does not lessen his crimes. It only makes Jake's victory smaller. Quieter. More complicated.

This is what revenge takes from Jake: the ability to feel pure about his violence.

## OBJECT #004 — THE SPECIMEN RECOVERY LEDGER

### What It Is
A leather-bound ledger, water-damaged but mostly legible. The cover bears the seal of the Imperial Naval Specimen Recovery Program — an official-looking insignia of a fish, a bird, and a dragon arranged around a sextant. Inside, page after page of meticulous accounting in Imperial Common: dates, locations, "specimen types," "fragment recoveries," numerical totals. The handwriting changes across decades, suggesting many different scribes over many years. The earliest entries are from 1554. The latest are from 1849 — three years before the game begins.

### Where Jake Finds It
Inside the Old Spanish Fort dungeon in The Mire, Rexa. The ledger is hidden in a sealed strongbox in the fort commander's quarters, behind a portrait of an imperial admiral. The strongbox key must be looted from Brother Cleaver (Mire Commander) earlier in the dungeon, OR the box can be broken open with enough force (consumes a Heavy weapon's durability significantly).

### What Is Said About It
There is no NPC explanation for this object. Jake reads it himself. The ledger contains hundreds of entries. Most are terse and bureaucratic. A few stand out for any player who pays attention:

> *"Entry 4471. Specimen type: Adult Wyrm, female. Location: Volcanic chain, southern archipelago. Status: Confirmed kill. Recovery: Heart shard (1), scale fragments (12), tooth (1). Note: Specimen sang during dispatch. First recorded vocalization. Investigate language."*

> *"Entry 4502. Specimen type: Juvenile Wyrm, gender unknown. Location: River cave, jungle island designated 'Moran' on local maps. Status: Confirmed kill. Recovery: Skull (1), bone fragments (47). Note: One fragment lost in retrieval, presumed taken by local fisherman. Pursuit deemed unnecessary."*

> *"Entry 4891. Final entry. Program suspended by order of Admiral V. — Specimen recoveries no longer cost-efficient. Estimated remaining population: less than 12. Officer Voss notes: 'I have hunted enough. They are people. We have always known this. I cannot continue.' — Promotion withheld pending review."*

That last entry. The signature at the bottom: **Lieutenant Commander Jeremiah Voss.**

### What It Truly Is (Hidden from Player, but findable through reading)
The ledger is the institutional history of the genocide of the dragons. The Empire systematically hunted intelligent dragon-kind for nearly 300 years, framing it as "specimen recovery" in their official records. The program ended not because dragons were extinct, but because **Draven Voss — then still Jeremiah Voss — refused to continue.** This was years before the village massacre that finally broke him entirely. This was the FIRST time he questioned the empire. This was the seed of everything.

The "fisherman" in entry 4502 was Old Bones' great-grandfather. The "lost fragment" is the glowing bone Jake first saw in Old Bones' hut.

### Connections
- **Object #001** — The Glowing Bone (entry 4502 directly references its origin)
- **Object #047** — Dragon Skull in the Cave (the same cave referenced in entry 4502)
- **Boss Encounter** — The Ashen Wyrm (entry 4471 is the killing of one of the Wyrm's "children" she mentions during her boss dialogue)
- **Character: Draven Voss** — This ledger is the first concrete evidence in the game of Draven's pre-pirate life and his original moral break. It recontextualizes the entire antagonist.
- **Story Flag** — Reading the ledger before fighting Draven in the finale unlocks unique dialogue between Jake and Draven about "the program."

### Theme Connection
The ledger is the largest and most quiet revelation of the theme. Every dragon in this book was killed by men who were taking revenge — revenge against creatures they had been told were monsters, revenge against threats they had invented, revenge against the wild for daring to exist. The Empire built itself on revenge against anything it could not control. And one man — Draven Voss — finally refused to continue, only to spend the rest of his life taking new revenge in new directions.

When Jake reads this ledger, he is reading his own future. If he is not careful.

## OBJECT #005 — MBAH SERUNI'S SONG ("THE DRAGON'S LULLABY")

### What It Is
This is not a physical object. It is a melody. A short song, six lines long, sung in Bahasa Gunung. Mbah Seruni — the elder grandmother of Desa Padi in Ashenmoor — knows it. She is one of the last three people alive who can still sing it correctly. The song has no written form; it must be heard and remembered.

When Jake learns the song, it appears in his journal as both the original Bahasa Gunung text and a translation:

> *Tidur, tidur, anak api  
> Bumi mengingat namamu  
> Saat dunia melupakan,  
> Aku akan bernyanyi  
> Sampai engkau pulang  
> Kepada saya.*

> *Sleep, sleep, child of fire  
> The earth remembers your name  
> When the world forgets,  
> I will sing  
> Until you come home  
> To me.*

### Where Jake Learns It
In Desa Padi (Ashenmoor, Chapter 6), as part of the side quest "The Dragon's Lullaby." Mbah Seruni teaches Jake the song over the course of three meetings. Each meeting requires Jake to bring her a specific offering: a coconut from Tanjung Hitam, a feather from the Stormspire path, and a piece of obsidian from Kota Api. On the third visit, she sings the song to him in her cracked old voice while he sits cross-legged on her floor.

### What Is Said About It
*Mbah Seruni, in High Register — the only Low-Register character in Dark Arisen who shifts to High Register, because she is singing something older than herself:*

> "This song was not made by my people. It was given to my people. The first ones learned it from the dragon herself. She sang it to her own children, before there were people to hear. When she had no more children, she gave it to us, so the song would not die.
>
> Now I am old. I will die soon. There are two others who know it correctly, and both are older than me. After us, the song will be wrong, or the song will be silent. So I am giving it to you. A stranger from the sea. I do not know why I am giving it to you. But I dreamed of it last night, and the dream said to give it. So I am giving it."

### What It Truly Is (Hidden from Player)
The song is, literally, the lullaby the Ashen Wyrm sang to her dragon children before they were all hunted to death by the Imperial Specimen Recovery Program. She gave the song to the first Anak Gunung when she had no more children to sing to. It has been passed down for nearly 300 years among a handful of village elders. The song is the only piece of dragon culture that has survived in any form.

### Connections
- **Boss Encounter** — The Ashen Wyrm (Chapter 7). If Jake has learned the song, he can sing it during Phase 2 of the dragon fight. The Wyrm freezes mid-attack. She lowers her head. She is utterly silent for ten seconds — the longest silence in any boss fight in the game. Then she says, in Pre-Human Language (subtitled): *"You. Where. Where did you find that."* When Jake answers, "An old woman in a rice village taught me," the Wyrm closes her eyes and weeps molten gold for the first time in 300 years. The fight then continues — but Phase 3's difficulty is reduced significantly, because the Wyrm cannot bring herself to fight at full strength against someone who knows her children's lullaby.
- **Object #001** — The Glowing Bone (the bone is from one of the children the lullaby was sung for)
- **Object #004** — The Specimen Recovery Ledger (entry 4471 mentions a dragon "singing during dispatch" — that singing was this lullaby)

### Theme Connection
The song is the opposite of revenge. It is a mother's love that survived three centuries of attempted erasure. When Jake sings it to the Ashen Wyrm in her chamber, he is — for one moment — choosing tenderness over violence. The dragon does not stop fighting (she is too tired and too old). But Jake has shown her, and shown himself, that he is capable of something other than the hunt. That moment matters more than any sword swing in the entire game.

If the player kills the Ashen Wyrm WITHOUT learning the lullaby, the dragon dies a stranger. If the player kills her AFTER singing the song, she dies known. The mechanical difference is small (slightly easier Phase 3). The emotional difference is enormous.

This is the catalog's argument: **the small things are the real story.**

## FINAL NOTE

Every object in this catalog must serve the theme or be cut. There is no "world-building filler." If we cannot answer "how does this object speak to revenge and what it takes from Jake?" then the object does not deserve a place in Dark Arisen.

The catalog is the lore. The catalog is the story behind the story. The catalog is what separates Dark Arisen from a generic pirate game with a coat of paint.

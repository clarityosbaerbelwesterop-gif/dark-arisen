# REGION 04 — THE QUIET COAST: THE THREE LIGHTHOUSES

**Status:** Design Draft v1.0  
**Related:** region_04_quiet_coast_overview.md, region_04_quiet_coast_villages.md, characters/draven_voss.md, style_bible.md  
**Purpose:** Detailed breakdown of the three lighthouses on the Quiet Coast, the three retired imperial officers who man them, and "What They Saw" — the questline that recontextualizes Draven Voss before the final confrontation in Chapter 9.

---

## 1. CONCEPT — THREE MEN WHO KNEW DRAVEN

The three lighthouse keepers are the most important NPCs in Region 04 for the player's understanding of the game's final antagonist. Each of them served with Draven Voss at a different point in his imperial naval career. Together, their stories form a complete portrait of who Draven was before he became the man Jake is hunting.

This is Style D lore at its most potent: the player learns the truth about their target not through exposition, but through three separate conversations with three separate old men who loved, respected, and failed him in different ways.

**Design principle:** The three lighthouses are optional content. Players who skip them will still understand Draven from his own words in Chapter 9. Players who visit all three will understand him COMPLETELY — and this completeness changes the emotional weight of the final boss fight. The more Jake knows about Draven, the harder it becomes to kill him with a clean conscience.

---

## 2. GEOGRAPHIC LAYOUT

The three lighthouses are spaced along the Quiet Coast from south to north. Jake will typically encounter them in this order:

- **Old Thornwatch Lighthouse** — Southernmost. Closest to Kaldvik. First encounter.
- **The Widow's Light** — Middle of the coast. Requires a trek inland through the Mournwood.
- **Black Stone Lighthouse** — Northernmost. On an isolated headland past Jernholm.

Each lighthouse is a single-NPC location. There are no enemies to fight. These are conversation locations, pure and simple. The only challenge is getting there — some of the paths are treacherous, exposed to weather, or require specific items to access.

## 3. OLD THORNWATCH LIGHTHOUSE

### 3.1 Location & Approach

Old Thornwatch sits on a jagged rock outcrop about 3km north of Kaldvik. The approach is along a narrow cliff path that runs above the sea. The path is exposed to wind — during storms, it is genuinely dangerous, and the game reflects this with reduced movement speed and a chance of falling if Jake rushes. In good weather, the walk takes about ten minutes of real time and offers some of the best views in Region 04.

The lighthouse itself is a stone tower, 18 meters tall, with a small attached cottage. The light at the top still burns every night — Commander Ivar Holt maintains it alone, as he has for forty years. The oil comes from a monthly delivery from Kaldvik, arranged by Torvald the Harbor Master.

### 3.2 First Impression

When Jake first approaches the lighthouse, he sees smoke rising from the cottage chimney. The door is open. An old man sits on a stone bench outside, cleaning a flintlock pistol with methodical care. He looks up as Jake approaches.

The old man is tall even while sitting. His hair is white and cut short in the old imperial naval style. He wears a faded imperial navy coat with the insignia carefully removed — the coat is older than Jake, but it has been cared for like a sacred object. His hands are liver-spotted but steady.

He watches Jake approach without standing, without reaching for a weapon, without any sign of concern. When Jake is within conversational distance, the old man speaks first, in a clear, calm voice.

### 3.3 Core Character: Commander Ivar Holt

**Age:** 73  
**Years at the Lighthouse:** 40  
**Served with Draven:** 1819-1824 (Draven's early career, ages 15-20)

**Register:** Middle Register. Ivar was an imperial naval officer for thirty years before he retired. He speaks with precision, patience, and the weight of a man who has made many hard decisions.

**Visual Description:**
- Tall and thin, slightly stooped from age
- White hair in the imperial cut
- Clean-shaven (still shaves every morning, a habit from his service days)
- Pale blue eyes, sharp as a man half his age
- Wears the imperial navy coat every day, even though the empire he served no longer exists as he knew it
- Keeps his cottage immaculately organized — every tool in its place, every book shelved, every cup washed and dried
- Walks with a slight limp from an old wound he never explains

**His Story:**

Ivar Holt was commissioned into the imperial navy at age seventeen. He spent five decades in service. His most memorable years were his first five as a lieutenant commander aboard the *HMS Vigilance*, where a fifteen-year-old cadet named Jeremiah Voss was assigned to his watch. Ivar was Draven's first commanding officer. He taught Draven how to navigate by stars, how to read wind, how to lead men twice his age, and how to stand on a deck during a storm without being afraid.

Ivar remembers Draven as brilliant. Too brilliant, in fact — a young man who absorbed naval theory the way other boys absorbed legends. Draven read every book in the ship's library in his first year. He asked questions Ivar sometimes could not answer. He cared about his men — not in the abstract, but personally, remembering every sailor's name, every sailor's family, every sailor's birthday.

Ivar also remembers what worried him about young Draven: the boy took everything personally. Every victory was a triumph. Every failure was a wound. When a sailor under Draven's command drowned during a routine rigging accident, Draven wrote a letter to the man's widow that was so long and so detailed it nearly prevented him from completing his other duties for a week. Ivar sat him down and told him: *"You cannot care this much. The navy will break you if you care this much. I am telling you this because I care about you. Learn to let the dead be dead. Learn to let the survivors grieve without you."*

Draven listened politely. He nodded. He did not learn.

Ivar retired in 1865, at age 67, after thirty-seven years of service. He came to the Quiet Coast because he had nowhere else to go — his wife had died in 1851, his children had moved to imperial cities, and the service was the only life he truly remembered. He took over the lighthouse at Old Thornwatch from the man who had manned it before him (a fisherman named Torkell, who died of old age and passed the keeping of the light to Ivar as a favor).

He has maintained the light ever since. He has spoken to perhaps forty strangers in forty years. Most of them were passing fishermen. Jake is the first stranger in twelve years to ask him about Jeremiah Voss.

### 3.4 The Conversation

Jake approaches. Ivar sets down the pistol he was cleaning. He does not stand.

> **IVAR:** *"Traveler. You have walked far. The path is rough. Sit, if you wish. I can offer you tea and hard bread and not much else. The offer is sincere if modest."*

Jake can choose to sit. If he does, Ivar brings out a simple clay cup and pours tea from a kettle over the fire. He waits for Jake to speak first.

**If Jake asks about the lighthouse:**
> *"I keep the light. I have kept it for forty years. Before me, a fisherman named Torkell kept it. Before him, an imperial officer kept it. Before him, nobody — this tower was built in 1671 by imperial engineers who wanted a warning for the northern shipping lanes. The lanes are mostly dead now. Ships do not come here the way they did. But I keep the light because if a ship does come, and it hits the rocks in the dark, someone will die who might have lived. That is reason enough for forty years of my life. I do not need more reason than that."*

**If Jake asks who he is:**
> *"I am Commander Ivar Holt. Retired. Imperial Navy, Fourth Fleet, Home Command. I served from 1819 to 1865. I was at the battle of Dunmore Sound. I was at the crossing of the Black Strait. I was at the defense of Port Vale. None of these things matter now. What matters now is the light, and the fish I catch for dinner, and the letters I write to a woman who has been dead for twenty-four years but whom I still miss every morning when I wake up. I am an old man on a rock by the sea. That is all I am."*

**If Jake asks about Jeremiah Voss:**

Ivar goes still. He sets his tea cup down carefully on the stone bench beside him. He looks at Jake for a long moment — not suspicious, just reading him.

> *"Jeremiah Voss. You say that name in a way that tells me you are not asking casually. You know him by another name now, I assume. The pirate name. I have heard it. I do not use it. I taught the boy how to navigate by stars when he was fifteen years old. I knew him when he was a cadet who cared too much about everything. I will use his real name when I speak of him, because the name he took afterward is a mask, and I do not help men wear masks."*

**Middle Register shifts slightly to a quieter mode — Ivar is remembering, not performing:**

> *"I will tell you what I knew of him. You can decide what it means. He was fifteen when he came aboard my ship as a cadet. His family had no navy tradition — his father was a minor merchant in the capital, his mother a seamstress. He got into the naval academy on merit, which was rare. He was the youngest cadet in his class by two years. He looked like a child standing on the deck of my ship in his new uniform, trying not to cry from the rough seas on his first day."*

> *"But he was extraordinary. Not because he was naturally gifted — many boys are naturally gifted — but because he cared. He cared the way few men I have met have ever cared. He knew every sailor's name within his first week aboard. He knew their wives' names within the first month. He remembered their children's birthdays. When one of them was injured in a rigging accident, Jeremiah stayed with him in the infirmary for three days straight. I had to order him to sleep."*

> *"His first command came at twenty. A minor patrol vessel. He was the youngest lieutenant commander the Fourth Fleet had ever commissioned. I attended his commissioning ceremony in the capital. His mother was there, wearing a dress she had clearly sewn herself. She cried. Jeremiah held her hand during the speeches and told her, 'Mama, I am going to make you proud.' He meant it. He was the kind of boy who could not say a thing he did not mean."*

Long pause. Ivar picks up his tea cup, realizes it has gone cold, sets it down again.

> *"I retired while he was still a young officer. I did not see him again after 1824. I heard his name through the navy grapevine over the years — rising rank, successful commands, difficult assignments. I heard about his wife Cassia. I heard about his daughter Elin. I heard nothing but good things. Until the year 1849, when I heard about the village. And then I heard nothing for many years. And then I heard the name Draven Voss, and I realized that the boy I had taught to navigate by stars had become something I did not want to understand."*

> *"Here is the only thing I want to tell you, stranger. When I sat young Jeremiah down in my cabin in 1822 and told him he cared too much and the navy would break him, I was not wrong. I was right. But I was also warning him about a wound that takes decades to bleed out, and I did not have the patience or the wisdom to tell him what to do instead of caring. I just told him to stop. I did not tell him how to keep caring without breaking. I did not have the answer. I still do not. I have spent forty years in this lighthouse trying to find the answer, and all I have learned is that the men who care are the men who break, and the men who do not care are the men who become comfortable. There is no third way. If there is, I have not found it."*

Ivar looks at Jake directly.

> *"If you are hunting him to kill him, I will not try to stop you. That is your right and probably your duty. I will ask one thing. When you kill him, if you have the chance to say something to him before the end, tell him this: 'Ivar Holt says he is sorry he did not have a better answer in 1822.' Do not explain it to him. He will understand."*

> *"I have one more thing to say. Two other men on this coast served with Jeremiah at different points in his life. Astrid Vang keeps the Widow's Light, in the middle of the coast. Sigur Brandt keeps Black Stone Lighthouse, in the far north. They both know him better than I do — Astrid knew him as a husband and father, Sigur knew him on the day he broke. If you want the full story, find them. If you do not, kill him and be done. Both are valid. Some men deserve to be understood completely before they die. Other men deserve to be killed quickly. Jeremiah Voss is both kinds of man, and you are the one who will decide which kind he is to you. I will not decide for you."*

The conversation ends. Ivar picks up his cleaning cloth and returns to the flintlock pistol. He does not invite Jake to stay longer, but he does not dismiss him either. If Jake lingers, Ivar will answer practical questions about the coast but will not return to the subject of Draven.

### 3.5 Story Flag

When Jake completes this conversation, the story flag `lighthouse_ivar_complete` is set. This unlocks the possibility of returning to Ivar later for the final questline payoff (see Section 6).

## 4. THE WIDOW'S LIGHT

### 4.1 Location & Approach

The Widow's Light sits on a small island about 400 meters off the coast, roughly between Graavig and Sorgsby. To reach it, Jake must either:

1. **Sail there in a small boat** — can be rented in Graavig for 50 Doubloons, or borrowed free if Jake has completed *The Shepherd's Loss* quest in Kaldvik (Bo the shepherd has a cousin in Graavig with a boat)
2. **Wait for low tide** and walk across a rocky exposed causeway. The causeway is only walkable for about 90 real minutes per in-game day. If Jake is caught on it when the tide rises, he must either swim back (dangerous) or wait the night on the island.

The lighthouse is smaller than Old Thornwatch — only 12 meters tall — but the keeper's cottage is larger and obviously well-cared-for. A small garden grows behind the cottage, with herbs and vegetables that should not realistically grow this far north. The keeper tends them carefully despite the soil being barely sufficient.

### 4.2 First Impression

When Jake arrives at the lighthouse, he sees a woman working in the garden. She is tall for a woman, sixty-eight years old, with hair once dark but now streaked with gray, pulled back in a practical knot. She wears work clothes — a heavy wool skirt, a thick sweater, leather boots. Her hands are dirty from the garden.

She looks up when she hears Jake approach. She straightens slowly — her back is not what it used to be. She studies him with clear intelligent eyes.

> **ASTRID:** *"Welcome to the Widow's Light, traveler. My name is Astrid Vang. I have been expecting a stranger to come asking difficult questions for about three weeks now — the wind has been unusual, and my old crow has been acting strange. Come in. I was about to make lunch. I have extra."*

The welcome is warmer than Ivar's but equally measured. Astrid was an imperial officer too, and her manners reflect thirty years of disciplined hospitality.

### 4.3 Core Character: Commander Astrid Vang

**Age:** 68  
**Years at the Lighthouse:** 12  
**Served with Draven:** 1832-1839 (Draven's middle career, ages 28-35, the years of his marriage and fatherhood)

**Register:** Middle Register, warmer than Ivar's but equally precise. Astrid has a dry humor that occasionally surfaces — when it does, it is Pillar 4 Style B (Sekiro-dry). She does not tell jokes. She observes the world with wry precision and expects the listener to notice.

**Visual Description:**
- Tall and strong-built, though age has stooped her slightly
- Gray-streaked dark hair, still long, usually worn in a practical knot
- Weathered face, more lined than Ivar's because Astrid spent her years on open decks in harsh weather
- Wears practical clothes: wool, leather, sturdy boots
- Has the remnants of an old burn scar on her left hand — she never talks about it
- Her garden is her pride. She spends hours in it daily, even in winter
- Keeps a single pet: an old white cat named Sigrid, who sleeps by the fire and occasionally deigns to acknowledge visitors

**Her Story:**

Astrid Vang was the third woman commissioned as an officer in the imperial navy — a rare enough thing in 1821 that her appointment was briefly controversial. She earned her rank through a combination of ability, stubbornness, and the advocacy of Commander Ivar Holt, who was stationed near her home port and recognized her potential when she was still a teenage volunteer.

She served with distinction for thirty-two years. Her most significant posting was aboard the *HMS Liberty* from 1832 to 1839, where she served alongside Captain Jeremiah Voss (he was captain, she was his executive officer and eventually his friend). The seven years they spent together were, according to Astrid, the happiest professional years of her life.

During those seven years, she attended Cassia and Jeremiah's wedding (1833). She attended the birth of their daughter Elin (1835) and was asked to be the child's godmother — an honor she accepted and took seriously. She was at Elin's first birthday party, her second, her third, her fourth, her fifth. She remembers Elin as a bright, curious child who adored her father and called Astrid "Aunty Astri" until she was old enough to call her by her proper name.

Astrid retired from the navy in 1853, age 59, after the village massacre of 1849 had forced her to reconsider her relationship with the empire she had served. Unlike Draven, she did not desert — she completed her tour of duty, served four more years in a minor home-port assignment, and retired with full honors. Unlike Ivar, she did not retire because she was old enough to. She retired because she could not look at the imperial flag without feeling sick anymore.

She came to the Quiet Coast in 2014 (twelve years before the events of Dark Arisen) because she had heard through old naval channels that the region was quiet, sparsely populated, and had an old lighthouse in need of a keeper. She took the position. She has maintained it for twelve years. She has not spoken to Jeremiah Voss since 1849.

### 4.4 The Conversation

Astrid leads Jake into her cottage. It is warm and clean, with a fire in the hearth and the smell of vegetable soup cooking. She ladles soup into two clay bowls and sets them on the small wooden table. She sits across from Jake. Sigrid the cat eyes the visitor disdainfully from her spot by the fire.

> **ASTRID:** *"Eat first. Talk after. The soup is my grandmother's recipe and the ingredients are from my garden and I do not make it for most people. You are receiving an honor you do not yet understand. Enjoy it."*

Jake eats. The soup is genuinely excellent. Astrid eats with him in companionable silence. When they are both finished, she clears the bowls, refills her cup of tea, and sits back down.

> **ASTRID:** *"Now. You have come to ask me about Jeremiah Voss. I can tell from the weight on your shoulders. Let me make this easy for you — I will tell you what I knew of him, and you will listen, and you will decide what to do with what I tell you. I will not try to influence your decision. I will just tell the truth."*

**Middle Register throughout. Astrid speaks with measured precision:**

> *"I met Jeremiah in 1832. He was twenty-eight years old and he had just been promoted to captain of the HMS Liberty. I was his newly-assigned executive officer. I was thirty-eight at the time, ten years older than him, and I did not want to serve under a man younger than me. I thought it would be humiliating. It was not. Within two weeks of serving under him, I realized he was the finest officer I had ever worked with, and I spent the next seven years trying to learn everything I could from him."*

> *"Here is what Jeremiah was like as a captain: he was demanding, but never cruel. He was precise, but never petty. He knew the name of every sailor on our ship — 180 men — and their families, and their troubles, and what they were afraid of. When one of our sailors lost his wife to illness back home, Jeremiah arranged for a full month of paid leave, which was unheard of, and used his own discretionary funds to cover the replacement sailor's salary so the grieving man would not lose his post. He did this without telling anyone. I only learned about it because the sailor's brother wrote Jeremiah a letter of thanks that I intercepted in the daily mail."*

> *"Here is what Jeremiah was like as a husband: he loved Cassia more than any man I have ever known loved any woman. They were married in 1833, in a small ceremony I attended. She was a schoolteacher's daughter — bright, quiet, kind. She had no interest in navy life, and he had no interest in making her pretend to. They had a small apartment in the capital where she continued her own work as a translator of old Fjordlund texts, and Jeremiah would come home between tours and the two of them would sit in their small kitchen drinking tea and reading each other passages from her translations. He would tell me about these evenings when we were back at sea. His face would change when he talked about them."*

> *"Here is what Jeremiah was like as a father: Elin was born in 1835. I was there. I was at the hospital when Cassia went into labor. Jeremiah was on a patrol mission — the navy had refused him leave — and he nearly resigned his commission when the news reached him. Instead, he sailed the HMS Liberty at full speed back to port in 36 hours, which was probably the fastest crossing of that strait in naval history, and he arrived at the hospital still in his uniform, covered in salt. He picked up his newborn daughter and he cried. Jeremiah Voss did not cry easily. He cried then. He held Elin and looked at me and said, 'Astrid. Astrid, I did not know I could love this much. I am frightened of it.' I did not know what to say. I said, 'Welcome to fatherhood, Captain.' He nodded. He kept crying. It was the only time in seven years I ever saw him cry."*

Astrid pauses. She takes a sip of her tea. Sigrid the cat has come over to sit beside her chair, and she scratches the cat's ears absently.

> *"I was Elin's godmother. I am still Elin's godmother, though I have not seen her in seventeen years and she probably does not remember me. I attended her birthdays from her first year through her fifth. Each year Jeremiah would throw a party at their apartment. Just a small one — Cassia, Jeremiah, Elin, me, and a few close friends. Elin would wear a special dress her mother had sewn. Cake would be served. Jeremiah would give a little speech about how proud he was of his daughter, and the speech would get longer every year, and by her fifth birthday the speech was so long and so emotional that Cassia had to gently touch his arm to make him stop. I remember the fifth birthday more than any of the others, because it was the last one I attended — I was transferred to a different command shortly after — and because I knew, even then, that something was wrong with Jeremiah."*

> *"What was wrong was this: the empire was asking him to do things he did not want to do. Raids on trading vessels that were barely connected to rebellion. Patrols through settlements that had never threatened anyone. The kind of missions that had nothing to do with defending the empire and everything to do with maintaining its grip on places that wanted to be left alone. He did not complain about these missions to me directly. He was too much of a professional. But I could see it eating him. He stopped laughing as much. He read his mail less eagerly. He wrote fewer letters home. Cassia wrote to me once, worried about him, asking if I knew what was happening to her husband. I wrote back and said, 'Watch him carefully. Something is building. I do not know what, but I have served with him for six years and I have never seen him like this.'"*

> *"I was transferred in 1839. I did not see him again for ten years. In 1849, I heard about the village. Everyone in the imperial navy heard about the village — we were not told the details, but the rumors were enough. Jeremiah's entire squadron had been ordered to conduct a 'reclamation operation' against a Fjordlund settlement that had refused to pay imperial taxes. The reclamation was successful. Over four hundred villagers died. Most of them were civilians. Jeremiah's forces then withdrew. Three weeks later, Jeremiah deserted the imperial navy. He took his flagship and forty loyal crew with him. He disappeared from official records. The navy hunted him for a year and then gave up."*

Astrid sets down her tea cup.

> *"Here is the part I want you to understand, stranger. I do not defend what Jeremiah did at that village. I do not defend what he has done since. I do not think he should be spared from the consequences of his actions. But I want you to know this: the man who committed that massacre was not the man I served with for seven years. Something had been eating him for years before 1849, and whatever it was finally consumed him. I have thought about this for twenty-eight years. I have tried to understand what happened. I have failed to understand it. All I know is this — the boy who cried when he held his newborn daughter is still somewhere inside the man who became Draven Voss, and I do not know how much of him is left, and I will never know, because I will never see him again in this life."*

Another long pause.

> *"Cassia wrote me a letter in 1850, a year after the massacre. She told me she had left Jeremiah. She told me she had taken Elin to a small village in the inland, where she still lives. She asked me to never tell Jeremiah where they were. I have never told anyone, including you. I will never tell anyone. I will carry that secret to my grave."*

> *"But I will tell you something else, which is this: Jeremiah knows where they are. He has always known. I do not know how he knows, but mothers and daughters cannot be hidden from the men who love them if those men have the resources to find them. Jeremiah has the resources. He has never approached them. He has never sent them a letter. But I know from a reliable source — I will not say who — that he sends them money every month through a third party. Anonymous donations arriving in Cassia's village from 'a benefactor who admired her husband's service.' Cassia has accepted the money for Elin's sake, I think. I do not think she has ever been fooled about where it comes from."*

Astrid looks at Jake directly.

> *"Elin is twenty-five years old now. She is a librarian in her small village. She is, by all accounts, happy. She has a life. She does not know her father is alive. Or maybe she does — I am not in a position to know what she has pieced together over the years. But the last time I spoke with Cassia — five years ago, through a mutual friend — Cassia told me that Elin had started asking questions about her father. Cassia had not yet answered them. She did not know what to say."*

> *"I am telling you this because if you kill Jeremiah — which you have the right to do — you are also killing a father who has been trying, in his broken and failing way, to be present in his daughter's life from a distance. I do not ask you to spare him. I ask you to know what you are killing. Some men deserve a clean kill. Jeremiah deserves a complicated one."*

> *"One more thing. Ivar Holt keeps the southernmost lighthouse. He taught Jeremiah to navigate. Sigur Brandt keeps the northernmost one. Sigur was at the massacre with him. Between the three of us, we know the whole man. I am the middle. I knew him when he was still becoming. Go to Sigur if you want to know how he ended. Then come back to one of us, and we will give you the thing we have been saving for the stranger who finally listens to all three of our stories. I cannot tell you what it is yet. You must earn it by listening. That is how this works."*

The conversation ends. Astrid rises, clears the tea cups, and returns to her garden. If Jake lingers, she will speak about her flowers but not about Draven. The moment has passed.

### 4.5 Story Flag

When Jake completes this conversation, the story flag `lighthouse_astrid_complete` is set.

## 5. BLACK STONE LIGHTHOUSE

### 5.1 Location & Approach

Black Stone Lighthouse is the hardest to reach. It sits on a headland about 8km north of Jernholm, accessible only via a path through the Mournwood and then along a wind-blasted coastal cliff. The journey takes about twenty minutes of real time. There are no enemies on the path, but the wind is so strong that Jake's movement is visibly affected.

The lighthouse is called Black Stone because it is built of dark volcanic rock that was shipped here from Ashenmoor two centuries ago (before the name Ashenmoor was common — the Fjordlund called it "The Burning Islands" in the old tongue). The stone is unusual on the Quiet Coast, and the lighthouse stands out from a distance like a black exclamation point against the gray cliffs.

The keeper's cottage is attached to the base of the tower. Smoke rises from the chimney. No garden. No pets. Just the stone, the light, and one old man.

### 5.2 First Impression

When Jake arrives at Black Stone, he sees the door to the cottage is closed. He knocks. A voice from inside — gravelly, tired, old — says: *"Come."*

Jake enters. The inside of the cottage is dark. A single oil lamp burns on a small wooden table. The fire in the hearth is low. An old man sits in a chair facing the fire, his back to the door. He does not turn around when Jake enters.

> **SIGUR:** *"Close the door. The wind gets in. Sit anywhere. I am not in the mood to be a host today, but I will speak with you because you have come a long way to find me, and I respect the effort if not the purpose."*

The old man finally turns his head to look at Jake. His face is worn, deeply lined, with a long white beard. His eyes are remarkable: one is the normal pale blue of a Fjordlund man, and the other is completely milky white — blind for decades. The blind eye does not track Jake. The seeing eye watches him carefully.

### 5.3 Core Character: Commander Sigur Brandt

**Age:** 81  
**Years at the Lighthouse:** 28  
**Served with Draven:** 1849 (one year only — the year of the massacre)

**Register:** Middle Register, but slower and heavier than the others. Sigur has been alone for 28 years and speaks with the careful pacing of a man who does not often use his voice.

**Visual Description:**
- The oldest of the three keepers and it shows
- Long white beard, untrimmed
- Long white hair, thinning, tied back loosely
- One blind milky eye, one sharp pale blue eye
- Deep lines across his face, especially around his mouth — the face of a man who has not smiled in decades
- Wears old imperial navy trousers and a heavy black wool sweater; no coat (his coat was burned long ago, and he never replaced it)
- Sits almost motionless in his chair for long hours each day, watching the fire
- Walks with a heavy cane
- Has a scar running from his hairline down across his blind eye to his cheek — the wound that took his sight

**His Story:**

Sigur Brandt is the oldest surviving imperial naval officer from the Fourth Fleet's 1849 reclamation operations. He was a commander of a sister ship to Jeremiah Voss's vessel during the months leading up to the massacre. Unlike Jeremiah, Sigur did not desert after the atrocity. He completed his orders. He retired with honors in 1898 after forty-nine years of service, at age 53.

He came to the Quiet Coast immediately after his retirement. He has been at Black Stone Lighthouse for twenty-eight years. In that time, he has spoken with perhaps twenty strangers. Most of them were fishermen who had taken wrong turns. A few were curious villagers from Jernholm who wanted to meet the famous hermit. None of them asked him about 1849 until Jake.

Sigur is not a good man. He is not a bad man either. He is a man who did what he was told to do for fifty years, and spent the remaining thirty years in a stone tower trying to understand what that meant.

### 5.4 The Conversation

Jake sits in a wooden chair across from Sigur. The fire crackles. Sigur does not offer tea or food. He simply watches Jake with his one good eye for a long moment before speaking.

> **SIGUR:** *"You are here to ask me about Jeremiah Voss. I know this because Astrid Vang sent me a letter last month warning me that a stranger might come looking for answers. Astrid is a thoughtful woman. She does not like surprises, and she did not want me to be surprised. I was not surprised. I have been waiting for this visit for twenty-eight years."*

**Middle Register, slow and deliberate:**

> *"I will tell you what you want to know. But I need to tell you in my own way, which is slowly, because the story is not simple, and I will not rush it to suit your convenience. If you have the patience to hear an old man speak, stay. If you do not, leave now, and I will not blame you."*

Jake stays.

> *"In 1849, I was commander of the HMS Tempest. Jeremiah Voss commanded the HMS Liberty. Our two ships were part of a squadron sent north on what the imperial command called a 'reclamation operation.' The operation was this: certain Fjordlund villages in the northern coast had stopped paying imperial taxes. The taxes were small. The resistance was largely symbolic — the villages did not have the resources for actual rebellion. But the empire does not tolerate symbolic resistance. The empire demanded that the villages be 'reclaimed' — a word that means, in practice, that the villagers were to be killed or enslaved, and the villages themselves burned, so that the remaining coastal settlements would understand that there was no such thing as symbolic resistance to imperial authority."*

> *"Our orders were specific. Each captain was assigned a village. Captain Voss was assigned to a settlement called Ørnstad. I was assigned to a settlement called Skogby. Ørnstad had approximately 420 inhabitants. Skogby had approximately 280. We were to execute our orders on the same day, to prevent any warning between the villages."*

> *"I followed my orders. I sailed to Skogby. I ordered my men to land. We killed the men who resisted — about forty of them. We burned the houses. We took the women and children and the remaining men as cargo to be sold at a processing station in the south. I did all of this by the book. I was a commander. I followed the orders of my superiors. This is what I was trained to do. This is what I did for fifty years."*

Sigur stops. He looks into the fire.

> *"I want you to understand something before I continue. I did not struggle with my orders. I did not lose sleep over them. I had committed similar acts before, in other theaters, against other populations. I was a man of the empire. I believed the empire was a force for order in a chaotic world. I believed that the small atrocities I committed were the necessary cost of larger peace. I believed this sincerely. I was not a monster who enjoyed killing. I was something worse — I was a man who had learned to find killing tolerable in service of a system I trusted. This is the kind of man the imperial navy made me into, and I make no excuses for it now."*

> *"I mention this because I want you to understand that I am not a better man than Jeremiah Voss. I am a worse one. You are about to learn that Jeremiah, after the massacre at Ørnstad, deserted. You will think: 'See, there was a man of conscience. A man who could not live with what he had done.' And that is true. He was a man of conscience. But I want you to understand that the men who stayed — men like me — we were the ones who could live with it. We were the ones the empire wanted. We were the men who made the system work. Jeremiah was the exception. I was the rule. The rule is always darker than the exception."*

Long pause. Sigur stirs the fire with an iron poker. Sparks rise.

> *"Now I will tell you what happened at Ørnstad, because I was there afterward — I sailed to meet Jeremiah's ship on the return voyage, to confirm the operation had been successful on both sides. When I arrived at the harbor where the HMS Liberty was anchored, Jeremiah came aboard my ship to make his report. He was alone. He had left his first officer in command of the Liberty. This was unusual — captains almost never leave their ships during active operations. But Jeremiah needed to speak with me privately."*

> *"We met in my cabin. I poured him a glass of whisky. He did not drink it. He sat across from me at my small desk and he looked at me with a face I had never seen on a human being before or since. It was the face of a man who had committed an act so large he could not fit it inside his own head anymore. He was still in shock, I think. He was not weeping. He was not angry. He was beyond those things. He was in that place that comes after horror, the place where the mind goes when the mind cannot process what the body has done."*

> *"He told me what had happened at Ørnstad. I will not repeat the details. They are in the imperial records if you can find them, but mostly they have been destroyed. What I will tell you is this: the villagers at Ørnstad fought back harder than expected. A little boy — Jeremiah estimated eight years old — threw a rock at one of Jeremiah's officers. The officer drew his pistol. Jeremiah drew his own pistol to stop him. The officer had been given orders to suppress all resistance by any means. Jeremiah had the authority to countermand that order. Jeremiah hesitated. The officer shot the boy. The boy died instantly. Jeremiah then shot the officer — his own officer, under his command. The boy was dead. The officer was dead. And Jeremiah, in that moment, crossed a line he did not know existed."*

> *"After the operation was complete, Jeremiah walked through the burned village alone. He counted the bodies. He wrote down every detail he could see in a small notebook he kept in his coat pocket. He found the dead boy again and sat with him for perhaps an hour before someone came to find him. The boy's mother was dead. The boy's father was dead. There was no one left to claim him. Jeremiah took the boy's body to the village's small church — which was still burning — and tried to find a place to lay him to rest that was not on fire. He could not. The whole building was going up. He finally laid the boy under a tree outside the village and covered him with his own coat. He left the coat there. That is why, when I met him on the Tempest a week later, he was not wearing a captain's coat — he was wearing the rough service coat of an ordinary sailor, which he had borrowed from his first officer."*

Sigur sets down the fire poker.

> *"He told me all of this in my cabin, in a voice that was flat and empty and absolutely clear. When he finished, he looked at me and said, 'Sigur. I am going to leave the navy. I am going to desert. I am telling you this because you were my friend once, and I want you to know that I am not disappearing — I am making a choice. I cannot serve this empire anymore. I do not know what I will do instead. I do not have a plan. But I cannot wear this uniform for another day.'"*

> *"I should have reported him. That was my duty. I should have had him arrested on the spot. I did not. I said nothing. I let him finish his whisky — he drank it then, finally — and I let him walk off my ship and back to the HMS Liberty, and I watched from my cabin window as the Liberty sailed away that night without permission. The next morning I wrote an official report stating that Captain Voss had gone missing along with forty of his crew, cause unknown. I knew the cause. I did not write it. That was my single act of rebellion in fifty years of service. I let one man escape. I think about it every day."*

Sigur looks at Jake directly with his one good eye.

> *"I do not know who Draven Voss has become in the twenty-eight years since. I have heard rumors — everyone on this coast has heard rumors — but I have not sought him out. I do not want to know. I am afraid of knowing. I am afraid that the man who became Draven Voss is worse than the man who committed the massacre, because that would mean he learned nothing from leaving. That would mean my one act of rebellion — letting him escape — was wasted. And I am afraid that the man who became Draven Voss is better than the man who committed the massacre, because that would mean he found something I never did, and I would have to confront the fact that I stayed in the navy for another fifty years while he tried to become something else."*

> *"Either answer breaks me. So I do not ask the question. I sit in my tower and I watch the fire and I wait for the grave. I am eighty-one years old. I will not live much longer. I am telling you this story because it is time for someone to know it, and you are the first person in twenty-eight years who has been willing to listen. I suspect you are also the last person who will ever ask. So you are my audience. I have been rehearsing this story for a long time. Thank you for being here to hear it."*

Sigur exhales slowly.

> *"Here is what I want you to do. When you find Jeremiah — and I know you will find him, because men who come this far always find their target — ask him one question for me. Ask him this: 'Was it worth it? Did becoming Draven Voss give you something that the empire could not? Did leaving the navy free you, or did it only change the shape of your cage?' I need to know the answer. I need to know before I die. If you kill him, which you have every right to do, please ask him this question before the end. And if you have the chance afterward, come back here and tell me what he said. I will wait. I have nothing but time."*

The conversation ends. Sigur turns back to the fire. He does not speak again. Jake can stay as long as he wants, but Sigur will not acknowledge him further. The old man has said what he needed to say.

### 5.5 Story Flag

When Jake completes this conversation, the story flag `lighthouse_sigur_complete` is set.

## 6. THE PAYOFF — "WHAT THEY SAW"

When Jake has completed all three lighthouse conversations (`lighthouse_ivar_complete`, `lighthouse_astrid_complete`, and `lighthouse_sigur_complete` are all set), a new dialogue option appears at any of the three lighthouses when Jake returns: **"Tell me what the others said."**

Jake can choose which of the three keepers to return to. His choice is meaningful and remembered by the game (story flag `log_given_by_[ivar/astrid/sigur]`), but the immediate outcome is the same at each location.

### 6.1 The Return Conversation

Whichever keeper Jake returns to, they listen as Jake recounts what the other two said. The keeper does not interrupt. They listen until Jake is finished. Then they nod slowly.

> **THE KEEPER:** *"So you have heard all three versions. The young officer. The husband and father. The man who broke. You are the first person in forty years to hear all three. Congratulations. I did not think it would ever happen. I am not sure it should have. But you are here, and you have listened, and now I am going to give you something that we three have been keeping."*

The keeper walks to a hidden compartment (behind a stone in the wall, under a floorboard, inside an old sea chest — depending on which lighthouse Jake returns to) and retrieves a heavy leather-bound book.

> **THE KEEPER:** *"This is called the Officer's Log. It has been written across all three of our careers, passed in secret between the three of us and two others who are now dead. It records imperial atrocities that the empire has covered up. Orders we all received. Acts we all committed or witnessed. Names of the dead. Dates. Locations. Full accounting. Over a hundred documented war crimes, spanning sixty years. If this book were ever delivered to the right people in the capital, it could bring down the imperial admiralty. Not that the admiralty would survive anyway — the empire is dying, and has been dying since 1849. But this book is the proof of what the empire was. We kept it so that someone, someday, might know the truth."*

> *"I am giving it to you. I am giving it to you because you have listened to three old people with broken hearts and you have not flinched, and because you are going to confront Jeremiah Voss, and because this book contains information that will change what that confrontation means."*

> *"Take it. Read it when you have time. Bring it to him if you want. Or destroy it if you prefer. It is yours now."*

The keeper gives Jake **The Officer's Log** (Lore Object #010 in the Lore Object Catalog — to be added in the next catalog update).

### 6.2 The Officer's Log — Description & Use

**Item:** The Officer's Log  
**Type:** Unique Quest Item / Lore Object  
**Weight:** Heavy  

**Description (displayed in inventory):**
> *A heavy leather-bound book, old and well-worn. The first entry is dated 1819. The last entry is dated 1898. The handwriting changes across decades, suggesting multiple authors. Inside are detailed records of imperial naval operations that were never reported to the public: raids, reclamations, massacres, disappearances. Over a hundred documented atrocities. The names of the dead are recorded whenever known. The names of the officers responsible are recorded in every case. Jeremiah Voss's name appears 47 times. Sigur Brandt's name appears 38 times. Astrid Vang's name appears 12 times (all as a witness, never as a commander of an atrocity). Ivar Holt's name appears 4 times (all pre-1830, and all involving operations he refused to participate in, noted as 'Officer Holt declined participation citing moral objection. Reassignment requested. Not granted.')*

**Mechanical Use 1 — The Draven Boss Fight:**

If Jake has the Officer's Log in his inventory when he confronts Draven Voss in Chapter 9, a unique dialogue option unlocks during Phase 1 of the boss fight:

> **JAKE:** *"Your officers remember you. All three of them. They have been waiting to hear if it was worth it."*

This line causes Draven to pause mid-combat. His defense temporarily drops for a single exchange (giving Jake one free critical-level hit). More importantly, Draven responds:

> **DRAVEN:** *"Ivar. Astrid. Sigur. They are alive? All three of them?"*

If Jake confirms, Draven is silent for a moment, then says:

> **DRAVEN:** *"Thank you. I have wondered for twenty years. I had assumed they were all gone. Knowing they were alive and thinking about me makes the ending easier. Please — tell them I am sorry. Tell them I did not forget them. Tell Sigur the answer to his question is both yes and no, and I think he will understand what that means."*

This does not save Draven from the fight. The fight continues. But the player now has additional emotional context that makes the final combat even heavier.

**Mechanical Use 2 — The Post-Game Epilogue:**

In the post-game epilogue scenes (after Draven is defeated, regardless of which ending the player chose), the Officer's Log is shown being delivered to Elin Voss (Draven's daughter) by an unnamed messenger. Elin reads it. She cries. She understands her father for the first time in her adult life. The game does not show whether this changes anything in her life — only that she knows now. This scene plays only if Jake kept the Officer's Log rather than destroying it or abandoning it.

**Alternative: The Destruction Option:**

At any point after receiving the Officer's Log, Jake can choose to destroy it. He can burn it in a fire, throw it into the sea, or bury it. Destroying it prevents Elin from ever learning the truth about her father. This is a valid choice — some players may feel that Elin deserves to be protected from this knowledge. The game does not judge this choice. It simply records it.

### 6.3 Story Flags

After the payoff conversation:
- `officer_log_received` — always set when the conversation completes
- `log_given_by_ivar` OR `log_given_by_astrid` OR `log_given_by_sigur` — depending on which keeper Jake returned to
- `officer_log_destroyed` — set only if the player chooses to destroy the log later

## 7. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE CHECK

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** All three keepers speak in Middle Register throughout. This is correct — they are former imperial officers, educated and precise. Their dialogue never drifts into High Register (that would be wrong for retired commanders talking about their own history) and never drops into Low Register (their military backgrounds would prevent it). Consistent and appropriate.
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** The Officer's Log is the ultimate Pillar 2 object. 60 years of imperial crimes documented on physical paper. The player can hold it, read it, carry it, destroy it. Every major lore beat about the empire's true nature comes through this one object. No codex entries. No info dumps. Just a book.
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** No boss deaths in this content. But the stories the keepers tell are FULL of death — the deaths at Ørnstad, the deaths at Skogby, the death of the eight-year-old boy. All handled with Style D weight (tragic, specific, named when possible). The cobbler from the village quest and the eight-year-old boy from Sigur's story serve similar narrative functions: named specifics that make abstract atrocity unbearably concrete.
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** Almost none. Appropriate. Astrid has the one dry observation about Sigrid the cat and her soup recipe being "an honor you do not yet understand" — this is Pillar 4 Style B (Sekiro-dry) used once, correctly, to break the weight of the conversation briefly before returning to heavier material.
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** This is the most direct examination of Pillar 5 in the entire game. Each of the three keepers represents a different failed response to the theme: Ivar (the mentor who did not have a better answer), Astrid (the colleague who witnessed the becoming), Sigur (the peer who stayed and let Draven leave). All three carry the same question: *what does revenge against the system you served take from you?* The answer each of them embodies is different: Ivar lost his answer, Astrid lost her faith, Sigur lost his justification. Draven lost everything. Jake is one conversation away from joining them.

## 8. WHY THIS CONTENT MATTERS

The three lighthouses are the single most important optional content in Dark Arisen. Players who skip them will still complete the game, still fight Draven, still get the epilogue. But players who complete *"What They Saw"* will understand Dark Arisen in a way that players who skip it never will.

This is not because the content contains secret game mechanics. It contains a secret understanding: **the revenge you take for one thing always costs you something else, and the cost is never paid once — it is paid every day, in small silences, in old men tending lighthouses, in women who lost their daughters and cannot hear the old songs anymore, in mothers who name their dead forever.**

The three keepers are, collectively, the single most important source of Dark Arisen's theme. If any player writes an essay about the game years after release, the essay will almost certainly discuss these three old people on their lonely rocks by the sea.

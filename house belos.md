## 1. THE DESIGN PROBLEM

### 1.1 Two Things That Must Both Be True

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_belos.md, highmoore_overview.md Section 6.4.

**House Belos must be a legitimate target.** Men of this House did what they did, four years ago, and the House knows, and the House protected them, and nothing was ever done.

**House Belos must be a place full of people.** Cooks, grooms, farriers, children, hostage-guests, old men, a garrison of ordinary levy who were eleven years old when it happened.

**Design law:** both. Simultaneously. Cross-reference princess_quest_belos.md — the nine minutes do not work if the player has been told Belos is monstrous, and they do not work if Belos is innocent.

### 1.2 The Inversion

Cross-reference house_arion.md Section 1.1 — Arion is the most beautiful place in the game.

**Belos is where the player expects the villains to be**, based on everything the world says about them, and cross-reference Section 8: **the world is wrong in the specific way that matters and right in the general way that does not.**

## 2. THE CASTLE

### 2.1 The Silhouette

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/castle_catalog.md Principle 1.5, highmoore_region.md Section 4.2.

Belos sits in the Stonecountry, on limestone, and it is built of the dark millstone grit hauled from the west — **the wrong stone for the country it is in**, and cross-reference Section 3.1: there is a reason, and it is four hundred years old, and nobody remembers it.

Squat. Wide. Low towers with wide tops. Cross-reference Section 4 — **every roofline in Belos is a shooting platform**, and the castle's whole geometry is an argument about range.

**From three kilometres it reads as a fist on the ground.** Cross-reference house_arion.md Section 2.2 for the intended contrast.

### 2.2 The Districts

- **The Outer Court** — the largest open ground of any Highmoore castle, and cross-reference Section 4.3: it is a range. Cross-reference princess_quest_belos.md — **this is where the nine minutes happen.**
- **The Butts** — permanent archery grounds, in use daily, by everyone. Cross-reference Section 4.2.
- **The Fletchery and the Bowyer's** — cross-reference highmoore_smiths.md: Belos's real industry
- **The Hall** — dark, warm, low-beamed, and cross-reference Section 6.3: it is not beautiful and it is extremely comfortable
- **The Kennels** — Belos runs the region's hounds
- **The Undercroft** — stores, and cross-reference princess_quest_belos.md Section 6: **where the household goes when the fighting starts, and where they are when the castle comes down**

### 2.3 The Walls

Cross-reference physics/destruction_physics.md, princess_quest_belos.md.

Thick, old, and cross-reference Section 2.1 — millstone grit, which is hard and which **shatters** rather than crumbles. Design note for the collapse: cross-reference physics/destruction_physics.md Section 3 — Belos does not fall gently.

## 3. LORD ALDRIC BELOS

### 3.1 Quick Reference

**Name:** Lord Aldric Belos
**Age:** 58
**Register:** blunt Middle-High — a lord who talks like a yeoman and knows it
**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_belos.md Section 7 for the encounter

### 3.2 The Character

Hard, fair by his own lights, and not cruel. Cross-reference Section 1.1 — **he is also the man who did nothing.**

He is respected and not liked. His holdings are well-run. His levy is the best-trained in Highmoore and he trains with them. He has buried two sons at the ford. Cross-reference house_system.md Section 8.2 — he administers the war correctly and has never once broken a settlement's terms.

### 3.3 What He Did

**Cross-reference:** Section 6, princess_quest_the_truth.md.

Four years ago, during a truce, a hostage-guest of House Arion was assaulted in his castle by men of his household.

**He found out.** Not immediately, and not fully, and cross-reference Section 6.4 — what he found out was partial and he did not look harder.

**He did nothing.** No hanging, no expulsion, no message to Arion, no record. Two of the men are still in his service. One is dead of something else. One left.

**His reasoning, which the game gives him and does not endorse:** the truce was holding. Four hundred people were not dying that year. Opening it would have meant war, and war would have meant the ford again, and he had already buried two sons at the ford.

**Cross-reference style_bible.md Pillar 3 and Section 8:** the game states this once, plainly, in his own voice, in the last ninety seconds of his life, and does not comment on it.

### 3.4 Voice

*"You'll be the one from over the mountain. Aye, we've heard. Sit down or don't."*

*"I don't keep a fine hall. I keep a full one. Warm, fed, and every man in it can put an arrow in a post at two hundred paces. You may prefer Arion. Most do."*

*"Cassian Arion is the most civilised man in this basin and I've never trusted a word out of him. That's not a feud talking. That's forty years of watching him be charming at people."*

### 3.5 The Last Ninety Seconds

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_belos.md Section 7.

He does not deny it. He does not plead. He explains the arithmetic and it is a real arithmetic and it does not help.

*"I knew some of it. Not all. I didn't look for the rest."*

*"There was a truce holding and four hundred of mine breathing and I'd put two boys in the ground at that ford already."*

*"So no, I didn't hang them. You want me to say it was the wrong sum. It might have been. I've had four years and I still don't know."*

## 4. THE ARCHERS

### 4.1 The Tradition

**Cross-reference:** house_system.md Section 5, physics/combat_physics.md, physics/wind_systems.md.

Highmoore's dominant military technology is the bow, and Belos is why. Cross-reference Section 2.2 — the butts are used daily by the whole household, and cross-reference Section 4.2: **it is not a military caste. It is everybody.**

### 4.2 The Butts

Every Belos holding has butts. Every child shoots. Cross-reference npcs/children_and_families.md — **and the protection rules hold absolutely**, and cross-reference Section 4.4: this becomes one of the most uncomfortable facts in Phase 8.

**Design note:** a player who visits Belos before the questline sees an entire population practising archery in the evening, casually, as ordinary recreation, the way another culture plays football.

### 4.3 The Range Doctrine

Cross-reference physics/combat_physics.md, physics/wind_systems.md Section 6.

Belos fights at distance and does not close. Their whole architecture (Section 2.1), their whole training, and their whole tactical thinking is **arrows in wind at range**, and cross-reference crystal_katana.md Section 4.2 — **the Crystal Katana is 74 centimetres and its answer to a hundred archers is to get closer.**

### 4.4 The Uncomfortable Fact

Cross-reference princess_quest_belos.md, highmoore_overview.md Section 6.4.

A hundred archers on the walls of the Outer Court, and cross-reference Section 4.2 — **every one of them learned in the butts as a child, in the evening, for fun.**

The design does not state this during the nine minutes. It establishes it twenty hours earlier and says nothing.

## 6. THE TRUCE, FOUR YEARS AGO

### 6.1 What Happened Officially

**Cross-reference:** house_system.md Section 4.1.

Arion and Belos agreed a truce. Standard terms, standard duration, standard exchange: cross-reference house_system.md — **each House sent a child.**

Belos sent a son, aged thirteen, to Arion. He was fine. He is now seventeen and he is in the Outer Court on the day. Cross-reference princess_quest_belos.md Section 5.

Arion sent a daughter, aged fifteen. Cross-reference the_princess.md Section 3.

The truce held eighteen months and was considered a success.

### 6.2 What Happened

**Cross-reference:** the_princess.md Section 3, princess_quest_the_truth.md.

**Design law — the absolute restraint rule.** Cross-reference highmoore_overview.md Section 6.3, npcs/children_and_families.md (the protection-rule precedent), style_bible.md Pillar 3:

- Nothing is depicted. Not in flashback, not in dialogue detail, not in implication beyond her own words.
- **Her real letter is the only account.** Cross-reference the_princess.md Section 9 — it is short, factual, and devastating because of what it does not say.
- No other character describes it. Not Aldric, not Cassian, not the Wizard.
- The men involved are never shown doing anything and are never given a scene.

### 6.3 What Was Done About It

Nothing. Cross-reference Section 3.3.

### 6.4 The Men

Four. One is dead of a fever. One left Belos service two years ago and is somewhere in the Folds. **Two are still in the household** and cross-reference princess_quest_belos.md Section 5 — they are in the Outer Court on the day, and they are not identified to the player, and they die among two hundred others without ever being singled out.

**Design law:** the game does not offer Jake the option to find and kill only them. Cross-reference princess_quest_belos.md Section 8 — that option's absence is the entire moral structure of the sequence.

## 8. THE WORLD'S OPINION AND WHY IT IS WRONG

### 8.1 What Highmoore Says

*"Belos don't forget and Belos don't forgive."*

*"They'll shoot you off a horse at two hundred paces and call it a warning."*

*"Cold house. Cold men."*

### 8.2 What Is Actually True

Cross-reference Sections 3.2, 6.3, 7.1.

Belos is a warm, well-run, disciplined House with a good lord, an ordinary population, excellent hounds, the best bows in the basin, and a shooting culture that is essentially recreational.

**And four years ago four of their men did something in this castle and nothing was done.**

### 8.3 The Design Statement

Cross-reference Section 1.1, style_bible.md Pillar 5.

The reputation is wrong. The reckoning is real. **Neither cancels the other**, and cross-reference princess_quest_belos.md — the player kills three hundred people in a place they had previously enjoyed being, over something four of those people did, and the game does not resolve this and does not intend to.

## 12. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** Aldric is a lord who talks like a yeoman and knows it, and he is the only High-status character in Highmoore who does. His register alone makes Arion's charm read differently in hindsight.
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** the wrong stone, hauled from the west four hundred years ago for a reason nobody remembers. Every roofline a shooting platform. The butts, worn down by children. A boundary stone with three sigils, at a lake.
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** the undercroft. Two hundred and ninety-six people who were not the four. And a man who explains his arithmetic in ninety seconds and says *"I've had four years and I still don't know."*
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** the butts, the yeomen taking Jake's money, Aldric's forty years of watching Cassian Arion be charming at people. **Belos is funnier than Arion**, and that is deliberate, and it is unbearable afterward.
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** the game does not let Jake find and kill only the four. It never offers that. Three hundred people, over four of them, in a place he had liked, with a sword that does not tire — and the House's actual crime was that a man with two sons buried at a ford decided a holding truce was worth more than looking harder.

## 14. CONCLUSION

Iron and Green. Dark millstone grit hauled from the wrong country four hundred years ago for a reason nobody remembers, squat and wide with low towers and every roofline a shooting platform — a fist on the ground, three kilometres out, and everything anybody in Highmoore says about it is cold and hard and unforgiving.

And inside: a warm low hall, the region's best hounds, a bowyer's shop that makes the finest weapons in the game, evening butts where the whole household shoots for fun and a stranger can lose an evening's money to a farmer, and a lord who talks like a yeoman, has buried two sons at the ford, keeps his settlements to the letter, and does not trust Cassian Arion because he has spent forty years watching him be charming at people.

Four years ago four men of this House did something to a fifteen-year-old guest, and he found out part of it, and did not look for the rest, and a truce held and four hundred people did not die that year.

He gets ninety seconds to explain that arithmetic. Two of the four are in the Outer Court on the day, unidentified, among two hundred. And the household is in the undercroft the whole time.

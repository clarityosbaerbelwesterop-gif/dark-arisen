# HIGHMOORE — THE SCORE

**Status:** Design Draft v1.0
**Phase:** Phase 8 — Highmoore (Movement Three — Document 19 of ~30)
**Related:** highmoore_overview.md, highmoore_region.md, princess_quest_the_ball.md, princess_quest_the_lake.md, princess_quest_arion_falls.md, princess_quest_belos.md, princess_quest_the_return.md, crystal_caves.md, crystal_guardian.md, physics/sound_physics.md, style_bible.md
**Purpose:** The complete musical design for Phase 8 — the instrumental palette, the silence discipline, diegetic music at Arion, the rain-dance cue, **the note that is taken away at the moment of her death**, the total absence of score at Belos, and the one cue in the return.
**Inspirations:** Red Dead Redemption 2 (restraint, and "That's the Way It Is" arriving once), The Assassination of Jesse James (Nick Cave & Warren Ellis — sparse, sad, unresolved), Arvo Pärt (Spiegel im Spiegel; tintinnabuli), Górecki's Third Symphony, The Leftovers (Max Richter — a single held idea), Barry Lyndon (Schubert's Piano Trio used as a wound), Hildur Guðnadóttir (subtraction as escalation)

---

## 1. THE SCORE'S PHILOSOPHY

### 1.1 Principle 1: Highmoore Is Mostly Silent

**Cross-reference:** physics/sound_physics.md, highmoore_region.md Section 1.3.

Highmoore has wind, hooves, rain, bells, and birds. Cross-reference mounted_travel.md Section 6.4 — **the twenty-five-minute rides are unscored**, and that is what makes the region feel enormous.

**Score enters rarely and leaves quickly.**

### 1.2 Principle 2: Classical, Small, and Wrong-Footed

Strings, a piano, one clarinet, and nothing else. **No percussion anywhere in Phase 8.** No brass. No choir. No drums, ever, for any reason.

**Cross-reference the archipelago's score for contrast** — Dark Arisen has had drums, brass, and volume. **Highmoore has a chamber ensemble and a lot of weather.**

### 1.3 Principle 3: Nothing Resolves

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_overview.md Section 1.3 — oppressive and beautiful simultaneously.

Every Highmoore cue is built on a suspension that does not resolve, or resolves late, or resolves somewhere the ear was not expecting. **The player should never once get the satisfaction of a cadence.**

### 1.4 Principle 4: Escalation Is Subtraction

**Cross-reference:** Section 5, and the design law that governs the whole phase.

When Highmoore's music intensifies, **instruments leave.** Nothing is ever added for emphasis. The most emotionally violent moment in Dark Arisen is achieved by removing one note.

### 1.5 Principle 5: The Worst Things Have No Music At All

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_belos.md, princess_quest_arion_falls.md.

Nine minutes at House Belos: **silent.** Eleven men in the Arion yard: **silent.** Cross-reference Section 8 — the absence is the statement.

## 2. THE PALETTE

### 2.1 The Ensemble

| Instrument | Function |
|---|---|
| **Solo cello** | Highmoore's voice. Present in almost every cue. |
| **Violin (one, sometimes two)** | Her. Cross-reference Section 4.3. |
| **Viola** | The land. Low, sustained, unmoving. |
| **Piano** | Used sparingly, always single notes, never chords under pressure |
| **Clarinet** | Warmth. The ball and the lake only. **Never heard again after minute twenty-two of the lake sequence.** |
| **Nothing else** | |

### 2.2 The Diegetic Layer

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_the_ball.md Section 4.1, house_arion.md Section 4.3.

Arion's musicians are **real, in the gallery, playing in the room**. Cross-reference physics/sound_physics.md Section 4.1 — the Great Hall's short warm reverb is built for them.

**Design law:** the ball has **no score**. Everything the player hears is coming from six people in a balcony, and cross-reference Section 3.2: **that is why the hall feels like a place and not a cutscene.**

## 3. THE CUES — MOVEMENTS ONE TO THREE

### 3.1 THE EMERGENCE

**Cross-reference:** crystal_caves.md Section 8.3, highmoore_region.md Section 1.4.

The second and last cue in the Crystal Caves. Cross-reference crystal_caves.md Section 6.3.

**Design:** solo cello, viola drone, and one violin entering late. Warm, minor, and **it does not resolve.** Ninety seconds. It ends before the player is finished looking.

**Cross-reference highmoore_overview.md Section 1.4** — a triumphant swell would kill Highmoore permanently and the cue is written specifically to avoid one.

### 3.2 THE BALL — DIEGETIC ONLY

Cross-reference Section 2.2.

Six musicians in the gallery. Period-plausible Highmoore dance music: a fiddle, a pipe, a drum *(the only percussion in Phase 8, and it is in the room, played by a man the player can see)*, and it is loud and cheerful and slightly out of tune.

**Cross-reference princess_quest_the_ball.md Section 6.4** — dialogue is mixed slightly too quiet against it and both characters lean in.

### 3.3 THE TERRACE

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_the_ball.md Section 7.

**No music.** Wind, and the hall's noise behind the door, and cross-reference physics/sound_physics.md Section 2.4: **it gets quieter each of the four times the door opens and closes.**

### 3.4 THE EJECTION

Cross-reference princess_quest_the_ball.md Section 8.4. **No music.** Hooves on a cobbled yard and a man being kind.

## 4. THE LAKE — THE CENTRAL CUE

### 4.1 THE RIDE

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_the_lake.md Section 4.

**No music for nine minutes.** Hooves, rain approaching, two voices, and cross-reference physics/wind_systems.md — the wind rising as the front comes in.

**Design note:** the silence across the entire ride is what makes Section 4.3 land.

### 4.2 THE LAKE ITSELF

Cross-reference princess_quest_the_lake.md Sections 5.1-5.4.

**No music.** Water, wind in reeds, one woman swimming, and a whetstone.

### 4.3 THE RAIN DANCE — "THE ONE CUE"

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_the_lake.md Section 7.3.

**This is the only piece of score in the entire lake sequence and it is the emotional centre of Phase 8.**

**Instrumentation:** clarinet, solo violin, solo cello, and single piano notes. No viola.

**Structure:**
- A simple warm figure in the clarinet, four bars, repeating
- The violin enters on the second pass with a line that **rises to a suspended fourth and holds it**
- The cello moves underneath, slowly, warm
- **The piano places single notes, off the beat, roughly one every two bars**

**The suspension resolves at the end of each eight-bar cycle.** Cross-reference Section 4.4 — **it has resolved three times before the arrow.** The ear learns it. The ear expects it.

**Dynamic:** quiet. Cross-reference physics/sound_physics.md — it sits *underneath* the rain, and the rain is louder than the music for the whole cue.

**Duration written:** three minutes forty. Cross-reference Section 5 — **it plays out its full length regardless of what happens at minute two.**

## 5. THE NOTE THAT IS MISSING

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_the_lake.md Section 8, princess_quest_overview.md Section 7.1.

### 5.1 The Design Law

**Nothing is added.** No cue enters. No stinger. No swell. No new instrument. No change in tempo, dynamic, or mix.

**Something is taken away, and the piece keeps going.**

### 5.2 What Happens, Exactly

At the moment of the arrow — mid-sentence, mid-step — the following occurs and **nothing else does:**

1. **The clarinet stops.** Not a fade. It simply is not there on the next bar, and it never returns for the remainder of Phase 8. Cross-reference Section 2.1.

2. **The violin's suspension does not resolve.** It reaches the fourth, as it has three times already, and **it holds**, and the resolution never arrives. It holds for the remaining ninety seconds of the cue.

3. **The cello drops one semitone** on its next note, and stays there. Cross-reference Section 5.3 — **this is the only harmonic change in the entire piece and it converts everything above it from warm to wrong**, without a single instrument playing anything new.

4. **The piano stops.**

**That is all. The tempo does not change. The volume does not change. Nobody plays anything they were not already playing.**

### 5.3 Why the Semitone

The cello's drop turns a major sixth into a minor sixth beneath a violin that is still holding a suspension it will never resolve.

**Cross-reference the user's brief:** *dezent traurig und wütend* — subtly sad and angry.

**The sadness is the unresolved suspension.** It is a question the piece asked three times and answered three times and will now not answer again.

**The anger is the semitone.** It is one voice moving down, once, and refusing to move back, underneath everything else, for ninety seconds.

**Neither is loud. Neither is dramatic. Both are permanent.**

### 5.4 The Ninety Seconds

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_the_lake.md Section 8.3.

The cue plays out its written length. Cross-reference Section 4.3 — three minutes forty, of which roughly ninety seconds remain after the arrow.

**During which:** Jake catches her badly, they go down onto the stones, the player retains full control, there is nothing to do, and **a violin holds a note that is not going to resolve while a cello sits a semitone lower than it was.**

### 5.5 The End

**The cue does not stop dramatically.** Cross-reference princess_quest_the_lake.md Section 8.3 — the violin releases, the cello releases, and there is rain.

**And no music plays again in Highmoore for approximately forty minutes of real time.** Cross-reference Sections 6, 7, and 8.

### 5.6 The Second Playthrough

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_the_truth.md Section 9.

A player who knows what is coming hears the suspension resolve three times and **counts them.**

## 8. BELOS — THE SILENCE

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_belos.md.

### 8.1 The Law

**There is no score at House Belos. At any point. In any variant.**

Nine minutes of combat against three hundred people, a castle collapse, a dying lord's ninety seconds, and a wizard's question — **and not one note.**

### 8.2 What Is There Instead

**Cross-reference:** physics/sound_physics.md, crystal_katana.md Section 3.2.

**The sword.** Cross-reference crystal_katana.md — it rings on every impact, pitched to the Cathedral's resonance, and cross-reference princess_quest_belos.md Section 5.7: **the reverb in a walled court builds across nine minutes until it is nearly continuous.**

Three hundred impacts. One note. Overlapping.

**Design statement:** cross-reference Section 1.4 — **Belos does have music. Jake is playing it.**

### 8.3 The Half-Tone

**Cross-reference:** crystal_katana.md Section 10.3.

After House Belos, the Crystal Katana's ring is a half-tone lower. Nobody comments. No document in the game notes it. **It is possible no player will ever notice.**

**Cross-reference Section 5.3** — it is the same semitone.

### 8.4 The Wizard

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_belos.md Section 9.

He arrives with no cue, no effect, and no sound. Cross-reference the_wizard.md — **and the reverb from the court is still dying while he speaks**, and it takes almost a minute, and he waits through part of it.

### 9.3 THE LAST CUE

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_the_return.md.

**One cue, at the very end of Phase 8**, and it is the only music heard since the lake.

**Instrumentation:** solo cello. Nothing else.

**Design:** it is the rain-dance cue's cello line, alone, played slowly, **at the pitch it dropped to.** Cross-reference Section 5.2 — a semitone below where it began.

**It does not resolve either.**

**Duration:** forty seconds. It stops before the scene does.

## 10. THE WIZARD'S THEME, WHICH DOES NOT EXIST

**Cross-reference:** the_wizard.md, highmoore_overview.md Section 4.

**He has no motif.** He appears three times in Phase 8 and there is no music on any of them.

**Design statement:** cross-reference highmoore_overview.md Section 4.3 — the magic register forbids spectacle, and a theme is spectacle. **The only person in Dark Arisen who can do impossible things is the only major character with no music attached to him at all.**

## 13. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** Arion's music is in the room, played by people, slightly out of tune. Highmoore's score is a chamber ensemble in a game that has had brass and drums for eighty hours.
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** the score's own object is the Crystal Katana, which rings a note, three hundred times, in a walled court — and rings a half-tone lower afterward and is never mentioned.
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** the most important death in Dark Arisen is scored by **removing a clarinet, refusing to resolve a suspension, and dropping a cello one semitone.** Nothing enters. Nothing swells. Nobody plays anything new.
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** six musicians in a gallery, loud and cheerful and slightly out of tune, with one drum — **the only percussion in the entire phase, and a man the player can see is playing it.**
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** the cello drops a semitone at the lake and never comes back up. Forty minutes of silence later, the last thing the player hears in Highmoore is that same cello, alone, at that same lowered pitch, still not resolving.

## 15. CONCLUSION

The Score. A chamber ensemble and a great deal of weather. No percussion, no brass, no choir, and nothing that resolves. Twenty-five-minute rides with nothing but hooves and wind, a ball scored by six people in a gallery who are slightly out of tune, and a terrace with no music at all where a door opens four times and the room behind it gets quieter each time.

And one cue. Three minutes forty of clarinet and violin and cello and single piano notes, sitting underneath the rain, with a suspension that rises and resolves three times so that the ear learns to expect it.

And then, mid-sentence, mid-step: the clarinet is simply not there on the next bar and never returns. The violin reaches the fourth and holds it and does not resolve it, for ninety seconds. The piano stops. And the cello moves down one semitone and stays there.

Nothing enters. Nothing swells. Nobody plays anything new. The tempo does not change and the volume does not change and the rain is still louder than all of it.

Forty minutes later, after eleven men and a courtyard and three hundred people and a burning castle and a question, the last thing the player hears in Highmoore is that cello. Alone. Still a semitone down. Still not resolving. Forty seconds, and it stops before the scene does.

**Phase 8 Document 19 of ~30.**

**Nothing is added. Something is taken away, and the piece keeps going.** 🎻

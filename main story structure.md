# MAIN STORY STRUCTURE — THE TEN CHAPTERS

**Status:** Design Draft v1.0
**Phase:** Phase 11 — Quests & Missions (Document 3 of 6)
**Related:** quest_design_philosophy.md, quest_activation.md, side_quest_catalog.md, progression_overview.md, colonial_war_overview.md, highmoore_overview.md, characters/jake_harlow.md, characters/ethan_harlow.md, bosses/crimson_armada.md, bosses/ashen_wyrm.md, npcs/named_crew_deep_dives.md, style_bible.md
**Purpose:** The structural spine — what each chapter does, what it opens, its gates and pacing, where the optional phases attach, the tutorial-as-story doctrine, and the three structural laws that govern the whole arc.
**Note:** this document defines **structure**. The plot of Chapters 1-3 and the endgame lives in the Phase 1-3 documents and bosses/crimson_armada.md; this is the scaffold they hang on.
**Inspirations:** Red Dead Redemption 2's chapter architecture, The Witcher 3's act structure, Elden Ring's world-gating without level-gating, and the refusal of the three-act formula

---

## 1. THE THREE STRUCTURAL LAWS

### 1.1 THE CRITICAL PATH IS SHORT

**Cross-reference:** progression_overview.md Section 6.1.

**Thirty-eight hours.** A thorough playthrough is 110-160. **The spine is 24-30% of the game.**

**Design law:** cross-reference quest_design_philosophy.md Law Four — **the optional content is not filler around a story. The story is a spine through a world.**

### 1.2 CHAPTERS OPEN, THEY DO NOT CLOSE

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/war_state_system.md, highmoore_overview.md Section 3.3.

**Nothing becomes unavailable at a chapter boundary except the fourteen expiring quests.** Cross-reference quest_design_philosophy.md Section 6 — and those expire on world state, not chapter number.

### 1.3 THE WORLD RUNS UNDERNEATH

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/war_state_system.md Section 6.

The colonial war advances whether Jake engages or not. **The main story does not pause it and is not paused by it.**

## 2. THE TUTORIAL DOCTRINE

**Cross-reference:** the user's direction — onboarding belongs to the first story missions.

### 2.1 There Is No Tutorial

**No tutorial island. No training dummy. No skippable primer. No "press X to jump."**

### 2.2 What There Is Instead

**Chapters 1-2 teach by requiring.** Cross-reference Section 3.1.

| Taught | How |
|---|---|
| Movement, interaction | walking through a place Jake knows |
| Combat basics | a fight Jake loses |
| **Deflection** | cross-ref Section 3.1 — **the first thing Jake wins by, and it is taught by being killed for not doing it** |
| Posture | the same fight |
| Ship handling | crewing before commanding |
| Navigation | Esteban, in dialogue, at sea |
| The journal | Jake writes in it in a cutscene and the player reads it afterward |

### 2.3 The Prompt Discipline

**Cross-reference:** technical_targets.md Section 8, quest_activation.md Section 3.1.

**Control prompts appear once, on first availability, small, in the corner, for four seconds, and never again.** They can be re-enabled in settings and are off by default on any difficulty above the lowest.

### 2.4 The First Death Is Scripted-Adjacent

Cross-reference Section 3.1 — **Jake loses the first real fight** and the game does not present it as a failure. Cross-reference quest_design_philosophy.md Law Two.

## 3. THE TEN CHAPTERS

### 3.1 CHAPTER 1 — THE BROTHER

**Length:** 2-3h · **Opens:** nothing · **Cross-reference:** characters/ethan_harlow.md, cutscene_catalog.md #1

**Function:** establish Jake, establish Ethan, establish the wound. Cross-reference Section 2.2 — and teach the entire control scheme without a single tutorial screen.

**Structural note:** cross-reference cutscene_catalog.md Section 3.1 — **the game's opening cutscene has no music until its last eight seconds.**

**Ends:** Ethan is dead and Jake has nothing.

## 5. THE GATES

### 5.1 Chapter Gates Are Story Gates

**Cross-reference:** progression_overview.md Section 1.3.

**No level requirements. No stat checks. No gear score.** A chapter advances when its spine content is complete.

### 5.2 The Three Soft Gates

Three places where the game is **harder** rather than closed:

- **The Crystal Guardian.** Cross-reference crystal_guardian.md Section 1.3 — a skill wall, and cross-reference crystal_guardian.md Section 8.4: **it can be walked past entirely.**
- **Admiral Sterling.** Cross-reference colonial_war/colonial_war_bosses.md Section 10 — the hardest engagement in the game, and cross-reference krone_colonies.md Section 5.4: **avoidable by alliance.**
- **Momentum Phase caps.** Cross-reference colonial_war/war_state_system.md Section 5 — **the war's escalation is capped by phase**, which is the only pacing gate in the colonial layer.

### 5.3 The One Hard Gate

**Chapter 10 requires Chapter 9.** That is the entire list.

## 7. PACING

**Cross-reference:** progression_overview.md Section 6.1.

### 7.1 The Spine's Rhythm

**Chapters 1-3 are dense** (9-12h of 9-12h available). **Chapters 4-8 are sparse** (3-6h of spine against 20-50h available). **Chapters 9-10 tighten again** (14-17h of spine against 65h).

**Design intent:** the game opens tight, opens out enormously, and closes tight. Cross-reference Section 1.1.

### 7.2 The Chapter 6-8 Problem

Cross-reference Section 6 — **three chapters carry 14-16 hours of spine and roughly 125 hours of optional content.**

**Design decision:** **that is correct and is not fixed.** Cross-reference quest_design_philosophy.md Law Four — a player who wants a 38-hour game gets one, and a player who wants to live in the archipelago gets that.

### 7.3 The Return-to-Spine Signal

**Cross-reference:** quest_activation.md Section 7.4 (no nagging).

**Nothing tells the player to get back to the story.** No reminder, no marker, no NPC prompt.

**Cross-reference npcs/named_crew_deep_dives.md** — the crew mention it, once per chapter, in the great cabin, and drop it.

---

## 8. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1:** ten chapters, and the crew's once-per-chapter mention of the story is delivered by five people who each care about it differently.
- ✅ **Pillar 2:** no tutorial screens; the control scheme is taught by a place Jake knows and a fight he loses.
- ✅ **Pillar 3:** Chapter 1 is a brother dying with no music until the last eight seconds.
- ✅ **Pillar 4:** cross-reference Section 7.3 — the crew mentioning the plot and being ignored for forty hours is a running joke that nobody writes down.
- ✅ **Pillar 5:** Rache unlocks in Chapter 9, and Highmoore is placed before it on purpose, so that the worst thing Jake ever does happens without the mechanic that would have made it feel like catharsis.

---

## 9. CONCLUSION

The Ten Chapters. Thirty-eight hours of spine inside a hundred and fifty of game — a story that is twenty-four percent of what is there, running underneath a war that resolves without it and a continent on the other side of a mountain that it never mentions.

No tutorial. A place Jake knows, a fight Jake loses, and deflection taught by being killed for not doing it. Prompts that appear once, small, for four seconds, and never again.

Chapters that open and do not close. One hard gate in the entire game. Three soft ones, two of which can be walked past and one of which is a woman who would rather be an ally. And an enormous slack middle — three chapters carrying sixteen hours of story against a hundred and twenty-five hours of everything else — which is not a pacing problem to be fixed but the shape the game is.

And nothing anywhere that tells the player to get back to the plot, except five people in a cabin who mention it once a chapter and then let it go.

**Phase 11 Document 3 of 6.**

**The story is a spine through a world, not a world around a story.** 📖

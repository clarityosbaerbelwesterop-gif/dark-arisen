# HIGHMOORE — THE REGION

**Status:** Design Draft v1.0
**Phase:** Phase 8 — Highmoore (Movement Two — Document 5 of ~30)
**Related:** highmoore_overview.md, crystal_caves.md, mounted_travel.md, house_system.md, highmoore_settlements.md, highmoore_weather.md, npcs/regional_populations.md, npcs/building_evolution.md, physics/wind_systems.md, physics/weather_physics.md, physics/sun_shadow_systems.md, physics/vegetation_physics.md, physics/sound_physics.md, style_bible.md
**Purpose:** The Highmoore region — the emergence shot, geography and scale, the golden-brown palette, the wind, the road network, the atmosphere of a land permanently at war with itself, the free-roam design, and what the player does here before any questline begins.
**Inspirations:** Red Dead Redemption 2 (the West Elizabeth reveal, and the feeling of a horizon that keeps going), Game of Thrones (the North's scale and house geography), Elden Ring (Limgrave's opening vista), The Witcher 3 (Velen's oppressive beauty), Kingdom Come: Deliverance (grounded medieval countryside), Yorkshire and Northumberland moorland, Bruegel

## 1. THE EMERGENCE

### 1.1 The Shot

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_overview.md Section 1.2, crystal_caves.md Section 8.

The counterweight lift comes up for ninety seconds in silence. The light goes from blue-white to grey to gold. The cart stops. There is a mine mouth, a shelf of rock, and wind.

**Jake steps out and the entire Highmoore basin is below him.**

### 1.2 What Is In Frame

- **The moor** — golden-brown, to the horizon, in every direction that is not mountain behind him. Cross-reference physics/vegetation_physics.md — heather, gorse, bracken, and grass, and cross-reference physics/wind_systems.md: **the wind moves across it in visible waves**, continuously, and that motion is the first thing the eye catches.
- **Water** — a river system, lakes, and cross-reference highmoore_weather.md: standing water everywhere, because the moor does not drain.
- **Roads** — pale lines, and they go *somewhere*, which after two hours underground is its own kind of information.
- **Castles.** Cross-reference Section 4. At least six visible from the shelf, at varying distances, and the furthest is a silhouette on the edge of sight.
- **Smoke** — from halls, from villages, and from two places where it should not be.

### 1.3 The Sound

**Cross-reference:** physics/sound_physics.md, physics/wind_systems.md Section 6.

The Crystal Caves had extreme reverb and near-silence. **The shelf has wind and nothing else** — no reverb at all, total openness, and the acoustic shift from the Cathedral to the shelf is as dramatic as the visual one.

**And a bell.** Very distant, from one of the castles, marking an hour. Cross-reference physics/sound_physics.md Section 2.2 — at that range it arrives seconds after it was struck.

### 1.4 The Score

**Cross-reference:** crystal_caves.md Section 6.3 — the second and last musical cue in the passage sequence.

**Design note:** it should be restrained. Cross-reference highmoore_overview.md Section 1.3 — Highmoore is oppressive and beautiful simultaneously, and a triumphant swell would kill it. The cue is warm, minor, and it does not resolve.

### 1.5 The Descent

The shelf connects to a switchback path down to the moor floor — **eight to ten minutes on foot**, and the player walks it, and the castles get closer and the scale keeps correcting upward.

**At the bottom there is a horse.** Cross-reference mounted_travel.md Section 2.

## 2. GEOGRAPHY

### 2.1 Scale

**Cross-reference:** the archipelago's regions for comparison.

Highmoore is **the largest contiguous landmass in Dark Arisen** — larger than Rexa, larger than Fjordlund, and unlike both it has no coastline the player can sail. Cross-reference mounted_travel.md: **crossing it end to end on horseback is roughly twenty-five minutes at a canter.**

### 2.2 The Basin

Highmoore sits in a bowl. Mountains on the north-western edge (the Sundered Spine, where Jake came through), high ground east and south, and the basin floor is the moor.

**Nobody comes in and nobody goes out.** Cross-reference highmoore_overview.md Section 5.4 — the Empire and Albion have never reached Highmoore, and the geography is why.

### 2.3 The Five Sub-Regions

**THE HIGH MOOR (centre and north)**
Open heather, exposed, windswept, and the largest area. Cross-reference physics/wind_systems.md — the wind here is a permanent gameplay factor. Sparse habitation, standing stones, and long sightlines.

**THE FOLDS (west)**
Broken country — valleys, becks, drystone walls, and small farms. Cross-reference physics/vegetation_physics.md. The most densely inhabited part of Highmoore and the most fought-over.

**THE WATERLANDS (south-east)**
Bog, tarn, and slow river. Cross-reference physics/water_physics.md — dangerous, impassable in places, and cross-reference princess_quest_the_lake.md: **the Highmoore Lake is here.**

**THE STONECOUNTRY (east)**
Rising ground, limestone pavement, quarries, and the region's iron. Cross-reference highmoore_smiths.md — the forges are here.

**THE BURNT MARCH (south)**
Cross-reference house_system.md Section 5.4 — territory that has changed hands so many times that nobody farms it. Ruins, and cross-reference npcs/building_evolution.md decay states applied at scale.

### 2.4 The Roads

**Cross-reference:** mounted_travel.md Section 6.

Highmoore has real roads — old, well-cut, and dangerous. They connect the Houses and they are where the ambushes happen. Cross-reference house_system.md: **road security is a House's most visible political statement**, and a road that is safe tells the player who is strong.

## 3. THE PALETTE AND THE LIGHT

### 3.1 Golden Brown

**Cross-reference:** the user's own framing — Highmoore should recall *golden brown* immediately and permanently.

- **Ground:** heather in bloom is purple-brown; out of bloom it is gold, ochre, rust, and dead-grass fawn
- **Stone:** grey limestone in the east, dark millstone grit in the west
- **Sky:** overcast more often than not. Cross-reference highmoore_weather.md.
- **The Houses:** each has colours, and against the moor they are the only saturated things in frame. Cross-reference house_system.md.

### 3.2 The Light

**Cross-reference:** physics/sun_shadow_systems.md.

Highmoore's defining light is **low and raking.** The sun is rarely overhead; cloud is frequent; and the moments when the cloud breaks and a shaft crosses the moor are the region's signature and should be common enough to be reliable and rare enough to be an event.

**Design note:** cross-reference physics/sun_shadow_systems.md Section 3 — the shadow of a cloud crossing open moorland at speed is one of the best free visuals available and Highmoore should use it constantly.

### 3.3 The Contrast With the Archipelago

Cross-reference physics/lava_heat_physics.md, sand_desert_physics.md, vegetation_physics.md.

The archipelago is tropical saturation, volcanic red-black, and Fjordlund's blue-white. **Highmoore is the only region in the game with a muted palette**, and after eighty hours of jungle and sea it reads as a different genre.

## 4. THE CASTLES ON THE HORIZON

**Cross-reference:** house_system.md, house_arion.md, house_belos.md, the_lesser_houses.md, colonial_war/castle_catalog.md.

### 4.1 The Standard

**Cross-reference colonial_war/castle_catalog.md Principle 1.5** — no generic castles, ever. That standard applies to Highmoore in full, and Highmoore has more castles than the rest of the game combined.

**Each House castle has:** a distinct silhouette readable at three kilometres, a colour, a defensive character, and a reason it is where it is.

### 4.2 Visible From the Shelf

Six castles are in frame at the emergence, and the player will end up at four of them:

- **Arion** — white and gold, the furthest and the most beautiful, on rising ground in the Folds. Cross-reference house_arion.md — **it is the one the eye goes to**, and the design should ensure that.
- **Belos** — iron-grey, squat, in the Stonecountry. Cross-reference house_belos.md.
- Four lesser Houses at various distances. Cross-reference the_lesser_houses.md.

### 4.3 The Reading

**Cross-reference:** style_bible.md Pillar 2.

A player who looks at the skyline can read Highmoore's politics before speaking to anyone:
- Which castles have smoke and which do not
- Which have banners flying and which have bare poles
- **Which have building work happening** — cross-reference npcs/building_evolution.md
- Which are ruins

## 5. THE ATMOSPHERE OF A LAND AT WAR WITH ITSELF

### 5.1 The Central Fact

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_overview.md Section 5.4.

There is no colonial power here, no unifying enemy, and no memory of peace. The Houses fight each other because there has never been anything larger to fight, and **the war has no cause and no end and everyone has stopped asking about either.**

### 5.2 What This Looks Like

- **Fortified farms.** Cross-reference highmoore_settlements.md — every building over a certain size in the Folds has defensive features, and they are not new.
- **Burnt ground** that has been burnt more than once
- **Boundary stones** with multiple sigils carved over each other
- **Roads with watchtowers** at intervals, half of them manned
- **Graveyards that are too large for their villages**

### 5.3 What This Sounds Like

**Cross-reference:** npcs/regional_populations.md Section 10, physics/sound_physics.md.

Highmoore's ambient dialogue is exhausted rather than frightened. Cross-reference style_bible.md Pillar 1 — the register is blunt moorland Low.

*"...Belos took the Ashby holdings again. Third time. They'll lose it by spring, they always do."*

*"...my father fought at the ford, and his father, and I've a boy old enough now and he asks about the ford."*

*"...nobody remembers what started it. Nobody's asked in my lifetime. It's just what we've got."*

*"...they'll bury him at the church and put a stone up and in forty years someone'll ask who he was and nobody'll know."*

### 5.4 The Beauty Underneath

**Design law:** cross-reference highmoore_overview.md Section 1.3 — Highmoore must be **beautiful**, not grim. The oppression is in the situation, not the art direction.

The moor at dawn. The light shaft crossing it. A hall lit up at night with music coming out of it. The lake in the Waterlands. **The player should want to be here**, and that is what makes the Princess questline work.

## 6. FREE-ROAM CONTENT

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_overview.md Pillar 3 — everything here is optional.

### 6.1 What Is Out There

- **The Houses.** Cross-reference the_lesser_houses.md — twelve to eighteen, each with a lord, a specialty, and at least one piece of business
- **The settlements.** Cross-reference highmoore_settlements.md — villages, market towns, abbeys, and the fortified farms
- **The road encounters.** Ambushes, travellers, House patrols, and cross-reference npcs/regional_populations.md
- **The moor itself.** Standing stones, barrows, abandoned steadings, and cross-reference colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md — **Highmoore has its own dungeon set, and they are barrows**
- **Hunting.** Cross-reference highmoore_fauna.md and the Phase 4 fauna systems
- **The forges.** Cross-reference highmoore_smiths.md — a smithing tradition the archipelago has never seen

### 6.2 The Discovery Standard

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 3.2 (the no-marker rule).

Highmoore uses the same discipline. No question marks on the map. The player rides, and sees a thing, and goes to it.

**The exception:** roads and castles are visible at distance by design. Cross-reference Section 4.3 — Highmoore's readability at range *is* its navigation system.

### 6.3 The First Hour

A player who emerges from the Caves and simply rides has, in the first hour:
- Crossed open moor with the wind and the cloud shadows
- Passed a fortified farm and been watched from it
- Met a House patrol on a road and been questioned
- Seen Arion from three kilometres and ridden toward it because it is the most beautiful thing on the horizon

**Design note:** that last one is the Princess questline beginning, and the player does not know it, and nothing has prompted them.

## 7. WEATHER AND WIND

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_weather.md (full document), physics/weather_physics.md, physics/wind_systems.md.

### 7.1 The Wind Is Permanent

Highmoore is the game's wind region. Cross-reference physics/wind_systems.md — the wind is always present, directional, and it affects:
- Cloth and hair constantly. Cross-reference physics/cloth_hair_physics.md — **Highmoore is where that system earns its budget**
- Sound propagation. Cross-reference physics/sound_physics.md Section 3
- Arrows. Cross-reference physics/combat_physics.md — **and cross-reference princess_quest_the_lake.md**
- Fire. Cross-reference physics/fire_physics.md — and cross-reference princess_quest_belos.md

### 7.2 The Weather States

Cross-reference physics/weather_physics.md. Highmoore's distribution: overcast dominant, rain frequent, clear rare and valued, and **fog on the high moor**, which is dangerous.

### 7.3 The Rain

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_the_lake.md.

Highmoore rain is not tropical. It is grey, persistent, cold, and it comes in from the west with warning — a player can watch it arrive across the moor for two minutes before it reaches them.

**Design note:** the lake scene happens in this rain. Cross-reference physics/weather_physics.md — the two-minute approach is the scene's structure, and the player watches it come.

## 8. THE POPULATION

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_populations.md (full document), npcs/regional_populations.md.

### 8.1 Who Lives Here

- **The Houses** — nobility, household knights, retainers. High register.
- **The Yeomanry** — free farmers, the Folds' backbone, armed and organised. Middle register.
- **The Villages** — labourers, craftsmen, the church. Low register.
- **The Roads** — carters, drovers, pedlars, and the people who move between Houses and are trusted by none of them

### 8.2 The Register

**Cross-reference:** style_bible.md Pillar 1, highmoore_overview.md Section 11.

**Highmoore is the game's fifth register:** Anglo-noble High in the halls, blunt moorland Low in the villages, and a yeoman Middle that is unlike anything in the archipelago — direct, unimpressed, and not deferential.

### 8.3 How They See Jake

**Cross-reference:** npcs/regional_populations.md Section 10 (the gossip web).

He is from nowhere. **There is no "nowhere" in Highmoore's understanding of the world** — everyone is from a House's land, and Jake is not.

*"Whose man are you?"* is the first question everyone asks, and Jake has no answer, and cross-reference princess_quest_the_ball.md: **he eventually invents one.**

## 10. MVP CORE vs FULL VISION

### 10.1 MVP CORE

- The emergence shot with the full audio and visual inversion
- The switchback descent and the horse
- Three sub-regions: the High Moor, the Folds, the Stonecountry
- Six visible castles with distinct silhouettes, two enterable (Arion, Belos)
- The wind as a permanent systemic factor
- The road network with patrols and encounters
- The exhausted ambient register

### 10.2 FULL VISION

- All five sub-regions including the Waterlands and the Burnt March
- All twelve to eighteen Houses
- The full skyline-readability system (smoke, banners, building work, ruins)
- Highmoore's barrow dungeon set
- The complete weather system including high-moor fog
- The two-minute rain approach

## 11. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** Highmoore is the game's fifth register, and the yeoman Middle — direct, unimpressed, undeferential — exists nowhere else in Dark Arisen. *"Whose man are you?"*
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** the skyline is the politics. Boundary stones with sigils carved over each other. Graveyards too large for their villages. Fortified farms whose defences are not new.
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** *"they'll put a stone up and in forty years someone'll ask who he was and nobody'll know."* The Burnt March. A war with no cause that everyone has stopped asking about.
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** the yeomanry are funny in a dry, unimpressed way that no other region in the game does, and Highmoore's first two movements need it badly, because the third has none.
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** a land that has been taking revenge on itself for longer than anyone can remember, where nobody knows what started it and everyone has a stone in the churchyard. **Jake rides into this and then adds to it.**

## 13. CONCLUSION

The Region. Ninety seconds up in a counterweight lift with the light going gold, and then a shelf of rock and wind and no reverb at all — and the whole basin below, heather to the horizon with the wind moving across it in waves, roads that go somewhere, six castles at six distances with the furthest on the edge of sight, and a bell arriving seconds after it was struck.

Golden brown and slate and iron under a sky that is overcast more often than not, with cloud shadows running across open moor and a shaft of light breaking through often enough to be reliable and rarely enough to be an event. Fortified farms whose defences are not new. Boundary stones with three sigils carved over each other. Graveyards too big for their villages.

A land that has been at war with itself for longer than anyone remembers, where nobody knows what started it, where the first question anyone asks a stranger is *whose man are you* — and where the most beautiful thing on the skyline is white and gold and eight kilometres away, and the player rides toward it because it is beautiful, and nothing prompted them, and that is how it starts.

**Phase 8 Document 5 of ~30.**

**Gold to the horizon. Whose man are you?** 🏔️🐎

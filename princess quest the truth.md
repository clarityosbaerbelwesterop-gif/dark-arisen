## 3. CASSIAN'S PLAN

### 3.1 What He Wanted

**Three things, and he achieved all three:**

1. **The pregnancy explained.** A foreign visitor spent a night in the castle and everyone in the hall saw it. Cross-reference princess_quest_the_ball.md Section 7.6.
2. **The pregnancy ended, and the daughter with it.** Cross-reference Section 3.3 — he did not phrase it that way to himself.
3. **A weapon pointed at Belos.** Cross-reference Section 3.4.

### 3.2 The Mechanism

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_the_lake.md Section 9.2, the_princess.md Section 7.5.

**He corrupted nobody.**

He did not bribe an archer, plant a man in Belos colours, or give a single order that could be traced. Cross-reference house_system.md Section 4.3 — he knew which stretch of the Belos boundary is patrolled, he knew what a Belos patrol does to trespassers at range, and he made certain his daughter and a foreign witness would be standing on it at the right hour.

**The murder weapon is Highmoore's customary law, used correctly, by a nineteen-year-old doing his job.**

### 3.3 How He Arranged It

- **He dictated the false letter** (Section 6.1) and watched her write it
- **He suggested the lake.** Cross-reference Section 3.5 — and this is the detail that makes it unbearable, because **it was already her plan**, and he found out, and he did not stop her. He *timed* her.
- **He ensured Jake would be there.** The false letter's line — *"there is a track past the eastern paddock"* — is his.

### 3.4 The Weapon

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_belos.md.

He expected Jake to go to Belos. He expected it to be violent. He expected it to fail and to give Arion a grievance and a martyr.

**He did not expect the Crystal Katana.** Cross-reference crystal_katana.md — nobody in Highmoore has any framework for what happens at Belos, and cross-reference Section 8.2: **Cassian's plan works far beyond its design and he is dead before he learns it.**

### 3.5 What He Did Not Do

**He did not invent her death.** Cross-reference the_princess.md Section 7.1 — she had decided eight weeks earlier, entirely independently, and cross-reference Section 3.3: he learned of a plan already in motion and improved it.

**Design law:** the distinction matters enormously and the game never states it. **Cassian did not kill his daughter. He found out she was going to and made it useful.**

## 4. WHAT THE LETTER SAYS, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

**Cross-reference:** the_princess.md Section 9.3 for the full text.

### 4.1 The Structure

Two hundred and eighty words. Six subjects, none of them elaborated:

1. The lie about the ambassador
2. The false letter, disowned
3. Her father — **one sentence and a refusal**
4. Belos — **nine words, and one question**
5. The lake, and why there
6. Jake, and why him
7. The rabbits

### 4.2 The Rabbits

*"He had them killed when I was twelve, to teach me that I should not want things. It worked, mostly."*

**Cross-reference:** the_princess.md Section 6, house_arion.md Section 7.4, princess_quest_the_ball.md Section 7.4.

**Three touches across roughly ten hours of play:**
- Six well-built, maintained, empty hutches in the Arion gardens, found before the ball, unremarked
- Her mentioning them on the terrace and changing the subject — **the only subject she changes all night**
- This line

**The player assembles it. The game never states it.**

### 4.3 The Question

*"I told my father when I came home and he asked me who else knew. That was the whole of it. That was what he asked."*

**Cross-reference:** the_princess.md Section 9.4.

**The most damaging sentence in Dark Arisen, and it is about a question, not an act.**

### 4.4 The Refusal

*"It is my father's. I will not write more about that. You will understand it or you will not and either way there is nothing in the world you can do with it."*

**Design law:** cross-reference Rule Three. **That is the entire textual footprint.** No character expands on it. No document in the bible expands on it. It is one sentence and a refusal and it stays that way.

### 4.5 The Instruction

*"Do not do anything. Please. You will want to and it will not help and it will not be for me."*

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_overview.md Section 8.1, princess_quest_belos.md.

## 5. WHO KNEW

### 5.1 CASSIAN — Everything, Late

Cross-reference Section 3. He knew about Belos from the day she came home at sixteen. He learned of the pregnancy about two weeks after the ball. He learned of her plan shortly after that.

### 5.2 MAERWYN — Two Years

**Cross-reference:** house_arion.md Section 4.2, the_princess.md Section 8.4.

**Her mother has known for two years and has done nothing except be kind to her and drink more.**

**Design law:** this is never stated, confirmed, or addressed. The only evidence is one line at breakfast:

*"You're very kind to her. People are. It's the one thing I can say for this house — everyone is very kind to her."*

*(a pause half a second too long)*

*"Have some more of the white. It's the only good thing we make."*

**Cross-reference princess_quest_arion_falls.md Section 8** — she is alive at the end, in the castle, and Jake does not touch her, and cross-reference Section 8.3 below: **what happens to her afterward is not resolved by the game.**

### 5.3 ALYS — Enough

**Cross-reference:** house_arion.md Section 4.2, the_princess.md Section 4.2.

Her maid, twenty-two. **The only person Elowen told anything.** Not everything. Enough.

She was given the real letter, told where to leave it, and told to go. **She went.**

**Cross-reference princess_quest_arion_falls.md Section 4** — she is not at Arion when Jake comes back, and she is never found, and cross-reference Section 8.4: **a Full Vision thread exists and the game does not require it.**

### 5.4 BROTHER PADARN — Nothing, and Something

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_settlements.md Section 5.3, the_princess.md Section 4.2.

He taught her Latin. He thought she should have stayed at the abbey. **He was not joking and she knew he was not joking.**

He knew nothing. He suspected that a bright girl who could have had a life was not going to get one, which in Highmoore is not a suspicion, it is arithmetic.

**Cross-reference Section 7.2** — he writes the record.

### 5.5 ALDRIC BELOS — Part, Four Years Ago

**Cross-reference:** house_belos.md Sections 3.3 and 6.

He found out some of it. Not all. **He did not look for the rest**, and a truce held, and four hundred of his people did not die that year, and he had buried two sons at the ford.

He knew nothing about the pregnancy, the lake, or the plan. Cross-reference princess_quest_belos.md Section 7 — **he learns why Jake is there in the last ninety seconds of his life.**

### 5.6 THE HOUSEHOLD — Nothing

Cross-reference house_arion.md Section 4. Two hundred people who liked her.

## 6. THE TWO LETTERS

### 6.1 The False One

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_the_lake.md Section 2.2.

In her hand. Dictated. Signed **Elowen Arion**, in full.

**Contains:** the child is Jake's. Do not come. If you come, use the eastern track.

**Cross-reference Section 3.3** — the *"do not come"* is hers and genuine and her father let it stand **because he understood the man he was writing to better than she did.**

### 6.2 The Real One

**Cross-reference:** the_princess.md Section 9.3.

In her hand. Hers. Signed **E.**

### 6.3 The Signature

**Cross-reference:** the_princess.md Section 9.4.

She signed the false letter in full because he was watching. She signed the real one with a letter.

**Design law:** the game never points at this. **The player has both documents in the inventory and can look at them side by side and the difference is right there.**

## 7. WHAT SHE ACHIEVED

### 7.1 The Aim

**Cross-reference:** the_princess.md Sections 7.2 and 10.3, her letter.

*"If I die at home he will bury it in the churchyard and in the ledger and nothing will have happened at all. If I die on Belos land it is a killing, and this basin has procedures for killings, and somebody will have to write something down."*

### 7.2 The Record

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_settlements.md Section 5.2, the_princess.md Section 10.3.

**It exists.** The abbey at Halford Cross keeps the region's records. Brother Padarn wrote it. Cross-reference house_system.md Section 4.4 — a killing across a boundary requires a written entry, and it names her, and it names the ground, and it gives the date.

**One line in a ledger.**

### 7.3 What It Does Not Say

Nothing about her father. Nothing about Belos four years ago. Nothing about why she was there.

**Cross-reference the_princess.md Section 10.3** — it is the only thing she wanted, it is one line, and it outlasts both Houses.

### 7.4 The Player Can Read It

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_settlements.md Section 5.4.

Before the questline: **the abbey ledger already contains her eighteen months at Belos**, in a fair hand, with a date, and nothing else. A player who reads the records at Halford Cross in Movement Two has seen it and it meant nothing.

After: **the new entry is three pages further on.**

## 9. THE SECOND PLAYTHROUGH

**Cross-reference:** the_princess.md Section 8, princess_quest_the_ball.md.

### 9.1 What Changes

Nothing mechanical. Cross-reference princess_quest_overview.md Section 10.1.

### 9.2 What Is Different

- **The empty hutches** in the garden, an hour before she mentions rabbits
- **Her calm**, in a region defined by exhaustion
- **How fast she reads Jake**, and how completely she does not care what he is
- **The one subject she changes**
- **Maerwyn's line**, which is now the worst sentence in the game
- **Cassian's line at the gate** — *"she is not something that can be spent"* — which he means sincerely
- **The three goodbyes on the ride**
- **The boundary stone at minute seven**
- **The signature on the false letter**

### 9.3 The Design Statement

**Cross-reference:** style_bible.md Pillar 2.

**Every one of those is an object or a sentence, placed in the world, unremarked, with no camera emphasis and no music.**

A first playthrough is a love story that ends badly. A second is thirty hours of watching a nineteen-year-old say goodbye to a house full of people who are very kind to her.

## 12. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** the whole truth is delivered in one voice — hers, plain, on paper — against a game that has spent eighty hours in five registers.
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** two letters, two signatures, side by side in an inventory. Six empty hutches. Two ledger entries three pages apart in an abbey a player visited for no reason.
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** nothing depicted, ever. One sentence and a refusal. Nine words about Belos. The worst thing in it is a question. And she is buried correctly, by him, exactly as she predicted.
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** none. This is the one document in Phase 8 with nothing funny in it, and the ball and the lake are as funny as they are precisely so that this lands.
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** a man who did not have to corrupt anyone, who used a working legal custom and a truthful daughter's own plan, and who is dead before he learns his weapon worked far past its design. And a woman who wanted one line in a ledger, and got it, and it says almost nothing, and it outlasts both Houses.

## 14. CONCLUSION

What Was Actually Happening. A girl of fifteen sent to sleep in an enemy hall under an institution the whole region is proud of, which mostly works. A girl of sixteen who came home and told her father and was asked *who else knows.* Six rabbits killed when she was twelve to teach her not to want things, which worked, mostly.

A woman of nineteen who decided eight weeks before a stranger rode up the road, and who chose a lake eleven yards over a boundary because a death at home goes in a churchyard and a death across a line goes in a ledger, and somebody has to write something down.

And a father who corrupted nobody. Who bribed no archer and gave no order that could be traced. Who found out what she was going to do and did not stop her and instead arranged the hour, dictated a letter, watched her sign it in full, and made certain a foreign witness would be standing beside her — because that made her death an explanation, a grievance, and a weapon all at once.

He was right about all three and he is dead before he learns how right.

She wanted one line in an abbey ledger. She got it. It gives her name, the ground, and the date, and nothing else at all.

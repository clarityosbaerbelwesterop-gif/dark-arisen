# THE WIZARD — THE MAN WHO HELD THE LETTER

**Status:** Design Draft v1.0
**Phase:** Phase 8 — Highmoore (Movement Three — Document 20 of ~30)
**Related:** highmoore_overview.md, wizard_questline.md, light_elves.md, princess_quest_the_lake.md, princess_quest_belos.md, princess_quest_the_return.md, the_princess.md, highmoore_settlements.md, highmoore_music.md, npcs/the_named_dead.md, style_bible.md
**Purpose:** The complete character — who he is, what the game refuses to explain, the eleven days, his three reasons and the order he gives them in, his standing at Belos and its collapse, and the question Jake can ask him back.
**Inspirations:** Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy (Smiley — competence and complicity in the same body), The Third Man (Harry Lime's reasoning), Gandalf inverted, The Master and Margarita's Woland, Doctor Manhattan (foreknowledge as moral disease), Hannah Arendt, A Man for All Seasons

## 1. THE DESIGN BRIEF

### 1.1 What He Is For

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_overview.md Section 7.1.

The Wizard is the questline's conscience, and cross-reference princess_quest_belos.md Section 9: **his question at House Belos is the best beat in Phase 8.**

### 1.2 The Correction

**Cross-reference:** Section 5.

**He does not have clean standing to ask it.**

He held her letter for eleven days. He knew what was in it. He knew what Jake would do with it, and he knew what it would cost Jake, and he needed Jake for something else.

**Cross-reference Section 8.4** — and Jake can say so, to his face, in the burning court, and the Wizard has an answer, and it is bad.

### 1.3 The Result

**Two men in a courtyard full of dead people, each of whom has a genuine and unanswerable accusation against the other**, and one of them asks the question anyway because it still needs asking.

**Design intent:** cross-reference style_bible.md Pillar 5 — the moral authority in Phase 8's final scene is compromised, and the question survives it.

## 2. WHO HE IS

### 2.1 Quick Reference

**Name:** **Owain.** Cross-reference Section 2.4 — he gives it once, without a surname, and it is disappointingly ordinary.
**Called:** **the Wizard**, by everyone including himself, dryly, because it is not accurate and he stopped correcting it decades ago.
**Age:** appears mid-fifties. Cross-reference Section 3.2 — the game does not confirm this.
**Register:** cross-reference Section 4 — **a register that belongs to nobody in Highmoore.**

### 2.2 First Appearance

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_settlements.md Section 4.3, wizard_questline.md Section 2.

The Drover's Rest in Coldbeck. Corner table. Four days. He pays. He does not drink much. **The landlord will not go near the table and will not be the one to ask him to leave.**

**HUW PENNANT:** *"He's been there four days. He pays. He doesn't drink much. I'd rather he moved on and I'm not going to be the one who says so."*

### 2.3 What He Looks Like

Ordinary. Cross-reference highmoore_overview.md Section 4.3 — **no robe, no staff, no beard, no symbols.** Travelling clothes, worn, good quality, and mud on them.

**Cross-reference Section 3.3** — the only unusual thing about him is that people are uneasy and cannot say why, and cross-reference physics/sound_physics.md: **rooms go slightly quieter around him and nobody notices they have done it.**

### 2.4 The Name

**Cross-reference:** Section 8.3.

He gives it once, at House Belos, after the question, if Jake asks who he is.

**THE WIZARD:** *"Owain."*
*(a beat)*
**THE WIZARD:** *"That's all of it. There isn't a second part. People find that disappointing and I've stopped apologising for it."*

## 4. VOICE

### 4.1 The Register

**Cross-reference:** style_bible.md Pillar 1.

Precise, dry, unhurried, and completely unlike Highmoore's three registers. Cross-reference highmoore_region.md Section 8.2 — he is not noble High, not yeoman Middle, and not village Low.

**He sounds like somebody explaining something they have explained many times to people who did not listen.**

### 4.2 The Work Register

*"There is a thing in the north of this basin. It has been there some while. It is getting closer to the villages and the villages are noticing and the Houses are not, because the Houses are busy."*

*"I need someone who can fight and who is not sworn to anybody. You are the only man in Highmoore who fits that description and I have looked."*

### 4.3 The Dry Register

*"They call me the Wizard. It is inaccurate and I have given up. If it helps, think of me as a man with a specific and unfashionable trade."*

*"You want to know what I can do. Everyone does. I'll tell you what I can't do — I can't make anyone listen. Thirty years and that one hasn't shifted."*

### 4.4 The Register He Uses Once

**Cross-reference:** Section 5.4.

*"I have had this eleven days."*

## 5. THE ELEVEN DAYS

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_the_lake.md Section 2.1, wizard_questline.md Section 8.

### 5.1 The Fact

He received her letter eleven days before he gave it to Jake.

### 5.2 What He Knew

**Cross-reference:** Section 3.1 — how is not explained. **What is:**

- That she was pregnant
- That the letter said the child was Jake's
- **That it was not true**
- That Jake would go the moment he read it
- **That it would break him**

### 5.3 The Three Reasons, and the Order He Gives Them In

**Cross-reference:** Section 8.4, and this is the character's entire architecture.

He gives three reasons for withholding it. **He gives them in ascending order of honesty, and he knows he is doing it, and he does it anyway.**

**REASON ONE — the one he says first:**
*"There was a thing in the north that was going to kill several hundred people and you were the only man who could go and deal with it, and you would not have gone."*

**REASON TWO — the one he says when pressed:**
*"I have read a great many letters. That one was written by someone who had already decided something. You were not going to change it by arriving eleven days sooner."*

**REASON THREE — the one he says last, and only if asked a third time:**

*(and cross-reference Section 8.4 — this is the one that matters)*

*"...and I knew what it would do to you."*

*"I have watched a number of men be broken by a piece of paper. I did not want to hand you yours. So I did not, for eleven days, and I told myself the other two reasons in that order, in that exact order, every morning."*

*"I am aware of what that is. I have been aware of it since the fourth day."*

### 5.4 The Delivery

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_the_lake.md Section 2.1, wizard_questline.md Section 8.

**THE WIZARD:** *"Before you open that — I have had it eleven days."*

*(and he says it first, before Jake reads a word, and cross-reference Section 6.1: **that is the single decent thing he does in Phase 8** and it does not help anybody)*

**THE WIZARD:** *"You would have gone. That is the whole of my reasoning and I am aware of what it is worth."*

### 5.5 What It Cost

**Cross-reference:** the_princess.md Section 7, princess_quest_the_truth.md Section 2.

**Nothing.** Cross-reference the_princess.md Rule 1 — she had decided eight weeks before the ball, and eleven days would not have changed it.

**The Wizard is right about Reason Two and it does not matter at all**, and cross-reference Section 8.4: **Jake does not know that when he accuses him, and neither does the player.**

## 6. WHAT HE IS ACTUALLY LIKE

### 6.1 He Is Not a Manipulator

**Cross-reference:** Section 5.4, house_arion.md Section 3.2 for the same principle applied to Cassian.

**Design law:** the Wizard is not written as sinister, is never played as sinister, and cross-reference Section 5.4 — **he volunteers the eleven days before Jake opens the letter.** Nobody made him.

### 6.2 He Is Genuinely Trying to Help

Cross-reference light_elves.md — the threat in the north is real, it is going to kill people, the Houses are not going to deal with it because the Houses are busy fighting each other, and **he has been trying to get somebody to listen for a considerable time.**

### 6.3 He Is Tired

Cross-reference Section 4.3 — *"thirty years and that one hasn't shifted."*

**Cross-reference wizard_questline.md** — he is not enigmatic for effect. He is a man who has spent a long career being right about things nobody wanted to hear.

### 6.4 He Likes Jake

**Cross-reference:** wizard_questline.md Sections 4-6.

Across four to eight hours of the Light Elf campaign, **they become something close to friends.** He is good company, he is funny in a dry way, and cross-reference npcs/named_crew_deep_dives.md — **he is the only person in Highmoore who talks to Jake the way the crew do.**

**Design intent:** the eleven days have to cost the player something, and they only can if the player liked him first.

### 8.4 IF JAKE ASKS HIM BACK

**Cross-reference:** Section 1.3, Section 5.3 — **this is the character's purpose.**

**Player option, available after the question:**
- **[ELEVEN DAYS]**

Jake says nothing else. Two words.

**And the Wizard does not deflect.**

**THE WIZARD:** *"Yes."*

*(a long time — cross-reference highmoore_music.md Section 8.1: there is no music and the court is burning)*

**THE WIZARD:** *"I have three reasons and you have earned all of them, so I will give you all three, and I will give them to you in the order I say them to myself, which will tell you something."*

*(and cross-reference Section 5.3 — **he delivers all three, in ascending order of honesty, in the burning court, over about ninety seconds**)*

**THE WIZARD:** *"So no. I do not have the standing to ask you what I just asked you."*

**THE WIZARD:** *"I asked it anyway, because there is nobody else here, and it needed asking, and I would rather be the wrong man asking it than have nobody ask it at all."*

*(and then, and this is the last thing he says in Phase 8)*

**THE WIZARD:** *"They are still under the west range, Captain. That part is still true regardless of what I am."*

## 9. AFTER

### 9.1 He Is Gone

**Cross-reference:** Section 7.3, princess_quest_the_return.md.

He is not in the return sequence. He is not in the reconstruction content. **He does not appear again in Dark Arisen**, and cross-reference npcs/the_named_dead.md Section 2.3 — the game offers no closure on him.

### 9.2 The Light Elves Are Still Dealt With

**Cross-reference:** light_elves.md, wizard_questline.md.

Whatever else he is, **he was right about the thing in the north**, and it was going to kill several hundred people, and it does not, and that is because of the two of them.

**Design law:** the game never uses this to redeem him and never uses it to condemn him. **It is simply also true.**

### 9.3 What He Was Right About

Cross-reference Section 5.5. **Reason Two.**

Eleven days would not have changed anything. He was correct. **And the player finds this out from her letter, not from him**, and cross-reference the_princess.md Section 9.3 — she says she had decided, and the arithmetic is there for anyone who counts back.

**Design note:** the player can work out that the Wizard was right, alone, hours after accusing him, with the Wizard permanently gone.

## 12. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** he uses a register that belongs to nobody in Highmoore — precise, dry, and tired — and it is the sound of a man who has been right about unpopular things for thirty years.
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** a letter carried for eleven days in a coat pocket, handed over with the truth about it first. And a name with no second part.
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** he is the one who says what is under the west range, in a court where the sword's reverb has not finished dying, with no music.
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** *"They call me the Wizard. It is inaccurate and I have given up."* He is genuinely funny across the Light Elf campaign, and that is deliberate, and it is why the eleven days land.
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** he held the letter partly to protect a man, partly because he needed him, and partly because he was right — and he says so in that order, knowing what the order reveals. And then he asks the question anyway, having admitted he has no standing to, because there is nobody else in the courtyard.

## 14. CONCLUSION

Owain. That is all of it — there is no second part, and people find that disappointing, and he stopped apologising for it decades ago. Everybody calls him the Wizard, which is inaccurate, and he gave up correcting them thirty years back along with several other things.

Four days at a corner table in Coldbeck, paying, not drinking much, while a landlord who has heard everything decides not to be the one who asks him to move on. A man with a specific unfashionable trade and a thing in the north that is going to kill several hundred people, who has been trying to get somebody to listen for a considerable time and has one working line: *I need someone who can fight and who is not sworn to anybody, and I have looked.*

Good company for four to eight hours. Dry, funny, and the only person in Highmoore who talks to Jake the way his crew do — which is why it costs something when he takes a letter out of his coat and says, before it is opened, *I have had this eleven days.*

He has three reasons and he says them in ascending order of honesty and he knows what the order reveals. The third one is that he knew what it would do to a man to be handed a piece of paper like that, and he has been aware of what that means since the fourth day.

And at the end, standing in a burning court where the sound has not finished dying, having admitted he has no standing whatsoever, he asks the question anyway — because there is nobody else there, and it needs asking, and he would rather be the wrong man asking it than have nobody ask it at all.

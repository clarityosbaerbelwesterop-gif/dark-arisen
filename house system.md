**Inspirations:** Game of Thrones (house politics, the hostage-ward practice), Kingdom Come: Deliverance (grounded feudal mechanics), the real Border Reivers and Anglo-Scottish march law, Icelandic saga feud structures, Romeo and Juliet (a feud nobody can name the cause of)

## 1. THE SYSTEM'S PHILOSOPHY

### 1.1 Principle 1: Nobody Remembers Why

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_region.md Section 5.1.

The Houses have been fighting for at least six generations. **There is no origin story and the game never supplies one.** Ask a lord and he will tell you a version. Ask another lord and he will tell you a different version. Ask a farmer and he will say nobody remembers and shrug.

**Design law:** no document in Dark Arisen ever states the cause of the Highmoore war. Cross-reference bosses/ashen_wyrm.md, crystal_guardian.md Section 8 — **the same refusal, applied to politics instead of magic.**

### 1.2 Principle 2: The War Is Maintenance, Not Conquest

Nobody is trying to win. Cross-reference colonial_war/war_state_system.md for the contrast — the colonial war has aims, fronts, and an endpoint. **Highmoore's does not.** Holdings change hands and change back. The Ashby fields have been taken eleven times in forty years.

**What the Houses actually do:** maintain position, avoid catastrophic loss, marry advantageously, and survive.

### 1.3 Principle 3: The Practices Are Older Than the Grievances

Hostage-guesting, marriage-brokering, road-oath, and the feud settlement are functioning institutions with rules everyone follows. **Highmoore is not chaos.** It is an extremely orderly system for conducting a war that will not end.

**Design intent:** the horror is the orderliness. Cross-reference Section 4.

### 1.4 Principle 4: Jake Has No Place In It

Cross-reference highmoore_region.md Section 8.3 — *"Whose man are you?"*

Every person in Highmoore is located: a House's land, a House's service, or a House's protection. **Jake is unlocatable**, and this makes him simultaneously useless, dangerous, and — to certain people — extremely convenient. Cross-reference the_princess.md Section 7.

### 1.5 Principle 5: The System Produced Her

**Cross-reference:** the_princess.md, princess_quest_the_truth.md.

Everything in this document exists so that the Princess questline is not a story about one evil man. **It is a story about a working institution**, and Lord Arion did nothing that Highmoore's practices do not permit.

## 2. STRUCTURE

### 2.1 The Anatomy of a House

Every House has:

- **A castle** — cross-reference colonial_war/castle_catalog.md Principle 1.5: no generic castles, ever. Distinct silhouette readable at three kilometres.
- **Colours and a sigil** — cross-reference Section 3
- **Land** — measured in *holdings*, the region's unit of everything
- **A lord**, an heir, and a household
- **Household knights** — sworn, quartered, and few
- **Levy** — the yeomanry, called and disbanded seasonally
- **A specialty** — cross-reference Section 5

### 2.2 Scale

Cross-reference the_lesser_houses.md.

- **Great Houses (4):** Arion, Belos, and two others. Twenty-plus holdings each, forty to eighty household knights, a levy of several hundred.
- **Middle Houses (6-8):** eight to fifteen holdings. Real power, no independence.
- **Minor Houses (6-10):** one to five holdings. Sworn to a greater House, and their oaths move.

### 2.3 Oath and Movement

Minor Houses swear to greater ones and **the oaths change.** Cross-reference colonial_war/war_state_system.md — Highmoore has its own version of the Control meter, and it is measured in sworn Houses rather than territory.

**Design note:** cross-reference highmoore_reconstruction.md — this is the system Jake eventually reorganises.

## 3. SIGILS AND COLOURS

### 3.1 The Function

**Cross-reference:** style_bible.md Pillar 2, highmoore_region.md Section 4.3.

Colours are how Highmoore is read. Banners on castles, surcoats on knights, cloth on the levy, paint on boundary stones, and cross-reference mounted_travel.md Section 4.4 — even the horses.

**A player who learns the colours can read a road, a hall, and a battlefield without a word of dialogue.**

### 3.2 The Great House Colours

- **ARION** — white and gold. A stag's head, gold on white. Cross-reference house_arion.md.
- **BELOS** — iron-grey and green. A drawn bow, green on grey. Cross-reference house_belos.md.
- **CADWALL** — blue and black. A tower.
- **MERE** — russet and white. Three fish.

### 3.3 The Boundary Stones

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_region.md Section 5.2.

Every holding boundary is stoned, and the stones are recut when the holding changes hands. **The old carvings are not removed.** They are cut over, badly, and cross-reference style_bible.md Pillar 2: a boundary stone with five sigils layered on it is forty years of history in one object, and the player can count them.

## 4. THE PRACTICES

### 4.1 HOSTAGE-GUESTING

**The single most important institution in Phase 8.** Cross-reference the_princess.md Section 3, house_belos.md Section 6.

**What it is:** when two Houses agree a truce, each sends a child of the house to live in the other's castle. Not a prisoner — a **guest**, quartered honourably, fed at the high table, educated alongside the host's children.

**Why it works:** while your daughter is in their hall, you do not burn their fields.

**How long:** one to three years, typically. Ages twelve to nineteen.

**How it is regarded:** as civilised. As the *alternative* to war. Highmoore is genuinely proud of it, and Highmoore is right that it saves lives.

**Design law — the restraint rule:** the game establishes hostage-guesting as **ordinary and largely benign** across at least six other examples before the player learns what happened to Elowen Arion. Cross-reference the_lesser_houses.md — hostage-guests appear in multiple Houses, they are fine, they have friends, one of them is very funny.

**Cross-reference style_bible.md Pillar 3:** the institution is not the villain. The institution mostly works. That is what makes the exception unbearable.

### 4.2 MARRIAGE-BROKERING

Cross-reference house_arion.md Section 4.

Marriages settle feuds, transfer holdings, and buy oaths. **A daughter of a Great House is the most valuable single asset in Highmoore politics**, and every lord in the region understands her that way, and says so openly, and does not consider this cruel.

**Cross-reference the_princess.md Section 2:** Elowen has been valued out loud, in her presence, since she was eleven.

### 4.3 ROAD-OATH

Cross-reference highmoore_region.md Section 2.4.

A House swears the roads through its holdings. A traveller robbed on a sworn road is the House's failure and its debt.

**Design function:** cross-reference mounted_travel.md — **road safety tells the player who is strong.** A House losing its grip has bandits on its roads before it has enemies at its walls.

### 4.4 THE SETTLEMENT

Feuds are settled by payment, marriage, hostage, or single combat, and the terms are recorded and kept. Cross-reference Section 6.4 — Jake can be hired for the fourth kind.

### 4.5 THE BURNING SEASON

Cross-reference highmoore_weather.md. Raiding happens in a season, by convention: after harvest, before the wet. **Everyone knows when it is coming.** Cross-reference highmoore_region.md Section 5.3 — the ambient dialogue counts down to it.

## 6. HOW JAKE INTERACTS

### 6.1 The Unlocatable Man

Cross-reference Principle 1.4. Jake belongs to no House, and every interaction begins with someone trying to locate him.

**Three responses available to the player:**
- **The truth** — *"I'm a ship's captain from across the mountain."* Nobody believes this. There is no across-the-mountain.
- **A lie** — cross-reference princess_quest_the_ball.md: **Jake invents an ambassador and parents in government**, and it works, and it works for the rest of the questline.
- **Nothing** — refusing to answer is read as being someone's man and hiding it, which is worse.

### 6.2 Guest-Right

Cross-reference highmoore_settlements.md. A stranger admitted to a hall is a guest, and guest-right is real and observed: fed, housed, and unharmed while under the roof.

**Cross-reference princess_quest_the_ball.md Section 8** — Lord Arion throws Jake out in the morning. **He does not have him killed, and he could have.** Guest-right held.

### 6.3 Work Available

- **Escort and road work** — cross-reference mounted_travel.md
- **Raiding and defence** in the burning season
- **Recovery** — Highmoore Houses lose things and pay to have them found
- **Single combat** — cross-reference Section 4.4: Jake can be a House's champion in a settlement, and this is the fastest way to become known
- **Nothing** — cross-reference highmoore_overview.md Pillar 3: none of it is required

### 6.4 Standing

Cross-reference npcs/regional_populations.md Section 10 (the gossip web).

Highmoore has no reputation *meter*, and cross-reference colonial_war/war_state_system.md Section 8.1 — the same design law. **Standing is read in the world:** whether a gate opens, whether a lord uses Jake's name, whether the yeomanry stop calling him *"you."*

## 7. THE HOUSE WAR AS A SYSTEM

### 7.1 What It Simulates

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/war_state_system.md — a deliberately lighter version.

Holdings change hands. Oaths move. Roads become safe and unsafe. The player can influence this and cross-reference highmoore_overview.md Pillar 3 — **the game never asks them to.**

### 7.2 What Jake Can Do

- Fight for a House in the burning season
- Champion a settlement
- Break a road (or make one safe)
- Cross-reference princess_quest_belos.md — **destroy a Great House entirely**, which has never happened in Highmoore's recorded history and which the system has no procedure for

### 7.3 The Aftermath Hook

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_reconstruction.md.

After the questline, Highmoore's balance is broken — one Great House gone, another decapitated. Cross-reference colonial_war/war_state_system.md Section 4.6 (the Fork): **the same vacuum logic applies**, and what fills it is the reconstruction content.

## 8. THE ORDERLINESS

**Cross-reference:** Principle 1.3, style_bible.md Pillar 3.

### 8.1 The Design Statement

Highmoore's war has: a season, rules of engagement, a settlement procedure, a hostage convention, a guest-right taboo, and a body of customary law that every lord and most farmers can recite.

**It is a very well-run atrocity**, six generations deep, and everybody involved considers themselves civilised, and by the standards available to them they are.

### 8.2 The Ambient Register

Cross-reference npcs/regional_populations.md, highmoore_region.md Section 5.3.

*"...Belos'll have the Ashby fields till spring and then Cadwall'll want them and then it'll be somebody's turn to die at the ford again. It's like the weather, love. You don't ask the weather why."*

*"...we sent our Meg to Cadwall two winters. Best two years of her life, she says. Came back with better manners than her brothers."*

*"...they'll settle it. They always settle it. Then in four years they'll unsettle it."*

### 8.3 The Trap

**Design law:** the player must find Highmoore's politics **charming** before the questline breaks it. The pageantry, the colours, the hostage children playing together in a courtyard, the formal courtesies, the settlement rituals.

Cross-reference the_princess.md, princess_quest_the_truth.md — **all of it is real, and none of it protected her, and the practices that failed her are the same practices the player has spent twenty hours enjoying.**

## 10. MVP CORE vs FULL VISION

### 10.1 MVP CORE

- Four Great Houses with colours, sigils, castles, and specialties
- Hostage-guesting fully established with at least three benign examples before the questline
- Guest-right, road-oath, and the burning season
- The *"whose man are you"* interaction and the three response types
- Boundary stones with layered carvings
- Standing read in the world, no meter

### 10.2 FULL VISION

- All 16-22 Houses with moving oaths
- The full settlement procedure including single combat as employment
- Marriage-brokering as playable content
- The holding-exchange simulation across chapters
- The complete post-questline vacuum
- Six or more benign hostage-guest characters, one of whom is very funny

## 11. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** the lords' Anglo-noble High, the yeoman Middle that is unimpressed by all of it, and the village Low that compares the war to the weather. Three registers describing the same institution and disagreeing about nothing.
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** boundary stones with five sigils cut over each other. Banners. Surcoats. Horses. The colours are the entire political information system.
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** *"somebody's turn to die at the ford again."* A war with a season, a procedure, and no cause anyone remembers, six generations deep.
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** Meg came back from two years' hostage-guesting with better manners than her brothers. The yeomanry's total lack of reverence for any of it. **Highmoore's politics must be charming**, and this is where the charm lives.
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** a functioning, orderly, six-generation revenge machine that everyone administers correctly and nobody can explain — and Jake will ride into it and do something it has no procedure for.

## 13. CONCLUSION

Highmoore's Politics. Six generations of war with a season, a settlement procedure, a hostage convention, and a body of law every farmer can recite — and no origin anybody can name. Boundary stones with five sigils cut over each other. Roads a House swears and answers for. Children sent to sleep in their enemies' halls so that nobody burns anybody's fields, and it works, and Meg came back with better manners than her brothers.

A very well-run atrocity, administered by people who consider themselves civilised, and who by the standards available to them are.

The player is supposed to find it charming. That is a design requirement, not a suggestion.

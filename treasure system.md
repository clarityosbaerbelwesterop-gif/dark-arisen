# TREASURE SYSTEM — WHAT IS WORTH FINDING

**Status:** Design Draft v1.0
**Phase:** Phase 10 — Presentation & Technical (FINAL — Document 6 of 6)
**Related:** economy_core.md, progression_overview.md, skill_tree.md, colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md, colonial_war/regional_dungeon_catalog.md, colonial_war/secret_bosses.md, colonial_war/castle_catalog.md, colonial_war/indigenous_liberation_alliance.md, npcs/black_market_fences.md, npcs/luxury_merchants.md, highmoore_smiths.md, style_bible.md
**Purpose:** The treasure system — the five treasure classes, why most of them are not money, the map economy, the nine state treasures, buried hoards, Highmoore's version, the return question, and the design law that the best treasure in the game cannot be spent.
**Inspirations:** Sea of Thieves' map-reading (the good part), Elden Ring (a reward is a verb or a story), Red Dead Redemption 2's treasure chains, real Age of Sail prize law, and the rejection of the loot-chest economy

## 1. THE LAWS

### 1.1 Most Treasure Is Not Money

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 7.2, economy_core.md Section 3.1.

**Dungeons do not pay in coin.** By Chapter 8 a player may hold hundreds of thousands of doubloons, and cross-reference economy_core.md Section 4: **most of it is committed.**

**Design law:** a treasure whose only property is a number stops being interesting at Chapter 6. **Roughly 15% of treasure in Dark Arisen is money.**

### 1.2 The Best Treasure Cannot Be Spent

**Cross-reference:** Section 4, colonial_war/castle_catalog.md Section 13.

**The nine state treasures are documents.** Cross-reference colonial_war/castle_catalog.md Section 13.2 — collecting all nine produces the complete written record of two empires' conduct, and **it is worth nothing at any merchant in the game.**

### 1.3 Treasure Is Found, Not Dropped

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 3.2 (the no-marker rule).

**No loot chests with contextual highlight. No sparkle. No pickup prompt visible at range.**

Cross-reference animation_system.md Section 8 — **everything is reached for.**

### 1.4 Most of It Belongs to Somebody

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/regional_dungeon_catalog.md Section 8.4, npcs/black_market_fences.md Section 6.2.

**Approximately forty artifacts across the game belong to living communities**, and cross-reference Section 7: sell, keep, or return, asked forty times, and cross-reference colonial_war/secret_bosses.md Section 2.1: **one lock in the game counts the answer.**

## 2. THE FIVE CLASSES

### 2.1 COIN — ~15%

### 2.4 MAP — ~15%

**Cross-reference:** Section 5.

### 2.5 OBJECT — ~20%

**Cross-reference:** Section 7.

Cultural artifacts, grave-goods, sacred items, and cross-reference npcs/luxury_merchants.md Section 3.3: **Doña Carmen Ruiz will refuse to buy some of them and say why.**

## 5. MAPS

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 3.1, indigenous_liberation_alliance.md Section 5.2.

### 5.1 Two Kinds

**COLONIAL SURVEY MAPS** — legible immediately, and cross-reference colonial_war/regional_dungeon_catalog.md Section 2.2: **one of them is wrong in an informative way.**

**ANCIENT MAPS** — written in cultural map-languages. **A map Jake cannot read shows him nothing**, and cross-reference colonial_war/indigenous_liberation_alliance.md Section 5.2: the languages are taught by the networks at trust, not sold.

### 5.2 The Chain

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/regional_dungeon_catalog.md Section 8.2.

**Dungeons contain maps to dungeons.** The Green Throat's survey maps lead to two sites; the Hollow City's ancient map opens two Tier E; the Singing Caverns' star-map opens Ashenmoor's; the Deep Harbour's charts open two feared-water regions.

**Design intent:** **finding one dungeon is finding three.**

### 5.3 Reading Is a Skill Node

Cross-reference skill_tree.md Section 3.3 — **reading an ancient map in its own language is one of the twelve specific-act Marks.**

## 6. BURIED HOARDS

**Cross-reference:** Section 1.3, npcs/black_market_fences.md.

### 6.1 The Chain Structure

**Not X-marks-the-spot.** Each hoard is a **three-to-five step chain**: a partial map or a letter, a landmark, a second document, and a place.

**Twelve chains across the archipelago. Four in Highmoore.**

### 6.2 What Is In Them

Coin, yes — cross-reference Section 2.1 — **and the last step of nine of the sixteen is not coin.** Cross-reference style_bible.md Pillar 2: it is somebody's, and cross-reference npcs/the_named_dead.md: **it is usually clear who, and they are dead.**

### 6.3 The One That Is Nothing

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/regional_dungeon_catalog.md Section 7.2 (The First House).

**One chain in Dark Arisen terminates in nothing at all.** Cross-reference colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Principle 1.5 — the deliberate exception, and the game lets the player complete it and receive nothing, and never explains.

## 7. THE RETURN QUESTION

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/regional_dungeon_catalog.md Section 8.4, npcs/black_market_fences.md Section 6.2.

### 7.1 Asked Forty Times

**Sell · keep · return.**

Cross-reference npcs/black_market_fences.md Section 6.2 — Tito Diego's question: *"Which captain are you?"*

### 7.2 The Economics

- **Sell:** cross-reference npcs/luxury_merchants.md Section 8.1 — 500 to 250,000 doubloons.
- **Keep:** cross-reference mechanics/inventory_crafting.md — some are usable and most are not.
- **Return:** **+5 network strength**, cross-reference colonial_war/indigenous_liberation_alliance.md Section 3.3, **and something that cannot be bought back.**

### 7.3 The Counted Lock

**Cross-reference:** colonial_war/secret_bosses.md Section 8.1.

**The Harbour That Was First** — Tito Diego's tier — is opened by a **number**: the total artifacts returned rather than fenced across the whole game.

**Cross-reference colonial_war/secret_bosses.md Section 8.2:** *"you have given back more than you have kept. That is the only reason I am telling you this. If it had been the other way I would have let you die not knowing."*

**Design law:** cross-reference colonial_war/secret_bosses.md Section 2.1 — **the number is never displayed.** A player who fenced everything cannot open that door and the game will never tell them why.

## 8. HIGHMOORE

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_region.md Section 6.1, the_lesser_houses.md Section 4.5, highmoore_smiths.md.

### 8.1 The Barrows

Highmoore's dungeon set. Cross-reference colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md — **grave-goods**, and cross-reference the_lesser_houses.md Section 4.5: **House Brae keeps Highmoore's oldest records and cannot read most of them**, which means the region's own people cannot tell Jake whose barrow it is.

### 8.2 Silver

Cross-reference economy_core.md Sections 1.2 and 2.3 — **Highmoore treasure is in silver marks and does not convert**, which means a hoard found in the Stonecountry is spendable only on a bridge, a forge, or a wall.

### 8.3 The One That Is Not Treasure

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_reconstruction.md Section 6.4, princess_quest_arion_falls.md Section 4.5.

**A letter on a pillow in a room nobody cleared.**

Cross-reference the_princess.md Section 9 — it is findable, months or years later, by a player who walked past the open door, and cross-reference highmoore_reconstruction.md Section 6.4: **reading it then is the worst moment Dark Arisen contains, and nothing warns anyone.**

**It is in the treasure document because a player will find it the way they find treasure: by going into a room and looking.**

## 9. IMPLEMENTATION

```cpp
struct Treasure {
    TreasureClass  cls;         // Coin, Material, Document, Map, Object
    OwnerCulture   belongsTo;   // NONE for ~60%; §7 for the rest
    bool           counted;     // TRUE if it feeds the returned-count lock §7.3
    PlaceID        origin;      // for the return path
    // no rarity tier, no colour code, no drop table
};
```

**Prohibited:** rarity colour-coding · randomised loot tables · a "treasure found" counter in the UI · **any display of the returned count.**

## 10. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1:** Tito Diego's question, Doña Carmen refusing a sale and saying why, and Brae's lord holding records he cannot read.
- ✅ **Pillar 2:** **twenty percent of all treasure in this game is paperwork**, and the nine most valuable objects in it are documents worth nothing at any counter.
- ✅ **Pillar 3:** nine of sixteen hoard chains end in something that belonged to somebody who is dead and usually identifiable. One ends in nothing.
- ✅ **Pillar 4:** a survey map that is wrong in an informative way.
- ✅ **Pillar 5:** forty times the game asks *sell, keep, or return*, never scores it, never shows the tally — and one door at the bottom of the ocean opens only for a captain who gave back more than he kept, and closes silently on one who did not.

## 12. CONCLUSION

What Is Worth Finding. Fifteen percent of it is money, in a game where by Chapter 8 the money is already committed to soldiers who eat forever and castles that run at a loss. Thirty percent is material for two craftspeople who need reputation and months more than they need coin. **Twenty percent is paperwork** — ledgers, patrol reports, sealing orders with a name on them, and four sets of labour records from four unrelated places that together let freed families find each other across a hundred years.

Nine state treasures that are all documents and are worth nothing at any counter in the world, and which together are the complete written record of two empires' conduct, and one of which is handed over voluntarily by a woman who kept the copies and has been waiting for somebody to ask.

Sixteen hoard chains of three to five steps each, nine of which end in something that belonged to a dead person you can name, and one of which ends in nothing at all and never explains.

Forty objects that belong to somebody, a question asked forty times, a tally the game keeps and never shows, and one door at the bottom of the ocean that opens for a captain who gave back more than he kept.

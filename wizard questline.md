### 1.1 It Is Real

**Cross-reference:** the_wizard.md Section 4.2.

**Design law:** the Light Elf campaign is not a manipulation dressed as a crisis. Cross-reference light_elves.md — the threat is genuine, it is worsening, Ellis has been right for nine years, and nobody will act.

### 1.2 It Is the Gap

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_overview.md Sections 2.1 and 6.

**Its structural job is to take Jake away for four to eight hours of play** while a woman thirty miles west finishes making a decision she made before he arrived.

**Cross-reference the_wizard.md Section 3.3** — eleven days is how long the campaign takes, and eleven days is how long Ellis held the letter, and those are the same eleven days.

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_settlements.md Section 4.3, the_wizard.md Section 2.

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_the_ball.md Section 8.4 — Jake has been walked to the Arion gate, given a grey, and asked not to come back.

**ELLIS:** *"You're the one they threw out of Arion."*

*(a beat)*

**ELLIS:** *"Don't look like that. Everyone in this room knows. Two hundred people watched you dance and a stable boy watched you leave and this basin has nothing else to do."*

**Cross-reference:** the_wizard.md Section 2.2, house_system.md Section 6.1.

*"Whose man are you?"* — and the answer is nobody, and that is the entire recruitment.

**ELLIS:** *"Every man in this basin is somebody's. You can ride onto Belos ground, Cadwall ground, Mere ground, and nobody has to explain you afterward."*

**ELLIS:** *"I need someone who can go everywhere. I have needed one for nine years."*

## 3. THE REFUSAL OF THE LORDS

### 3.1 Stage One — The Rounds

**Cross-reference:** the_lesser_houses.md, house_system.md Section 6.

Ellis sends Jake to four Houses with the same request and **all four say no**, in four different registers, and cross-reference style_bible.md Pillar 1: the four refusals are a register showcase.

**LORD GWILYM CADWALL** (blue and black, cautious, never lost a holding):
*"I have outlived four Belos lords by declining to be interesting. Your friend has been asking me this for nine years and my answer has improved with practice: no, and have some wine."*

**LADY ANWEN MERE** (russet and white, the bog):
*"I lose two or three a year to it. My grandmother lost two or three a year to it. Show me the year somebody lost none and I will listen to you about it getting worse."*

**LADY ISOLDE THORN** (black and white, iron, sells to everyone):
*"I sell to Arion and Belos both and I will be here when they are not. I do not go up onto the moor after the light and neither does anybody who works for me. That is my policy on the matter and it has kept sixty people alive."*

**SER EDMUND ASHBY** (green and yellow, the table everyone eats off):
*"I'd help. I would. I've nine holdings and I had eleven in spring. Come back when I've a spare man."*

### 3.2 What the Refusals Establish

**Cross-reference:** light_elves.md Section 4.

**Nobody disbelieves in the Light Elves.** Cross-reference house_system.md Principle 1.3 — Highmoore has *practices* about them, observed absolutely, by people who describe them as superstition.

**The lords are not sceptics. They are people who have folded a permanent loss rate into the accounts.**

## 4. THE CAMPAIGN — FIVE STAGES

### 4.1 STAGE ONE — THE TAKINGS

**Cross-reference:** light_elves.md Section 3.

Investigation. Four villages, four families, four accounts, and cross-reference the_wizard.md Section 5.4: **all four describe the light identically and the figure differently.**

**What Jake does:** rides, listens, reads the abbey records at Halford Cross (cross-reference highmoore_settlements.md Section 5.2), and cross-reference light_elves.md Section 3.4: **counts.**

**The finding:** cross-reference light_elves.md Section 5 — the rate has roughly tripled in nine years and **nobody has counted before because nobody keeps the records in one place except an abbey nobody visits.**

### 4.2 STAGE TWO — THE ONE WHO CAME BACK

**Cross-reference:** light_elves.md Section 6, npcs/children_and_families.md.

A man in his forties, taken eleven years ago at nineteen, returned after four days.

**Design law — the protection and restraint standards:** cross-reference npcs/children_and_families.md, style_bible.md Pillar 3. **Nothing is depicted.** He is not damaged in any visible or dramatic way. He is polite, employed, married, and entirely functional.

**And he cannot describe four days.** Not *will not.* Cannot.

**THE MAN:** *"I've had eleven years to make a sentence out of it and I have not got one. My wife has stopped asking and I do not blame her."*

**THE MAN:** *"I'll tell you the only thing I have. It was not unkind. That is not the same as saying it was kind and I want you to hear the difference."*

### 4.3 STAGE THREE — THE PRACTICES

**Cross-reference:** house_system.md Principle 1.3, light_elves.md Section 4.

Jake learns Highmoore's customs about the light — the hours, the places, the charms nailed over doors, the reason nobody stands in a shaft on open moor.

**And cross-reference light_elves.md Section 5.2: the practices are failing**, and Ellis's nine-year argument is that they are failing because something changed and not because people stopped observing them.

**Cross-reference physics/sun_shadow_systems.md, physics/wind_systems.md** — the player has now spent twenty hours watching cloud shadows and light shafts cross open moorland as Highmoore's signature visual, and **from this stage forward the player watches them differently.**

### 4.4 STAGE FOUR — THE PLACES

**Cross-reference:** light_elves.md Section 7, colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md.

Three sites on the High Moor. Cross-reference highmoore_region.md Section 2.3 — standing stones, a barrow, and a stretch of ground where nothing grows.

**Cross-reference colonial_war/dungeon_design_philosophy.md Section 5** — Highmoore's puzzle language is its own, and it is **the practices themselves**: the hours, the placements, and what the charms are actually doing.

**Design note:** a player who paid attention in Stage Three solves Stage Four. A player who did not, does not.

### 4.5 STAGE FIVE — THE SHAFT

**Cross-reference:** light_elf_boss.md.

The boss.

## 6. THE ELEVEN DAYS AS PLAYABLE TIME

### 6.1 The Structure

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_overview.md Section 6.3.

Four to eight hours of play. Cross-reference mounted_travel.md Section 6.1 — **no fast travel**, and the campaign is deliberately spread across the whole basin so that the eleven days are ridden rather than skipped.

### 6.2 What Else Happens In Them

**Cross-reference:** highmoore_overview.md Pillar 3.

The player is free. Cross-reference the_lesser_houses.md Section 7 — House business, hunting, the barrows, Thorn's crafting, the butts at Belos.

**Design note:** cross-reference house_belos.md Section 7.1 — **a player may well spend an evening losing money to Belos yeomen at the butts during these eleven days.**

### 6.3 What Is Not Signalled

**Cross-reference:** princess_quest_overview.md Section 5.3.

**Nothing at all points west.** No letter arrives. No rumour reaches Coldbeck. No NPC mentions Arion.

Cross-reference the_wizard.md Section 3.1 — **the letter is in Ellis's coat the entire time.**

## 10. STYLE BIBLE COMPLIANCE

- ✅ **Pillar 1 (Staggered Register):** four lords refusing the same request in four registers, and Ellis in a fifth that belongs to none of them.
- ✅ **Pillar 2 (Lore Through Objects):** charms nailed over doors. Abbey records nobody has ever added up. A letter in a coat for eleven days.
- ✅ **Pillar 3 (Layered Death):** a man who has had eleven years to make a sentence and has not got one, and *"it was not unkind — that is not the same as saying it was kind."*
- ✅ **Pillar 4 (Layered Humor):** Huw Pennant's *"sit anywhere, not there."* Cadwall's answer improving with practice. Ashby offering a man he does not have.
- ✅ **Pillar 5 (Theme — Revenge):** eleven days in which Jake does genuine good for a man who is keeping something from him for two reasons he cannot rank — and the player spends some of those days losing money at Belos butts to men they will kill in a week.

## 11. CONCLUSION

The Eleven Days. A pub in Coldbeck with an empty ring of chairs around one table, and a man who says *sit anywhere, not there*, and another who says *you're the one they threw out of Arion, don't look like that, this basin has nothing else to do.*

Four lords who refuse in four different registers and none of whom disbelieve a word of it — because Highmoore does not disbelieve in the light, Highmoore has *practices* about it and a loss rate folded into the accounts, and a lady of the Waterlands who says show me the year somebody lost none.

A man of forty who was taken at nineteen and came back after four days and has had eleven years to make a sentence out of it and has not got one, and who wants it understood that *it was not unkind* is not the same as saying it was kind.

And eleven days of riding a basin doing something that genuinely needed doing, while thirty miles west a woman finishes what she started eight weeks ago, and a letter sits in a coat pocket the entire time.
